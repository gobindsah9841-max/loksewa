## 3.2 Marks arithmetic and negative marking

**Rule:** correct answer **+2**; wrong answer **−0.4** (20% of 2); blank **0**.

**Net score = (2 × correct) − (0.4 × wrong)**

| Correct | Wrong | Blank | Net score |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 0 | 30 | 40.0 |
| 22 | 10 | 18 | 40.0 |
| 25 | 25 | 0 | 40.0 |
| 30 | 10 | 10 | 56.0 |
| 35 | 15 | 0 | 64.0 |
| 40 | 10 | 0 | 76.0 |
| 45 | 5 | 0 | 88.0 |

**Should you guess?** Let *p* be your chance of being right. The expected gain from answering is 2*p* − 0.4(1 − *p*) = 2.4*p* − 0.4.

| Your position on a question | Chance of being right | Expected gain per question |
|---|---|---|
| Pure blind guess among four options | 25% | **+0.2** |
| One option removed | 33.3% | **+0.4** |
| Two options removed | 50% | **+0.8** |
| Break-even point | 16.7% | 0 |

**What the arithmetic says**

- With a 20% penalty, answering has a positive expected value whenever your chance is above 16.7%. Even a blind guess is slightly positive on average.
- "On average" is not a promise. Ten blind guesses will most often give 2 or 3 correct, but they can give none, which costs 4 marks.
- **Working rule:** always answer when you can remove at least one option. For a pure blind guess, answer only if you are already safely above your target; if you are near a cut-off, leave it blank.

## 3.3 What score to aim for

- The **pass mark is 40** in Koshi, Madhesh, Bagmati, Gandaki and Sudurpashchim, and **45%** in Lumbini. The Karnali pass mark could not be read.
- The syllabus fixes only the pass mark. How many candidates are called to the next stage is decided by the commission for each advertisement. **Do not treat the pass mark as your target.**
- **Where Paper I counts in the merit list** (Koshi, Madhesh, Bagmati, Gandaki, Sudurpashchim): every extra mark stays with you until the final result. Aim as high as you can.
- **Where Paper I is a screening test** (Lumbini, Karnali): you need to cross the cut-off with a safe margin. After that, extra marks give no benefit, so move your study time to the subjective papers.
- This book gives no "expected cut-off" figures, because no official cut-off data were verified.

## 3.8 Three methods that raise marks

**1. The error log.** Keep one notebook. Each wrong answer gets one line: the question point, the right fact, and why you were wrong (did not know / confused two facts / misread / careless sum). Before the examination, read the log, not the book.

**2. Spaced revision.** Revise a topic after 1 day, 7 days and 21 days. Three short revisions hold a fact better than one long one.

**3. Fact families.** Learn facts in groups that examiners can mix: largest / smallest / longest / highest; first / last; east-to-west order; pairs such as place–district, peak–height, treaty–year. The tables in this book are built this way.

**For facts that change** (population, forest cover, installed electricity capacity, budget size, office-holders): learn the figure together with its year and its source. Every such fact is marked 🔄 in this book.

**For facts on which sources disagree** (marked ⚖️): learn both versions and which one is the traditional textbook answer.

## 3.9 How questions are labelled, and the past-question policy

| Label | Used when | Count so far |
|---|---|---|
| `[PYQ – Verified]` | The question was found in an official question paper or official answer key, and the examination and year can be cited | **0** |
| `[Pattern-Based – Reconstructed]` | The question follows an official format — a Level II type or a model question printed in a syllabus — but the wording is the author's | Used |
| `[Expected – Practice]` | An original practice question on a point named in the official syllabus | Used |

**Why there are no `[PYQ – Verified]` questions yet.** During this research the commissions' websites yielded syllabi and notices, but no question paper or answer key that could be opened and read. Coaching sites and mobile applications publish "previous year questions", but they give no way to check the paper, set or year. Printing such questions as "previous year questions" would be dishonest, so they are not used.

**What this means in practice**

- No claim is made that any question in this book "was asked" or "is repeated".
- "Priority" in this book reflects the **official number of questions per unit**, not past-paper frequency.
- If you hold an official question paper, it can be added to the book with the verified label.

## 3.10 Method in the examination hall

**Suggested division of the 45 minutes**

| Province type | First | Second | Last |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 GK + 20 mental ability | GK: 15 minutes (about 30 seconds each) | Mental ability: 25 minutes (about 75 seconds each) | 5 minutes: marked questions and checking the answer sheet |
| Koshi 35 + 15 | GK: 18 minutes | Mental ability: 22 minutes | 5 minutes |
| Bagmati 40 + 10 | GK: 23 minutes | Mental ability: 17 minutes | 5 minutes |

**Three passes**

1. **First pass:** answer every question you know at once. Put a mark beside any question that needs thought, and move on.
2. **Second pass:** go back to the marked questions. Remove options. Answer if at least one option is removed.
3. **Final pass:** check that each answer is filled against the correct question number. One shifted row can spoil many answers.

**Checkpoints for a 30 + 20 paper:** minute 15 — GK finished; minute 30 — at least 12 mental-ability questions done; minute 40 — second pass finished.

**Reasoning questions:** if a question has taken 90 seconds and you are not close, mark it and leave it. One hard question is worth the same 2 marks as an easy one.

## 3.11 How to handle Level II questions

About 12 of the 30 GK questions in Madhesh, Gandaki, Lumbini and Sudurpashchim are Level II.

| Type | Method |
|---|---|
| **True/false combinations** ("Which statements are correct? 1 and 2 / 2 and 3 / …") | Find the one statement you are sure about. It normally removes two options. |
| **Matching** (List A with List B) | Fix the one pair you know for certain; check which options contain it. You rarely need all four pairs. |
| **Sequence** (arrange east to west, old to new, large to small) | Find the first or the last item. It usually leaves only one or two options. |
| **Odd one out** | Name the common feature of three items aloud. The fourth is the answer. Beware of a second possible feature. |
| **Cause and effect / assertion and reason** | Test each statement alone first. Only then ask whether the reason really explains the assertion. |
| **Fill in the blank** | Read the sentence with each option placed in the blank; grammar often removes one. |

## 3.13 Common mistakes in preparation

1. Studying a different province's pattern — for example, preparing for 30 + 20 when your paper is 35 + 15 or 40 + 10.
2. Leaving mental ability for the last month. It is 20–40% of the paper and needs daily practice.
3. Ignoring Level II formats and practising only plain recall questions.
4. Memorising "previous year questions" from unverified collections as if they were certain to repeat.
5. Learning a changing figure without its year — for example, a forest-cover or electricity figure from an old guidebook.
6. Not practising with a clock. Knowledge without speed loses marks in a 45-minute paper.
7. Guessing blindly on many questions when already close to the cut-off.
8. Skipping one's own province because "it is only two questions".
9. In Lumbini and Karnali: spending months on the screening test and too little on the subjective papers that decide the merit list.
10. Not reading the notes (द्रष्टव्य) of one's own syllabus — the rule on which version of a law applies is written there.
