## 12.1 Profile of the province (प्रदेशको परिचय)

#### 1 · Why it matters

The commonest province questions ask for a number or a name: how many districts, how many local levels, which is the capital, when the province was named. Bagmati is also the answer to several national questions in Chapters 1, 5 and 6 — the most populous province, the one with the most metropolitan cities, the largest Assembly, the largest economy, the highest literacy. This topic fixes those basic facts and adds the thirteen districts one by one.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

**Bagmati** is the name of the river that rises on Shivapuri hill and flows through the Kathmandu Valley. The province named after it is the middle block of Nepal. It climbs from the plain of Chitwan, 141 metres above the sea, to the snow of Langtang on the Chinese border, more than seven thousand metres high. In between lie the Kathmandu Valley, the hills of the Tamang people and the old gateways of Sindhuli and Makwanpur.

The federal capital, **Kathmandu**, is inside the province, but it is **not** the capital of the province. The provincial capital is **Hetauda**, a town of the inner Terai in Makwanpur district. So Bagmati has two centres: the valley, where half of its people live, and Hetauda, where its government sits.

Like every province, Bagmati was formed on the day the Constitution came into force and was first known only by a number. The Provincial Assembly chose the name and the capital on one day in 2076 BS.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Formation and name**

- **Formed:** 3 Ashoj 2072 (20 September 2015), the day the Constitution came into force. Schedule 4 of the Constitution lists its 13 districts.
- **First name:** Province No. 3.
- **Temporary seat:** the Government of Nepal named **Hetauda** on 3 Magh 2074 (17 January 2018).
- **Name and capital:** on **27 Poush 2076 (12 January 2020)** the Provincial Assembly named the province **Bagmati** and made **Hetauda** the permanent capital. Both were decided on the same day.
  - The **name** was passed without a vote against it: all **108** members present voted for it.
  - The **capital** received **105** votes. A rival proposal for **Dhulikhel** (Kavrepalanchok), moved by a small party, was rejected.
  - Two-thirds of all 110 members — **74** — were needed (Articles 288 (2) and 295 (2)).
  - The proposal for the name was moved by Indra Bahadur Baniya, then the leader of the Nepali Congress in the Assembly; the proposal for the capital was moved by Chief Minister Dormani Paudel.
- Bagmati was the **fourth** province to be named, after Karnali, Gandaki and Sudurpashchim.
- **Why "Bagmati":** the river is holy to Hindus and Buddhists, flows past Pashupatinath, and was also the name of the old zone that held eight of the thirteen districts.

**Position**

- **North:** China (Tibet Autonomous Region). Four districts touch it: Dolakha, Sindhupalchok, Rasuwa and Dhading.
- **South:** Madhesh Province and, at **Chitwan** only, India (the state of Bihar).
- **East:** Koshi Province. **West:** Gandaki Province.
- It touches **three provinces** and **two countries**. Only four provinces touch both India and China: Koshi, Bagmati, Gandaki and Sudurpashchim.

**Size and people**

- **Area:** 20,300 sq km — about 13.8 percent of Nepal; the **fifth** largest province.
- **Population (Census 2078):** **6,116,866** — 20.97 percent of Nepal; the **most populous** province, ahead of Madhesh by only 2,266.
- **Density:** 301 persons per sq km — the second highest, after Madhesh (633).
- **Literacy:** 82.1 percent — the highest.

**Divisions**

- **Districts:** 13.
- **Local levels:** **119** = 3 metropolitan cities + 1 sub-metropolitan city + 41 municipalities + 74 rural municipalities. That is 15.8 percent of Nepal's 753 local levels.
- **Wards:** 1,121.
- **Metropolitan cities (महानगरपालिका):** **Kathmandu, Lalitpur and Bharatpur** — three of Nepal's six.
- **Sub-metropolitan city (उपमहानगरपालिका):** **Hetauda**, the only one.
- **Kathmandu and Bhaktapur** districts have no rural municipality. **Rasuwa** has no municipality.

**Seats**

- **House of Representatives:** 33 constituencies — the most of any province.
- **Provincial Assembly:** 110 members = 66 directly elected + 44 proportional — the largest Assembly.
- **National Assembly:** 8 members, like every province.

#### 6 · Memory aid

- **"3 – 1 – 41 – 74"** makes 119. Compare Koshi's "1 – 2 – 46 – 88" (137) and Madhesh's "1 – 3 – 73 – 59" (136).
- **The three great cities — "Ka-La-Bha":** काठमाडौँ, ललितपुर, भरतपुर. Bhaktapur is **not** a metropolitan city.
- **"8 + 2 + 3 = 13":** eight districts from Bagmati zone, two from Narayani, three from Janakpur.
- **"27 Poush, 2076":** the name and the home on one day. "108 for the name, 105 for the home."
- **"Province 3, three metros, 33 seats."**
- **The four that touch China, east to west — "Do-Si-Ra-Dha":** Dolakha, Sindhupalchok, Rasuwa, Dhading.
- **Headquarters that differ from the district name:** Charikot, Manthali, Sindhulimadhi, Chautara, Dhulikhel, Bidur, Dhunche, Dhading Besi, Hetauda, Bharatpur.

#### 7 · Exam traps

- **Kathmandu is the federal capital, not the provincial capital.** The capital of Bagmati Province is **Hetauda**.
- Hetauda is in **Makwanpur**, not in Chitwan. It is a **sub-metropolitan** city, not a metropolitan one.
- **Most people, but not the most districts or local levels.** Koshi has 14 districts and 137 local levels; Bagmati has 13 and 119.
- **First in people by a hair:** Bagmati leads Madhesh by only 2,266. Madhesh leads in **density**.
- **Fifth in area**, not first. Karnali is the largest.
- **Province No. 3**, not Province No. 2.
- The name and the capital were decided on the **same day** (as in Madhesh). In Koshi they were decided almost four years apart.
- The capital got **105** votes, the name **108**. Do not swap them.
- **Bharatpur**, not Bhaktapur, is the third metropolitan city. Bhaktapur is a municipality.
- Bagmati touches India **only at Chitwan**; it does not touch Uttar Pradesh.
- **Simara airport** and **Birgunj** are in Madhesh Province, not in Bagmati, although the old Narayani zone held them together with Makwanpur and Chitwan.
- "Bagmati" was once the name of a **zone of eight districts**. The province of thirteen districts is larger.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Give a short introduction to Bagmati Province.

**Model answer.** **Position:** Bagmati is the central province of Nepal. China lies to its north, Koshi Province to its east, Gandaki Province to its west, and Madhesh Province and — at Chitwan — the Indian state of Bihar to its south. **Founding:** it was formed on 3 Ashoj 2072 as Province No. 3; on 27 Poush 2076 the Provincial Assembly named it Bagmati, after the river of the Kathmandu Valley, and made Hetauda of Makwanpur its capital. **Size:** 20,300 sq km, the fifth-largest province, with 6,116,866 people in the Census of 2078 — the most of any province; almost half of them live in the three districts of the Kathmandu Valley. **Divisions:** 13 districts and 119 local levels — the metropolitan cities of Kathmandu, Lalitpur and Bharatpur, the sub-metropolitan city of Hetauda, 41 municipalities and 74 rural municipalities. **Representation:** 33 seats in the House of Representatives and 110 in the Provincial Assembly, both the highest. **Special features:** the federal capital, two World Heritage properties (the Kathmandu Valley and Chitwan National Park), the highest literacy (82.1 percent) and the largest economy (36.7 percent of Nepal's output). **Conclusion:** Bagmati is the political and economic heart of the country, and its chief task is to spread the wealth of the valley to its own hill districts.

## 12.2 Physical geography (भौतिक भूगोल)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Unit 1.1.2 of the Bagmati syllabus asks for Nepal's landforms, water, wildlife, soil and climate. The province has examples of every one of them inside its own borders: the lowest plain of the inner Terai, the lake-bed valley of Kathmandu, the first national park, the first Himalayan national park, glacial lakes and the peak that fixes Nepal's time. Unit 1.7.4 asks for earthquakes, floods and landslides, and the province has suffered the two worst disasters of recent years.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Think of the province as a staircase with four steps.

- **The bottom step** is the **inner Terai**: the valleys of Chitwan, Hetauda and Sindhuli, shut in between the Chure hills and the Mahabharat range. Chitwan is flat, hot and fertile.
- **The second step** is the **Mahabharat range and the middle hills** — Makwanpur, Kavrepalanchok, Ramechhap, Nuwakot, Dhading.
- **The third step** is the **Kathmandu Valley**, a bowl about 1,300 metres high that was once a lake.
- **The top step** is the **Himalaya** along the Chinese border: Rolwaling and Gaurishankar in Dolakha, Jugal in Sindhupalchok, Langtang in Rasuwa and Ganesh Himal on the edge of Dhading.

Two great river systems share the province. The east drains to the **Koshi** through the Sunkoshi; the west drains to the **Narayani (Gandaki)** through the Trishuli. Between them the **Bagmati**, a smaller river born in the Mahabharat range, carries the water of the Kathmandu Valley south to Madhesh.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Height**

- **Lowest point:** about **141 metres**, at Golaghat in Chitwan.
- **Highest peak:** **Langtang Lirung**, about **7,227 metres**, in Rasuwa (the province's own profile).
- ⚖️ Some lists give **Ganesh Himal** (about 7,422 metres) as the highest point. It stands on the western edge of the province and is shared with Gandaki Province and China.
- By the usual grouping of districts the province has **3 mountain districts** (Dolakha, Sindhupalchok, Rasuwa), **9 hill districts** and **1 Terai district** (Chitwan, strictly inner Terai).

**Peaks**

- **Langtang Lirung** (Rasuwa) — the nearest high range to Kathmandu.
- **Gaurishankar** (Dolakha), about 7,134 metres — the meridian of **Nepal Standard Time**, 86°15′ E, passes through it.
- **Dorje Lakpa** (Sindhupalchok), **Langtang Ri** (Rasuwa), the **Jugal** and **Rolwaling** ranges.
- **Yalung Ri** (Dolakha) — the avalanche of 17 Kartik 2082 (3 November 2025) killed seven climbers there.
- The province's profile counts **61** of Nepal's 414 listed peaks above 5,000 metres.

**Hills around the Kathmandu Valley**

- **Phulchoki** (Lalitpur), about 2,782 metres — the highest; **Shivapuri**, about 2,732 metres; Chandragiri; Nagarjun.
- The valley floor is old lake mud (lacustrine soil). The water leaves through the **Chobhar gorge**.

**Rivers**

- **Koshi system (east):** **Sunkoshi** — the longest river inside the province, about 160 km; **Tamakoshi** (Dolakha, Ramechhap); **Bhote Koshi** of Sindhupalchok; **Indrawati**; Roshi; Khimti; **Likhu**, on the border with Koshi Province.
- **Narayani system (west):** **Trishuli**, whose upper course in Rasuwa is also called **Bhote Koshi** — the river of the flood of 2083; **Budhi Gandaki**, on the border with Gandaki Province; **East Rapti** (Makwanpur, Chitwan).
- **Devghat:** the Trishuli meets the Kali Gandaki and becomes the **Narayani**, on the western edge of Chitwan.
- **Bagmati:** rises at **Bagdwar** on Shivapuri, passes Pashupatinath, takes in the Bishnumati and Manohara, leaves the valley at Chobhar and reaches Madhesh, where it forms the boundary of Sarlahi and Rautahat.
- **Kamala** and **Marin** rise in Sindhuli; **Lal Bakaiya** rises in Makwanpur.
- The profile counts **10 sub-basins and 33 main rivers**.
- **Two rivers named Bhote Koshi:** one in Sindhupalchok (it joins the Sunkoshi), one in Rasuwa (it becomes the Trishuli).

**Lakes**

- **Gosaikunda** (Rasuwa) — a holy mountain lake; Ramsar site since 2007.
- **Bishazari** and its lakes (Chitwan) — Ramsar site since 2003.
- **Tsho Rolpa** (Dolakha) — one of Nepal's largest and most watched glacial lakes.
- **Indra Sarovar** (Kulekhani, Makwanpur) — man-made, for the Kulekhani power stations.
- **Panch Pokhari** (Sindhupalchok), **Jatapokhari** (Ramechhap), **Taudaha** (Kathmandu).

**Protected areas**

- **Chitwan National Park** (1973) — Nepal's **first** national park; 952.63 sq km; World Heritage Site (1984); in Chitwan and Makwanpur, and in Nawalpur and Parsa outside the province.
- **Langtang National Park** (1976) — 1,710 sq km; Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Sindhupalchok; red panda; Gosaikunda lies in it.
- **Shivapuri Nagarjun National Park** (2002) — 159 sq km; the water source of the Kathmandu Valley.
- **Gaurishankar Conservation Area** (2010) — 2,179 sq km; Dolakha, Ramechhap, Sindhupalchok; managed by the National Trust for Nature Conservation.
- **Parsa National Park** reaches into Makwanpur.
- 🔄 **Wildlife:** Chitwan has the most tigers of any area, **145** of Nepal's 429 (count announced 13 Shrawan 2083), and 694 of the 752 one-horned rhinoceroses (count of 2021).

**Land and climate**

- **Land use (official profile):** forest 53.7 percent, shrub 7.57 percent, farmland 24.34 percent.
- **Climate:** every belt from sub-tropical Chitwan to the tundra of Langtang.
- **Rain:** Bharatpur about 2,550 mm a year, Hetauda about 2,070 mm, Kathmandu about 1,360 mm (encyclopaedia table).

**Disasters**

- **Earthquake of 12 Baisakh 2072 (25 April 2015):** the centre was at Barpak in Gorkha (Gandaki), but **12 of the 14 worst-hit districts** are in Bagmati — every district except Chitwan. Sindhupalchok lost the most lives, about 3,500 (from memory).
- **Aftershock of 29 Baisakh 2072 (12 May 2015):** centred near the border of Dolakha and Sindhupalchok.
- 🔄 **Rasuwagadhi flood, 24 Ashad 2082 (8 July 2025):** swept away the border bridge.
- 🔄 **Bhote Koshi flood, 10 Bhadra 2083 (26 August 2026):** ice and rock fell above the Lhende valley near Rasuwagadhi; the flood ran down the Bhote Koshi and the Trishuli through Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading to Chitwan. Figures of early November 2026: **more than 1,450 dead**, about **5,285 missing**, loss about **Rs 408 billion**, 13 hydropower projects (759 megawatts) damaged. It is the worst disaster in Nepal since the earthquake.
- 🔄 **Air:** on 10 Baisakh 2083 (23 April 2026) Kathmandu was the second most polluted city in the world, with an index of 247.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Describe the physical features of Bagmati Province and the natural hazards it faces.

**Model answer.** **Relief:** the province rises like a staircase from about 141 metres at Golaghat in Chitwan to about 7,227 metres at Langtang Lirung. It has the inner Terai valleys of Chitwan, Hetauda and Sindhuli; the Mahabharat range and the middle hills; the lake-bed bowl of the Kathmandu Valley; and the Rolwaling, Jugal and Langtang ranges on the Chinese border. **Water:** the east drains to the Koshi through the Sunkoshi, Tamakoshi and Indrawati, the west to the Narayani through the Trishuli, Budhi Gandaki and East Rapti; the Bagmati rises on Shivapuri and carries the valley's water south. Gosaikunda and Bishazari are Ramsar sites and Tsho Rolpa is a large glacial lake. **Nature:** over half the land is forest; Chitwan (the first national park and a World Heritage Site), Langtang and Shivapuri Nagarjun National Parks and Gaurishankar Conservation Area protect the tiger, the rhinoceros and the red panda. **Hazards:** (1) earthquakes — twelve of the fourteen worst-hit districts of 2072 are here; (2) floods and falls of ice from the high mountains — the Bhote Koshi flood of 10 Bhadra 2083 killed more than 1,450 people; (3) landslides on steep young hills in every monsoon; (4) glacial lakes such as Tsho Rolpa; (5) the dirty air of the Kathmandu Valley. **Conclusion:** the same steep land that gives the province water, power and beauty also makes it the most disaster-prone; early warning, safe building and care of the upper valleys are its first needs.

## 12.3 History and archaeology (इतिहास र पुरातत्त्व)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Units 1.2.2 to 1.2.4 of the Bagmati syllabus cover the Kirat, Lichhavi and medieval periods, unification and the movements for democracy. Most of that story took place inside this province: the Kathmandu Valley was "Nepal" for centuries, and the roads by which the Gorkhali army reached it — Nuwakot, Makwanpur and Sindhuli — are here too. Chapter 2 tells the national story. This topic picks out the places.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

For most of history the word **Nepal** meant the Kathmandu Valley, also called **Nepal Mandal**. Dynasty followed dynasty there: Gopal, Mahispal, Kirat, Lichhavi and Malla. After King Yaksha Malla the valley broke into three small kingdoms — **Kantipur, Lalitpur and Bhaktapur** — which competed in building the palaces and temples that are now World Heritage.

Outside the valley stood other small states. The **Sen** kings ruled **Makwanpur** from their fort above today's Hetauda. **Dolakha** was a trade town on the road to Tibet.

Prithvi Narayan Shah of Gorkha closed in on the valley step by step: **Nuwakot** in the north-west (1744), **Makwanpur** in the south (1762), and **Sindhuligadhi** in the south-east, where his troops beat a force of the East India Company (1767). Then the three cities fell in 1768 and 1769, and Kathmandu became the capital of Nepal. Everything that came later — the Kot massacre, the Rana palaces, the return of King Tribhuvan, the people's movements, the Constituent Assemblies and the movement of 2082 — happened in the same city.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Ancient period**

- Legend says the valley was a lake, drained by **Manjushri** at **Chobhar**. Geology agrees that it was a lake.
- Dynasties of the valley: **Gopal → Mahispal → Kirat → Lichhavi → Malla** (Chapter 2).
- **Changu Narayan** (Bhaktapur): the pillar inscription of King **Manadeva**, 464 AD — the oldest dated inscription of the Kathmandu Valley. Manadeva is the first Lichhavi king proved by an inscription.
- **Maligaon** (Kathmandu): a statue of King Jaya Varma, dated by scholars to 185 AD, was found there (from memory).
- Other Lichhavi places: **Hadigaun**, **Pashupati**, **Budhanilkantha**, and **Palanchok** in Kavrepalanchok.

**Medieval period**

- **Malla kingdoms:** Kantipur (Kathmandu), Lalitpur (Patan) and Bhaktapur (Bhadgaon).
- Buildings of the Malla kings: **Krishna Mandir** of Patan (Siddhi Narsingh Malla, 1637), **Nyatapola** of Bhaktapur (Bhupatindra Malla, 1702), the Hanuman Dhoka palace, the 55-window palace.
- **Sen kingdom of Makwanpur:** capital at **Makwanpurgadhi**, about 17 km north-east of Hetauda. The province's profile says the Sen kings ruled from it for 197 years.
- **Dolakha:** an old kingdom and trade town; textbooks credit its king Indra Simha Deva with Nepal's first silver coin (from memory).

**Unification**

- **Nuwakot:** won in 1744. Prithvi Narayan Shah built the seven-storey palace there and used Nuwakot as his base. He died at **Devighat** in Nuwakot in 1775.
- **Makwanpur:** won in 1762. Early in 1763 the Gorkhalis defeated an army sent by Mir Qasim, the Nawab of Bengal, at Makwanpur.
- **Sindhuligadhi:** in 1767 the Gorkhali force defeated the East India Company's troops under **Captain Kinloch**, who had come to help the King of Kantipur.
- **Kirtipur:** won in 1766 after three attacks; Kalu Pande was killed in the first, in 1757.
- **Kantipur and Lalitpur:** 1768. **Bhaktapur:** 1769.
- Kathmandu became the capital of the united kingdom.

**Later history in the province**

- **Betrawati** (on the border of Nuwakot and Rasuwa): the treaty of 1792 after the war with Tibet and China.
- **Rasuwagadhi:** the fort on the Tibetan border was built in 1912 BS, in the time of the war of 1855–56. **Thapathali** (Kathmandu): the treaty of 1856.
- **Kot massacre** (1846) and the Rana period: Durbar High School, Bir Hospital, Tri-Chandra College, Singha Durbar.
- **The four martyrs** of 1997 BS were executed in Kathmandu.
- **2007 BS:** King Tribhuvan took shelter in the Indian Embassy in Kathmandu; the proclamation of 7 Falgun ended Rana rule.
- **Tribhuvan Highway** (1956) — the first motor road into the valley — passes Hetauda and Daman. Before it, travellers walked from Bhimphedi over Chisapanigadhi and Chitlang to Thankot.
- **Chitwan** was thinly peopled forest until malaria was controlled; hill families were settled in the Rapti valley from the 2010s BS.
- **Movements:** the protests of 2046 and of 2062/63 in Kathmandu and Lalitpur; **Sindhuli** was one of the four places attacked on 1 Falgun 2052; the firing of **23 Bhadra 2082** at New Baneshwor, after which Singha Durbar and the parliament building were burnt.

**Old administrative units**

- In 1956 the area was grouped as "Kathmandu Kshetra".
- From 2018 BS: **Bagmati, Narayani and Janakpur zones**.
- From 2029 BS: the **Central Development Region**, with its centre at Kathmandu.
- From 2072 BS: Province No. 3, now Bagmati Province.

**Forts (गढी)**

- **Makwanpurgadhi** (Makwanpur), **Sindhuligadhi** (Sindhuli), **Rasuwagadhi** (Rasuwa), **Chisapanigadhi** (Makwanpur), **Hariharpurgadhi** (Sindhuli), and the palace-fort of **Nuwakot**.

- **Kirtipur** is in **Kathmandu** district, not Lalitpur.
- The Kathmandu Valley had **three** Malla kingdoms at the end, not two.
- **Rasuwagadhi** is both an old fort and today's trade point; the fort dates from 1912 BS.
- The **Central Development Region** had 19 districts; only 13 are in Bagmati Province.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Explain the place of the present Bagmati Province in the unification of Nepal.

**Model answer.** **Background:** in the eighteenth century the land of the present province held the three rich Malla kingdoms of the Kathmandu Valley and, outside it, the Sen kingdom of Makwanpur and smaller hill states. The valley was the prize of the unification; the other places were its gates. **The steps:** (1) **Nuwakot, 1744** — its capture cut the valley's trade road to Tibet and gave Prithvi Narayan Shah a base beside the valley; (2) **Makwanpur, 1762** — the Sen kingdom was won, the southern road to the plains was closed, and in 1763 an army of Mir Qasim of Bengal was beaten there; (3) **Kirtipur, 1766** — taken at the third attempt; (4) **Sindhuligadhi, 1767** — the East India Company's force under Captain Kinloch, sent to help Kantipur, was defeated, so no outside help could reach the valley; (5) **Kantipur and Lalitpur, 1768, and Bhaktapur, 1769** — the three cities fell and Kathmandu became the capital. **Importance:** the campaign shows a clear plan — surround, cut off trade, then enter; the weapons taken at Makwanpur and Sindhuli strengthened the army; and the wealth and craftsmen of the valley gave the new kingdom its centre. **Conclusion:** the unification began in Gorkha, but it was decided on the soil of today's Bagmati Province, whose forts at Nuwakot, Makwanpur and Sindhuli remain its monuments.

## 12.4 Society and culture (समाज र संस्कृति)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Unit 1.3 of the Bagmati syllabus asks for religion, castes, languages, culture, art, literature and heritage. Unit 1.7.1 asks for the division of population by province, urbanisation and migration. Bagmati is the province with the most people, the highest literacy, the most city-dwellers and the most World Heritage monuments, so many national questions have a Bagmati answer.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Bagmati has two faces. One is the **Kathmandu Valley** — three small districts where almost half of the province lives, where people from every district of Nepal have settled, and where the **Newar** people built a city culture of temples, festivals and crafts. The other is the **ring of hills** around the valley, the homeland of the **Tamang** people, with Brahman and Chhetri villages among them and smaller peoples such as the Chepang, Thami, Jirel, Hyolmo, Majhi and Danuwar.

No single group is a majority. The four largest — Tamang, Brahman (Hill), Chhetri and Newar — each make up between a sixth and a fifth of the people. Nepali is the mother tongue of a little more than half; Tamang and Nepal Bhasa come next, and the province has made both of them official languages.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Population** (Census 2078; Chapter 6)

- **6,116,866** people — 20.97 percent of Nepal.
- **The valley:** Kathmandu, Lalitpur and Bhaktapur hold 3,025,386 people — about **49.5 percent** of the province on about 4.4 percent of its land.
- **Kathmandu district** alone has 2,041,587 people — one-third of the province and the most of any district in Nepal. It is also the most crowded district: about 5,170 persons per sq km.
- **Rasuwa** has only 46,689 people, about 30 per sq km.
- **Migration:** Bagmati has the highest rate of in-migration; people come to the valley and to Chitwan from every province.
- **Literacy:** **82.1 percent** — the highest of the seven provinces.

**Castes and ethnic groups** (Census 2078)

- **Tamang** about 20 percent — the largest group.
- **Brahman (Hill)** about 18 percent; **Chhetri** about 17 percent; **Newar** about 16 percent.
- Then Magar (about 5 percent), Kami, Gurung, Rai, Tharu, Sarki, Damai, **Chepang** (about 1.3 percent) and Sherpa.
- The province's own profile still prints the figures of the Census of 2068 (Tamang 20.42, Brahman 18.28, Chhetri 17.28, Newar 16.92 percent). Use the Census of 2078.

**Peoples and their homes**

- **Tamang:** Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading, Sindhupalchok, Kavrepalanchok, Makwanpur, Sindhuli, Ramechhap. Their drum is the *damphu*, their song the **Tamang Selo**, their new year **Sonam Lhosar** (in Magh).
- **Newar:** the Kathmandu Valley and old trade towns such as Banepa, Dhulikhel, Dolakha and Bidur. Their calendar is **Nepal Sambat**; their social trust is the *guthi*.
- **Chepang:** the hills of Makwanpur, Chitwan and Dhading — a highly marginalised people.
- **Thami:** Dolakha and Sindhupalchok. **Jirel:** Jiri in Dolakha. **Hyolmo:** Helambu in Sindhupalchok. **Sherpa:** the north of Dolakha and Sindhupalchok.
- **Majhi and Danuwar:** river valleys of Sindhuli, Ramechhap and Kavrepalanchok.
- **Tharu, Darai, Kumal and Bote:** Chitwan.
- Three of the ten "endangered" nationalities live mainly here: **Bankariya** (Makwanpur), **Surel** (Dolakha) and **Hayu** (Ramechhap, Sindhuli) — from memory.

**Languages**

- **Nepali** about 56 percent; **Tamang** about 18 percent; **Nepal Bhasa** about 12 percent.
- Then Magar, Tharu, Maithili, Bhojpuri, Gurung and Chepang, each between 1 and 2.5 percent.
- 🔄 **Official languages:** under the Province Official Language Act, 2080, **Tamang and Nepal Bhasa** are used in government beside Nepali. They came into use on **24 Baisakh 2081 (6 May 2024)**. Bagmati was the **first** province to do this, and it keeps the day as a language day with a provincial holiday. Koshi followed on 25 Kartik 2083 with thirteen languages (Chapter 6).

**Religion** (Census 2078, encyclopaedia figures)

- Hindu about 72 percent; **Buddhist about 22 percent**; Christian about 3.3 percent; Kirat about 1.3 percent; Islam about 0.8 percent.
- Bagmati has the largest share of Buddhists of any province (from memory).

**Festivals**

- **Indra Jatra** (Kathmandu) — the chariot of the living goddess **Kumari**. 🔄 The present Kumari, **Aryatara Shakya**, was installed on 14 Ashoj 2082 (30 September 2025).
- **Bisket Jatra** (Bhaktapur) — at the New Year of Baisakh.
- **Rato Machhindranath Jatra** (Lalitpur) — the long chariot festival that ends with Bhoto Jatra.
- **Gai Jatra** and **Ghode Jatra** in the valley; **Maha Shivaratri** at Pashupatinath.
- **Sonam Lhosar** of the Tamang; **Janai Purnima** fair at **Gosaikunda**; fairs of **Dolakha Bhimsen** and **Kalinchok Bhagwati**; **Makar Sankranti** fair at **Devghat**; **Maghi** of the Tharu in Chitwan.

**Heritage**

- **Two of Nepal's four World Heritage properties** are in the province: the **Kathmandu Valley** (1979, cultural) and **Chitwan National Park** (1984, natural).
- **Seven monument zones of the Kathmandu Valley:** the Durbar Squares of **Hanuman Dhoka, Patan and Bhaktapur**; the stupas of **Swayambhu and Bauddha**; the temples of **Pashupati and Changu Narayan**.
  - Four are in Kathmandu district, one in Lalitpur, two in Bhaktapur.
- On Nepal's tentative list: **Panauti** (Kavrepalanchok) and the **palace of Nuwakot**.
- **Holy places outside the valley:** Gosaikunda (Rasuwa); Dolakha Bhimsen and Kalinchok Bhagwati (Dolakha); Namobuddha and Palanchok Bhagwati (Kavrepalanchok); Devghat (Chitwan side of the river junction).

**Arts**

- **Architecture:** pagoda (Nyatapola, Pashupatinath), shikhara (Krishna Mandir), stupa (Swayambhu, Bauddha).
- **Crafts:** *paubha* painting; metal casting of Patan; wood-carving and pottery of Bhaktapur.
- **Performing arts:** Lakhe and other mask dances of the Newar; Tamang Selo.
- **Literature:** Laxmi Prasad Devkota, Balkrishna Sama and Motiram Bhatta were of Kathmandu; Chittadhar Hridaya wrote in Nepal Bhasa.

**Education and health**

- **Universities:** Tribhuvan University (Kirtipur, 2016 BS), Kathmandu University (Dhulikhel, 2048 BS), the Agriculture and Forestry University (Rampur, Chitwan) and Nepal Open University (Lalitpur) — the last two from memory.
- **Hospitals:** Bir Hospital, the oldest; B.P. Koirala Memorial Cancer Hospital at Bharatpur.

#### 7 · Exam traps

- **Tamang**, not Newar and not Chhetri, is the largest group of the province. In Nepal as a whole the Chhetri are the largest.
- **Newar** is the people; **Nepal Bhasa** is their language. The share of the language (about 12 percent) is lower than the share of the people (about 16 percent).
- **Manakamana** is in **Gorkha** (Gandaki), although its cable car begins at Kurintar in Chitwan.
- **Changu Narayan** is in **Bhaktapur**; **Bauddha** and **Swayambhu** are in **Kathmandu**.
- **Lumbini** and **Sagarmatha National Park** are the other two World Heritage properties; neither is in Bagmati.
- The Kathmandu Valley is **one** World Heritage property with **seven** zones — not seven properties.
- **Sonam Lhosar** is the Tamang new year; **Tamu Lhosar** is Gurung; **Gyalpo Lhosar** is Sherpa.
- **Bisket Jatra** belongs to Bhaktapur, not Kathmandu.
- Bagmati's official languages are **Tamang and Nepal Bhasa**. Maithili and Limbu are the pair the Language Commission recommended for Koshi, which brought thirteen languages into use in Kartik 2083.
- Highest literacy does not mean the same everywhere: the valley districts are far ahead of the hill districts.

## 12.5 Economy (अर्थतन्त्र)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Unit 1.4 of the Bagmati syllabus asks for gross domestic product and the share of its main sectors, income per person, poverty, the periodic plan, the budget and the sources of revenue. Paper II asks, in so many words, for "basic knowledge of the periodic plan of Bagmati Province". The province is also the answer to the national questions "which province has the largest economy, the highest income, the largest budget, the least dependence on grants".

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Bagmati earns more than a third of everything Nepal produces. The reason is not its farms or its factories but its **services**: the federal capital, the head offices of banks and companies, the main international airport, the largest hospitals and colleges, tourism and trade are all here. Services give about three-quarters of the province's output; farming and industry give about one-eighth each.

This wealth is very unevenly spread. The valley and Chitwan are rich; Rasuwa, Ramechhap, Sindhuli and the Chepang hills are not. Almost half of the people still work on farms, though farming earns little of the income.

The provincial **government** is small beside this economy. Its budget for 2083/84 is Rs 66.93 billion — the largest of the seven provinces, yet less than 3 percent of the province's output.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Size and income** 🔄 (estimates for 2082/83; Chapter 5)

- **Share of Nepal's GDP:** **36.7 percent** — about Rs 2,423 billion out of about Rs 6,600 billion. It is more than the next two provinces, Koshi (15.8) and Lumbini (14.2), put together.
- **Growth:** **5.40 percent** — the highest of the seven (Nepal 3.85).
- **Income per person:** **USD 2,644** — the highest; about 2.8 times that of Madhesh (USD 934).
- **Poverty:** 12.59 percent — the second lowest, after Gandaki (11.88); Nepal 20.27. **Multidimensional poverty:** 7.0 percent — the lowest.

**Structure** 🔄

- **Services about 76.5 percent; farming (primary sector) about 11.8 percent; industry about 11.7 percent** (Economic Survey 2082/83). Nepal Rastra Bank's report on the province gives 76.53, 11.23 and 12.24 percent.
- The largest single activity is **wholesale and retail trade**. Bagmati is the **only** province where farming is not the largest activity.
- **Farm work:** about 44.5 percent of the people are engaged in farming (provincial economic survey of 2081/82), yet farming gives about 12 percent of the output.

**Farming**

- Farmland is about 24 percent of the land.
- **Chitwan:** poultry, maize, rice, mustard, banana — Nepal's poultry centre.
- **Kavrepalanchok, Dhading, Makwanpur:** vegetables and milk for the Kathmandu market.
- **Sindhuli and Ramechhap:** *junar* (sweet orange). **Dolakha:** potato, kiwi.
- **Nuwakot and Rasuwa:** rainbow trout. **Rasuwa:** yak cheese.

**Industry**

- **Industrial estates:** **Balaju** (2016 BS — the first in Nepal), **Patan**, **Hetauda** and **Bhaktapur**.
- **Hetauda** is the industrial town of the province: cement, food and drink and other factories, on the junction of the Tribhuvan and East–West Highways.
- About a third of Nepal's manufacturing output comes from the province (31.7 percent in the provincial survey of 2081/82).

**Energy**

- 🔄 **Installed capacity:** about **1,698 megawatts — 45 percent of Nepal's** — the largest share of any province (Economic Survey 2082/83, before the flood).
- **Upper Tamakoshi** (456 megawatts, Dolakha) — the largest station now operating.
- **Kulekhani I** (60 megawatts, Makwanpur, 1982) — the first storage project.
- **Pharping** (Kathmandu, 1911) — the first hydropower station of Nepal.
- Others: Khimti, Bhote Koshi, Chilime, Rasuwagadhi, Trishuli, Devighat.
- 🔄 The Bhote Koshi flood of 10 Bhadra 2083 damaged **13 hydropower projects of 759 megawatts** in the Trishuli valley.

**Transport and projects**

- **Tribhuvan International Airport** (Kathmandu); airports at **Bharatpur** and **Ramechhap**.
- **Highways:** Tribhuvan (1956), Prithvi, Araniko (to Kodari), B.P. (Dhulikhel–Sindhuli–Bardibas), Pasang Lhamu (to Rasuwagadhi), Kanti (Lalitpur–Hetauda).
- 🔄 **Nagdhunga–Sisnekhola tunnel:** opened 11 Shrawan 2083 (27 July 2026); 2.688 km; a toll is charged since Kartik 2083.
- 🔄 **Kathmandu–Terai Expressway:** about 72 km, to Nijgadh in Madhesh; under construction.
- **Sunkoshi–Marin tunnel:** in Sindhuli; it will carry water to the Bagmati irrigation system in Madhesh.
- **Melamchi:** brings drinking water from Sindhupalchok to the Kathmandu Valley.
- **Trade points with China:** **Rasuwagadhi** (Rasuwa) and **Tatopani** (Sindhupalchok). 🔄 The customs point at Rasuwagadhi was destroyed by the flood.

**Tourism**

- The monuments of the Kathmandu Valley; **Chitwan National Park** (Sauraha); **Langtang** and **Gosaikunda**; **Nagarkot**, **Dhulikhel**, **Daman**, **Kalinchok**, **Jiri**.

**Provincial budget for 2083/84** 🔄

- **Rs 66.93 billion** (Rs 66,93,33,33,000) — the largest of the seven provinces. The budget of 2082/83 was Rs 67.48 billion, so it is slightly smaller than the year before.
- Presented on **1 Ashad 2083 (15 June 2026)** by **Prabhat Tamang**, Minister for Economic Affairs and Planning in the government of Chief Minister Indra Bahadur Baniya; passed by a majority on 20 Ashad 2083 (4 July 2026).
- **Capital spending Rs 43.97 billion (65.69 percent); current spending Rs 22.96 billion (34.31 percent).**
- **Sources:** tax revenue Rs 30.15 billion; other revenue Rs 7.74 billion; federal fiscal transfers Rs 15.31 billion; cash balance and other receipts Rs 12.77 billion; loan recoveries Rs 0.96 billion. The reports do not separate the province's own taxes from its share of federal taxes.
- **Grants:** about 23 percent of the budget — the lowest share of any province. ⚖️ Chapters 4 and 5 use Rs 15.21 billion (22.7 percent); the budget speech as reported says Rs 15.31 billion.
- **Largest allocations:** Physical Infrastructure Development Rs 25.60 billion; Health and Social Development Rs 9.06 billion; Drinking Water, Energy and Irrigation Rs 8.91 billion; transfers to local levels Rs 6.92 billion.
- **Main policy line:** "समृद्ध बागमतीको अभियान: सुशासन, प्रविधि र उत्पादन" — good governance, technology and production.
- **Named programmes:** Chief Minister's housing for the poor; Chief Minister's innovation programme; interest subsidy for self-employed youth.

**Periodic plans** 🔄

- **First plan, 2076/77–2080/81.** Vision: "सुसंस्कृत र सुखी जनता, समाजवाद उन्मुख समृद्ध प्रदेश" — cultured and happy people, a socialism-oriented prosperous province.
- **Second plan, 2081/82–2085/86.** Passed by the Province Development Council on 22 Jestha 2081 and approved by the Council of Ministers on **32 Jestha 2081 (14 June 2024)**.
  - Long-term vision (as shown on the official site): "**बागमती प्रदेशको रुपान्तरण: आर्थिक समृद्धि र समतामूलक समाज**" — transformation of Bagmati Province: economic prosperity and an equitable society.
  - Goal: "सुशासन अभिवृद्धि गर्दै समृद्ध तथा समतामूलक प्रदेश निर्माण गर्ने" — to build a prosperous and equitable province while improving governance.
  - It stresses roads, farming, tourism, industry, education and health. **Its number targets were not read.**
- The plans are prepared by the **Province Policy and Planning Commission** (प्रदेश नीति तथा योजना आयोग), chaired by the Chief Minister.

**Question.** "Bagmati has the largest economy of the seven provinces, but its wealth is not evenly shared." Discuss.

## 12.6 Government and institutions (प्रदेश सरकार र संस्था)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Unit 1.8 of the Bagmati syllabus — "Nepal's system of governance: Federation, Province, local level and districts" — is the only governance unit in any province's Paper I, and it carries three questions. Unit 1.10 asks for current events and important persons. "Who is the Chief Minister?", "how many members has the Assembly?", "under which Article was the Chief Minister appointed?" are ready-made questions, and in Bagmati the answers changed in Bhadra 2083.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

The province is governed in the same way as the country, one size smaller (Chapter 4).

- The **Province Chief** stands for the President. He is appointed by the President and acts on the advice of the provincial government.
- The **Chief Minister** leads the government. He must have the support of more than half of the Provincial Assembly.
- The **Provincial Assembly** makes the laws and passes the budget. In Bagmati it has **110** members and sits at **Hetauda**.

Because the Assembly has 110 seats, a party needs **56** to rule alone. That happened only once, after the first election in 2074. Since the second election in 2079 no party has had a majority, so every government has been a coalition, and when a partner walks out the government falls. This is why Bagmati has had **seven Chief Ministers in less than nine years** — four of them since 2079.

#### 3 · Key facts

**The Assembly**

- **110 members:** 66 elected directly (two from each of the 33 House constituencies) and 44 by proportional representation (Article 176).
- **Majority of all seats: 56. Two-thirds: 74.** Ministers, with the Chief Minister, may be at most 20 percent of the members — **22** (Article 168 (9)).
- 🔄 On 13 November 2026 only **104** members were in office and six seats were vacant, so a majority of the members then in office was **53**.
- **Term:** five years; the present Assembly's term ends on 4 Mangsir 2084 (19 November 2027).

**First Assembly** (elections of 10 and 21 Mangsir 2074)

- CPN (UML) **58**; CPN (Maoist Centre) 23; Nepali Congress 21; Bibeksheel Sajha Party 3; Nepal Majdoor Kisan Party 2; three other parties 1 each — total 110.
- The CPN (UML) had a majority on its own — the only time one party has had it.
- **Speaker:** Sanu Kumar Shrestha. **Deputy Speaker:** Radhika Tamang.

**Second Assembly** (election of 4 Mangsir 2079; first meeting 18 Poush 2079)

- Nepali Congress **37** (25 direct + 12 proportional); CPN (UML) 27 (13 + 14); CPN (Maoist Centre) 21 (14 + 7); Rastriya Prajatantra Party 13 (7 + 6); CPN (Unified Socialist) 7 (5 + 2); Nepal Majdoor Kisan Party 3 (1 + 2); Hamro Nepali Party 2 (1 + 1) — total 110.
- 🔄 **Now:** Nepali Congress 36; Nepali Communist Party 26; CPN (UML) 25; Rastriya Prajatantra Party 12; Nepal Majdoor Kisan Party 3; Hamro Nepali Party 2; vacant 6. The Maoist Centre and the Unified Socialist members now sit as the Nepali Communist Party.
- **Speaker:** **Bhuwan Kumar Pathak** (Rastriya Prajatantra Party) was elected on 29 Poush 2079 (13 January 2023) with 62 votes. He announced his resignation on 24 Bhadra 2082, after the firing of 23 Bhadra, and gave it formally on 1 Ashoj 2082 (17 September 2025).
- 🔄 **The post has been vacant for more than a year.** **Deputy Speaker Apsara Chapagain** (CPN (UML)) chairs the meetings.

**The seven Chief Ministers**

1. **Dormani Paudel** (CPN (UML)) — 28 Magh 2074 (11 February 2018) to 2 Bhadra 2078 (18 August 2021). The first.
2. **Astalaxmi Shakya** (CPN (UML)) — 2 Bhadra 2078 to 10 Kartik 2078 (27 October 2021); 70 days. The **first woman Chief Minister** of any province of Nepal.
3. **Rajendra Prasad Pandey** (CPN (Unified Socialist)) — Kartik 2078 to Poush 2079.
4. **Shalikram Jamkattel** (CPN (Maoist Centre)) — 26 Poush 2079 (10 January 2023) to 7 Shrawan 2081 (22 July 2024). The first of the second Assembly.
5. **Bahadur Singh Lama** (Nepali Congress) — 9 Shrawan 2081 (24 July 2024) to 19 Shrawan 2082 (4 August 2025).
6. **Indra Bahadur Baniya** (Nepali Congress) — 20 Shrawan 2082 (5 August 2025) to 1 Bhadra 2083 (17 August 2026).
7. 🔄 **Kiran Thapa Magar** (CPN (UML)) — since **4 Bhadra 2083 (20 August 2026)**; a member elected from Bhaktapur.

**The change of Bhadra 2083** 🔄

- **6 Shrawan 2083 (22 July 2026):** the CPN (UML) withdrew its support and its six ministers resigned. The Baniya government fell into a minority.
- **1 Bhadra 2083 (17 August 2026):** on the day fixed for his vote of confidence, Chief Minister Baniya announced his resignation in the Assembly. He could count on 38 members (Nepali Congress 36, Hamro Nepali Party 2); 53 were needed.
- **4 Bhadra 2083 (20 August 2026):** Province Chief Deepak Prasad Devkota appointed **Kiran Thapa Magar** under **Article 168 (2)**. He had claimed the support of **63** members — CPN (UML) 25, Nepali Communist Party 26, Rastriya Prajatantra Party 12. Six ministers were sworn in the same day.
- The central leadership of the Rastriya Prajatantra Party opposed this support and took action against its leader in the Assembly and the three members who became ministers.
- **9 Bhadra 2083 (25 August 2026):** he won the **vote of confidence** under Article 168 (4) with **57 votes** — Nepali Communist Party 26, CPN (UML) 24, Rastriya Prajatantra Party 7 — and none against. 58 of the 104 members were present; the Nepali Congress and the two smallest parties stayed away. The Deputy Speaker announced the result.
- The same day the three parties signed a **six-point understanding**: no more than eight ministries; the Speaker to come from the Nepali Communist Party; a common minimum programme within seven days.
- **2 Ashoj 2083 (18 September 2026):** Rama Ale Magar (Nepali Communist Party) was sworn in as Minister for Agriculture, Livestock, Forest and Environment.
- A writ against the appointment was before the High Court. **Its outcome was not followed.**

**Province Chiefs**

1. **Anuradha Koirala** — Magh 2074 to Kartik 2076. The first; a well-known social worker.
2. **Bishnu Prasad Prasain** — Kartik 2076 to Bhadra 2078.
3. **Yadav Chandra Sharma** — 4 Bhadra 2078 (20 August 2021) to Shrawan 2081.
4. 🔄 **Deepak Prasad Devkota** — since 17 Shrawan 2081 (1 August 2024).

**Ministries** 🔄

- **Eight, counting the Chief Minister's office.** On 22 Baisakh 2083 (5 May 2026) the Baniya government decided to cut **14 ministries to 8** from 1 Shrawan 2083 (17 July 2026).
- The eight: (1) Office of the Chief Minister and Council of Ministers; (2) Economic Affairs and Planning; (3) Internal Affairs, Law and Cooperatives; (4) Physical Infrastructure Development; (5) Health and Social Development; (6) Drinking Water, Energy and Irrigation; (7) Industry, Tourism, Labour and Transport; (8) Agriculture, Livestock, Forest and Environment.

**Province Public Service Commission** (प्रदेश लोक सेवा आयोग, बागमती प्रदेश)

- Set up under **Article 244** and the province's **Province Public Service Commission Act, 2076**; office at **Hetauda**.
- 🔄 **Chairperson:** **Maheshwar Shrestha**, appointed by the Province Chief on 4 Kartik 2081 (20 October 2024) after the post had been empty for about seven months. **Members** shown on its site: Ram Bahadur Khadka and Punam Ghimire.
- The chairperson is recommended by a three-member committee: the Chief Minister, the Speaker and the leader of the opposition.
- Its motto as printed on its site: "स्वच्छता, निश्पक्षता, तटस्थता हाम्रो प्रतिवद्धता" — cleanliness, fairness and neutrality are our commitment.
- Its syllabus for the fifth-level non-technical posts came into force on 2077/09/12 (27 December 2020).

**Courts and other bodies**

- **High Court:** the **Patan High Court** (Lalitpur), with a bench at **Hetauda**; 13 district courts.
- The **Supreme Court**, the Federal Parliament and Singha Durbar are in Kathmandu, but they belong to the Federation, not to the province.
- **Province Policy and Planning Commission:** prepares the periodic plans (topic 12.5).
- The name of the Chief Attorney was not checked.

**Local governments and federal seats**

- **119 local levels** with 1,121 wards; 13 District Coordination Committees.
- **House of Representatives:** 33 seats — Kathmandu 10; Chitwan 3; Lalitpur 3; Bhaktapur, Kavrepalanchok, Sindhupalchok, Nuwakot, Dhading, Makwanpur and Sindhuli 2 each; Dolakha, Ramechhap and Rasuwa 1 each.
- 🔄 In the House election of **21 Falgun 2082 (5 March 2026)** the Rastriya Swatantra Party won **31 of the 33** seats; the Nepali Congress won one (Rasuwa) and the Nepali Communist Party one. The Provincial Assembly was not dissolved in 2082, so its parties are still those elected in 2079.

## 12.7 The province in numbers and superlatives; fact sheet (तथ्याङ्क, विशेषता र तथ्य-पत्र)

#### 1 · Why it matters

This topic is the page to learn by heart. It collects the figures of topics 12.1 to 12.6, shows where Bagmati is first among the provinces and where it is not, gives one line for each district and lists the events of 2082 and 2083. Candidates of the other six provinces need only this topic.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Remember Bagmati by four ideas.

- **The centre:** it holds the federal capital, the main airport and the meeting point of the country's roads.
- **The most:** the most people, the most seats, the largest economy, the highest income, the highest literacy, the largest budget.
- **Not the most:** it is only fifth in area and does not have the most districts or local levels.
- **The most changeable:** seven Chief Ministers, and the two worst disasters of the decade.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Where Bagmati is first among the seven provinces**

- **Population:** 6,116,866.
- **Metropolitan cities:** three of six.
- **Seats:** 110 in the Provincial Assembly; 33 in the House of Representatives.
- **Economy:** 36.7 percent of GDP; growth 5.40 percent; income USD 2,644 🔄.

- **Literacy:** 82.1 percent.
- **Budget:** Rs 66.93 billion for 2083/84 🔄 — and the lowest dependence on grants.

- **Tigers:** Chitwan, 145 🔄.

- **Area:** fifth (20,300 sq km). Karnali is first.

- **Density:** second (301). Madhesh is first (633).
- **Poverty by the poverty line:** second lowest (12.59 percent). Gandaki is lowest.
- **Municipalities and wards:** Madhesh has the most (73 and 1,271).
- **Naming:** fourth to be named.

**National "firsts" found in the province**

- First national park — **Chitwan** (1973). First hydropower station — **Pharping** (1911). First storage project — **Kulekhani I**. Largest operating station — **Upper Tamakoshi**.
- First industrial estate — **Balaju**. First motor road to the capital — **Tribhuvan Highway** (1956). First university — **Tribhuvan University**.
- Smallest district — **Bhaktapur**. Most populous district — **Kathmandu**.
- First woman Chief Minister — **Astalaxmi Shakya**.
- The meridian of Nepal's time — **Gaurishankar**.

**Events of 2082 and 2083 in the province** 🔄

- **10 Baisakh 2083:** Kathmandu the second most polluted city in the world that day.
- **12 and 25 Baisakh 2083:** removal of riverside settlements in the Kathmandu Valley began; the Supreme Court stopped it for the time being.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** In what ways does Bagmati lead the provinces of Nepal, and in what ways does it not?

**Model answer.** **Where it leads:** (1) **people** — 6,116,866, about 21 percent of Nepal, and three of the six metropolitan cities; (2) **representation** — 33 seats in the House of Representatives and 110 in its Provincial Assembly; (3) **economy** — 36.7 percent of the country's output, the highest growth (5.40 percent) and income per person (USD 2,644), and about 45 percent of the installed electricity; (4) **human development** — literacy of 82.1 percent and the lowest multidimensional poverty; (5) **public finance** — the largest provincial budget, Rs 66.93 billion, with the least dependence on grants; (6) **heritage** — two of the four World Heritage properties; (7) **language policy** — the first province to use its own languages in government. **Where it does not lead:** it is fifth in area; it has fewer districts and local levels than Koshi; it is second to Madhesh in density and second to Gandaki in the poverty rate. **Where it does worst:** governments change most often — seven Chief Ministers since 2074 and no Speaker since Ashoj 2082; and it bore the heaviest losses of the earthquake of 2072 and the Bhote Koshi flood of 2083. **Reason:** most of its lead comes from holding the federal capital, not from the work of the provincial government seated at Hetauda. **Conclusion:** Bagmati is first in size of population and economy; to be first in governance and in fairness between its valley and its hills is the work still to be done.

### D. Five short-answer questions

**1. Describe how Province No. 3 became Bagmati Province, and explain why Hetauda was chosen as its capital.**
*Points for the answer:* the rule — Articles 288 (2) and 295 (2): capital and name by two-thirds of all members of the Provincial Assembly, 74 of 110 · the start — formed 3 Ashoj 2072 as Province No. 3; Hetauda named temporary seat on 3 Magh 2074 · the delay — two years of disagreement inside and between parties · the decision — 27 Poush 2076: name passed by all 108 present, capital by 105; Dhulikhel proposal rejected · why "Bagmati" — the holy river of the valley, the name of the old zone, a neutral name for a mixed province · why Hetauda — already the working seat; between the valley and the plain at the junction of the Tribhuvan and East–West Highways; land for offices; an aim to draw development out of the crowded valley · result — the fourth province to be named; federal capital and provincial capital in different districts.

**2. Explain the importance of the Kathmandu Valley for Bagmati Province and the problems its growth has created.**
*Points for the answer:* size — three districts, about 4.4 percent of the land, about 49.5 percent of the people; Kathmandu district alone 2,041,587 · importance — federal capital; services that give three-quarters of the province's output; airport; universities and hospitals; seven monument zones of World Heritage; living festivals · problems — crowding and loss of farmland; air pollution (second worst in the world on 10 Baisakh 2083); polluted Bagmati river; water shortage (Melamchi); riverside settlements of landless people; earthquake risk on old lake soil; pressure on heritage · gap — hill districts such as Rasuwa and Ramechhap lose people to the valley · measures — satellite towns, the Nagdhunga tunnel and the Expressway, clean transport, river cleaning, strict building rules, services in the hill districts.

**3. Describe the Bhote Koshi flood of 2083 and the lessons it holds for disaster management.**
*Points for the answer:* event — 10 Bhadra 2083 (26 August 2026); ice and rock fell above the Lhende valley near Rasuwagadhi; a dam of debris burst; flood down the Bhote Koshi and Trishuli through Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading to Chitwan · loss (early November 2026) — more than 1,450 dead, about 5,285 missing; about Rs 408 billion; 7,570 houses; 13 hydropower projects of 759 megawatts; Rasuwagadhi customs point and road · response — rescue of more than 13,700 people; ordinance of 23 Ashoj 2083 on declaring the missing dead; relief of Rs 200,000 for a family that lost one member · earlier warning — the flood of 24 Ashad 2082 at the same place · lessons — watch the high valleys and glaciers with China; early-warning sirens along rivers; no settlements and labour camps on river banks; safer design and insurance of hydropower; three-tier coordination under the Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act · conclusion — climate hazards in the Himalaya are growing; prevention costs less than relief.

**4. Give a basic account of the periodic plans of Bagmati Province.**
*Points for the answer:* why plans — Paper II of this province's syllabus names them; a plan links the budget of each year to a five-year aim · who — Province Policy and Planning Commission, chaired by the Chief Minister; Province Development Council; approval by the Council of Ministers · first plan — 2076/77 to 2080/81; vision "सुसंस्कृत र सुखी जनता, समाजवाद उन्मुख समृद्ध प्रदेश" · second plan — 2081/82 to 2085/86; approved 32 Jestha 2081; long-term vision "बागमती प्रदेशको रुपान्तरण: आर्थिक समृद्धि र समतामूलक समाज"; goal of a prosperous and equitable province with better governance; stress on roads, farming, tourism, industry, education and health · link — same years as the Sixteenth Plan of Nepal ("good governance, social justice and prosperity") · difficulties named in its concept paper — no party has a clear majority, so priorities change with governments · state honestly that the number targets must be read from the plan itself.

**5. What are the main institutions of the provincial government of Bagmati, and what difficulties have they faced?**
*Points for the answer:* Province Chief — Deepak Prasad Devkota; appointed by the President · Chief Minister and Council of Ministers — Kiran Thapa Magar under Article 168 (2); eight ministries after the cut from 14 · Provincial Assembly — 110 members at Hetauda; makes laws, passes the budget; Speaker's post vacant since Ashoj 2082 · Province Public Service Commission — Hetauda; Article 244 and the Act of 2076; chairperson Maheshwar Shrestha · Province Policy and Planning Commission · High Court at Patan with a bench at Hetauda · 119 local levels · difficulties — seven Chief Ministers; coalitions that break; delay in appointments; offices spread between Hetauda and the valley; rebuilding after the flood · strengths — largest revenue base, least dependence on grants, first language law · way forward — stable coalition, full Assembly leadership, plan-based budgeting.

### E. Common mistakes

1. Writing **Kathmandu** as the capital of Bagmati Province. The capital is **Hetauda**.
2. Placing Hetauda in **Chitwan**. It is in **Makwanpur**.
3. Calling Bagmati the **largest** province. It is **fifth** in area; it is first in **population**.
4. Writing **137** or **136** local levels. Bagmati has **119 = 3 + 1 + 41 + 74**.
5. Listing **Bhaktapur** as a metropolitan city. The three are **Kathmandu, Lalitpur and Bharatpur**.
6. Swapping the votes: the **name** got **108**, the **capital** **105**.
7. Giving **3 Magh 2078** (Madhesh) or **17 Falgun 2079** (Koshi) as the day of naming. Bagmati: **27 Poush 2076**.
8. Saying the earthquake of 2072 began in Bagmati. It began at **Barpak, Gorkha**; Bagmati had the worst losses.
9. Mixing the two **Bhote Koshi** rivers — Sindhupalchok's and Rasuwa's — and the two floods of **Ashad 2082** and **Bhadra 2083**.
10. Calling **Gaurishankar** a national park. It is a **conservation area**; **Langtang** is the national park.
11. Giving the **Newar** as the largest group. **Tamang** are the largest.
12. Placing **Manakamana** or **Gorkha** in Bagmati. Both are in Gandaki.
13. Saying farming is the largest activity. In Bagmati it is **trade**; services give about three-quarters of output.
14. Giving **63** as the vote of confidence. It was **57**; 63 was the support claimed at appointment.
15. Calling Kiran Thapa Magar the **sixth** Chief Minister, or saying he presented the budget of 2083/84. He is the **seventh**; the budget was Baniya's.
16. Naming a **Speaker** in office. The post has been vacant since Ashoj 2082; the **Deputy Speaker** presides.
