## 11.1 Profile of the province (प्रदेशको परिचय)

#### 1 · Why it matters

The commonest province questions ask for a number or a name: how many districts, how many local levels, which is the capital, when the province was named. Madhesh is also the answer to several national questions in Chapter 1 — the smallest province, the most crowded, the one with the fewest districts and the most municipalities. This topic fixes those basic facts and adds the eight districts one by one.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

**Madhesh** (मधेश) means the plain that lies south of the Chure hills. The word is thought to come from the Sanskrit *Madhya Desh*, "the middle country". Madhesh Province is one part of that plain: a narrow strip in the south-east of Nepal, from the Koshi river in the east to the forest of Parsa in the west. Its eight districts stand side by side like books on a shelf. Every one of them touches India on the south, and none touches China.

The Constitution made the province on 3 Ashoj 2072, but gave it only a number: Province No. 2. It left two decisions to the Provincial Assembly: the **capital** (Article 288) and the **name** (Article 295). Each needs **two-thirds** of all the members. The first Assembly could not agree for four years. On **3 Magh 2078** it settled both on the same day: the name **Madhesh Province** and the capital **Janakpurdham**.

The province is small in land and large in people. It has about one-fifteenth of Nepal's area and more than one-fifth of Nepal's people.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Name, capital, founding**

- **Formed:** 3 Ashoj 2072 (20 September 2015), the day the Constitution came into force. Schedule 4 of the Constitution lists its 8 districts.
- **Temporary seat:** the Government of Nepal named **Janakpur** on 3 Magh 2074 (17 January 2018).
- **Name and capital:** the Provincial Assembly decided both on **3 Magh 2078 (17 January 2022)**.
  - **Name: Madhesh Province** (मधेश प्रदेश) — **80 votes**, under Article 295 (2).
  - **Capital: Janakpurdham** (जनकपुरधाम), in Dhanusha district — **78 votes**, under Article 288 (2).
  - **99** members voted. The Assembly then had 104 sitting members. Press reports counted two-thirds of the 104, which is 70; two-thirds of the full 107 seats is 72. Both decisions passed either test.
  - The Speaker who announced the result was **Saroj Kumar Yadav**. The Chief Minister was **Lalbabu Raut**.
- **Other names that were proposed:** Janaki Pradesh, Mithila Bhojpura and Madhya Madhesh. A proposal to make **Birgunj** the capital did not get enough votes.
- The provincial government gave a public holiday on the next day, 4 Magh 2078.
- It was the **sixth** of the seven provinces to be named. Only Koshi came later (2079 BS).
- By chance the two dates match: the temporary seat was named on **3 Magh 2074** and the permanent one on **3 Magh 2078**, exactly four years apart.
- A commentary on the decision gives the bounds of the capital area as Dhalkebar on the East–West Highway in the north, the Kamala river in the east, the Mithila Madhyamiki Parikrama road in the west and the border in the south.

**Position and boundaries**

- **South:** India — the state of **Bihar** only.
- **East:** **Koshi Province**. The Koshi river separates Saptari from Sunsari.
- **North:** Koshi Province (Udayapur) and **Bagmati Province** (Sindhuli and Makwanpur).
- **West:** Bagmati Province (Chitwan). The forest of Parsa National Park and Chitwan National Park lies along this edge.
- **All eight districts touch India. No district touches China.**
- Madhesh is one of **two** provinces that touch India but not China; the other is Lumbini. Karnali touches China but not India. The other four touch both.
- It is the **only province made of Terai districts alone**. It has no mountain district and no hill district, though the Chure hills run along its northern edge.
- The province is long and thin: roughly 250 km from east to west and, on average, under 40 km from the border to the Chure (rounded from the map and the area; not an official measure).

**Area and divisions**

- **Area:** **9,661 sq km** — about **6.6 percent** of Nepal. It is the **smallest** province. Karnali, the largest, is about 2.9 times as big.
- **Districts:** **8** — the fewest of any province. From east to west: **Saptari, Siraha, Dhanusha, Mahottari, Sarlahi, Rautahat, Bara, Parsa**.
- **Local levels:** **136** — second after Koshi (137).
  - **1 metropolitan city** (महानगरपालिका): **Birgunj** (Parsa).
  - **3 sub-metropolitan cities** (उपमहानगरपालिका): **Janakpurdham** (Dhanusha), **Kalaiya** and **Jitpur Simara** (both in Bara).
  - **73 municipalities** (नगरपालिका) — the most of any province.
  - **59 rural municipalities** (गाउँपालिका).
  - **1,271 wards** — the most of any province.
- Madhesh is the only province where **towns outnumber rural municipalities**: 77 of its 136 local levels are municipalities of one kind or another.
- **Sarlahi** has the most local levels (20). **Parsa** has the fewest (14).
- **Rautahat** has the most municipalities (16) and only 2 rural municipalities. **Parsa** has the most rural municipalities (10).
- **Largest district:** Saptari (1,363 sq km). **Smallest:** Mahottari (1,002 sq km). The eight districts are close in size.
- **Old zones:** the eight districts came from three of the former fourteen zones — **Sagarmatha** (Saptari, Siraha), **Janakpur** (Dhanusha, Mahottari, Sarlahi) and **Narayani** (Rautahat, Bara, Parsa). Saptari and Siraha were in the old Eastern Development Region; the other six were in the Central.

**People** (Census of 2078; census day 9 Mangsir 2078, 25 November 2021)

- **Population:** **6,114,600** — **20.97 percent** of Nepal; the **second** of the seven provinces. Bagmati has only **2,266** more.
- **Men:** 3,065,751. **Women:** 3,048,849. There are **100.55 men for every 100 women** — Madhesh is the only province where men are more than women.
- **Households:** 1,156,715. The average household has 5.29 members.
- **Density:** **633** persons per sq km — the highest of any province, and more than three times the figure for Nepal (198).
- **Literacy:** **63.5 percent** (men 72.5; women 54.7) — the lowest of any province.
- **Growth:** about 1.19 percent a year. The population was 5,404,145 in the Census of 2068; it grew by about 710,000 in ten years.
- **Most populous district:** Dhanusha (867,747). **Least populous:** Parsa (654,471). Every district has more than 650,000 people.
- **Most crowded district:** Dhanusha (735 per sq km). **Least crowded:** Parsa (484 per sq km) — still about two and a half times the national figure.
- **Largest city:** **Birgunj**. The capital, Janakpurdham, is the second city.
- About 73 percent of the people live in municipalities, as reported from the census — but many "municipalities" are groups of villages.

**Seats**

- **House of Representatives:** **32** first-past-the-post constituencies — **four in each district**.
- **Provincial Assembly:** **107** members — **64** direct (two for each House constituency, so eight in each district) and **43** proportional.
- **National Assembly:** **8** members, like every province.
- In the Assembly a **majority is 54** and **two-thirds is 72**.

- **"1 – 3 – 73 – 59"** makes 136. Koshi's "1 – 2 – 46 – 88" makes 137.
- **"3 Magh twice":** temporary seat on 3 Magh 2074, name and capital on 3 Magh 2078.
- **"80 for the name, 78 for the home."**
- **"Four, eight, and the rest":** 4 House seats in every district (32), 8 Assembly seats in every district (64), and 43 proportional seats make 107.

#### 7 · Exam traps

- **Smallest in area, second in people.** Do not call Madhesh the most populous province: Bagmati leads by 2,266.
- **136, not 137.** Koshi has 137 local levels. Madhesh leads only in **municipalities** (73) and **wards** (1,271).
- The **capital** is **Janakpurdham** (Dhanusha). **Birgunj** (Parsa) is the largest city and the only metropolitan city, but it is not the capital.
- The name and the capital were decided on the **same day**. In Koshi they were decided almost four years apart.
- **Province No. 2**, not Province No. 1.
- **Saptari and Siraha** are in Madhesh, not in Koshi, although they were in the Eastern Development Region.
- **Parsa district** is not the same as **Parsa National Park**: the park also lies in Bara and in Makwanpur of Bagmati Province.
- **Siraha** is both a district and its headquarters; **Lahan**, a larger town, is in the same district but is not the headquarters.
- The name was passed by the **Provincial Assembly**, not by the Federal Parliament and not by the Government of Nepal.
- Madhesh touches **Bihar only**. Uttar Pradesh begins further west.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Give a short introduction to Madhesh Province.

**Model answer.** **Position:** Madhesh is a narrow strip of plain in the south-east of Nepal. India (the state of Bihar) lies to its south, Koshi Province to its east and north-east, and Bagmati Province to its north and west. It is the only province made of Terai districts alone, and all of its districts touch India. **Founding:** it was formed on 3 Ashoj 2072 as Province No. 2. On 3 Magh 2078 the Provincial Assembly named it Madhesh Province, by 80 votes, and made Janakpurdham the capital, by 78 votes. **Size and divisions:** with 9,661 sq km it is the smallest province. It has 8 districts — Saptari, Siraha, Dhanusha, Mahottari, Sarlahi, Rautahat, Bara and Parsa — and 136 local levels: one metropolitan city (Birgunj), three sub-metropolitan cities (Janakpurdham, Kalaiya, Jitpur Simara), 73 municipalities and 59 rural municipalities. **People:** the Census of 2078 counted 6,114,600 people, 20.97 percent of Nepal — second only to Bagmati. The density of 633 persons per sq km is the highest in the country, and the literacy rate of 63.5 percent the lowest. **Representation:** the province sends 32 directly elected members to the House of Representatives and 8 members to the National Assembly; its own Assembly has 107 members. **Conclusion:** Madhesh is the smallest province in land and almost the largest in people, so its strength is its people and its fertile soil, and its main task is to raise their education and income.

## 11.2 Physical geography (भौतिक भूगोल)

#### 1 · Why it matters

"Geography of Nepal" carries four questions in the Madhesh paper and "environment" three. A province question here is short: which river divides two districts, where a park lies, which hazard struck in which year. The drought of 2082 and the damage to the Chure hills are also current affairs.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Stand on the Chure hills and look south. The land falls in three steps.

1. **Chure** (चुरे, the Siwalik range) — low, young hills of loose sand and gravel along the northern edge of the province.
2. **Bhabar** (भावर) — a belt of gravel at the foot of the hills. Rain water sinks into it. Forest still stands here.
3. **Terai** (तराई) — the flat, fertile plain where almost all the people live. The water that sank in the Bhabar comes back here in wells and springs.

The rivers run from north to south, one beside the other, and cross the province in a few hours' flow before they enter Bihar. There is one great snow-fed river on the eastern edge (the Koshi), two middle rivers that rise in the Mahabharat range (the Kamala and the Bagmati), and many small Chure rivers that flood in the rains and turn to sand in winter.

The climate is hot. Summer brings heat and then heavy rain; winter brings fog and cold waves.

#### 3 · Key facts

**The land**

- Almost all of the province lies **below 300 m**. Only the Chure ridge in the north rises to several hundred metres; one encyclopaedia page gives 920 m as the highest point (not an official figure).
- **Three belts** from north to south: Chure, Bhabar, Terai.
- The soil is **alluvium** laid down by rivers. General-knowledge books call the province the **"granary of Nepal"** (अन्नको भण्डार).
- ⚖️ **Lowest point:** recent reports name **Mukhiyapatti Musharniya** in **Dhanusha**, at about 58 to 59 m, as the lowest point of the province — and of Nepal. Textbooks name Kechana Kawal in Jhapa (Chapter 1). Learn both.
- **Jaleshwar** (Mahottari) is named in general-knowledge books as the lowest district headquarters of Nepal (not checked against a survey).

**Rivers**

- **First grade (snow-fed):** the **Koshi**. It is the eastern boundary of the province, between Saptari and Sunsari. The **Koshi Barrage** stands on it near the border, built under the Koshi Agreement of 1954 (Chapter 8).
- **Second grade (from the Mahabharat range):**
  - the **Kamala** — the boundary between **Siraha** and **Dhanusha**;
  - the **Bagmati** — the boundary between **Sarlahi** and **Rautahat**.
- **Third grade (Chure rivers):** from east to west they include the **Khando** and **Balan** (Saptari, Siraha), the **Ratu** (Mahottari), the **Lakhandehi** (Sarlahi), the **Lal Bakaiya** (Rautahat), the **Bakaiya** and **Pasaha** (Bara) and the **Sirsiya** (Parsa, through Birgunj). The **Trijuga** joins the Koshi on the north-eastern edge.
- **Every river flows south into Bihar** and reaches the Ganga.
- **River problems:** beds filled with sand from the Chure; banks cut away; water held back near the border by embankments and roads on the Indian side, which floods towns such as Gaur; pollution of the Sirsiya by the factories of Birgunj.

**Climate**

- **Tropical monsoon** climate, among the hottest in Nepal. Summer days pass 40°C, with a hot dry wind (लू) before the rains.
- **Rain** falls mainly from Jestha to Ashoj. One encyclopaedia table gives yearly rain at eight towns: Rajbiraj about 1,220 mm, Lahan 1,230, Siraha 1,290, Jaleshwar 1,490, Janakpur 1,520, Gaur 1,590, Malangwa 1,820 and Birgunj 1,860. **In this province the west is wetter than the east** — the opposite of the rule for Nepal as a whole.
- **Winter:** fog and **cold waves** (शीतलहर) in Poush and Magh shut schools and kill the poor and the old.

**Hazards**

- **Flood and inundation** (डुबान) almost every monsoon.
- **Fire** in the dry, windy months of Chaitra and Baisakh, when thatched houses burn.
- **Cold wave**, **lightning** and **windstorm**. A violent windstorm struck **Bara and Parsa** on 17 Chaitra 2075 (31 March 2019) (Chapter 7).
- **Drought.** In 2082 BS the monsoon rain failed here while it fell elsewhere. Hand pumps and wells dried and paddy could not be planted.
  - The provincial government declared the eight districts drought-hit on 26 Ashad 2082 (10 July 2025).
  - The Government of Nepal declared **all eight districts a disaster crisis zone** (विपद् संकटग्रस्त क्षेत्र) on **7 Shrawan 2082 (23 July 2025)**, for three months, under the Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act, 2074.

**The Chure**

- The Chure hills are the youngest and weakest hills of Nepal. They hold the forest, catch the rain and refill the ground water of the plain.
- **Damage:** forest cut for timber and settlement; sand, gravel and stone dug from river beds.
- **Results:** flash floods, fields buried in sand, and a falling water table — one reason why the drought of 2082 hurt so much.
- The **President Chure–Terai Madhesh Conservation Development Board** was formed in **2071 BS** to protect it (Chapter 1).

**Forest and wildlife**

- **Forest:** about **237,600 hectares**, which is about **24.6 percent** of the province; with other wooded land, **25.8 percent** (forest survey figures as reported).
- The forest lies in the north, in the Chure and the Bhabar — the remains of the old *Charkose Jhadi* (चारकोसे झाडी). **89 of the 136 local levels have no forest at all.**
- Main trees: **sal**, sissoo and khair.
- **Parsa National Park** (पर्सा राष्ट्रिय निकुञ्ज):
  - a wildlife reserve from **1984**; a national park since **2017**;
  - **627.39 sq km** — 499 sq km at first, enlarged by 128 sq km in 2015;
  - in **Parsa** and **Bara** (Madhesh) and **Makwanpur** (Bagmati);
  - a buffer zone of 285.3 sq km since 2005;
  - joined to **Chitwan National Park** on the west and to the Valmiki Tiger Reserve of Bihar on the south;
  - wild elephant, gaur, tiger — 🔄 **71 tigers** in the count published in 2026 (Chapter 7);
  - the lakes **Halkhoriya** and **Kamini** (Bara).
- **Koshi Tappu Wildlife Reserve** (175 sq km; 1976): partly in **Saptari**, with Sunsari and Udayapur of Koshi Province. It is Nepal's only wildlife reserve, its first Ramsar site (1987) and the home of the wild water buffalo (अर्ना).
- **Chitwan National Park** touches Parsa district (Chapter 1).
- The province has **no Ramsar site and no World Heritage Site wholly its own**.

**Water for fields and power**

- **No large hydropower:** the land is flat. But the **Dhalkebar** sub-station (Dhanusha) is the Nepal end of the 400 kV line to Muzaffarpur, the main cross-border power line (Chapter 1).
- **Canals:** the Chandra Canal and the Koshi Western Canal in the east, the Kamala and Bagmati irrigation projects in the middle, the Narayani irrigation system in the west. General-knowledge books name the **Chandra Canal** of Saptari as Nepal's oldest modern canal.
- **Sunkoshi–Marin Diversion** 🔄 (a national pride project):
  - a tunnel **13.3 km** long carries water from the Sunkoshi to the Marin stream in Sindhuli, and so to the Bagmati; tunnel-boring began on 28 Ashoj 2079 (14 October 2022);
  - it is designed to water **122,000 hectares** in **Bara, Rautahat, Sarlahi, Mahottari and Dhanusha** through the Bagmati irrigation canals, and to produce **31.07 megawatts**;
  - the cost is given as Rs 49.42 billion; the whole project was **36.83 percent** built by Bhadra 2082.
- **Ponds:** Janakpurdham is called the city of ponds — Ganga Sagar, Dhanush Sagar, Ratna Sagar and many more.

#### 7 · Exam traps

- Madhesh has **no hill district**, but it is **not without hills**: the Chure runs along its north.
- **Parsa National Park** is not only in Parsa: it lies in Parsa, Bara and Makwanpur. It was a **wildlife reserve** until 2017.
- **Koshi Tappu** is a **wildlife reserve**, not a national park, and most of it is in Koshi Province.
- The **Koshi** is a first-grade river; the **Kamala** and **Bagmati** are second-grade; the **Ratu**, **Lakhandehi** and **Sirsiya** are third-grade.
- The **Bagmati** that floods Rautahat is the same river that flows through Kathmandu.
- The disaster crisis zone of **2082** was declared for **drought**, not for flood.
- The **Sunkoshi–Marin** tunnel is in **Sindhuli** (Bagmati Province); the fields it will water are in Madhesh.
- The **lowest point** is disputed ⚖️: Dhanusha in recent reports, Jhapa in textbooks.
- The **Dhalkebar** sub-station handles power; it does not produce it.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Describe the physical features of Madhesh Province and the natural hazards it faces.

**Model answer.** **Landform:** Madhesh Province is a plain. Almost all of it lies below 300 m. From north to south it has three belts: the Chure hills on the northern edge, the gravel belt of the Bhabar with its forest, and the flat, fertile Terai where the people live. **Rivers:** the snow-fed Koshi is the eastern boundary. The Kamala, between Siraha and Dhanusha, and the Bagmati, between Sarlahi and Rautahat, rise in the Mahabharat range. Many Chure rivers — Balan, Ratu, Lakhandehi, Lal Bakaiya, Sirsiya — flood in the rains and dry in winter. All flow into Bihar. **Climate:** tropical monsoon, with very hot summers, heavy summer rain and foggy winters. **Forest and wildlife:** about a quarter of the land is forest, nearly all in the north; Parsa National Park in the west and a part of Koshi Tappu Wildlife Reserve in the east protect elephant, tiger, gaur and wild water buffalo. **Hazards:** (1) flood and inundation in the monsoon; (2) drought when the rain fails — in Shrawan 2082 all eight districts were declared a disaster crisis zone; (3) cold waves in winter; (4) fire and windstorm before the rains, such as the storm of Chaitra 2075 in Bara and Parsa. **Root problem:** the loss of forest and the digging of river beds in the Chure bring floods and sand in the rains and dry wells in summer. **Conclusion:** protecting the Chure, storing water and bringing river water through projects such as the Sunkoshi–Marin Diversion are the keys to the safety of the plain.

## 11.3 History and archaeology (इतिहास र पुरातत्त्व)

#### 1 · Why it matters

"History of Nepal" carries two questions in the Madhesh paper. For this province the likely points are few and clear: King Janak and Sita of Mithila, the Karnat kingdom of Simraungadh, the Madhesh movement of 2063, and the naming of the province. Old sites and the dates of the two agreements are also asked.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

The history of the province has four layers.

1. **Ancient — Videha.** The kingdom of Videha, or Mithila, had its capital at Janakpur. Its kings bore the title Janak. Sita, the daughter of one of them, is the heroine of the Ramayana.
2. **Medieval — the Karnat kingdom.** From 1097 to 1324 a kingdom with its capital at Simraungadh, in today's Bara, ruled the plain.
3. **Unification.** The plain joined Nepal when Prithvi Narayan Shah's army won Makwanpur in 1762 and the eastern Sen kingdoms in 1773–1774.
4. **Modern — a voice for the plain.** Border towns such as Birgunj were the doors of the armed movement of 2007 BS. In 2063 BS the Madhesh movement put federalism into the constitution. In 2072 BS the plain between the Koshi and Parsa became Province No. 2, and in 2078 BS it took the name Madhesh.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Ancient: Videha and Mithila**

- The old kingdom of **Videha** (विदेह) had its capital at **Mithila**, identified with today's **Janakpurdham**.
- Its kings were called **Janak**. **Siradhwaj Janak** was the father of **Sita** (Janaki), who married **Ram** of Ayodhya.
- Tradition makes Janak's court a seat of learning, with the sages **Yajnavalkya**, **Gargi**, **Maitreyi** and **Ashtavakra**.
- **King Janak** and **Sita** are two of Nepal's eighteen **national heroes** (राष्ट्रिय विभूति) (Chapter 6).
- **Salhesh** (सलहेस) is the folk hero of the plain, remembered as a king of Mahisautha in today's **Siraha** and honoured above all by the Dusadh (Paswan) community. In the **Salhesh garden** (सलहेस फूलबारी) near Lahan a flower is said to bloom on 1 Baisakh each year.

**Medieval: the Karnat kingdom**

- **Nanyadeva** founded the **Karnat dynasty** in **1097 AD**. The capital was **Simraungadh** (सिम्रौनगढ) in today's **Bara**. The kingdom is also called Tirhut or Mithila; the chronicles of the Kathmandu Valley call it the Doya kingdom.
- The last king, **Harisimhadeva**, fled to the hills in **1324** when **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq** of Delhi attacked. Tradition says his family carried the goddess **Taleju** to Bhaktapur (Chapter 2).
- Afterwards the plain came under the **Sen kings** — broadly, Makwanpur in the west and Chaudandi in the east.
- Tradition says the ascetic **Sur Kishor Das** found an image of Sita at Janakpur in **1657 AD** and founded the present town.

**Unification and the war with the East India Company**

- **1762:** Makwanpur was won, and with it the plain below it. **1773–1774:** Chaudandi and Bijayapur were won, and with them Saptari and Siraha (Chapter 2).
- **War of 1814–1816:** a British post at **Parsa** was destroyed in 1815. In 1816 the British advanced through this plain towards Makwanpur, and the Treaty of Sugauli followed. Much of the Terai, including this region, came back to Nepal in **December 1816**.

**The Rana period**

- **1847:** the **Alau Parva** — forces loyal to the exiled King Rajendra were defeated at **Alau** in **Parsa**.
- **1897:** Prime Minister **Bir Shamsher** founded **Birgunj** and gave it his name. The older name of the place was **Gahawa**.
- **1967 BS (1910):** the **Janaki Temple** was completed (topic 11.4).
- **1927:** the **Raxaul–Amlekhgunj** railway, Nepal's first, opened through Parsa and Bara. **1937:** the Jaynagar–Janakpur line opened.
- General-knowledge books add two items of this period: the **Chandra Canal** of Saptari, and **Rajbiraj**, laid out as a planned town.

**From 2007 to 2046 BS**

- **Kartik 2007:** the Nepali Congress **Mukti Sena** took **Birgunj**, one of the first towns to fall in the armed movement against the Ranas.
- **Magh 2018 (January 1962):** a bomb was thrown at King Mahendra's vehicle in **Janakpur**. **Durgananda Jha** was executed for it in 2020 BS; he is remembered as a martyr.
- **2019 BS:** the eight districts were placed in three zones — Sagarmatha, Janakpur and Narayani.
- Parties that spoke for the plain grew in these years; the best known is the **Nepal Sadbhavana** movement of **Gajendra Narayan Singh** of Saptari.

**The Madhesh movements**

- **First movement, Magh 2063.**
  - The Interim Constitution was issued on 1 Magh 2063 (15 January 2007). It did not promise a federal state.
  - On **5 Magh 2063 (19 January 2007)** **Ramesh Mahato** was shot dead at **Lahan** (Siraha). He is remembered as the first martyr of the movement. Protest spread across the Terai for about three weeks, led by the **Madheshi Janadhikar Forum** of **Upendra Yadav**.
  - Result: the Interim Constitution was amended to promise a **federal system** and more constituencies for the plain.
- **Gaur, 7 Chaitra 2063 (21 March 2007):** a clash at Gaur (Rautahat) killed 27 people.
- **22-point agreement, 13 Bhadra 2064 (30 August 2007):** signed by **Ram Chandra Poudel** for the Government and **Upendra Yadav** for the Madheshi Janadhikar Forum, Nepal.
- **Second movement, Falgun 2064**, ended in the **eight-point agreement** of **16 Falgun 2064 (28 February 2008)** between Prime Minister **Girija Prasad Koirala** and the **United Democratic Madheshi Front**.
- **2065 BS:** **Dr Ram Baran Yadav** of **Dhanusha** became the **first President** of Nepal on 6 Shrawan 2065 (21 July 2008). The first Vice-President, **Parmananda Jha**, also came from this region.
- **Third movement, 2072 BS:** protest against the boundaries of the provinces in the new Constitution. Press reports count about fifty deaths between September 2015 and February 2016. Border points, above all Birgunj, were blocked for months. The **first amendment** of the Constitution was passed on 9 Magh 2072 (23 January 2016) (Chapter 3).
- The slogan **"One Madhesh, one province"** (एक मधेश, एक प्रदेश) was not met: Madhesh Province covers only eight of the Terai districts.

**The province**

- **3 Ashoj 2072:** Province No. 2 formed — the only province made wholly of Terai districts.
- **21 Mangsir 2074 (7 December 2017):** the first election of its Assembly.
- **Falgun 2074:** the first government, under **Lalbabu Raut**. It was the **only** provincial government then led by Madhesh-based parties; the other six were led by the alliance of the CPN (UML) and the Maoist Centre.
- **3 Magh 2078 (17 January 2022):** named Madhesh Province, with Janakpurdham as capital.
- **2082 BS:** four persons held the office of Chief Minister within one year (topic 11.6).

**Old sites**

- **Simraungadh** (Bara): ruins of the Karnat capital — walls, ponds and stone images.
- **Janakpurdham** (Dhanusha): the Janaki Temple and the old ponds.
- **Dhanushadham** (Dhanusha): the place where, in tradition, a piece of Shiva's bow fell when Ram broke it.
- **Murtiya** (Sarlahi): old stone images.
- **Parasgadhi** (Parsa): a fort of the war of 1814–1816.
- **Salhesh garden** (Siraha).

#### 6 · Memory aid

- **"J-K-S-M":** Janak (ancient) → Karnat (medieval) → Sen (before unification) → Madhesh movement (modern).
- **Karnat: "N to H, 1097 to 1324":** Nanyadeva to Harisimhadeva.
- **"22 before 8":** the 22-point agreement (Bhadra 2064) came before the eight-point agreement (Falgun 2064).
- **"Lahan lit it, Gaur bled, Janakpur was crowned":** Lahan 2063, Gaur 2063, capital 2078.
- **Three towns, three Ranas' years:** Alau 1847, Birgunj 1897 — fifty years apart.

#### 7 · Exam traps

- **Simraungadh** is in **Bara**, not in Dhanusha. **Janakpur** is the old capital of **Videha**, not of the Karnat kings.
- **Ramesh Mahato** died at **Lahan** (Siraha); the clash of Chaitra 2063 was at **Gaur** (Rautahat).

- The Madhesh movement of **2063** was about the **Interim Constitution**; the movement of **2072** was about the **Constitution of Nepal**.
- **Durgananda Jha** belongs to the Janakpur bomb case of 2018 BS, not to the Madhesh movement.
- **Bir Shamsher** founded Birgunj; **Bir**atnagar is not named after him.
- The **first President** came from **Dhanusha**. The first Chief Minister of the province came from **Parsa**.
- The plain returned in **1816**; the *Naya Muluk* of the far west returned in **1860**.
- The province was formed in **2072** but named in **2078**.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Describe the Madhesh movement of 2063–2064 BS and its results.

**Model answer.** **Background:** the people of the Terai had long felt left out of the State — in the army and civil service, in the number of constituencies, in language and in citizenship. The Interim Constitution of 1 Magh 2063 did not promise a federal state. **Course:** (1) the Madheshi Janadhikar Forum of Upendra Yadav protested against it; (2) on 5 Magh 2063 Ramesh Mahato was shot dead at Lahan in Siraha, and protest spread across the Terai for about three weeks; (3) the Prime Minister addressed the nation and the Interim Constitution was amended; (4) a clash at Gaur on 7 Chaitra 2063 killed 27 people; (5) the Government and the Forum signed a 22-point agreement on 13 Bhadra 2064; (6) a second movement in Falgun 2064 ended in the eight-point agreement of 16 Falgun 2064 with the United Democratic Madheshi Front. **Results:** (1) **federalism** entered the Interim Constitution — the greatest result; (2) constituencies were re-drawn so that the plain received seats nearer to its share of the population; (3) inclusion of Madheshi people in State bodies was promised, and proportional inclusion later became a constitutional principle; (4) Madhesh-based parties became a force in the Constituent Assembly, and Dr Ram Baran Yadav of Dhanusha became the first President; (5) in the long run, Province No. 2 was formed in 2072 and took the name Madhesh in 2078. **What was not met:** the demand for one province for the whole plain. **Conclusion:** the movement changed Nepal from a unitary to a federal state, which is why it is counted among the turning points of recent history.

## 11.4 Society and culture (समाज र संस्कृति)

#### 1 · Why it matters

"Social and cultural condition" carries four questions in the Madhesh paper — as many as geography. Questions on this province ask for a language, a festival and its month, a temple and its district, or a social problem and the law against it. Chapter 6 gives the national picture; this topic gives the province's own.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Three things make the society of Madhesh different from the rest of Nepal.

1. **Language.** Three languages lie side by side from east to west: **Maithili** in the east and centre, **Bajjika** in the middle-west, **Bhojpuri** in the west. Nepali is the mother tongue of only about six people in a hundred.
2. **Faith and custom.** Most people are Hindu, and the old culture of **Mithila** — its paintings, songs and festivals — gives the province its face. About one person in eight is **Muslim**, the largest share in any province.
3. **A society of many castes.** No single group is large: the biggest, the Yadav, is about 15 percent. Dalit communities of the plain are about one-sixth of the people and are the poorest.

The same society carries heavy problems: the lowest literacy in Nepal, above all among women; dowry; child marriage; caste discrimination; landlessness.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Peoples** (Census of 2078, as given by encyclopaedia pages that quote the census)

- **Yadav** 15.2 percent — the largest group.
- **Musalman** 13.0 percent.
- **Teli** 5.3; **Tharu** 4.9; **Kushwaha (Koiri)** 4.5; **Chamar** 4.4; **Dhanuk** 3.6; **Kurmi** 3.0; **Musahar** 2.9; **Dusadh (Paswan)** 2.9; **Mallah** 2.3.
- Hill groups are small: Brahman (Hill) about 2.3, Tamang about 2.2, Chhetri about 2.0 percent.
- **Dalits of the plain** — Chamar, Musahar, Dusadh, Dom, Tatma, Khatwe and others — are about **16 percent** together (added here from the same list).
- General-knowledge books name **Saptari** as the district with the most Dalit people and **Rautahat** as the district with the largest share of Muslims.

**Languages** (Census of 2078)

- **Maithili** about **41.7 percent** — the first language of the province and the second language of Nepal. (One line of the same encyclopaedia page gives 45.4 percent ⚖️.)
- **Bhojpuri** 18.8 percent; **Bajjika** 18.4 percent.
- **Nepali** 5.8 percent — only the **fourth**. Madhesh is the **only province where Nepali is not the largest mother tongue**.
- **Tharu** 4.2; **Urdu** 4.1; **Magahi** 3.5; **Tamang** 1.6 percent.
- Maithili's old script is **Mithilakshar** (Tirhuta); its great poet is **Vidyapati** (Chapter 6).
- The **Language Commission** recommended **Maithili, Bhojpuri and Bajjika** as languages of official work in the province besides Nepali, with **Urdu, Tharu and Tamang** for certain areas and purposes.
- 🔄 No provincial law on languages of official work was found (report of October 2026, Chapter 6). Koshi began the official use of thirteen languages on 25 Kartik 2083.

**Religion** (Census of 2078)

- **Hindu 84.2 percent; Islam 13.3 percent; Buddhist 2.2 percent;** Christian about 0.2 percent.
- Islam's share is the **highest of any province** (Nepal: 5.09 percent).

**Festivals**

- **Chhath** (छठ): worship of the setting and the rising sun at ponds and rivers, on the sixth day of the bright half of Kartik — 🔄 29 Kartik in 2083. It is the greatest festival of the plain.
- **Jitiya** (जितिया): mothers fast for the long life of their children, in Ashoj.
- **Sama Chakewa** (सामा चकेवा): a festival of brothers and sisters with clay birds, in Kartik.
- **Jur Sital** (जुड शीतल): the Maithili new year, in the first days of Baisakh.
- **Bibaha Panchami** (विवाह पञ्चमी): the wedding of Ram and Sita, at Janakpurdham, on the fifth day of the bright half of Mangsir — 🔄 28 Mangsir 2083 (14 December 2026).
- **Ram Navami** (Chaitra) and **Janaki Navami** (Baisakh) at Janakpurdham.
- **Holi** (फागु): kept in the Terai a day after the hills.
- **Eid** and **Muharram** among Muslims.
- **Mithila Madhyamiki Parikrama** (मिथिला माध्यमिकी परिक्रमा): a fifteen-day walk around old Mithila in Falgun, through Nepal and India, ending at Janakpurdham; its length is given as 133 or about 140 km.
- **Gadhimai fair** (गढीमाई मेला): every **five years** at **Bariyarpur**, Mahagadhimai Municipality, **Bara**. It is known across the world for animal sacrifice, which courts and campaigners have tried to reduce. The last fair was in Mangsir 2081; the days of sacrifice were 23 and 24 Mangsir 2081 (8 and 9 December 2024). 🔄 The next is due in 2086 BS.
- **Salhesh fair** on 1 Baisakh at the Salhesh garden, Siraha.

**Holy places and heritage, district by district**

- **Saptari:** **Chhinnamasta Bhagawati** (Sakhada), **Kankalini** (Bhardaha), Rajdevi (Rajbiraj).
- **Siraha:** the **Salhesh garden** near Lahan.
- **Dhanusha:** the **Janaki Temple**, the Ram Temple, the wedding pavilion (विवाह मण्डप) and the ponds of Janakpurdham; **Dhanushadham**.
- **Mahottari:** **Jaleshwar Mahadev**; **Matihani**, tied by tradition to the wedding rites of Sita.
- **Sarlahi:** the old images of **Murtiya**.
- **Rautahat:** the town of Gaur and the temples along the Bagmati.
- **Bara:** **Gadhimai** (Bariyarpur); **Simraungadh**.
- **Parsa:** **Gahawa Mai** of Birgunj; Parasgadhi.

**The Janaki Temple** (जानकी मन्दिर)

- Completed in **1967 BS (1910 AD)**.
- Built by **Queen Vrisha Bhanu of Tikamgarh**, in central India.
- It is said to have cost nine lakh, so it is also called the **Naulakha Mandir** (नौलखा मन्दिर).
- A white building of three storeys and 60 rooms, in a mixed Hindu–Mughal style; Chapter 6 lists it under the **dome style**.
- It stands where, in tradition, the image of Sita was found in 1657.
- The **Ram Janaki Temple** is on Nepal's **tentative list** for World Heritage (Chapter 6).

**Arts**

- **Mithila painting** (मिथिला चित्रकला): made by Maithil women, first on the mud walls of houses and now on paper and cloth; Janakpurdham is its centre. Floor designs are called **aripan**.
- **Dances:** **Jhijhiya** (women carry clay pots with lamps on their heads, at Dashain); **Jat-Jatin** (from the full moon of Shrawan to that of Bhadra; the story of the lovers Jat and Jatin); **Domkach**; the Salhesh dance.
- **Dress:** dhoti and kurta, sari; the **paag** (पाग), the headdress of honour of Mithila.
- **Food:** curd and beaten rice, fish, litti-chokha, makhana (fox nut), tilkor tarua. General-knowledge books add the **peda of Barmajhiya** (Saptari).

**Education**

- **Literacy 63.5 percent** — the lowest of the seven provinces. **Women: 54.7 percent.** **Rautahat** is the lowest district (57.75 percent).
- 🔄 By Bhadra 2082 four provinces had been declared literate; Madhesh was not among them (Chapter 6).
- **Universities:** **Rajarshi Janak University** (राजर्षि जनक विश्वविद्यालय), Janakpurdham, set up in **2074 BS**; **Madhesh Agricultural University**, Saptari (2079 BS); **Madhesh University**, Parsa (2079 BS).
- **"Beti Padhau, Beti Bachau"** (बेटी पढाऊ, बेटी बचाऊ — "teach the daughter, save the daughter"): a campaign begun by Chief Minister **Lalbabu Raut** in **2075 BS**.
  - An education insurance for girls born on or after 1 Magh 2075 (15 January 2019).
  - Bicycles for girls of Class 8 in community schools.
  - Reports give different days for its launch ⚖️. The purchase of bicycles later came under investigation, and the budget for 2083/84 revised the programme. 🔄

**Social problems**

- **Low literacy of women**, and girls leaving school early.
- **Dowry** (दाइजो, तिलक) and **child marriage**. The legal age of marriage is 20; asking for dowry is an offence (Chapter 6).
- **Caste discrimination** against Dalits; violence on the charge of **witchcraft** (Act of 2072).
- **Landlessness and bonded farm labour:** the **harawa–charawa** (हरवा–चरवा) of the Terai, ploughmen and herders tied to landlords by debt, were declared free in **2079 BS**.
- **Men working abroad:** many households live on money sent from the Gulf and Malaysia.
- 🔄 **Communal tension:** in Shrawan 2083 violence that began in Sunsari reached Siraha, Saptari and Dhanusha, and curfews were ordered; a curfew was also ordered in Birgunj on 22 Poush 2082 (6 January 2026) (Chapter 9).

**People and sport**

- **Dr Ram Baran Yadav** (Dhanusha), first President of Nepal.
- Leaders of Madhesh-based politics such as Gajendra Narayan Singh, Mahantha Thakur and Upendra Yadav.
- News reports called Prime Minister **Balendra Shah** the first Prime Minister from the Madhesh (Chapter 9).
- **Janakpur Bolts** were the first champions of the Nepal Premier League of cricket (2081 BS).

## 11.5 Economy (अर्थतन्त्र)

#### 1 · Why it matters

"Economic condition and planning" carries three questions in the Madhesh paper. Province questions ask where a crop, a factory zone or a customs point is, and how the province ranks in income. The figures of 2082/83 are current affairs: Madhesh grew the slowest of the seven provinces.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

The economy of Madhesh stands on four legs.

1. **Farming** on the fertile plain — paddy first, then sugarcane, vegetables, fish and mango.
2. **Industry** in one belt, the **Bara–Parsa corridor** from Birgunj to Simara and Pathlaiya.
3. **Trade** through **Birgunj**, the largest customs point of Nepal.
4. **Money sent home** by workers abroad.

Even so, the income of the average person here is the lowest in Nepal. The reasons are plain: very many people on little land, low education, factories in only one corner, and farming that depends on the monsoon. When the rain failed in 2082, the whole economy slowed.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Size of the economy** 🔄 (preliminary estimates of the National Statistics Office for 2082/83)

- **Gross domestic product:** about **Rs 863 billion** — **13.1 percent** of Nepal's. Madhesh is the **fourth** economy, after Bagmati (36.7), Koshi (15.8) and Lumbini (14.2).
- **Growth: 1.31 percent** — the **lowest** of the seven provinces. The year before it was 4.43 percent. The main cause was a fall in farm output, estimated at 1.26 percent, after the drought.
- **GDP per person: USD 934** — the **lowest** of the seven. Nepal's figure is USD 1,513; Bagmati's is USD 2,644.
- **Structure** (Economic Survey 2081/82, as reported): farming about 36.7 percent, industry about 10.7 percent, services about 52.6 percent. Farming weighs far more here than in Nepal as a whole (24.03 percent).
- **Poverty: 22.53 percent** (Living Standards Survey 2079/80), above the national 20.27 percent. Because the province is so populous, it has the **largest number** of poor people.
- **Human Development Index:** about 0.51, in the "low" group.

**Farming**

- About **574,000 hectares** of land can be tilled (an encyclopaedia figure). About two-thirds of working people farm.
- **Paddy** is the main crop. In the harvest reported in 2082 the province grew about **1.34 million tonnes** on about 383,000 hectares — about **23.4 percent** of Nepal's paddy. 🔄
- **Sugarcane:** Sarlahi, Rautahat, Bara. **Tobacco:** Dhanusha, Mahottari, Sarlahi (Chapter 5).
- **Mango:** Saptari and Siraha. **Vegetables:** the tomato of Lalbandi, Sarlahi.
- **Fish:** about **45,600 tonnes** from ponds in 2080/81 (a Nepal Rastra Bank study, as reported). **Bara** produces the most and **Parsa** the least.
- **Maize** is small here: Jhapa district alone grows more than the whole province (about 254,000 tonnes).
- **Problems:** small plots; tenancy; dependence on rain; late fertiliser; late payment to cane farmers by sugar mills; cheap imports across the open border.

**Industry**

- **Bara–Parsa industrial corridor:** the belt of Birgunj, Jitpur Simara and Pathlaiya — with the Sunsari–Morang corridor, one of Nepal's two great industrial belts. Steel, cement, food, drink, tobacco, medicine and plastics are made here.
- **Simara Special Economic Zone** (विशेष आर्थिक क्षेत्र), **Bara** — one of Nepal's two zones under the Special Economic Zone Act, 2073; the other is at Bhairahawa.
- **Industrial estate** at **Rajbiraj** (Saptari).
- **Sugar mills** in Sarlahi, Rautahat, Bara and other districts.
- State-owned factories of the past — the cigarette factory of Janakpur, the sugar mill and the farm-tool factory of Birgunj — have closed.

**Trade**

- **Birgunj** (Parsa) is Nepal's **largest customs point** and the main gate for goods from the port of Kolkata. It is called the **"gateway of Nepal"** (नेपालको प्रवेशद्वार).
- **Integrated Check Post**, Birgunj: opened on 24 Chaitra 2074 (7 April 2018) — the first in Nepal.
- **Dry port** at Sirsiya, Birgunj: the only one in Nepal joined to the Indian railway.
- **Motihari–Amlekhgunj petroleum pipeline:** from Bihar to **Amlekhgunj** in **Bara**; opened in September 2019; the first cross-border petroleum pipeline of South Asia (Chapter 8).
- Other border points: Gaur, Malangwa, Jaleshwar, and the rail crossing from Jaynagar.

**Transport**

- **Roads:**
  - the **East–West (Mahendra) Highway** along the north of the province;
  - the **Postal Highway** (हुलाकी राजमार्ग) along the south;
  - the **Tribhuvan Highway** from Birgunj to the hills — the first highway to link Kathmandu with the Terai (2013 BS);
  - the **B.P. Highway**, which ends at **Bardibas** (Mahottari);
  - the **Kathmandu–Terai Expressway**, about 72 km, which will end at **Nijgadh** (Bara). 🔄
- General-knowledge books say that **Parsa** is the only Terai district the Mahendra Highway does not pass through.
- **Railway:** the **Jaynagar–Janakpur–Bijalpura** line (Jaynagar is also written Jayanagar) — about 52 km in use, the **only passenger railway** running in Nepal. Jaynagar–Kurtha opened on 19 Chaitra 2078 (2 April 2022) and Kurtha–Bijalpura on 16 July 2023. The line crosses Dhanusha and Mahottari; the last part, to Bardibas, is not yet built. 🔄
- **Airports:** **Janakpur**, **Simara** and **Rajbiraj**. An international airport is **proposed** at **Nijgadh** (Bara). 🔄

**Tourism**

- **Religious tourism** is the strength: Janakpurdham (Bibaha Panchami, Ram Navami), Dhanushadham, Jaleshwar, Gadhimai, Chhinnamasta, the Salhesh garden.
- **Nature:** Parsa National Park; the birds of Koshi Tappu and the Koshi Barrage.
- **Culture:** Mithila painting and the ponds of Janakpurdham.

**Work abroad**

- Districts of this province are among those that send the most workers abroad, and remittance pays for much of what households spend (general statement; district figures were not checked).

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Madhesh Province has fertile land and the country's largest trade point, yet its income per person is the lowest. Explain the reasons and suggest remedies.

**Model answer.** **The facts:** in 2082/83 Madhesh produced 13.1 percent of Nepal's output, the fourth-largest share, but only USD 934 a person, the lowest; its growth of 1.31 percent was also the lowest. **Reasons:** (1) **Many people on little land** — 633 persons per sq km on the smallest area; plots are small and many farmers are tenants or landless. (2) **Farming depends on rain** — when the monsoon failed in 2082, farm output fell and the whole economy slowed; canals reach only part of the land. (3) **Low education and skills** — literacy is 63.5 percent, so most workers earn little at home or abroad. (4) **Industry in one corner** — the Bara–Parsa corridor is strong, but the six eastern districts have few factories; old public factories have closed. (5) **Trade passes through, it does not stay** — Birgunj collects customs for the Federation, while the goods and profits move on to Kathmandu. (6) **Floods, droughts and the damage to the Chure** destroy crops and roads. (7) **Unstable government** — six Chief Ministers in less than eight years — slows spending and plans. **Remedies:** (1) finish the Sunkoshi–Marin Diversion and other irrigation; protect the Chure; (2) raise yields with seed, fertiliser, storage and fair prices; build factories that use farm produce — rice, sugar, fish, mango, vegetables; (3) invest in schools, girls' education and technical training; (4) extend roads, the railway and industrial estates to the eastern districts; (5) use Janakpurdham and the Mithila culture for tourism; (6) spend the capital budget fully and keep policy steady. **Conclusion:** the province is poor not for want of resources but for want of water, skills and stable management; the vision "आत्मनिर्भर र समृद्ध मधेश, सुखी मधेशी" can be reached only by correcting these three.

## 11.6 Government and institutions (प्रदेश सरकार र संस्था)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Current affairs is the largest unit of the Madhesh paper, with five questions. The names of the Chief Minister, the Province Chief and the Speaker, the size of the budget and the vision of the plan are the most likely province questions of all. The Province Public Service Commission, which sets your examination, also belongs here. 🔄 Almost every fact in this topic can change; check it in the week before the examination.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

A province has three organs, like the Federation (Chapter 4).

- The **Province Chief** (प्रदेश प्रमुख) is appointed by the President and acts on the advice of the provincial government.
- The **Chief Minister** (मुख्यमन्त्री) and the Council of Ministers run the government.
- The **Provincial Assembly** (प्रदेश सभा) of 107 members makes provincial laws and passes the budget.

In Madhesh **no party has ever won a majority** — 54 of the 107 seats. Every government has been a **coalition**. When one partner leaves, the Chief Minister must win a new vote of confidence or go. That is why the province has had **six Chief Ministers** since 2074 BS, four of them within the single year 2082.

Article 168 gives the steps (Chapter 4): clause (1) the leader of a party with a majority; clause (2) a member who has the support of two or more parties; clause (3) the leader of the largest party. A Chief Minister appointed under clause (2) or (3) must win a vote of confidence within 30 days.

#### 3 · Key facts

**The Assembly and its numbers**

- **107 members:** 64 elected directly and 43 by the proportional system.
- **Majority: 54. Two-thirds: 72.** The Council of Ministers may have at most **21** members, counting the Chief Minister (20 percent of 107).
- It is the **second-largest** Provincial Assembly, after Bagmati's (110).
- **High Court:** Janakpur, with benches at Rajbiraj and Birgunj (Chapter 3).

**The first Assembly (2074–2079 BS)**

- Election: 21 Mangsir 2074 (7 December 2017).
- Seats: Federal Socialist Forum, Nepal **29**; Rastriya Janata Party Nepal **25**; CPN (UML) **21**; Nepali Congress **19**; CPN (Maoist Centre) **11**; Nepal Federal Socialist Party 1; independent 1.
- The Forum and the Rastriya Janata Party formed the government — the only provincial government of that time led by Madhesh-based parties. In 2077 BS the two united as the Janata Samajbadi Party.
- **First Chief Minister: Lalbabu Raut**, from Parsa, from Falgun 2074. He served the whole term — the only Chief Minister of the province to do so.
- **First Speaker: Saroj Kumar Yadav.** **First Province Chief: Ratneshwar Lal Kayastha**, appointed in Magh 2074.
- This Assembly named the province on 3 Magh 2078.

**The second Assembly (2079 BS onwards)**

- Election: 4 Mangsir 2079 (20 November 2022). First meeting: 18 Poush 2079 (2 January 2023).
- Seats won (direct + proportional): CPN (UML) **23** (15 + 8); Nepali Congress **22** (13 + 9); Janata Samajbadi Party **16** (9 + 7); Janamat Party **13** (6 + 7); Loktantrik Samajwadi Party **9** (7 + 2); CPN (Maoist Centre) **8** (4 + 4); CPN (Unified Socialist) **7** (4 + 3); Rastriya Prajatantra Party 1; Nepal Federal Socialist Party 1; Nagrik Unmukti Party 1; independents **6**.
- The largest party had only 23 seats — fewer than half of a majority.
- Later changes: independents joined parties; the Maoist Centre and the Unified Socialist became the **Nepali Communist Party**; the Loktantrik Samajwadi Party united with the **Janata Samajbadi Party Nepal**, which so became the largest party.
- 🔄 **Seats as reported on 25 Kartik 2083:** Janata Samajbadi Party Nepal **27**; CPN (UML) **23**; Nepali Congress **22**; Nepali Communist Party **15**; Janamat Party **12**; Nepal Federal Socialist Party 1; Rastriya Prajatantra Party 1. (These add to 101; the remaining seats were vacant, under suspension or not named in the report.)

**The six Chief Ministers**

1. **Lalbabu Raut** — Federal Socialist Forum, later Janata Samajbadi Party; Falgun 2074 to Poush 2079.
2. **Saroj Kumar Yadav** (of Bara) — Janata Samajbadi Party; Poush 2079 to Jestha 2081.
3. **Satish Kumar Singh** — Janamat Party; from 25 Jestha 2081 (7 June 2024) to Ashoj 2082.
4. **Jitendra Prasad Sonal** — Loktantrik Samajwadi Party; from 29 Ashoj 2082 (15 October 2025). He resigned on 22 Kartik 2082 (8 November 2025), a few hours before a vote of confidence, after 25 days.
5. **Saroj Kumar Yadav** (of Mahottari) — CPN (UML); sworn in on 24 Kartik 2082 (10 November 2025) under Article 168 (3); resigned on 17 Mangsir 2082 (3 December 2025) before a vote of confidence.
6. **Krishna Prasad Yadav** — Nepali Congress; appointed on **19 Mangsir 2082 (5 December 2025)** under **Article 168 (2)**, with the backing of 77 of the 104 sitting members.

Two different men named **Saroj Kumar Yadav** have been Chief Minister: one from Bara (the second) and one from Mahottari (the fifth).

**The crisis of Kartik–Mangsir 2082**

- When Chief Minister Sonal resigned, seven parties asked for a government under Article 168 (2); the CPN (UML), the largest party, asked for clause (3).
- Province Chief **Sumitra Subedi Bhandari** left Janakpurdham at about 4 am on 24 Kartik 2082, saying she was unwell, and at a hotel in **Bardibas** (Mahottari) swore in the UML leader at 5.20 am. The act was widely criticised.
- She was replaced. **Dr Surendra Labh Karna** took office as Province Chief on **25 Kartik 2082 (11 November 2025)**.
- The Assembly removed Speaker **Ram Chandra Mandal** (CPN-UML) on **3 Mangsir 2082 (19 November 2025)**: a motion that he had not behaved as his office requires passed by **76 votes to none**, more than two-thirds.
- **Ram Ashish Yadav** (Janata Samajbadi Party Nepal; Dhanusha) was elected Speaker on **6 Poush 2082 (21 December 2025)** by 72 votes to 24. The Deputy Speaker is **Babita Kumari Raut Ishar** (Janamat Party).

**The government of Krishna Prasad Yadav** 🔄

- **First vote of confidence, 4 Poush 2082 (19 December 2025):** all 92 members present voted for him, the opposition too.
- **21 Baisakh 2083 (4 May 2026):** the Janamat Party withdrew its support. The Chief Minister changed partners: the **CPN (UML)** and the **Nepali Communist Party** came in; the **Janata Samajbadi Party Nepal** and the Janamat Party went out.
- **Second vote of confidence, 8 Jestha 2083 (22 May 2026):** 62 for, 36 against; 100 members present.
- The three ruling parties signed a **15-point agreement**; the Council of Ministers was completed on 12 Jestha 2083 (26 May 2026).
- **30 Ashoj 2083 (16 October 2026):** the CPN (UML) withdrew its support, saying the 15-point agreement had not been carried out; its four ministers resigned. (A later news-agency summary gives 25 Ashoj ⚖️.)
- **20 Kartik 2083 (6 November 2026):** the Nepali Communist Party withdrew; its four ministers resigned, among them the Minister for Economic Affairs, Yuvaraj Bhattarai.
- **Third vote of confidence: fixed for 28 Kartik 2083 (14 November 2026).** The Nepali Congress has 22 seats; 54 are needed. **The result was not known when this chapter was written.**

**Budget for 2083/84** 🔄

- **Rs 41.1386 billion** (Rs 41 arab 13 crore 86 lakh).
- Presented on **1 Ashad 2083 (15 June 2026)** by the Minister for Economic Affairs, **Yuvaraj Bhattarai**. Passed unanimously on 31 Ashad 2083 (15 July 2026).
- **Recurrent:** Rs 14.66 billion. **Capital:** Rs 26.48 billion. Worked out here, that is about 35.6 and 64.4 percent; press reports print slightly different percentages.
- It is the **second-largest** provincial budget, after Bagmati's (Rs 66.93 billion), and about 12 to 13 percent smaller than the budget of 2082/83 (Rs 46.98 billion).
- **Sources, as reported:** own revenue Rs 11.23 billion; revenue shared by the Federation Rs 12.83 billion; federal grants Rs 9.50 billion; cash left from 2082/83 Rs 6.09 billion; internal loan Rs 1.50 billion. These five add exactly to the total.
- **Federal grants in full:** equalisation Rs 7.85 billion, conditional Rs 8.10 billion, complementary Rs 0.95 billion, special Rs 0.70 billion — **Rs 17.60 billion**, the largest sum given to any province (Chapter 4).
- ⚖️ **A point to notice:** the Rs 9.50 billion counted among the sources equals the equalisation, complementary and special grants. The conditional grant is reported beside them, not inside the Rs 41.14 billion. So the share "42.8 percent" shown for Madhesh in Chapter 5 compares all four grants with a total that seems to leave one of them out. This is this book's arithmetic from press figures; the budget speech itself was not read.
- **Priorities:** farming, education, health, roads and other works, forest.
- **Slogans in the speech:** "Made in Madhesh, Samriddha Pradesh"; a "nutrition pack" for pregnant women; a Chief Minister's dashboard to follow works. One report says the pay and allowances of provincial staff rose by 10 percent.

**Periodic plans**

- **First plan:** 2076/77–2080/81. It aimed at an income of USD 1,000 a person; about USD 932 was reached, as reported. Its vision wording was not found.
- **Second plan:** **2081/82–2085/86** — the same five years as the national Sixteenth Plan. It was prepared by the **Province Policy and Planning Commission** (प्रदेश नीति तथा योजना आयोग).
- **Vision (सोच):** **"आत्मनिर्भर र समृद्ध मधेश, सुखी मधेशी"** — a self-reliant and prosperous Madhesh, happy Madheshi people.
- **Goal (लक्ष्य):** "कृषिमा सबलीकरण, उच्च मानव विकास सूचकांकको प्राप्ति र सम्मानजनक रोजगारीमार्फत समृद्धि हासिल गर्ने" — prosperity through stronger farming, a high Human Development Index and decent work.
- **Four objectives:** (1) more farm output and self-reliance; (2) quality health and education, jobs and social justice; (3) industry, tourism and markets; (4) balance between development and environment — forest, watersheds, biodiversity and climate adaptation.
- **Targets for 2085/86, from third-party notes only:** growth of 7.5 percent; poverty down to 15 percent; GDP per person of USD 1,144. Compare the actual figures of 2082/83: growth 1.31 percent and USD 934.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Why have governments in Madhesh Province been unstable? Explain with the events of 2082 and 2083, and suggest remedies.

**Model answer.** **The fact:** since Falgun 2074 the province has had six Chief Ministers and six Province Chiefs; four persons were Chief Minister in the single year 2082. **Reasons:** (1) **A hung Assembly** — in 2079 the largest party won only 23 of 107 seats, and a majority needs 54, so at least three or four parties must join. (2) **Many parties of nearly equal size** — the UML, the Congress, the Janata Samajbadi, the Janamat and others each hold between 12 and 27 seats, so many different coalitions are possible. (3) **Coalitions made for office, not for a programme** — partners quarrel over ministries; the 15-point agreement of Jestha 2083 broke within five months. (4) **Influence of the centre** — changes of alliance in Kathmandu are copied in Janakpurdham. (5) **Misuse of constitutional offices** — the swearing-in at Bardibas on 24 Kartik 2082 and the removal of the Speaker on 3 Mangsir 2082 show the Province Chief's and the Speaker's offices drawn into party struggle. **Events:** Sonal resigned after 25 days; Saroj Kumar Yadav of the UML resigned before a confidence vote; Krishna Prasad Yadav was appointed on 19 Mangsir 2082, changed partners in Jestha 2083, lost the UML on 30 Ashoj and the Nepali Communist Party on 20 Kartik 2083, and faces a third vote of confidence on 28 Kartik 2083. **Effects:** budgets are spent late, ministers change before they learn their work, and plans such as the cut in ministries are not carried out. **Remedies:** (1) written common programmes and coordination committees for coalitions; (2) respect for the rules — Article 168 followed step by step, a neutral Province Chief and Speaker; (3) fewer ministries and a fixed division of them; (4) stronger party discipline and, in the long run, electoral reform that reduces splintering; (5) a permanent, professional administration that keeps services running whoever governs. **Conclusion:** stability cannot be ordered by law alone; it comes when parties treat the provincial government as a place to deliver development rather than to share posts.

## 11.7 The province in numbers and superlatives; fact sheet (तथ्याङ्क, विशेषता र तथ्य-पत्र)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Province questions are short facts. This topic puts the facts of the whole chapter on one page and sorts the "firsts", "mosts" and "leasts" that examiners like. Candidates of other provinces can read this topic alone: the superlatives of Madhesh are also answers to questions about Nepal.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

A **superlative** is a "most", a "least", a "first" or an "only". Madhesh has more of them than any other province, because it stands at the end of so many lists.

1. **At the top:** the most crowded, with the most towns and wards.
2. **At the bottom:** the smallest, with the fewest districts, the lowest literacy and the lowest income per person.
3. **The only one:** the only province of plain alone, the only one where Nepali is not the first language.

One sentence holds its shape: **one-fifth of Nepal's people live on one-fifteenth of its land and produce about one-eighth of its output.**

#### 3 · Key facts

**"Most" and "first" in Nepal**

- **Most densely populated province:** 633 persons per sq km.
- **Most municipalities:** 73 (77 with the metropolitan and sub-metropolitan cities). **Most wards:** 1,271.
- **Largest share of Muslims** of any province: 13.3 percent.
- 🔄 **Largest federal grants** for 2083/84: Rs 17.60 billion.
- **Largest customs point** of Nepal: Birgunj. **First Integrated Check Post:** Birgunj (2018).
- **Only passenger railway now running** in Nepal: Jaynagar–Janakpur–Bijalpura.
- **First railway of Nepal** ran here: Raxaul–Amlekhgunj (1927).
- **First cross-border petroleum pipeline of South Asia** ends here: Amlekhgunj (2019).
- **First President of Nepal:** Dr Ram Baran Yadav of Dhanusha.
- **First champions of the Nepal Premier League:** Janakpur Bolts.
- ⚖️ **Lowest point of Nepal** in recent reports: Mukhiyapatti Musharniya, Dhanusha.

**Question.** "Madhesh is the province of the smallest and the most." Explain with facts.

**Model answer.** **The smallest:** (1) in **area** — 9,661 sq km, about 6.6 percent of Nepal; (2) in **districts** — only eight; (3) in **literacy** — 63.5 percent, and 54.7 percent among women; (4) in **income** — USD 934 a person in 2082/83, with the slowest growth, 1.31 percent. **The most:** (1) in **density** — 633 persons per sq km, more than three times the figure for Nepal; (2) in **municipalities** (73) and **wards** (1,271); (3) in its share of **Muslims** (13.3 percent) and in the use of languages other than Nepali — Maithili, Bhojpuri and Bajjika; (4) in **federal grants** — Rs 17.60 billion for 2083/84; (5) in trade — Birgunj is Nepal's largest customs point. **Nearly the most:** second in population (6,114,600, only 2,266 behind Bagmati), in local levels (136), in Assembly seats (107), in House seats (32) and in the size of the budget. **The only:** the only province made of Terai districts alone, the only one where Nepali is not the first mother tongue, and the only one with more men than women in the last census. **Meaning:** a fifth of Nepal's people live here on a fifteenth of its land and produce about an eighth of its output. The numbers show both the weight of the province in the nation and the size of its task — to turn its many people into educated, productive citizens. **Conclusion:** the "most" of Madhesh is its people; the "smallest" is, so far, what each of them receives.

### D. Five short-answer questions

**1. Describe how Province No. 2 became Madhesh Province, and explain the importance of the name.**
*Points for the answer:* the rule — Articles 288 (2) and 295 (2): capital and name by two-thirds of all members of the Provincial Assembly · the delay — the first Assembly, elected in 2074, could not agree for four years; names proposed were Madhesh, Janaki, Mithila Bhojpura and Madhya Madhesh; some members wanted Birgunj as capital · the decision — 3 Magh 2078 (17 January 2022): 99 members voted; 80 for the name "Madhesh Province", 78 for Janakpurdham; a public holiday the next day · importance — recognition of the identity for which the Madhesh movements of 2063, 2064 and 2072 were fought; the first time the word "Madhesh" entered the map of the State; an end to a dispute that had held up other work · limits — the province covers only eight Terai districts, not the whole plain; a name does not by itself bring development · conclusion.

**2. Why are the Chure hills called the lifeline of Madhesh? What harms them and how can they be protected?**
*Points for the answer:* what the Chure is — the youngest, weakest hills, of sand and gravel, along the north of the province · why a lifeline — holds the forest; catches the rain; refills the ground water of the Terai through the Bhabar; slows floods · harms — felling and encroachment; sand, gravel and stone taken from river beds; roads and settlement · results — flash floods and sand on fields; river beds rising; wells and hand pumps drying; the drought of 2082 and the disaster crisis zone of 7 Shrawan 2082 · protection — the President Chure–Terai Madhesh Conservation Development Board (2071 BS); control of mining; tree planting and community forests; ponds and recharge works; alternative livelihoods; cooperation of Bagmati and Koshi Provinces upstream and of the three levels of government · conclusion: without the Chure there is no water, and without water no Madhesh.

**3. Discuss the potential of Janakpurdham as a centre of religious and cultural tourism, the problems, and the measures needed.**
*Points for the answer:* potential — capital of old Videha; birthplace of Sita; the Janaki Temple (1967 BS) and the wedding pavilion; Bibaha Panchami and Ram Navami; the ponds; Dhanushadham; Mithila painting, songs and food; the fifteen-day Mithila Madhyamiki Parikrama; links with Ayodhya and the Ramayana circuit; the railway from Jaynagar and the airport · the Ram Janaki Temple is on Nepal's tentative list for World Heritage · problems — dirty and encroached ponds; poor roads, drainage and lodging; visitors who stay only a few hours; weak publicity; unstable government · measures — a master plan for the heritage zone; clean ponds and streets; hotels and home-stays; guides and a museum of Mithila art; a festival calendar; the railway extended to Bardibas; better border facilities for pilgrims; work towards World Heritage listing · conclusion.

**4. Explain the place of Birgunj and the Bara–Parsa corridor in the economy of Nepal.**
*Points for the answer:* Birgunj — the "gateway of Nepal"; the largest customs point; the nearest route to the port of Kolkata; the Integrated Check Post (2018) and the rail-linked dry port; the Tribhuvan Highway to Kathmandu · the corridor — Birgunj, Jitpur Simara, Pathlaiya: steel, cement, food, medicine; the Simara Special Economic Zone; the petroleum pipeline to Amlekhgunj (2019); Simara airport · what is coming — the Kathmandu–Terai Expressway to Nijgadh; the proposed airport at Nijgadh; proposed railway links 🔄 · problems — pollution of the Sirsiya river; strikes and border closures; curfews such as that of 22 Poush 2082; power and transport costs; the eastern districts left behind · conclusion: the corridor earns much of the Federation's revenue; the task is to spread such industry eastwards along the plain.
