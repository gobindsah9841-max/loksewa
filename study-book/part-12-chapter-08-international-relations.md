## 8.1 Foreign policy of Nepal (नेपालको परराष्ट्र नीति)

#### 1 · Why it matters

At least five of the seven syllabi open this unit with "Nepal's relations" with other countries. Before the single countries come the general questions: the bases of foreign policy, Panchsheel, the Zone of Peace proposal, the first country to have relations with Nepal, and the dates on which Nepal joined world bodies.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

**Foreign policy** (परराष्ट्र नीति) is the set of aims and rules by which a state deals with other states. **Diplomacy** (कूटनीति) is the skill and the method used to carry it out — talks, visits, treaties and embassies.

Nepal is a small, landlocked country between two very large neighbours. Its foreign policy therefore has two fixed aims: to keep the country independent and whole, and to win help for its development. It tries to be a friend of both neighbours and an ally of neither.

#### 3 · Key facts

**What the Constitution says** (text in Chapter 3)

- **Article 5** lists the basic elements of the **national interest** (राष्ट्रिय हित): freedom, sovereignty, territorial integrity, nationality, independence and dignity of Nepal, the rights of Nepalis, border security, and economic well-being and prosperity.
- **Article 50 (4)** — a directive principle: international relations shall raise the dignity of the nation in the world community, on the basis of **sovereign equality**.
- **Article 51 (m)** — the policy on international relations:
  - an **independent foreign policy** based on the **Charter of the United Nations, non-alignment, the principles of Panchsheel, international law and the norms of world peace**;
  - review of past treaties, and new treaties on the basis of **equality and mutual interest**.
- **Article 278** — only the **Federation** may make treaties. It must consult a Province on a matter in the provincial list. A Province may, with the consent of the Government of Nepal, make **contractual agreements on financial and industrial matters** only.
- **Article 279** — a treaty on four subjects needs **two-thirds of all members of both Houses**: peace and friendship; defence and strategic alliance; the boundaries of Nepal; natural resources and the sharing of their use.
- **Article 282** — the **President** appoints ambassadors and receives the credentials of foreign ambassadors. **Article 292** requires a parliamentary hearing before an ambassador is appointed.
- **Schedule 5** places foreign and diplomatic affairs, international relations and United Nations matters in the federal list.

**Ideas that guide the policy**

- **"A yam between two boulders" (दुई ढुङ्गाबीचको तरुल).** In his *Dibya Upadesh*, Prithvi Narayan Shah advised keeping friendship with both the ruler of the north and the ruler of the south.
- **Non-alignment (असंलग्नता).** Nepal joins no military bloc. Non-alignment is not isolation: Nepal is active in world bodies.
  - Nepal took part in the **Bandung Conference** of Asian and African states in April 1955.
  - It was a **founding member of the Non-Aligned Movement** at Belgrade in September 1961.
- **Panchsheel (पञ्चशील)** — five principles of peaceful co-existence, first written in an agreement between India and China on 29 April 1954:
  1. mutual respect for each other's territorial integrity and sovereignty;
  2. mutual non-aggression;
  3. mutual non-interference in each other's internal affairs;
  4. equality and mutual benefit;
  5. peaceful co-existence.
- **Zone of Peace (शान्ति क्षेत्र).** King Birendra proposed on **13 Falgun 2031 (25 February 1975)**, at his coronation, that Nepal be declared a Zone of Peace. In time 116 countries supported it. India did not, and the proposal was dropped after 2046 BS.
- **"Amity with all, enmity with none" (सबैसँग मित्रता, कसैसँग शत्रुता छैन)** — the common summary of the policy.

**Stages of the policy**

| Period | Feature |
|---|---|
| Unification | Balance between the two neighbours; defence of independence |
| Rana period | Isolation from the world; close ties with British India |
| After 2007 BS | Opening to the world; relations with many countries; United Nations membership in 1955 |
| Panchayat period | Non-alignment; wider relations; Zone of Peace proposal; SAARC |
| After 2046 BS | Economic diplomacy; regional cooperation; labour migration |
| Federal republic | Foreign policy written into the Constitution; a written policy in 2077 BS |

**Foreign Policy, 2077 (परराष्ट्र नीति, २०७७)**

- The Council of Ministers approved it in **Kartik 2077 (October 2020)**. It is the first single written statement of Nepal's foreign policy.
- It gives priority to the neighbours. It stresses **economic diplomacy** (अर्थ कूटनीति), the safety of Nepalis working abroad, **public diplomacy** (the use of culture and image, also called soft power) and the mountain and climate agenda.
- **Economic diplomacy** means using foreign relations to win trade, investment, tourists, aid, technology and safe jobs abroad.

**Who carries out the policy** 🔄

- The **Ministry of Foreign Affairs** (परराष्ट्र मन्त्रालय), Singha Durbar. Minister: **Shisir Khanal**. Foreign Secretary: **Amrit Bahadur Rai**.
- Under it: the Department of Passports, the Department of Consular Services and the Institute of Foreign Affairs.
- **Missions abroad: 44** — 31 embassies, 3 permanent missions to the United Nations (New York, Geneva, Vienna) and 10 consulates general (June 2026). The government was reviewing the number of missions in 2026.

**Diplomatic relations** 🔄

- The **first** country was the **United Kingdom, in 1816**. Before 2007 BS Nepal had relations with only four countries: the United Kingdom, the United States, India and France.
- Nepal now has relations with **188 countries**. Five were added in September 2026 alone:

**Recent direction** 🔄

- The government formed on 13 Chaitra 2082 (27 March 2026) under Prime Minister **Balendra Shah** calls its approach **"development diplomacy"** — foreign relations in the service of projects, investment and jobs.
- The Foreign Minister visited **India on 5–7 June 2026** and **China on 13–17 June 2026**.
- The Prime Minister's first trip abroad was to the **81st session of the United Nations General Assembly**. He spoke in the general debate on **8 Ashoj 2083 (24 September 2026)**. After the Bhote Koshi flood he asked for **climate justice** and proposed a **Himalayan Climate Resilience Mechanism** led by Nepal, India and China.
- His first bilateral visit, to India, was planned for 22–25 November 2026.

#### 6 · Memory aid

- **Bases of foreign policy: "U – N – P – I – W"** — UN Charter, Non-alignment, Panchsheel, International law, World peace (the same aid as in Chapter 3).
- **Panchsheel: "R – N – N – E – P"** — Respect, Non-aggression, Non-interference, Equality, Peaceful co-existence.
- **First four friends: "U – U – I – F"** — United Kingdom (1816), United States (April 1947), India (June 1947), France (1949).
- **Two-thirds treaties: "P – D – B – N"** — Peace, Defence, Boundary, Natural resources.
- **"Mahendra joined, Birendra proposed."** Membership of the United Nations and the Non-Aligned Movement came in King Mahendra's time; the Zone of Peace was King Birendra's proposal.

#### 7 · Exam traps

- The first country was the **United Kingdom**, not India. In 1947 the **United States** came seven weeks **before India**.
- The **Zone of Peace** was proposed by **Birendra** in 1975, not by Mahendra.
- **Panchsheel** comes from an India–China agreement of 1954, not from the United Nations Charter. The Constitution of Nepal names it.
- **Non-alignment** does not mean neutrality in everything or isolation.
- **188** is the number of countries with diplomatic relations. The number of **embassies** is **31**.
- A **Province** cannot make a treaty. It can make only contractual agreements on financial and industrial matters, with federal consent.
- The count of countries changes. Check the latest figure before the examination.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** State the bases of Nepal's foreign policy. What are its main aims and its main challenges?

**Model answer.** Foreign policy is the set of aims and rules by which a state deals with other states. **Bases:** Article 51 (m) of the Constitution names five — the Charter of the United Nations, non-alignment, the principles of Panchsheel, international law and the norms of world peace. Article 5 defines the national interest, and Article 50 (4) asks for relations based on sovereign equality. **Aims:** (1) to protect sovereignty, territorial integrity and independence; (2) to keep balanced and friendly relations with both neighbours; (3) to win trade, investment, tourists, aid and technology through economic diplomacy; (4) to protect Nepalis working abroad; (5) to raise Nepal's voice on climate change, peace-keeping and the needs of landlocked and least developed countries. **Challenges:** (1) rivalry between large powers, which pulls a small state in different directions; (2) the boundary questions with India; (3) heavy dependence on one neighbour for trade and transit; (4) a large trade deficit and dependence on remittance; (5) frequent changes of government and of ambassadors; (6) small budgets and few trained diplomats. **Conclusion:** a steady policy built on national consensus, with economic diplomacy at its centre, serves Nepal best.

## 8.2 Nepal and India (नेपाल–भारत सम्बन्ध)

#### 1 · Why it matters

India is the first country named in every list of "neighbours". Questions ask for the treaty of 1950, the river agreements and their years, the open border, the boundary dispute and the newest visits and projects.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

India surrounds Nepal on three sides — east, south and west. The border is open: citizens of both countries cross without a passport or visa. The two peoples share religion, language, marriage ties and markets. India is Nepal's largest partner in trade, the route to the sea, and the source of petroleum.

The relation is close but not always easy. Nepal wants some old treaties reviewed, and the two countries disagree about parts of the boundary.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Basics**

- Diplomatic relations: **13 June 1947**.
- Boundary: about **1,880 km** ⚖️ (Chapter 1). Five Indian states touch Nepal — from east to west **Sikkim, West Bengal, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh and Uttarakhand**.
- **Open border (खुला सिमाना):** no passport or visa for citizens of either country.
- Six of Nepal's seven provinces touch India. **Karnali** does not.

**Treaties and agreements**

- **Sugauli Treaty, 1816** — with the East India Company; it fixed the Mechi and the Mahakali as boundaries (Chapter 2).
- **Treaty of Peace and Friendship, 16 Shrawan 2007 (31 July 1950)** — signed in Kathmandu by Prime Minister Mohan Shamsher and the Indian Ambassador Chandreshwar Prasad Narayan Singh.
  - It has **10 Articles**. It promises peace and friendship, and gives the citizens of each country equal treatment in the other in residence, property, trade and movement.
  - Either side may end it with **one year's notice** (Article 10).
  - Nepal has long asked for it to be reviewed.
- **Koshi Agreement, 25 April 1954** — the Koshi Barrage and embankments; revised in 1966.
- **Gandak Agreement, 4 December 1959** — the Gandak (Narayani) Barrage and canals.
- **Mahakali Treaty, 29 Magh 2052 (12 February 1996)** — one treaty for the Sarada Barrage, the Tanakpur Barrage and the **Pancheshwar Multipurpose Project**. Parliament ratified it by a two-thirds majority.
- **Trade and transit:** a Treaty of Trade, a Treaty of Transit and an agreement to control unauthorised trade. A revised Treaty of Transit was signed on 1 June 2023. Nepal's sea trade uses the ports of **Kolkata–Haldia** and **Visakhapatnam**.
- **Electricity:** a Power Trade Agreement in 2014; an agreement of **4 January 2024** under which India is to buy **10,000 megawatts within ten years**; Nepali power has reached Bangladesh through India's grid since 15 November 2024 (Chapters 1 and 5).

**Economy and links**

- India takes about **61 percent** of Nepal's foreign trade 🔄 (Chapter 5). All petroleum comes from the Indian Oil Corporation.
- The Nepali rupee is fixed to the Indian rupee at **Rs 1.60 = 1 Indian rupee**.
- **Motihari–Amlekhgunj petroleum pipeline** — opened in September 2019; the **first cross-border petroleum pipeline of South Asia**.
- **Jaynagar–Kurtha railway** — passenger trains since April 2022; the line was later extended to Bijalpura.
- **Integrated check posts:** Birgunj (2018), Biratnagar (2020), Nepalgunj (2023). One at Bhairahawa has been built; one at Dodhara-Chandani is planned.
- Indian companies are building large hydropower projects, among them **Arun III** (900 megawatts).
- Nepalis serve in the **Gorkha regiments of the Indian Army** under the Tripartite Agreement of 1947. Recruitment has been at a halt since India brought in its short-service "Agnipath" scheme in 2022.

**Joint bodies**

- **Joint Commission** — led by the two foreign ministers; its seventh meeting was in Kathmandu in January 2024.
- **Boundary Working Group** — set up in 2014 for boundary pillars and technical work; its seventh meeting was in New Delhi on 28–29 July 2025.
- **Eminent Persons Group** — four members from each side, formed in 2016 to review the whole relation, including the 1950 treaty. It finished one joint report in 2018. The two prime ministers have not yet received it.

**Problems**

- **Kalapani – Lipulekh – Limpiyadhura (Darchula).**
  - Nepal's claim rests on the Sugauli Treaty: the **Kali (Mahakali) river** is the boundary, and Nepal holds that it rises at **Limpiyadhura**.
  - India opened a road to Lipulekh on 8 May 2020. Nepal published a new map on **7 Jestha 2077 (20 May 2020)** and put it in the coat of arms by the second amendment (Chapter 3).
  - India and China agreed to trade through Lipulekh in May 2015 and again in August 2025. Nepal objected each time.
  - 🔄 On **20 Baisakh 2083 (3 May 2026)** Nepal sent diplomatic notes to **both India and China** against a pilgrimage route to Kailash–Mansarovar through Lipulekh.
- **Susta (Nawalparasi West).** The Narayani river has changed its course, and both countries claim the land.
- **Floods and embankments** along the border; a very large **trade deficit**; delay in Pancheshwar; Nepal's wish for more **air-entry routes**; the supply blockade at the border in 2015–2016.

**The year 2026** 🔄

- **17 Jestha 2083 (31 May 2026):** Prime Minister Shah told Parliament that boundary questions should be settled on facts and through talks.
- **5–7 June 2026:** visit of Foreign Minister Khanal to New Delhi; talks with External Affairs Minister **S. Jaishankar**.
  - A link between India's Unified Payments Interface (UPI) and Nepal's National Payments Interface was opened for sending money between persons.
  - India handed over 72 health buildings and 12 heritage projects built after the earthquake of 2072.
- **August 2026:** after the Bhote Koshi flood India sent relief goods, a tunnel-rescue team and forensic experts.
- **Early November 2026:** India's Foreign Secretary **Vikram Misri** came to Kathmandu to prepare the Prime Minister's visit.
- **22–25 November 2026 (planned):** the first bilateral visit abroad of Prime Minister Shah, for talks with Prime Minister **Narendra Modi**. Check what it produced.

## 8.3 Nepal and China (नेपाल–चीन सम्बन्ध)

#### 1 · Why it matters

China is the second neighbour named in every list. Questions ask for the date of diplomatic relations, the two treaties of 1960 and 1961 and who signed them, the border points, the transit agreement and the Belt and Road papers.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

China lies to the north, beyond the Himalaya. Nepal touches only one part of China — the **Tibet Autonomous Region**. The high mountains make travel hard, so trade with China is far smaller than trade with India. Even so, China is Nepal's second-largest source of imports and a large giver of aid and loans.

Nepal follows the **one-China policy**: it treats Tibet and Taiwan as parts of China and does not allow its soil to be used against China. China, for its part, says it respects Nepal's independence and its own choice of path.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Old ties** (Chapter 2)

- Princess Bhrikuti in Tibet; the artist Araniko at the court of Kublai Khan; the Chinese pilgrims who visited Lumbini.
- Treaties after wars: Kerung (1789), Betrawati (1792), Thapathali (1856).

**Modern relations**

- **Diplomatic relations: 1 August 1955**, on the basis of Panchsheel. The 70th anniversary was marked in 2025.
- **Treaty of Peace and Friendship, 28 April 1960** — signed in Kathmandu by Prime Minister **B.P. Koirala** and Premier **Zhou Enlai**.
- **Boundary Treaty, 5 October 1961** — signed in Beijing by **King Mahendra** and Chairman **Liu Shaoqi**. The boundary was then marked on the ground.
- Boundary: about **1,439 km** ⚖️ (Chapter 1). Madhesh and Lumbini are the two provinces that do not touch China.
- **State visit of President Xi Jinping, 12–13 October 2019** — the first by a Chinese president in 23 years. The two sides named the relation a "strategic partnership of cooperation featuring ever-lasting friendship for development and prosperity".
- **8 December 2020:** Nepal and China together announced the height of Sagarmatha — **8,848.86 metres** (Chapter 1).

**Transit and the Belt and Road**

- **Transit and Transport Agreement** — signed in Beijing in March 2016. Its **Protocol** was signed in April 2019. Nepal may use:
  - four sea ports — **Tianjin, Shenzhen, Lianyungang, Zhanjiang**;
  - three dry ports — **Lanzhou, Lhasa, Xigatse**.
- **Belt and Road Initiative (BRI)** — China's plan of roads, railways, ports and power lines across many countries.
  - Memorandum of Understanding: **12 May 2017**, Kathmandu.
  - **Framework for Belt and Road Cooperation: 4 December 2024**, Beijing. Ten projects were named, among them the Tokha–Chhahare tunnel, the Hilsa–Simikot road, the Kimathanka–Khandbari road and the Nepali part of a cross-border railway.
- The aim often stated is to turn Nepal from a **"landlocked" into a "land-linked"** country.

**Border points**

- **Rasuwagadhi–Kerung** (Rasuwa) — the main trade point in recent years.
- **Tatopani (Kodari)–Khasa** (Sindhupalchok) — on the Araniko Highway.
- **Korala** (Mustang), **Hilsa** (Humla), **Kimathanka** (Sankhuwasabha) and others (Chapter 1).
- Floods cut the Rasuwagadhi route twice: on 24 Ashad 2082 (8 July 2025) the border bridge was swept away, and on 10 Bhadra 2083 (26 August 2026) the Bhote Koshi flood destroyed the customs point (Chapter 7).

**Chinese aid and loans — examples**

| Project | Note |
|---|---|
| Araniko Highway (Kathmandu–Kodari) | Opened in the 1960s; the first road to the northern border |
| Kathmandu Ring Road | Built, and later widened, with Chinese help |
| Civil Service Hospital, Kathmandu | Gift of China |
| Pokhara International Airport | Loan from China's Export–Import Bank; opened on 1 January 2023 |

**Nepal, China and world bodies**

- Nepal is a **founding member of the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank**, whose seat is Beijing.
- Nepal is a **dialogue partner** of the **Shanghai Cooperation Organisation** and has asked to be made an observer.

**The years 2025 and 2026** 🔄

- **30 August – 3 September 2025:** Prime Minister K.P. Sharma Oli visited China for the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation's meeting at Tianjin and a parade in Beijing, and met President Xi Jinping.
- **20 Baisakh 2083 (3 May 2026):** Nepal's note to China said that Lipulekh is Nepali land (topic 8.2).
- **13–17 June 2026:** visit of Foreign Minister Khanal to Beijing; talks with Foreign Minister **Wang Yi** on 15 June.
  - Nepal repeated the one-China policy.
  - Both sides agreed to speed up projects already agreed and to work on roads, power lines, ports and air links under the Belt and Road.
- **August 2026:** after the Bhote Koshi flood China sent relief goods and a tunnel-rescue team.

## 8.4 Nepal and the United Kingdom, the United States and Japan (नेपाल र बेलायत, अमेरिका, जापान)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Gandaki, Lumbini, Sudurpashchim, Karnali and Bagmati name these three countries after the neighbours. Each has one or two facts that examiners like: the oldest relations and the Gurkhas (United Kingdom), the MCC compact (United States), and aid projects and the anniversary of 2026 (Japan).

#### 2 · Simple explanation

These three are far from Nepal but have been friends and helpers for a long time. The **United Kingdom** is the oldest partner; Nepalis have served in its army for two centuries. The **United States** was the second country to open relations and gave Nepal its first foreign aid. **Japan** is one of the largest givers of grants and cheap loans, and many Nepalis study and work there.

A country that gives aid or loans for development is called a **development partner** (विकास साझेदार).

#### 3 · Key facts

**United Kingdom (बेलायत)**

- **Oldest diplomatic relations: 1816**, after the Sugauli Treaty. A British Resident then lived in Kathmandu.
- **Treaty of 6 Poush 1980 (21 December 1923)** — Britain recognised Nepal as fully independent (Chapter 2).
- Nepal's **first mission abroad** was its legation in **London, opened in 1934**.
- **Gurkhas (गोर्खा सैनिक):** recruitment into the British army began in 1815. The **Tripartite Agreement of 1947** between Nepal, India and Britain divided the Gurkha regiments between the Indian and British armies. Nepali Gurkha soldiers have won 13 Victoria Crosses.
- The two countries marked **200 years** of relations in 2016.
- 🔄 **Gurkha veterans** ask for pensions equal to those of British soldiers. Talks between the two governments and the veterans were held in London in March 2026 and brought no settlement; the Foreign Minister raised the matter again in June and July 2026.
- 🔄 Head of state: **King Charles III**. Prime Minister: **Andy Burnham**, since 20 July 2026, after Keir Starmer resigned.

**United States (संयुक्त राज्य अमेरिका)**

- **Diplomatic relations: 25 April 1947** — the second country. An agreement of commerce and friendship was signed that day in Kathmandu.
- The United States gave Nepal its **first foreign aid**, under an agreement of January 1951. The **Peace Corps** has sent volunteers since 1962.
- **Millennium Challenge Corporation (MCC) compact:**
  - signed in Washington on **14 September 2017**;
  - ratified by Parliament on **15 Falgun 2078 (27 February 2022)** with a **12-point interpretive declaration**;
  - in force from **13 Bhadra 2080 (30 August 2023)** for **five years**;
  - money: **USD 550 million** as a grant from the United States and **USD 197 million** from Nepal — USD 747 million in all;
  - work: about 315 km of 400-kilovolt power line with substations, and road maintenance.
  - 🔄 On 10 June 2026 work formally began on the Ratmate and New Damauli substations and on 297 km of line.
- 🔄 The aid agency **USAID**, long the main channel of American help, was closed down by the United States government in 2025. The MCC compact continued.
- The **80th anniversary** of relations falls in 2027.
- 🔄 President: **Donald Trump**, since 20 January 2025.

**Japan (जापान)**

- **Diplomatic relations: 1 September 1956.** Nepal opened its embassy in Tokyo in 1965; Japan opened its embassy in Kathmandu in 1967.
- Early links: the monk **Ekai Kawaguchi** came to Nepal in 1899; eight Nepali students went to Japan in 1902.
- Japan helps through the **Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA)** with grants, cheap loans and volunteers. Examples: the Kulekhani hydropower stations, the B.P. Highway (Banepa–Sindhuli–Bardibas) and the Tribhuvan University Teaching Hospital.
- 🔄 **Nagdhunga–Sisnekhola tunnel** — Nepal's **first road tunnel**, 2.688 km long, built with a Japanese loan; opened on **11 Shrawan 2083 (27 July 2026)**.
- 🔄 **70th anniversary, 2026:** President Ram Chandra Paudel paid an official visit to Japan in February 2026 and met Emperor **Naruhito** and Prime Minister **Sanae Takaichi**. The two foreign ministers exchanged messages on 1 September 2026.
- Japan is a growing place of study and work for Nepalis.

**What the three have in common**

- All three are democracies and old development partners.
- Japan and the United States were among the countries that sent help after the Bhote Koshi flood of Bhadra 2083 🔄.
- None has a boundary or a boundary dispute with Nepal.

## 8.5 Nepal and the Gulf countries, South Korea, Malaysia and Israel (नेपाल र खाडी मुलुक, दक्षिण कोरिया, मलेसिया र इजरायल)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Only Bagmati names these countries, in unit 1.9.1. They have one thing in common: Nepalis go there to work. Elsewhere the same facts come under the economy and current affairs — remittance, labour agreements, and the war of 2026 in the Gulf.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Remittance is the largest source of foreign money for Nepal (Chapter 5). Most Nepali workers abroad are in two regions: the **Gulf** and **Malaysia**. **South Korea** and **Israel** take fewer workers but pay more, and they take them under agreements between governments.

Relations with these countries are therefore mainly **labour diplomacy** (श्रम कूटनीति): agreements on recruitment, pay and safety; embassies that help workers in trouble; and rescue in time of war.

#### 3 · Key facts

**The Gulf countries (खाडी मुलुक)**

- The six states of the **Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC)** — founded in 1981, seat Riyadh — are **Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain and Oman**. Iran and Iraq lie on the Gulf but are not members.
- Nepal opened relations with **Kuwait in 1972** and with the other five in **1977**.
- Nepal opened its embassy in Doha on 11 July 2000. Qatar opened its embassy in Kathmandu in August 2011.
- The Amir of Qatar, Sheikh **Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani**, paid a state visit to Nepal on 23–24 April 2024 — the first by an Amir of Qatar.
- Workers there are tied to their employers by the **sponsorship (*kafala*) system**, which makes it hard to change jobs.
- 🔄 The government said in March 2026 that more than **1.7 million** Nepalis live in the region. Other estimates are higher or lower.

**The war of 2026 and Nepali workers** 🔄

- **16 Falgun 2082 (28 February 2026):** war began between the United States and Israel on one side and Iran on the other. Iranian missiles and drones also struck Gulf states. A Nepali worker was killed in Abu Dhabi.
- **17 Falgun 2082 (1 March 2026):** the Council of Ministers **stopped labour approvals** for the Gulf. They were reopened in part after about 17 days.
- The Ministry of Foreign Affairs opened an online register; more than 82,000 Nepalis signed it.
- A memorandum signed on 17 June 2026, with Pakistan as go-between, stopped the fighting. It broke down in July 2026. The matter was not settled when this chapter was written.

**South Korea (दक्षिण कोरिया)**

- Diplomatic relations: **15 May 1974**.
- Nepalis go under the **Employment Permit System (EPS)** — an arrangement between the two governments. The memorandum was signed in 2007 and the first workers went in 2008.
- A worker must pass a **Korean-language test**. There is no private recruiting agency in between.
- 🔄 More than **125,000** Nepalis have found work through the system. The quota announced for 2026 was **7,200**.
- South Korea also gives aid through its agency **KOICA**.

**Malaysia (मलेसिया)**

- Diplomatic relations: **1 January 1960**.
- One of the two largest destinations of Nepali workers, mainly in factories, security work and plantations.
- **Labour memorandum of 12 Kartik 2075 (29 October 2018):** the **employer pays** the costs of recruitment, travel and visa.
- 🔄 In September 2026 the two labour ministers agreed to make recruitment more orderly and to stop practices that load costs on the worker.

**Israel (इजरायल)**

- Diplomatic relations: **1 June 1960**, under Prime Minister B.P. Koirala. Nepal was the **first country of South Asia** to open relations with Israel.
- Nepalis work there as carers, and Nepali students of agriculture go for training under a "learn and earn" programme.
- In the attack by Hamas on **7 October 2023**, ten Nepali students were killed. One student, **Bipin Joshi**, was taken hostage; his body was returned in **October 2025**.
- 🔄 On 21 Poush 2082 (5 January 2026) the Council of Ministers approved new labour papers with Israel for farm work and for care at home.

**Offices that deal with foreign employment** (Chapter 5)

- The ministry in charge of labour; the Department of Foreign Employment; the Foreign Employment Board; labour attachés in the embassies.
- Law: the Foreign Employment Act, 2064.

## 8.6 The United Nations (संयुक्त राष्ट्रसङ्घ)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Every syllabus names the United Nations. Gandaki and Lumbini ask for its "introduction, organs, specialised agencies and activities". The six organs, the dates of 1945, the Secretaries-General and Nepal's own record are asked year after year. The change of Secretary-General at the end of 2026 makes the topic current.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

The **United Nations (UN)** is the world body of states, set up after the Second World War to keep peace. Almost every country is a member. It has four aims: to keep peace and security, to build friendly relations among nations, to solve world problems together and promote human rights, and to be a centre where nations work in harmony.

Its basic law is the **Charter** (बडापत्र). Its work is done by six **principal organs** and by many funds, programmes and agencies. The agencies are in topic 8.10.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Birth**

- The **League of Nations** (1920–1946, Geneva) came before it and failed to prevent a second world war.
- The name "United Nations" was suggested by the American President **Franklin D. Roosevelt**. It was first used in the Declaration by United Nations of 1 January 1942.
- The Charter was written at the **San Francisco Conference** and signed on **26 June 1945** by 50 countries. Poland signed later, so there are **51 original members**.
- The Charter came into force on **24 October 1945** — **United Nations Day**.
- The Charter has a Preamble, **19 Chapters and 111 Articles**.

**Basic facts**

- Headquarters: **New York**. Other main offices: Geneva, Vienna and Nairobi.
- Members: **193**. The newest is **South Sudan** (2011). The Holy See and Palestine are observers.
- Official languages: **six** — Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian and Spanish.
- Flag: a white map of the world between two olive branches on a light-blue field.

**The six principal organs**

| Organ | Members | Main work |
|---|---|---|
| **General Assembly** (महासभा) | All 193; one vote each | Debates all questions; passes the budget; elects members of other organs. A new session opens every September |
| **Security Council** (सुरक्षा परिषद्) | 15: 5 permanent and 10 elected for two years | Keeps peace and security; its decisions bind all members |
| **Economic and Social Council** (आर्थिक तथा सामाजिक परिषद्) | 54, elected for three years | Coordinates economic and social work and the specialised agencies |
| **Trusteeship Council** (न्यास परिषद्) | The 5 permanent members | Watched over trust territories; work **suspended on 1 November 1994**, when Palau became independent |
| **International Court of Justice** (अन्तर्राष्ट्रिय न्यायालय) | 15 judges, nine-year terms | Settles legal disputes between states; sits at **The Hague**, the Netherlands |
| **Secretariat** (सचिवालय) | Staff under the Secretary-General | Carries out the day-to-day work |

- The five **permanent members** of the Security Council are **China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom and the United States**. Each has the **veto** (निषेधाधिकार). A decision needs nine votes and no veto.
- The **International Court of Justice** is the only principal organ that is not in New York.
- The **Secretary-General** is appointed by the General Assembly on the recommendation of the Security Council (Article 97), for five years.

## 8.7 SAARC (दक्षिण एसियाली क्षेत्रीय सहयोग सङ्गठन — सार्क)

#### 1 · Why it matters

SAARC is named in all seven syllabi, and its Secretariat is in Kathmandu. Questions ask for the founding date and place, the members, the summits held in Nepal, the Secretaries-General, the regional centres and the free-trade agreement.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

**SAARC** — the **South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation** — is the body of the eight countries of South Asia. It was formed so that neighbours with the same problems — poverty, illiteracy, disease, disasters — could work together.

It decides everything by **unanimity**, and it does not discuss quarrels between two members. Because of the quarrel between India and Pakistan it has not held a summit since 2014. It still exists, and its Secretariat and centres still work, but slowly.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Birth and members**

- The idea came from President **Ziaur Rahman** of Bangladesh in 1980.
- The Charter was signed at the first summit in **Dhaka on 8 December 1985**. 8 December is **SAARC Charter Day**.
- **Seven founding members:** Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan and Sri Lanka.
- **Afghanistan** became the **eighth member** at the 14th Summit (New Delhi, 2007).
- **Nine observers:** Australia, China, the European Union, Iran, Japan, the Republic of Korea, Mauritius, Myanmar and the United States.
- South Asia holds about a quarter of the world's people on about three percent of its land.

**Principles (Charter)**

- Sovereign equality, territorial integrity, political independence, non-interference and mutual benefit.
- Decisions at all levels are taken by **unanimity**.
- **Bilateral and contentious issues** are kept out of its discussions.

**Structure, from top to bottom**

1. **Summit** — heads of state or government.
2. **Council of Ministers** — foreign ministers.
3. **Standing Committee** — foreign secretaries.
4. **Programming Committee** and technical committees.
5. **Secretariat** — headed by the Secretary-General.

**Secretariat and Secretaries-General**

- The Secretariat was set up in **Kathmandu on 16 January 1987** (2 Magh 2043).
- The **Secretary-General** is appointed by the Council of Ministers for **three years**. The post passes among the members in alphabetical order.
- First Secretary-General: **Abul Ahsan** (Bangladesh).
- Nepalis in the post: **Yadav Kant Silwal** (1994–1995) and **Arjun Bahadur Thapa** (2014–2017).
- The first woman in the post was Fathimath Dhiyana Saeed (Maldives), the tenth.
- 🔄 The **15th**, **Golam Sarwar** (Bangladesh), finished his term in October 2026. The **16th** is **Dasho Sonam Topgay** of Bhutan (his name is also written Tobgay). The Ministry of Foreign Affairs said at the end of October 2026 that he would arrive in Kathmandu soon.
- Afghanistan's turn was passed over in 2023, because the members have not recognised the Taliban government.

**Summits**

- **18 summits** have been held. Nepal has hosted three: the **3rd (1987)**, the **11th (2002)** and the **18th (2014)**.
- **18th Summit:** Kathmandu, **10–11 Mangsir 2071 (26–27 November 2014)**; theme "Deeper Integration for Peace and Prosperity".
- The **19th Summit**, due in Islamabad in November 2016, was put off and has never met. **Nepal has remained the chair since 2014.** 🔄

**Agreements and institutions**

- **SAPTA** — the preferential trading arrangement, signed in 1993.
- **SAFTA** — the **South Asian Free Trade Area** agreement, signed at the 12th Summit in **Islamabad in January 2004**; in force from **1 January 2006**.
- **SAARC Social Charter** (2004); agreement on trade in services (2010); framework agreement on energy cooperation in electricity (Kathmandu, 2014).
- **SAARC Development Fund** — Thimphu. **South Asian University** — New Delhi. **SAARC Arbitration Council** — Islamabad. **South Asian Regional Standards Organisation** — Dhaka.

**Regional centres**

| Centre | Place |
|---|---|
| SAARC Agriculture Centre | Dhaka, Bangladesh |
| SAARC Energy Centre | Islamabad, Pakistan |
| SAARC Cultural Centre | Colombo, Sri Lanka |
| SAARC Tuberculosis and HIV/AIDS Centre | Thimi, Bhaktapur, Nepal |
| SAARC Disaster Management Centre | Gandhinagar, India |

**Why SAARC is stuck**

- Tension between India and Pakistan; the rule of unanimity lets one member stop everything.
- Trade inside the region is very small — about five percent of the members' total trade.
- No summit for twelve years; meetings above the level of officials are rare.
- Some members now prefer smaller groups such as BIMSTEC (topic 8.8).

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** What are the aims of SAARC? Assess its achievements and its problems.

**Model answer.** SAARC is the regional body of eight South Asian countries. Its Charter was signed in Dhaka on 8 December 1985, and its Secretariat has been in Kathmandu since 1987. **Aims:** to promote the welfare of the peoples of South Asia; to speed up economic growth, social progress and cultural development; to build self-reliance and mutual trust; and to cooperate with other developing countries and world bodies. **Achievements:** (1) a regular meeting place for eight neighbours; (2) the free-trade agreement SAFTA, in force since 2006; (3) institutions — the Development Fund in Thimphu, the South Asian University in New Delhi, the Food Bank and five regional centres, one of them in Bhaktapur; (4) the Social Charter and agreements on services, energy and the fight against terrorism and drugs; (5) contact among peoples through visas, culture and sport. **Problems:** (1) the quarrel between India and Pakistan — no summit has met since the 18th in Kathmandu in 2014; (2) the rule of unanimity, which lets one member block all; (3) very little trade inside the region; (4) poor transport links and many non-tariff barriers; (5) weak funds and slow carrying-out of decisions; (6) the unsettled place of Afghanistan. **Conclusion:** South Asia's shared problems of poverty, climate and disasters still need a common body. As the chair, Nepal should keep pressing for the next summit while working in smaller groups as well.

## 8.8 BIMSTEC (बिमस्टेक)

#### 1 · Why it matters

BIMSTEC is named in every syllabus that was read. It is younger than SAARC and is growing while SAARC stands still, so examiners now ask as much about it: the full name, the founding declaration, the members, the Secretariat, the summit held in Kathmandu and the sector Nepal leads.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

**BIMSTEC** is the **Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation**. It joins seven countries around, or dependent on, the Bay of Bengal — five from South Asia and two from South-East Asia. It is therefore called a **bridge between South Asia and South-East Asia**.

Nepal and Bhutan have no coast, but the Bay of Bengal is their nearest sea. BIMSTEC gives Nepal a link to Thailand and Myanmar and a second regional forum beside SAARC.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Birth and name**

- Founded on **6 June 1997** by the **Bangkok Declaration**. 6 June is BIMSTEC Day.
- First name: **BIST-EC**, from its four founders — **B**angladesh, **I**ndia, **S**ri Lanka, **T**hailand.
- **Myanmar** joined in December 1997, and the name became BIMST-EC.
- **Nepal and Bhutan** joined in **February 2004**. At the first summit, in Bangkok on 31 July 2004, the present name was adopted.

**Members**

- **Seven:** Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Myanmar, Nepal, Sri Lanka and Thailand.
- Five are also in SAARC. Two — **Myanmar and Thailand** — are also in ASEAN.
- **Pakistan, Afghanistan and the Maldives** are in SAARC but not in BIMSTEC.
- The members hold more than a fifth of the world's people.

**Secretariat and Charter**

- Secretariat: **Dhaka**, Bangladesh, opened in **2014**.
- First Secretary-General: Sumith Nakandala (Sri Lanka).
- 🔄 Secretary-General now: **Indra Mani Pandey** (India), the fourth, in office since January 2024.
- The **BIMSTEC Charter** was signed at the 5th Summit on 30 March 2022 and came into force on **20 May 2024**. Until then the body had worked for 25 years without a charter.
- The chair passes among the members in alphabetical order.

**Summits**

| 1 | Bangkok, Thailand | 31 July 2004 |
| 2 | New Delhi, India | November 2008 |
| 3 | Nay Pyi Taw, Myanmar | March 2014 |

| 5 | Colombo, Sri Lanka (held online) | 30 March 2022 |
| 6 | Bangkok, Thailand | 4 April 2025 |

- **4th Summit, Kathmandu:** theme "Towards a Peaceful, Prosperous and Sustainable Bay of Bengal Region"; it issued the Kathmandu Declaration, and a memorandum on linking the members' electricity grids was signed.
- 🔄 **6th Summit, Bangkok, 4 April 2025:** theme "BIMSTEC: Prosperous, Resilient and Open".
  - It adopted the **Bangkok Vision 2030**.
  - An **Agreement on Maritime Transport Cooperation** was signed.
  - The chair passed from Thailand to **Bangladesh**.
- 🔄 The Secretary-General said in July 2026 that Bangladesh might host the next summit in 2027, the 30th year of BIMSTEC.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Introduce BIMSTEC. How does it differ from SAARC, and why is it important for Nepal?

**Model answer.** BIMSTEC — the Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation — was founded by the Bangkok Declaration of 6 June 1997. It has seven members: Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Myanmar, Nepal, Sri Lanka and Thailand. Nepal joined in February 2004. Its Secretariat is in Dhaka, and its Charter came into force on 20 May 2024. **Differences from SAARC:** (1) it links South Asia with South-East Asia, while SAARC covers South Asia alone; (2) Pakistan, Afghanistan and the Maldives are not in it, so the India–Pakistan quarrel does not block it; (3) it works sector by sector, with one lead country for each of seven sectors; (4) it is meeting — its 6th Summit was held in Bangkok in April 2025 — while SAARC has had no summit since 2014. **Importance for Nepal:** (1) a road to the markets of Thailand and Myanmar and, through them, of ASEAN; (2) transport and transit links to the Bay of Bengal; (3) the sale of electricity through joined grids; (4) tourism on the Buddhist circuit, in the sector Nepal leads; (5) joint work on disasters and climate change. **Limits:** slow decisions, a free-trade area that has not started, and unrest in Myanmar. **Conclusion:** BIMSTEC does not replace SAARC; Nepal gains most by keeping both alive.

## 8.9 ASEAN, the European Union and other groupings (आसियान, युरोपेली सङ्घ र अन्य समूह)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Koshi, Gandaki, Lumbini and Sudurpashchim name ASEAN; Gandaki and Lumbini also name the European Union. Nepal is a member of neither, so the questions are simple: founding date and place, number of members, newest member, headquarters and currency. Other groups — the G7, the G20, BRICS — come as current affairs.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

**ASEAN** — the **Association of Southeast Asian Nations** — is the regional body of South-East Asia. It is the most successful regional body of developing countries and is often held up as a model for SAARC.

The **European Union (EU)** goes much further than any other regional body. Its members share a single market, common laws and, for most of them, a single currency — the **euro**. People, goods, services and money move freely among them.

#### 3 · Key facts

**ASEAN**

- Founded on **8 August 1967** by the **Bangkok Declaration** (also called the ASEAN Declaration). 8 August is ASEAN Day.
- **Five founders:** Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore and Thailand.
- Later members: Brunei (1984), Vietnam (1995), Laos and Myanmar (1997), Cambodia (1999).
- 🔄 **Timor-Leste became the 11th member on 26 October 2025**, at the summit in Kuala Lumpur.
- Secretariat: **Jakarta**, Indonesia.
- The **ASEAN Charter** came into force on 15 December 2008. Motto: **"One Vision, One Identity, One Community"**.
- The **ASEAN Community** (2015) has three pillars: political-security, economic and socio-cultural.
- The chair changes every year. 🔄 2025 — Malaysia. **2026 — the Philippines**, in place of Myanmar, whose turn was passed over. Theme of 2026: "Navigating Our Future, Together".
- 🔄 Summits of 2026: the 48th at Cebu in May; the 49th in Metro Manila in mid-November.
- 🔄 Secretary-General: **Kao Kim Hourn** (Cambodia), for 2023–2027.
- Related forums: the ASEAN Regional Forum, ASEAN Plus Three (China, Japan, South Korea) and the East Asia Summit.
- Nepal is not a member. Its link is through BIMSTEC, which has two ASEAN members.

**European Union**

- **Beginnings:** six countries — Belgium, France, West Germany, Italy, Luxembourg and the Netherlands — formed the Coal and Steel Community in 1951 and the **European Economic Community** by the **Treaty of Rome in 1957**.
- **The Union itself** was created by the **Maastricht Treaty**, signed on 7 February 1992 and in force from **1 November 1993**.
- **Members: 27.** The newest is **Croatia** (2013). The **United Kingdom left on 31 January 2020** ("Brexit").
- Headquarters: **Brussels**, Belgium.
- **Euro (€):** introduced in 1999; notes and coins from 2002. 🔄 **Bulgaria** became the **21st** country to use it on **1 January 2026**.
- **Schengen area:** travel without border checks among most member states.
- The Union won the **Nobel Peace Prize in 2012**. Europe Day is 9 May.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Introduce ASEAN and the European Union in brief. What can SAARC learn from them?

**Model answer.** **ASEAN**, the Association of Southeast Asian Nations, was founded by the Bangkok Declaration of 8 August 1967 by Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore and Thailand. It now has eleven members — Timor-Leste joined in October 2025 — and its Secretariat is in Jakarta. Its members trade freely with one another and meet twice a year at summit level. The **European Union** grew from the Treaty of Rome of 1957 and was created by the Maastricht Treaty, in force since 1 November 1993. It has 27 members and its headquarters is in Brussels. It has a single market, common laws, a parliament and a common currency, the euro, used by 21 members. **Lessons for SAARC:** (1) keep political quarrels apart from economic cooperation, as ASEAN does; (2) meet regularly — a body that does not meet cannot work; (3) begin with trade, transport and energy, where every member gains; (4) build strong common institutions and give them money and power; (5) let people, goods and capital move more freely; (6) allow groups of willing members to move ahead first, as the euro countries did. **Conclusion:** both bodies show that neighbours who were once rivals can grow rich together. SAARC has the same need and the same chance, if its largest members find the will.

## 8.10 Specialised agencies and international financial institutions (विशिष्टीकृत संस्था र अन्तर्राष्ट्रिय वित्तीय संस्था)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Gandaki, Lumbini and Sudurpashchim ask for the "specialised agencies" of the United Nations and their activities. Bagmati names seven bodies one by one: the World Bank, the International Monetary Fund, the Asian Development Bank, the International Labour Organization, the World Health Organization, the Food and Agriculture Organization and the International Cooperative Alliance. Headquarters, founding years and heads make easy matching questions.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

A **specialised agency** (विशिष्टीकृत संस्था) is an independent world body that works in one field — health, labour, food, money — and is linked to the United Nations by an agreement made through the Economic and Social Council. There are **15** of them. Each has its own members, budget and head.

An **international financial institution** is a bank or fund owned by many governments. It lends money to countries for development or in a crisis. The World Bank and the International Monetary Fund are both specialised agencies and financial institutions. The Asian Development Bank is a regional bank and is not part of the United Nations.

#### 3 · Key facts

**The Bretton Woods twins**

- In **July 1944**, at **Bretton Woods** in the United States, 44 countries agreed to set up the **International Monetary Fund** and the **World Bank**. Both have their headquarters in **Washington, D.C.**
- **Nepal joined both in 1961.**

**World Bank Group (विश्व बैंक समूह)**

- Five institutions:
  - **IBRD** — International Bank for Reconstruction and Development: loans to middle-income countries;
  - **IDA** — International Development Association (1960): very cheap loans and grants to the poorest countries. Nepal borrows from IDA;
  - **IFC** — International Finance Corporation (1956): lends to private companies;
  - **MIGA** — Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency (1988): insures investors against political risk;
  - **ICSID** — International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes (1966).
- "The World Bank" in the narrow sense means IBRD and IDA together.
- The President has always been a citizen of the United States. 🔄 President: **Ajay Banga**.
- Main report: the *World Development Report*. The Bank classes Nepal as a lower-middle-income country (Chapter 5).
- 🔄 In October 2026 Finance Minister Swarnim Wagle met the President to discuss help for rebuilding after the Bhote Koshi flood.

**International Monetary Fund (अन्तर्राष्ट्रिय मुद्रा कोष)**

- Work: keeps the world's system of payments stable; advises on economic policy; lends to countries that cannot pay for their imports.
- Each member pays a **quota**, which decides its votes and how much it can borrow. The Fund's unit of account is the **Special Drawing Right (SDR)**.
- The Managing Director has always been a European. 🔄 Managing Director: **Kristalina Georgieva** (Bulgaria).
- Main report: the *World Economic Outlook*.
- 🔄 The yearly meetings of the Fund and the Bank were held in **Bangkok in October 2026**. The Managing Director said there that emergency help for Nepal after the flood was expected soon.

**Asian Development Bank (एसियाली विकास बैंक)**

- Founded on **19 December 1966**. Headquarters: **Manila**, the Philippines.
- **Nepal is a founding member**; 2026 is the 60th year of the partnership.
- Japan and the United States are the largest shareholders. The President has always been Japanese. 🔄 President: **Masato Kanda**.
- Its cheap window for poor members is the Asian Development Fund. Main report: the *Asian Development Outlook*.
- 🔄 The Bank's plan for Nepal for 2025–2029 promises about **USD 2.4 billion**. The President visited Kathmandu in July 2026. The Bank gave an emergency grant of USD 5 million two days after the flood of 26 August 2026.
- In Nepal the Bank has paid for roads, power lines, irrigation, city water supply and Gautam Buddha International Airport.

**International Labour Organization (अन्तर्राष्ट्रिय श्रम सङ्गठन)**

- Founded in **1919** under the Treaty of Versailles; in 1946 it became the **first specialised agency** of the United Nations. Headquarters: **Geneva**.
- It is **tripartite**: governments, employers and workers all sit and vote.
- It makes labour standards in the form of **conventions** and recommendations. Its aim is "decent work".
- **Nobel Peace Prize, 1969.** Nepal joined in **1966**.
- 🔄 Director-General: **Gilbert F. Houngbo** (Togo). The 114th International Labour Conference met in Geneva on 1–12 June 2026.

**World Health Organization (विश्व स्वास्थ्य सङ्गठन)**

- Founded on **7 April 1948** — World Health Day. Headquarters: **Geneva**.
- Its governing body is the **World Health Assembly**, which meets every May.
- It has six regions. Nepal is in the **South-East Asia Region**, whose office is in **New Delhi**.
- Nepal joined in **1953**. The Organization's findings on Nepal — polio, rubella, COVID-19 — are in Chapter 7.
- 🔄 Director-General: **Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus** (Ethiopia). His second term ends in August 2027. Seven candidates were proposed in October 2026; the Assembly of May 2027 will choose.
- 🔄 The **United States left** the Organization on 22 January 2026. Argentina has also given notice of leaving.

**Food and Agriculture Organization (खाद्य तथा कृषि सङ्गठन)**

- Founded on **16 October 1945** at Quebec, Canada — **World Food Day**. Headquarters: **Rome**.
- Work: to defeat hunger; to improve farming, forestry and fisheries; to gather world food statistics.
- Nepal joined in **1951** — the first United Nations agency it entered.
- 🔄 Director-General: **Qu Dongyu** (China).
- Two other food bodies are also in Rome: the World Food Programme and the International Fund for Agricultural Development.

**International Cooperative Alliance (अन्तर्राष्ट्रिय सहकारी महासङ्घ)**

- Founded in **London in 1895**. Headquarters: **Brussels**.
- It is a **non-governmental** body of cooperative federations — **not** an agency of the United Nations.
- It keeps the definition of a cooperative and the **seven cooperative principles** (1995).
- The **International Day of Cooperatives** is the first Saturday of July. The United Nations named **2012 and 2025** International Years of Cooperatives.
- Nepal's National Cooperative Federation is a member.
- 🔄 President: **Ariel Guarco** (Argentina).

**Other agencies often asked**

- **UNESCO** — education, science and culture; Paris; founded 1945. 🔄 Director-General since 15 November 2025: **Khaled El-Enany** (Egypt). Nepal joined in 1953; the World Heritage sites are in Chapter 6.
- **World Trade Organization** — Geneva; began on **1 January 1995** in place of the GATT of 1947; 166 members. It is **not** a specialised agency. Nepal became the **147th member on 11 Baisakh 2061 (23 April 2004)**. 🔄 Director-General: **Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala** (Nigeria). Its 14th Ministerial Conference met at Yaoundé, Cameroon, in March 2026.
- **International Atomic Energy Agency** — Vienna; founded 1957; also not a specialised agency.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** What do the World Bank, the International Monetary Fund and the Asian Development Bank do? Describe their part in Nepal's development.

The Asian Development Bank lends and gives grants for development in Asia and the Pacific. **In Nepal:** (1) the World Bank and the Asian Development Bank are the two largest lenders; they have paid for roads, bridges, hydropower and power lines, irrigation, drinking water, schools and health; (2) the Asian Development Bank's plan for 2025–2029 promises about USD 2.4 billion; (3) the Fund has supported Nepal in hard times, as after the earthquake and during COVID-19; (4) all three offered help after the Bhote Koshi flood of Bhadra 2083; (5) their reports and advice shape the budget and reforms. **Concerns:** loans add to public debt; conditions can limit the government's choices; and projects are often slow. **Conclusion:** Nepal should borrow for productive projects only, improve its spending capacity, and use the cheap loans well before it leaves the list of least developed countries.

### D. Five short-answer questions

**1. What is economic diplomacy? Why does Nepal need it, and how can it be made effective?**
*Points for the answer:* meaning — use of foreign relations and missions to win trade, investment, tourists, aid, technology and safe jobs abroad · need — large trade deficit; dependence on remittance; falling grants; graduation from the list of least developed countries in 2029; need of investment in hydropower, tourism and information technology · means — embassies and consulates, labour attachés, trade fairs, non-resident Nepalis, visits and joint commissions · weaknesses — small budgets, few trained staff, missions without ambassadors, poor coordination among ministries · remedies — clear targets for each mission; choice of ambassadors on merit; export promotion and market study; one-window service for investors; labour agreements; use of regional bodies · conclusion — the Foreign Policy of 2077 and the present "development diplomacy" both put it at the centre.

**2. What are the advantages and the difficulties of Nepal's position between India and China?**
*Points for the answer:* position — landlocked, between two very large and fast-growing neighbours; "yam between two boulders" · advantages — two huge markets for electricity, tourism and farm goods; transit both ways; aid, loans and investment from both; chance to be a bridge ("land-linked") · difficulties — dependence on one side for trade and transit; boundary questions in the west; rivalry between the two and with outside powers; pressure over the Belt and Road and other projects; high mountains on the north · policy — balanced and friendly relations with both; no use of Nepali soil against either; decisions by national interest (Article 5); wider relations with other countries and bodies · conclusion — geography cannot be changed; a steady, trusted policy turns it into an asset.

**3. Describe Nepal's part in the peace-keeping work of the United Nations.**
*Points for the answer:* beginning — 1958, five military observers in Lebanon; first full contingent in the 1970s · scale — more than 158,000 members of the Nepali Army in 44 missions; first among troop-contributing countries 🔄; Nepal Police and the Armed Police Force also serve · places — the Middle East, Africa, Haiti, Timor-Leste and others · training — Birendra Peace Operations Training Centre, Panchkhal · benefits — respect for Nepal; experience and income for the forces; support for Nepal's voice in the United Nations · costs and concerns — deaths and injuries in service; cuts in the missions since 2025; need for more women and for senior posts for Nepalis · conclusion — peace-keeping is the most visible part of Nepal's foreign policy and follows from its commitment to the Charter.

**4. What is a specialised agency of the United Nations? Describe the work of the World Health Organization, the International Labour Organization and the Food and Agriculture Organization, with examples from Nepal.**
*Points for the answer:* meaning — an independent world body in one field, linked to the United Nations through the Economic and Social Council; 15 in all · WHO — 1948, Geneva; sets health standards, guides the fight against disease, warns of epidemics; Nepal — polio-free region 2014, rubella eliminated 2025, guidance in COVID-19 · ILO — 1919, Geneva; tripartite; conventions on labour rights, child labour, forced labour, safety; Nepal — member since 1966; labour law, work on child labour and on migrant workers · FAO — 1945, Rome; food security, farming, forests, fisheries, statistics; Nepal — member since 1951; help with seed, livestock, forestry and the agricultural census · conclusion — the agencies bring knowledge and standards more than money; Nepal gains most when it uses their advice in its own plans.

**5. What is the Non-Aligned Movement? Is non-alignment still useful for Nepal?**
*Points for the answer:* origin — Bandung Conference 1955; first summit at Belgrade, 1961; leaders such as Nehru, Tito, Nasser, Sukarno and Nkrumah; Nepal a founding member · meaning — not joining the military blocs of the Cold War; an independent judgement on each question; not neutrality or isolation · today — about 120 members; no fixed seat; a summit every three years or so · criticism — the Cold War is over; members are divided; little practical work · use for Nepal — written into Article 51 of the Constitution; lets a small state between large powers keep its freedom of choice; fits Panchsheel and "amity with all" · conclusion — the name is old, but the idea — no military alliance, friendship with all — is still the safest course for Nepal.

### E. Common mistakes

1. Naming **India** as the first country with which Nepal had diplomatic relations. It was the **United Kingdom (1816)**.
2. Giving the **Zone of Peace** proposal to King Mahendra. It was **King Birendra's**, in 1975.
3. Confusing the **date of signing** of the UN Charter (26 June 1945) with the **date it came into force** (24 October 1945).
4. Placing the **International Court of Justice** in New York or Geneva. It is at **The Hague**.
5. Saying SAARC's Secretariat is in **Dhaka**. SAARC was *founded* in Dhaka; its Secretariat is in **Kathmandu**. BIMSTEC's is in Dhaka.
6. Mixing the two **Bangkok Declarations**: 1967 for ASEAN, 1997 for BIMSTEC.
7. Counting **Afghanistan** as a founder of SAARC, or **Maldives** as a member of BIMSTEC.
8. Writing that the **19th SAARC Summit** was held. The last one is the **18th (Kathmandu, 2014)**.
9. Mixing the **friendship treaty with China (1960)** and the **boundary treaty (1961)**, or their signers.
10. Putting **Susta** in Darchula or **Kalapani** in Nawalparasi.
11. Calling the **MCC** money a loan, or giving 2017 as the year it came into force. It is a **grant**, in force since **30 August 2023**.
12. Saying the European Union has **28** members, or that all of them use the euro. It has **27**; **21** use the euro.
13. Calling the **World Trade Organization** or the **International Cooperative Alliance** a specialised agency of the United Nations.
14. Placing the **Asian Development Bank** in Tokyo or Beijing. It is in **Manila**.
15. Exchanging **World Health Day (7 April)** and **World Food Day (16 October)**.
16. Quoting a **moving fact** — the number of countries, an office-holder, the latest summit — without checking its date.
