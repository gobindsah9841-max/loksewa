## 13.1 Profile of the province (प्रदेशको परिचय)

#### 1 · Why it matters

The commonest province questions ask for a number or a name: how many districts, how many local levels, which is the capital, when the province was named. Gandaki is also the answer to several national questions of Chapter 1 — the largest metropolitan city by area, the least populated district, the district called the desert of Nepal. This topic fixes those basic facts and adds the eleven districts one by one.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

**Gandaki** is the name of a family of rivers. Seven large branches — the Sapta Gandaki — come down from the Himalaya, join one another and leave Nepal as the Narayani. The province named after them is the block of land that these rivers drain, west of the Kathmandu side and east of the Karnali side.

The province is like a staircase. Its lowest step is the plain of **Nawalpur**, about 100 metres above the sea, on the border of India. The middle steps are green hills with the lake town of **Pokhara** among them. The top step is the snow wall of Dhaulagiri and Annapurna, more than 8,000 metres high. Behind that wall lie two dry, cold districts, **Manang** and **Mustang**, which reach the border of China.

Like every province, Gandaki was formed on the day the Constitution came into force and was first known only by a number. In 2075 BS its Provincial Assembly chose Pokhara as the capital and, four days later, "Gandaki" as the name.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Formation and name**

- **Formed:** 3 Ashoj 2072 (20 September 2015), the day the Constitution came into force. Schedule 4 of the Constitution lists its eleven districts; one entry reads "Nawalparasi (east of Bardaghat Susta)".
- **First name:** Province No. 4.
- **Temporary seat:** the Government of Nepal named **Pokhara** on 3 Magh 2074 (17 January 2018).
- **First meeting of the Provincial Assembly:** 22 Magh 2074 (5 February 2018) — the day the province keeps as its birthday ⚖️ (an encyclopaedia page gives 4 February 2018).
- **Capital:** on **18 Ashad 2075 (2 July 2018)** the Assembly made **Pokhara** the permanent capital. All **52** members present voted for it; nobody voted against.
- **Name:** on **22 Ashad 2075 (6 July 2018)** the Assembly named the province **Gandaki**. Of **56** members present, **54** voted for and **2** against.
  - Two-thirds of all 60 members — **40** — were needed for each decision (Articles 288 (2) and 295 (2)).
  - The two votes against came from members of the Naya Shakti Party, who wanted a name that showed the identity of the peoples of the province. Their amendment for the name "Gandaki–Tamuwan" was rejected.
  - A committee of the Assembly had listed four possible names: **Gandaki, Tamuwan–Magarat, Gorkha and Dhaulagiri**. Its report was tabled on 10 Ashad 2075.
- Gandaki was the **second** province to be named, after Karnali and before Sudurpashchim.
- **Why "Gandaki":** the rivers of the Gandaki family drain every district of the province; "Gandaki" was also the name of the old zone that held six of the eleven districts; and a river name belongs to no single people.
- **Gandaki Province Establishment Day (गण्डकी प्रदेश स्थापना दिवस):** **22 Magh**, a public holiday in the province. Until 2081 the Assembly kept 22 Magh and the government kept 4 Falgun, the day the first Chief Minister took the oath in 2074; since Magh 2081 only 22 Magh is kept 🔄.

**Position**

- **North:** China (Tibet Autonomous Region). Three districts touch it: **Gorkha, Manang and Mustang**.
- **South:** Lumbini Province and, at **Nawalpur** only, India (the state of Bihar).
- **East:** Bagmati Province (Dhading and Chitwan districts).
- **West:** Lumbini Province and, in the north-west, Karnali Province (Dolpa district).
- It touches **three provinces** and **two countries**. Only four provinces touch both India and China: Koshi, Bagmati, Gandaki and Sudurpashchim.

**Size and people**

- **Area:** 21,504 sq km — about 14.6 percent of Nepal; the **fourth** largest province.
- **Population (Census 2078):** **2,466,427** — 8.46 percent of Nepal; the **sixth** of the seven. Only Karnali has fewer people.
- **Women and men:** 1,295,594 women (52.53 percent) and 1,170,833 men (47.47 percent) — about 90 men for every 100 women, because many men work elsewhere.
- **Growth:** the province had 2,403,757 people in the Census of 2068, so it grew by only about 0.25 percent a year. Kaski grew fastest (1.90 percent a year); Manang lost people (minus 1.39 percent a year).
- **Density:** 115 persons per sq km — the sixth; only Karnali (60) is thinner.
- **Town and village:** 65.8 percent of the people live inside municipalities.
- **Literacy:** 81.7 percent — the second highest, after Bagmati (82.1).

**Divisions**

- **Districts:** 11.
- **Local levels:** **85** = 1 metropolitan city + 0 sub-metropolitan cities + 26 municipalities + 58 rural municipalities. That is 11.3 percent of Nepal's 753 local levels. Only Karnali (79) has fewer.
- **Wards:** 759.
- **Metropolitan city (महानगरपालिका):** **Pokhara**, the only one. It is the **largest metropolitan city of Nepal by area** and, with 513,504 people in the Census of 2078, the second most populous city after Kathmandu. The old towns of Pokhara and Lekhnath were joined to make it.
- **No sub-metropolitan city** — like Karnali.
- **Manang and Mustang** have no municipality at all. **Kaski** has only five local levels: Pokhara and four rural municipalities.
- **Syangja** has the most municipalities (five); **Gorkha** has the most rural municipalities (nine).

**Seats**

- **House of Representatives:** 18 constituencies.
- **Provincial Assembly:** 60 members = 36 directly elected + 24 proportional. Only Karnali (40) and Sudurpashchim (53) have smaller Assemblies.
- **National Assembly:** 8 members, like every province.

#### 6 · Memory aid

- **"1 – 0 – 26 – 58"** makes 85. Compare Koshi's "1 – 2 – 46 – 88" (137), Madhesh's "1 – 3 – 73 – 59" (136) and Bagmati's "3 – 1 – 41 – 74" (119).
- **"6 + 4 + 1 = 11":** six districts from Gandaki zone, four from Dhaulagiri zone, one piece of Lumbini zone.
- **"Home on the 18th, name on the 22nd — Ashad 2075."** First the home (Pokhara), then the name (Gandaki).
- **"52 – 0 for the home, 54 – 2 for the name."**
- **"Province 4 is fourth in area."**
- **The three that touch China, east to west — "Go-Ma-Mu":** Gorkha, Manang, Mustang.
- **Headquarters that differ from the district name:** Besisahar, Damauli, Putalibazar, Pokhara, Chame, Jomsom, Beni, Kusma, Kawasoti. Only **Gorkha** and **Baglung** share the name of their district.

#### 7 · Exam traps

- **Pokhara is in Kaski district.** There is no district called Pokhara.
- The capital and the name were decided on **different days** — 18 and 22 Ashad 2075. In Bagmati and Madhesh both were decided on one day.
- The capital was chosen **without a vote against**; the name was **not** — two members voted against it. Do not write that the name was unanimous.
- **Province No. 4**, not Province No. 3 (Bagmati) or No. 5 (Lumbini).
- **Gandaki zone** had six districts; **Gandaki Province** has eleven.
- **Nawalpur** (Gandaki) and **Nawalparasi West** (Lumbini) are two districts made from the old Nawalparasi. Kawasoti is in Nawalpur; Parasi and Ramgram are in Lumbini Province.
- **Palpa, Gulmi and Arghakhanchi** are in Lumbini Province, although they lie in the same hills and the Kali Gandaki flows beside them.
- **Chitwan** is in Bagmati Province, although the Narayani river divides it from Nawalpur.
- **Manang** has the fewest people of any district of Nepal, but it is **not** the smallest in area; the smallest district of the province is **Parbat**.
- **Gorkha** (3,610 sq km), not Mustang (3,573), is the largest district of the province. The largest district of Nepal, **Dolpa**, is in Karnali Province.
- Gandaki is **sixth** in population, not seventh; **Karnali** has the fewest people.
- Gandaki touches India **only at Nawalpur**; it does not touch Uttar Pradesh.
- **4 Falgun** was the government's own day until 2081; the day now kept is **22 Magh**.

## 13.2 Physical geography (भौगोलिक स्वरूप)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Unit 1.3 of the Gandaki syllabus — the geography of Nepal — carries four questions, and many of the standard answers of that unit lie inside this province: three peaks above 8,000 metres, the deep gorge of the Kali Gandaki, the wettest and the driest places of Nepal, the largest protected area, the lakes of Pokhara. The land also explains topic 13.5: people earn their living differently on each step of the staircase.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

In Gandaki the land climbs very fast. From Pokhara, at about 800 metres, a person can see peaks of 8,000 metres only some 30 kilometres away. Because the wall of **Annapurna** and **Dhaulagiri** is so high and so near, it stops the wet monsoon wind. The south side of the wall is the wettest part of Nepal. The north side — Manang and Mustang — gets almost no rain and looks like a cold desert. Geographers call such a dry area a **rain shadow** (वृष्टिछाया).

One river cuts right through the wall. The **Kali Gandaki** rises in Upper Mustang, flows south between Dhaulagiri and Annapurna in a very deep gorge, and has made a natural road to Tibet that traders and pilgrims have used for centuries.

All the rivers of the province belong to one family. They join one by one and meet at **Devghat**, where the river takes the name **Narayani** and enters the plain.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Height**

- **Highest point:** **Dhaulagiri I, 8,167 m**, in Myagdi — the seventh-highest peak of the world and the **highest peak that stands wholly inside Nepal**.
- **Lowest point:** about **100 m**, at Triveni Susta in Binayi Tribeni Rural Municipality, Nawalpur, where the Narayani leaves Nepal ⚖️ (sources print 87 m, 93 m and "about 100 m").
- **Three belts** (the province's own profile, worked on an older area figure): Himalaya **26.8 percent**, hills **67.2 percent**, Terai and inner Terai **6 percent**. Two-thirds of Gandaki is hill country.
- The province lies between about 27°20′ and 29°20′ north and between 82°52′ and 85°12′ east.

**Peaks**

- **Three of Nepal's eight peaks above 8,000 metres** are here: **Dhaulagiri I** (8,167 m, Myagdi, 7th in the world), **Manaslu** (8,163 m, Gorkha, 8th) and **Annapurna I** (8,091 m, Kaski, on the Myagdi border, 10th).
- **Annapurna I** was the **first** 8,000-metre peak ever climbed — in 1950, three years before Sagarmatha. Manaslu was first climbed in 1956 and Dhaulagiri I in 1960.
- **Machhapuchhre** ("Fishtail", 6,993 m, Kaski) stands above Pokhara. It is holy and is **not open** to climbers.
- Other peaks often named (from memory): Annapurna II (7,937 m), Himalchuli (7,893 m), Nilgiri, Tilicho Peak, Lamjung Himal and Himlung.

**Passes**

- **Thorong La** (5,416 m) joins Manang and Mustang on the Annapurna Circuit. It is an **internal** pass; it does not lead to Tibet.
- **Korala** (Mustang) and **Gya La** (Gorkha) lead to Tibet (Chapter 1). Korala is the province's road link with China.
- **Larkya La** (Gorkha) is the high pass of the Manaslu trek (from memory).

**Rivers**

- The whole province drains to the **Gandaki** — called **Sapta Gandaki** in the hills and **Narayani** in the plain; in India it is the **Gandak**.
- **The seven Gandakis, from east to west:** Trishuli, Budhi Gandaki, Daraundi, Marsyangdi, Madi, Seti and Kali Gandaki.
- **Kali Gandaki:** rises in Upper Mustang; passes Jomsom, Beni, Kusma and Ridi; forms the border with Lumbini Province for a long way. Its gorge between Dhaulagiri and Annapurna, near Dana in Myagdi, is often called the **deepest gorge in the world** ⚖️. Black fossil stones called **shaligram** (शालिग्राम), worshipped as a form of Vishnu, are found in it.
- **Seti:** rises below Machhapuchhre and Annapurna and flows **through Pokhara**, in places in a slot so narrow and deep that the river cannot be seen from above.
- **Marsyangdi:** rises in Manang, flows through Lamjung and meets the Trishuli at **Mugling**.
- **Budhi Gandaki:** drains northern Gorkha around Manaslu and is the border with Dhading (Bagmati Province).
- **Devghat:** the Kali Gandaki and the Trishuli meet here, where **Tanahun, Nawalpur and Chitwan** touch one another. Below Devghat the river is the **Narayani**, the deepest river of Nepal in the textbooks.
- **Triveni** (Nawalpur): the Narayani leaves Nepal here. The barrage of the **Gandak Agreement** of 1959 with India stands just below (Chapter 8).

**Lakes**

- **Phewa** (Kaski): the lake of Pokhara, with the temple of **Tal Barahi** on an island; often called the second-largest lake of Nepal, after Rara.
- **Begnas** and **Rupa** (Kaski): on the eastern side of the Pokhara Valley.
- **Lake Cluster of Pokhara Valley:** a **Ramsar site since 2016**. It has **nine** lakes: Phewa, Begnas, Rupa, Dipang, Maidi, Khaste, Neureni, Kamalpokhari and Gunde.
- **Tilicho** (Manang, 4,919 m): the textbook answer for the **highest lake** of Nepal ⚖️. A lake found in Manang in 2019, **Kajin Sara**, is said to lie higher, at about 5,000 metres; it has not replaced Tilicho in the textbooks.
- **Damodar Kunda** and **Dhumba** (Mustang); **Birendra Tal** (Gorkha, below Manaslu).

**Climate**

- **Lumle** (Kaski): the **wettest place** of Nepal — more than 5,000 mm of rain a year. Study pages call it "the Cherrapunji of Nepal".
- **Mustang** and **Manang:** the **driest** districts, in the rain shadow. Mustang is called **"the desert of Nepal"** and "the district beyond the Himalaya" (हिमालपारिको जिल्ला).
- Within about 100 kilometres the climate changes from hot subtropical (Nawalpur) through temperate (Pokhara and the hills) to alpine and tundra (the high Himalaya).

**Protected areas**

- **Annapurna Conservation Area** (1992): **7,629 sq km** — the **largest protected area** of Nepal and its **first conservation area**. It lies in five districts — Kaski, Lamjung, Manang, Mustang and Myagdi — and covers 87 wards of 15 local levels. It is managed by a trust together with local people, not by park wardens (from memory).
- **Manaslu Conservation Area** (1998): 1,663 sq km, in Gorkha.
- **Dhorpatan Hunting Reserve** (1987): 1,325 sq km — the **only hunting reserve** of Nepal; shared by Baglung and Myagdi with Rukum East (Lumbini Province); known for blue sheep (नाउर).
- **Chitwan National Park:** a part of the park and of its buffer zone lies on the Nawalpur side of the Narayani.
- Animals to remember: snow leopard, blue sheep, Himalayan tahr and musk deer in the north; rhinoceros and tiger in the Nawalpur buffer zone.

**Hazards**

- **Earthquake of 12 Baisakh 2072 (25 April 2015):** the epicentre was at **Barpak, Gorkha**.
- **Seti flood, 23 Baisakh 2069 (5 May 2012):** a sudden flood from the Annapurna side killed scores of people at Kharapani above Pokhara (from memory).
- 🔄 **2083:** an earthquake of magnitude 4.9 near Lo Manthang, Mustang, on 23 Bhadra 2083 (8 September 2026); an avalanche near Himlung, Manang, on 27 September 2026; a flood on the **Budhi Gandaki** in northern Gorkha on 25 Ashoj 2083 (11 October 2026), when a dam of landslide debris burst (Chapters 7 and 9).
- **Thulagi** glacial lake in Manang is on the list of lakes that are watched (Chapter 7).

#### 7 · Exam traps

- The **highest peak of the province** is **Dhaulagiri I**, not Annapurna I, although the conservation area and the trek carry the name Annapurna.
- Dhaulagiri I is the highest peak **wholly inside Nepal**; Sagarmatha and Kanchenjunga stand on the border.
- **Annapurna I is 10th** in the world, not 9th, and it was climbed **first** (1950).
- **Manaslu is in Gorkha**, not in Manang. Manang lies behind Annapurna.
- **Dhaulagiri is in Myagdi**; the old zone of that name had its headquarters at Baglung.
- **Machhapuchhre** is below 7,000 metres and has never been opened for climbing.
- **Thorong La** is not a border pass. **Korala** is.
- The **Annapurna Conservation Area** is the largest protected area, but it is **not a national park**. The largest national park is Shey Phoksundo (Karnali).
- **Dhorpatan** is a **hunting reserve**, and only part of it is in Gandaki.
- **Manaslu Conservation Area** is in Gorkha; Gorkha is **not** one of the five districts of the Annapurna Conservation Area.
- **Tilicho** is in **Manang**, not in Mustang. **Rara**, the largest lake, is in Karnali.
- The Ramsar site is a **cluster of nine lakes**, not Phewa alone.
- **Lumle** is in **Kaski** and is the wettest place although it is in the west of Nepal, not the east.
- The **Seti** of Pokhara is not the Seti of Sudurpashchim; they are two rivers.
- The **Narayani** begins at **Devghat**; above it the names are Kali Gandaki and Trishuli.

## 13.3 History and archaeology (इतिहास र पुरातत्त्व)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Unit 1.5 of the Gandaki syllabus — the history of Nepal — carries two questions. The story of modern Nepal begins in this province: the small kingdom of **Gorkha** became the kingdom of Nepal. A Gandaki paper may ask about Gorkha's kings, the twenty-four small states, the heroes born here, or the old palaces and forts. Chapter 2 tells the national story; this topic tells the part that happened on Gandaki soil.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Before Nepal was one country, the hills of the Gandaki were divided among many small kingdoms. People counted them as the **Chaubise** (चौबीसे) — "the twenty-four". Each had a hilltop fort called a **kot** (कोट) and a king of a Thakuri, Khas or Magar line. They fought and made friends with one another by turns.

In 1559 a prince of Lamjung, **Drabya Shah**, made himself king of one of these states, **Gorkha**. Two hundred years later his descendant **Prithvi Narayan Shah** set out from Gorkha to join the small states into one kingdom. He won the Kathmandu Valley and moved his capital there. After his death the regents of his family brought Gorkha's old neighbours — Lamjung, Tanahun, Kaski, Parbat and the rest — into Nepal.

Far in the north, the walled town of **Lo Manthang** in Mustang kept its own local raja under the kings of Nepal until our own time. In the twentieth century roads from Kathmandu and from the Indian border met at **Pokhara**, and the lake town became the centre of western Nepal.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Before the Shah kings**

- The hills were the home of **Magar** and **Gurung** chiefs. Old writings call the Magar country **Magarat** (मगरात) and the Gurung country **Tamuwan** (तमुवान). These names came back in 2075 in the debate on the name of the province (topic 13.1).
- **Ghale** chiefs ruled parts of Lamjung and Gorkha. Tradition says that the chief of **Liglig** in Gorkha was chosen each year by a **race**, and that Drabya Shah took Liglig on the day of the race before he took Gorkha (tradition; from memory).

**The Chaubise states**

- About **fourteen** of the twenty-four lay inside today's Gandaki Province ⚖️ (lists differ): **Gorkha, Lamjung, Kaski, Tanahun, Parbat, Galkot, Nuwakot, Bhirkot, Garahun, Satahun, Rising, Ghiring, Dhor and Paiyun**.
- The others — **Palpa**, Gulmi, Argha, Khanchi, Isma, Musikot, Dhurkot, Pyuthan — lay in today's Lumbini Province. Palpa was the largest of all.
- **Parbat**, also called **Malebum**, was the strongest state west of the Kali Gandaki. **Tanahun** was ruled by a branch of the **Sen** family of Palpa.
- **Kaski and Lamjung** were ruled by the family from which the Shah kings came. **Yasho Brahma Shah** was king of Lamjung; his youngest son was Drabya Shah.

**Gorkha**

- **1559 AD (1616 BS):** **Drabya Shah** founded the Shah kingdom of Gorkha.
- **Ram Shah** (17th century) fixed weights and measures and was famous for justice: **"न्याय नपाए गोर्खा जानू"** — "If you do not get justice, go to Gorkha."
- **Prithvi Narayan Shah** was born in Gorkha on **27 Poush 1779 BS (11 January 1723)** and became king in **1799 BS (1743)**. His first victory was **Nuwakot** in 1744 — the Nuwakot of today's Bagmati Province. After the Kathmandu Valley fell in 1768–1769 he moved the capital from Gorkha to Kathmandu.
- **Gorkha Durbar** — the palace-fort on the ridge above Gorkha town, with the shrines of Gorakhnath and Kalika — is on Nepal's **tentative list** for World Heritage (Chapter 6).
- The words **Gorkhali** and **Gurkha**, used for Nepali soldiers across the world, come from this town.

**Gandaki joins Nepal**

- Prithvi Narayan Shah did not conquer his nearest neighbours. Lamjung, Tanahun, Kaski and Parbat fought against Gorkha during his life.
- After his death in 1775 the regents **Rajendra Laxmi** and **Bahadur Shah** carried on the campaign. Lamjung, Tanahun, Kaski and then Parbat were joined to Nepal between about **1782 and 1786** (as textbooks give it; from memory).
- **1856:** a royal order gave Jung Bahadur Rana the title **"Maharaja of Kaski and Lamjung"**. The Rana prime ministers held the title, and the income of the two districts, until 2007 BS.

**People of this province in the nation's history**

- **Kalu Pande** — Kaji of Gorkha; killed at Kirtipur in 1757.
- **Bhimsen Thapa** — born in Gorkha; Mukhtiyar from 1806 to 1837, counted as the first Prime Minister.
- **Amar Singh Thapa** (Bada Kaji) — from Gorkha; commander in the west in the war of 1814–1816.
- **Bhakti Thapa** — from Lamjung; killed at Deuthal in 1815, aged over 70.
- **Lakhan Thapa Magar** — of Gorkha; rose against Jung Bahadur and was hanged in **1933 BS (1877)**; honoured as the **first martyr** of Nepal.
- Of Nepal's national heroes (राष्ट्रिय विभूति), **Ram Shah, Prithvi Narayan Shah, Bhimsen Thapa, Amar Singh Thapa, Bhakti Thapa** and the poet **Bhanubhakta Acharya** (Tanahun) belong to this province (birthplaces from memory).

**Mustang (Lo)**

- Upper Mustang was the small kingdom of **Lo**, with its walled capital **Lo Manthang**. It came under Nepal in the time of the regents but kept its own raja as a dependant.
- The law that ended the small vassal states took away the ruling powers of such rajas from **2017 BS** (Chapter 2). The raja of Mustang kept an honorary title until the republic ended it in 2065 BS (from memory).
- **Lo Manthang** is on Nepal's tentative list for World Heritage.

**From zones to province**

- **5 Mangsir 2019 (20 November 1962):** the government decided to divide Nepal into 14 zones and 75 districts. **Gandaki zone** had its headquarters at **Pokhara** and **Dhaulagiri zone** at **Baglung**.
- **2029 BS (1972):** Pokhara became the centre of the **Western Development Region**.
- Two roads made Pokhara a city: the **Prithvi Highway** from the Kathmandu side and the **Siddhartha Highway** from the Indian border at Sunauli (Chapter 5).

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Why is Gorkha important in the history of Nepal?

**Model answer.** **Beginning:** Gorkha was one of the Chaubise states of the Gandaki hills. In 1559 AD (1616 BS) Drabya Shah, a prince of Lamjung, founded the Shah kingdom there. **Good rule:** in the 17th century Ram Shah fixed weights and measures and gave such fair judgments that people said, "If you do not get justice, go to Gorkha." **Unification:** Prithvi Narayan Shah was born in Gorkha on 27 Poush 1779 BS and became its king in 1799 BS. From Gorkha he won Nuwakot in 1744, Makwanpur in 1762 and the Kathmandu Valley in 1768–1769, and so began the making of Nepal; his successors joined Gorkha's own neighbours — Lamjung, Tanahun, Kaski and Parbat — to the kingdom. **People:** Kalu Pande, Bhimsen Thapa and Amar Singh Thapa came from Gorkha, and so did Lakhan Thapa Magar, the first martyr. **Name:** the words Gorkhali and Gurkha, known across the world, come from it. **Later events:** the armed conflict of 2052 began with an attack there among others, and the earthquake of 2072 had its epicentre at Barpak. **Heritage:** the palace of Gorkha is on Nepal's tentative list for World Heritage. **Conclusion:** Gorkha is honoured as the birthplace of modern Nepal, and 27 Poush is kept as Prithvi Jayanti and National Unity Day.

## 13.4 Society and culture (समाज र संस्कृति)

#### 1 · Why it matters

This is one of the two topics that the Gandaki syllabus names. Sub-unit 1.6.3 asks for "the special customs, traditions, values and norms, religions, peoples, languages, culture, art, literature and music found in Gandaki Province" (गण्डकी प्रदेशमा पाइने विशेष प्रथा, परम्परा, मूल्य र मान्यता, धर्म, जातजाति, भाषाभाषी, संस्कृति, कला, साहित्य, संगीत). Unit 1.6 carries four questions. Paper II asks for the same matter in short written answers. The key facts below follow the words of the sub-unit one by one.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Gandaki is the home of two great hill peoples, the **Gurung** — who call themselves **Tamu** — and the **Magar**. Beside them live Brahman, Chhetri and Dalit families who speak Nepali, Newar traders in the old market towns, Thakali innkeepers on the Kali Gandaki, Tibetan-speaking peoples behind the Himalaya, and Tharu farmers on the plain of Nawalpur. No group is a majority.

Three things mark the culture of these hills. First, **singing together**: the Gurung evening house called Rodhi, the duet song called Dohori, the fiddle of the wandering Gandarbha. Second, **helping one another**: shared labour in the fields, the Thakali savings circle called Dhikur, the mothers' groups of the villages. Third, **going out and coming back**: for two hundred years young men of these hills have served as soldiers abroad — the famous Gurkhas — and their pay and pensions have shaped the villages.

Nepali literature also has deep roots here. The first poet of the language, **Bhanubhakta**, and two of its most honoured later poets were born in Tanahun, Kaski and Lamjung.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Peoples (जातजाति)** — Census 2078

- **107** castes and ethnic groups were counted in the province.
- **Largest groups:** Brahman (Hill) **20.0 percent**; **Magar 18.8 percent**; Chhetri **13.1 percent**; **Gurung 11.0 percent**; Bishwakarma (Kami) 9.3 percent; Sarki 4.6 percent; Pariyar (Damai) 4.1 percent; Newar 4.1 percent; Tamang 2.0 percent; Tharu 1.9 percent; Thakuri 1.7 percent; Kumal 1.6 percent; Bhujel 1.2 percent.
- **In three blocks:** Brahman, Chhetri and Thakuri together are about 35 percent; Magar and Gurung together about 30 percent; the three large Dalit groups about 18 percent.
- **Homes of the peoples** (from memory):
  - **Gurung (Tamu):** Lamjung, Kaski, Gorkha, Manang, Tanahun, Syangja, Parbat.
  - **Magar:** Tanahun, Syangja, Nawalpur, Baglung, Myagdi, Parbat, Gorkha.
  - **Thakali:** the Thak Khola valley of the Kali Gandaki in Mustang.
  - **Loba:** Lo Manthang and Upper Mustang. **Nyeshang (Manange):** Manang.
  - **Dura:** Lamjung. **Chhantyal:** Myagdi and Baglung. **Baram:** Gorkha. **Kumal** (potters), **Bote**, **Majhi** and **Darai:** river valleys of Gorkha, Tanahun and Nawalpur. **Tharu:** Nawalpur.
  - **Gandarbha (Gaine):** singers with the sarangi; Batulechaur in Pokhara is their best-known village.
  - **Newar:** old market towns — Pokhara, Bandipur, Gorkha, Baglung.

**Languages (भाषाभाषी)** — Census 2078

- **Nepali 73.4 percent**; **Magar 9.6 percent**; **Gurung 8.4 percent**; Tharu 1.7 percent; Nepal Bhasa 1.5 percent; Tamang 1.4 percent.
- The Magar are 18.8 percent of the people, but only 9.6 percent speak Magar as mother tongue; the Gurung are 11.0 percent and Gurung speakers 8.4 percent. Many families of both peoples now speak Nepali at home.
- 🔄 **Official languages.** The Provincial Assembly passed a Bill to use **Magar and Gurung** as languages of official business, beside Nepali, on **24 Ashad 2082 (8 July 2025)**. The Province Chief authenticated it on **19 Shrawan 2082 (4 August 2025)**. It was made under **Article 7 (2)** of the Constitution.
  - Gandaki is the **second** province with such a law. Bagmati was first (Tamang and Nepal Bhasa, in use from 24 Baisakh 2081); Koshi followed in Kartik 2083 (Chapter 6).
  - The Language Commission had recommended **Magar, Gurung and Bhojpuri** for Gandaki in 2078 (Chapter 6). The Act took the first two.
  - The Bill was moved by the Minister for Social Development; members of the Nepali Congress and the CPN (UML) supported it and the other parties stayed neutral.
  - How far offices really use the two languages was not checked. The Gurung community uses three scripts and had not agreed on one in 2082.
- **Small languages** (from memory): Thakali, the Tibetan speech of Lo and of Manang, Chhantyal, Baram, Kumal, Darai, Bote. **Dura** of Lamjung has almost no speakers left.

**Religion (धर्म)** — Census 2078

- **Hindu 82.2 percent**; **Buddhist 11.5 percent**; Christian 2.4 percent; **Bon 2.2 percent**; Islam 0.9 percent; Prakriti 0.7 percent; Kirat 0.1 percent.
- **Bon** (बोन) is the old religion of the Himalayan peoples, older than Buddhism in these hills. Many Gurung keep it through their own priests; that is why Bon is more than 2 percent here although it is very small in Nepal as a whole.
- **Shared holy places:** **Muktinath** (Mustang) is holy to Hindus and Buddhists alike; the **shaligram** of the Kali Gandaki is worshipped as Vishnu; **Manakamana** (Gorkha), **Bindhyabasini** and **Tal Barahi** (Pokhara), **Galeshwar** (Myagdi), **Baglung Kalika**, **Damodar Kunda** (Mustang), **Devghat** (Tanahun side) and **Triveni** (Nawalpur) draw pilgrims from all over Nepal and from India.
- The monasteries of **Lo Manthang** keep an old tradition of Tibetan Buddhism.

**Special customs and traditions (विशेष प्रथा र परम्परा)**

- **Rodhi** (रोधी) — Gurung: an evening house where the young people of a village gather to sing, dance and work together (Chapter 6).
- **Arghun** or **Pae** (अर्घौँ) — Gurung: the great rite for a dead person, led by the Gurung priests — **Pachyu**, **Klepri** (Ghyabre) and the **Lama** (from memory).
- **Nogar** — Gurung: a group of neighbours who plant and harvest each other's fields in turn (from memory).
- **Dhikur** (ढिकुर) — Thakali: a savings circle in which members pay in equal sums and take the whole fund in turn (Chapter 6). The Thakali are known as traders and innkeepers, and "Thakali kitchen" has become a name for good Nepali food.
- **Lha Phewa** — Thakali: the "coming of the gods", kept once in **twelve years** (from memory).
- **Lahure tradition** — Gurung, Magar and others: service in the British and Indian armies. A soldier home on leave is called a *lahure* (लाहुरे). Pokhara has a recruiting and pension centre for the British Gurkhas (Chapter 8).
- **Sarangi and karkha** — Gandarbha: wandering singers carry news and the deeds of heroes from village to village in ballads called *karkha*.
- **Mothers' groups** (आमा समूह) — village women who raise funds by singing and use them for paths, taps and schools; the custom is strong in the Gurung and Magar hills (from memory).
- **Panche baja** — the band of five instruments, played by Pariyar musicians at weddings and festivals across the hills.
- **Pottery** of the Kumal, **boat-work and fishing** of the Bote and Majhi, **weaving of radi and pakhi** (woollen rugs) in Gurung villages — crafts that are also family traditions.

**Values and norms (मूल्य र मान्यता)**

- **Welcome to the guest** (अतिथि सत्कार): the base of the village homestays of Sirubari, Ghandruk and Ghale Gaun.
- **Working together** (सहकार्य): Nogar, Parma (exchange of labour), Dhikur, mothers' groups, community forests.
- **Courage and loyalty:** the name of the Gurkha soldier.
- **Respect for elders and for the dead:** blessings at Dashain and Lhosar; long rites such as Arghun.
- **Living side by side:** Hindu, Buddhist and Bon worship in the same villages and at the same shrines; no group is a majority.
- **Justice:** the memory of Ram Shah — "If you do not get justice, go to Gorkha."
- **Care of nature:** holy mountains that may not be climbed (Machhapuchhre), holy lakes, and local management of the Annapurna area.

**Festivals and fairs (चाडपर्व र मेला)**

- **Tamu Lhosar** — the Gurung new year, **15 Poush**; a public holiday. *Lho* means year and *sar* means new. In 2083 it falls on 30 December 2026.
- **Maghe Sankranti** — 1 Magh; the chief festival of the Magar; fairs at river meetings such as Devghat and Triveni.
- **Tiji** — Lo Manthang, three days of masked dances in early summer.
- **Yartung** — horse races at Muktinath and in Manang at the end of summer.
- **Toran La** — the Thakali festival, in Falgun (from memory).
- **Bhanu Jayanti** — 29 Ashad, the birthday of Bhanubhakta.
- **Dashain, Tihar and Teej** are kept by most families.
- 🔄 **Holidays added by the province for 2083:** Bhanu Jayanti (29 Ashad), **Kojagrat Purnima** (8 Kartik) and **Gandaki Province Establishment Day** (22 Magh).

**Art (कला)**

- **Buildings:** Gorkha Durbar; the mud-walled town and painted monasteries of Lo Manthang; the cliff caves of Upper Mustang; the Newar houses of Bandipur.
- **Crafts:** *radi* and *pakhi* rugs, *bhangra* cloth of nettle fibre, bamboo work, Kumal pottery, hand-made *lokta* paper (Baglung is called "the district of lokta"), the making of sarangi.
- **Museums in Pokhara** (from memory): the International Mountain Museum and the Gurkha Memorial Museum.

**Literature (साहित्य)**

- **Bhanubhakta Acharya** — **Adikavi**, the first poet; born at **Chundi Ramgha, Tanahun**; put the Ramayana into simple Nepali.
- **Lekhnath Paudyal** — **Kavi Shiromani**; born in **Kaski**; *Tarun Tapasi*, *Ritu Bichar*, *Pinjada ko Suga*.
- **Madhav Prasad Ghimire** — **Rashtra Kavi**; born in **Lamjung**; *Gauri*, *Malati Mangale*.
- From memory: **Dharma Raj Thapa** of Pokhara — **Jana Kavi Keshari**, a poet of folk song; **Bhupi Sherchan**, born at Thak Tukuche in Mustang — *Ghumne Mech Mathi Andho Manchhe*; **Ali Miyan** of Pokhara, a folk poet; **Saru Bhakta** of Pokhara — the novel *Pagal Basti*.
- The Gurung keep long **oral texts** recited by their priests; the Gandarbha keep ballads.

**Music and dance (संगीत र नृत्य)**

- **Ghatu** and **Sorathi** — Gurung dances that tell old stories (Chapter 6).
- **Kaura** (Kauda) and **Maruni** — Magar and western-hill dances; Kaura is best known in Tanahun and Syangja.
- **Dohori** — the duet song of question and answer between two sides; it grew from the Rodhi, and Pokhara is one of its centres.
- From memory: **Salaijo**, a Magar song of Baglung, Myagdi and Parbat; **Thado Bhaka** of Lamjung, Gorkha and Tanahun; **Chutka** and **Jhyaure** of the middle hills; **Krishna Charitra** (Balun) of the Brahman and Chhetri villages.
- **Sarangi** — the four-stringed fiddle of the Gandarbha. **Jhalak Man Gandarbha** of Kaski made the song *Aamale Sodhlin Ni* known to the whole country (from memory).

- **Tamu Lhosar** (15 Poush) is the Gurung new year. **Sonam Lhosar** (Tamang) and **Gyalpo Lhosar** (Sherpa) come later, in Magh and Falgun.
- **Rodhi** is Gurung; **Dhikur** is Thakali. Do not swap them.
- **Tiji** is at **Lo Manthang** (Mustang); **Mani Rimdu** is at Tengboche (Koshi).
- **Bhanubhakta** was born in **Tanahun**, not in Gorkha or Kaski. **Lekhnath** is Kaski's; **Madhav Prasad Ghimire** is Lamjung's.
- **Laxmi Prasad Devkota**, the Mahakavi, was born in Kathmandu — not in this province.
- **Bon** is a religion counted separately from Buddhism in the census.
- **Muktinath** is in **Mustang**, **Manakamana** in **Gorkha**. Both are in Gandaki.
- **Deuda** is the song of the far west, not of Gandaki.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Describe the special customs and traditions of the peoples of Gandaki Province.

**Model answer.** **Setting:** Gandaki has 107 castes and ethnic groups; Brahman (Hill), Magar, Chhetri and Gurung are the largest, and no group is a majority. Each people keeps customs of its own. **Gurung (Tamu):** the Rodhi, an evening house where young people sing and work together; Tamu Lhosar, the new year on 15 Poush; Arghun, the great rite for the dead led by their own priests; the story dances Ghatu and Sorathi. **Magar:** Maghe Sankranti as the chief festival, and the Kaura and Maruni dances. **Thakali:** the Dhikur savings circle, the yearly festival Toran La and a festival kept once in twelve years. **Peoples beyond the Himalaya:** the Tiji masked dances of Lo Manthang and the Yartung horse races of Mustang and Manang. **Gandarbha:** songs of news and heroes sung with the sarangi. **Shared traditions:** service in foreign armies (the lahure), duet singing (Dohori), exchange of labour, mothers' groups, the panche baja at weddings, and pilgrimage to Muktinath, Manakamana and the river meetings. **Values behind them:** welcome to guests, working together, courage, respect for elders, and peace among Hindu, Buddhist and Bon believers. **Present state:** migration to towns and abroad is weakening customs such as the Rodhi, while homestays, festivals and the law of 2082 that made Magar and Gurung official languages help to keep them alive. **Conclusion:** these customs are the identity of the province and a base of its tourism.

## 13.5 Economy and ways of living (अर्थतन्त्र र जीविकोपार्जन)

#### 1 · Why it matters

This is the second topic that the Gandaki syllabus names. Sub-unit 1.3.5 asks for "the economic and social activities of Gandaki Province and the variety seen in ways of earning a living" (गण्डकी प्रदेशमा भएका आर्थिक तथा सामाजिक क्रियाकलाप र जीविकोपार्जनमा देखिएको विविधता). It sits in unit 1.3, which carries four questions; unit 1.7 (economy, infrastructure and plans) carries three more. Paper II asks for "the main bases of economic development of Gandaki Province", its current periodic plan and the bases of its revenue.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

A family in Gandaki earns its living according to the step of the staircase on which it lives (topic 13.2).

- **Behind the Himalaya,** in Mustang and Manang, little rain falls. People keep yaks and goats, grow barley, buckwheat, potatoes and apples, run lodges for trekkers and pilgrims, and trade.
- **In the hills,** families farm terraces, keep cattle and goats, grow oranges and coffee — and send a son or daughter to the army or to work abroad. Money sent home is a large part of many household budgets.
- **In Pokhara,** most people live by services: hotels, shops, banks, colleges, hospitals, transport and building.
- **On the plain of Nawalpur,** people grow rice and vegetables and work in factories and trade along the East–West Highway.

Taken together, the province is small in output — about 9 percent of Nepal's — but comfortable: its income per person is the second highest of the seven provinces and its poverty the lowest. Its three great resources are **scenery** (tourism), **falling water** (hydropower) and **people who work away from home** (remittances).

#### 3 · Key facts

**Size and shape of the economy** 🔄

- **Share of Nepal's GDP:** **9.0 percent** in 2082/83 — about Rs 592 billion by the estimate of the National Statistics Office; the **fifth** of the seven provinces.
- **Growth in 2082/83:** **5.01 percent** — the second highest, after Bagmati (5.40). Only Bagmati and Gandaki grew faster than Nepal as a whole (3.85 percent).
  - ⚖️ The province's own Economic Survey, presented on 1 Ashad 2083, gives growth of **4.83 percent** and output of about Rs 570 billion. Learn 5.01 percent as the national office's figure.
- **Income per person:** **USD 1,651** — the second highest, after Bagmati (2,644), and above the national figure (USD 1,513).
- **Poverty:** **11.88 percent** — the **lowest** of the seven provinces (Nepal: 20.27 percent).
- **Structure** (the province's survey for 2081/82): services about **56 percent**, agriculture about **27 percent**, industry about **17 percent**. Services lead; farming still employs the most people.
- **Banks and firms:** 68,149 industries were registered with the province in 2081/82, giving work to 139,684 people.

**Ways of earning a living, belt by belt** (जीविकोपार्जनको विविधता)

- **High valleys (Manang, Mustang, northern Gorkha):** herding of yak, sheep and mountain goats; barley, buckwheat and potato; **apples**; lodges, guiding, ponies and jeeps for visitors; trade; collecting herbs; moving to lower places in winter.
- **Middle hills (Gorkha, Lamjung, Tanahun, Syangja, Parbat, Baglung, Myagdi, hill Kaski):** terrace farming of rice, maize and millet; **oranges**, **coffee**, vegetables and honey; cattle, buffalo and goats; service in the Nepali, Indian and British armies; **foreign employment**; pensions; homestays; teaching and small trade.
- **Pokhara Valley:** hotels, restaurants, trekking and travel offices, paragliding and boating; wholesale and retail trade; banks; colleges and hospitals; transport; building and land business; fishing in the lakes.
- **Plain and inner Terai (Nawalpur):** rice, wheat, mustard and vegetables; fish ponds; factories and warehouses along the East–West Highway; trade; jungle tourism and homestays beside Chitwan National Park.
- **Traditional occupations:** Gandarbha singers, Kumal potters, Bote and Majhi boatmen and fishers, makers of *radi* rugs and *lokta* paper (topic 13.4).

**Farming**

- Only about **22 percent** of the land can be farmed; about 47 percent is forest (planning commission's figures).
- Modern irrigation reaches only about **25 percent** of the land that could be irrigated.
- 🔄 **Mustang apples:** **7,038.54 tonnes** in 2083, worth about **Rs 1.15 billion**, from 662 hectares — 5.29 percent more than the year before (report of 12 Kartik 2083).
- 🔄 **Coffee:** 163 tonnes of green beans from 1,571 hectares in nine districts in 2082/83; **Lamjung** has the largest area. The coffee board says the province could grow more than 20,000 tonnes.
- **Oranges** of Syangja and its neighbours; honey, cardamom and vegetables in the hills.
- By the end of the first plan the province produced 381 kg of food grain per person and met its own need for milk, eggs and meat.

**Tourism** 🔄

- Tourism is the province's best-known business. **Pokhara** was declared the **"tourism capital of Nepal"** on **4 Chaitra 2080 (17 March 2024)** by the Prime Minister of the day.
- **Annapurna Conservation Area:** **299,831** foreign visitors in **2025** — the highest ever. That equals about a quarter of all foreign visitors to Nepal that year (1,162,365). In 25 years (2001–2025) the area received 2,632,004 foreign visitors; the lowest year was 2021 (16,105).
- **Treks:** the Annapurna Circuit over Thorong La, Annapurna Base Camp, Ghorepani–Poon Hill, Mardi Himal, the Manaslu Circuit, Upper Mustang.
- **Pilgrimage:** Muktinath, Manakamana, Damodar Kunda, Devghat, Triveni.
- **Village tourism:** **Sirubari** (Syangja) is known as Nepal's first village-tourism village; **Ghandruk** (Kaski) and **Ghale Gaun** (Lamjung) are Gurung villages with homestays.
- **Adventure:** paragliding from Sarangkot, boating on Phewa, rafting, the long suspension bridges and bungee jump of Kusma (Parbat).
- **Pokhara International Airport:** opened on **17 Poush 2079 (1 January 2023)**; built with a **loan from China** (the Export–Import Bank of China); a national pride project. It has had very few international flights, and three corruption cases about its building are in court (Chapter 9). In 2081 Nepal asked China to turn the loan into a grant.

**Water and energy**

- The Gandaki basin has a theoretical hydropower potential of **20,650 MW** (Chapter 1).
- **Kali Gandaki A** — 144 MW, Syangja, 2002; the largest station of Nepal until 2021.
- **Middle Marsyangdi** — 70 MW, Lamjung, 2008. **Marsyangdi** — 69 MW, 1989.
- 🔄 **Tanahu Hydropower Project** — 140 MW, a **storage** project on the Seti in Tanahun; 79 percent built in Kartik 2083; its target of Ashad 2083 was missed.
- **Budhi Gandaki** — 1,200 MW storage project on the border of Gorkha and Dhading; planned.
- Almost every household has electricity (99.5 percent at the end of the first plan).

**Industry and minerals**

- **Pokhara Industrial Estate**; factories in the Gaindakot–Kawasoti belt of Nawalpur; small and cottage industries everywhere.
- **Iron** deposit at **Dhaubadi** (Nawalpur); signs of **uranium** in Upper Mustang.
- Industry is the weak side: the first plan aimed at growth of 13.3 percent a year in industry and reached less than 1 percent.

**Work abroad and migration**

- In the Census of 2078, **286,593** people of Gandaki were recorded as absent abroad — about **13 percent** of all Nepalis abroad, from a province with 8.46 percent of the people.
- **Effects:** money for houses, schooling and business; but hill villages lose people, fields are left empty, the population grows old (13.3 percent are 60 or older) and families move down to Pokhara and the plain. Kaski and Nawalpur grow; Manang, Mustang and many hill districts shrink.

**Social activities (सामाजिक क्रियाकलाप)**

- **Education:** literacy 81.7 percent; Pokhara is a university town (Pokhara University; the province's own Gandaki University; large campuses).
- **Health:** teaching hospitals in Pokhara; at the end of the first plan 77 percent of the people lived within 30 minutes of a health facility and 71 percent of births took place in one.
- **Services:** drinking water for 95 percent; internet for 65 percent. The province was declared free of open defecation in 2074.
- **Groups:** mothers' groups, cooperatives, community forest user groups, youth clubs.
- **Sport:** the Ninth National Games were held at Pokhara in 2079 (Chapter 9); paragliding and mountain races draw visitors.

**Roads and airports**

- **Prithvi Highway** (Naubise–Pokhara) and **Siddhartha Highway** (Sunauli–Pokhara) meet at Pokhara. The **East–West Highway** crosses Nawalpur.
- The **Mid-Hill Highway** crosses the province from east to west; the **Kali Gandaki Corridor** runs from the plain up the river to **Korala** on the Chinese border — a north–south link between India and China. Both are still being built.
- Roads reach all but two local levels. The roads to Manang and Mustang are rough and often cut in the monsoon.
- Seven airports have been built; three are in use — the new and the old airports of Pokhara and **Jomsom**.

**The province's budget for 2083/84** 🔄

- Presented on **1 Ashad 2083 (15 June 2026)** by the Minister for Economic Affairs, **Jit Bahadur Sherchan**.
- **Total: Rs 33.00 billion** (exactly Rs 32.9999 billion) — the **smallest** of the seven provinces.
- **Recurrent** Rs 12.72 billion (38.56 percent); **capital** Rs 20.03 billion (60.68 percent); **financing** Rs 0.25 billion (0.76 percent).
- **Where the money comes from:** revenue shared by the Federation Rs 10.16 billion; equalisation grant Rs 7.88 billion ⚖️ (one newspaper prints 7.85); conditional grant Rs 3.93 billion; complementary grant Rs 0.55 billion; special grant Rs 0.40 billion; royalties Rs 0.51 billion; internal loan Rs 1.75 billion; the rest from the province's own revenue and money left from the year before.
- The four federal grants together are about Rs 12.7 billion — nearly **39 percent** of the budget (Chapter 5).
- **Largest shares:** physical infrastructure Rs 11.96 billion; energy, water resources and drinking water Rs 4.77 billion.
- The budget for 2082/83 was Rs 31.98 billion. Over its first eight years the province spent on average 61 percent of what it budgeted.

**Periodic plans**

- **First plan:** 2076/77–2080/81. Its long-term vision to 2100 BS: **"समृद्ध प्रदेश, सुखी नागरिक"** — a prosperous province, happy citizens.
- **Second plan:** 2081/82–2085/86 — the **current** plan 🔄.
  - **Vision:** **"आत्मनिर्भर र समुन्नत प्रदेश: सुखी प्रदेशवासी"** — a self-reliant and advanced province: happy people of the province.
  - **Goal:** "आत्मनिर्भरता, सामाजिक न्याय र समुन्नति हासिल गर्ने" — to achieve self-reliance, social justice and advancement.
  - **Three objectives:** (1) quality infrastructure, production, jobs and income; (2) human capital, fair sharing of the gains of development, and social justice; (3) quality public services and good governance.
  - **Eight** strategies and **fourteen** main fields; tied to the Sixteenth Plan of Nepal.
  - ⚖️ The wording changed while the plan was written. A first base paper (Chaitra 2080) said "…सम्मानित प्रदेशवासी" (respected people); a second (Jestha 2081, under another government) said "समृद्ध प्रदेश, सुखी प्रदेशवासी". The words given above are those in the vice-chairperson's own account of the finished plan.
- The plans are made by the **Province Policy and Planning Commission**, chaired by the Chief Minister.

#### 7 · Exam traps

- **Lowest poverty** is Gandaki's; the **highest income** is Bagmati's. Gandaki is second in income.
- Gandaki has the **smallest budget** but **not** the smallest economy; the smallest economy is **Karnali** (4.2 percent).
- **Services**, not farming, give the largest share of output — although farming gives work to the most people.
- **5.01 percent** is the National Statistics Office's growth figure; **4.83 percent** is the province's own.
- Pokhara is the **tourism capital** by a declaration of 2080; it has been the **provincial capital** since 2075. Two different things.
- Pokhara International Airport was built with a **Chinese loan**, not a grant; Gautam Buddha International Airport (Lumbini) was built with Asian Development Bank money.
- **Kali Gandaki A** is in **Syangja** and is **no longer** the largest station; Upper Tamakoshi (456 MW, Bagmati) is.
- **Tanahu** is a **storage** project on the **Seti**; **Budhi Gandaki** is on the border of Gorkha and **Dhading**.
- The figure **20,650 MW** is the potential of the whole Gandaki **basin**, part of which lies in Bagmati Province.
- The **Annapurna** area's count is of **foreign** visitors only; Nepali visitors are not in it.
- The **first** plan's long-term vision ("सुखी नागरिक") and the **second** plan's vision ("सुखी प्रदेशवासी") are different sentences.
- The budget is presented on **1 Ashad** in every province — a rule of law, not a choice.

**Question.** Describe the economic and social activities of Gandaki Province and the variety seen in its ways of earning a living.

**Model answer.** **Overview:** Gandaki gives about 9.0 percent of Nepal's GDP; services provide about 56 percent of its output, agriculture 27 and industry 17. Its income per person (USD 1,651) is the second highest and its poverty (11.88 percent) the lowest of the seven provinces. **Variety by belt:** (1) in the high valleys of Manang and Mustang people live by herding, barley and potato, apples, lodges and trade; (2) in the middle hills by terrace farming, oranges, coffee, livestock, army service and work abroad; (3) in Pokhara by tourism, trade, banks, education and health services; (4) on the plain of Nawalpur by rice and vegetables, factories and highway trade. **Main bases:** tourism — Pokhara, the tourism capital, and the Annapurna area with a record 299,831 foreign visitors in 2025; hydropower — Kali Gandaki A, the Marsyangdi stations and the Tanahu project; remittances and pensions — 286,593 people were abroad at the last census. **Social activities:** high literacy (81.7 percent), colleges and hospitals in Pokhara, mothers' groups, cooperatives and community forests, and near-complete access to electricity and drinking water. **Problems:** weak industry, little irrigation, empty hill villages and an ageing population, an under-used international airport, and dependence on federal money. **Conclusion:** the variety of land gives a variety of work; the second periodic plan aims to turn it into a "self-reliant and advanced province".

## 13.6 Government and institutions (प्रदेश सरकार र संस्था)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Questions on the Chief Minister, the Province Chief, the Speaker and the seats of the Assembly belong to current affairs, the unit with the most questions (five) in the Gandaki paper. Papers II and III ask about the Office of the Chief Minister and Council of Ministers, the Provincial Assembly Secretariat and the Province Public Service Commission by name. Gandaki's story is also the clearest example in Nepal of how Articles 168 and 188 of the Constitution work, because the Supreme Court has had to decide who may be its Chief Minister.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Chapter 4 explains the machine: a Province Chief named by the President; a Provincial Assembly elected by the people; a Chief Minister who must keep the support of more than half of the Assembly.

In Gandaki the Assembly has **60** members, so a government needs **31**. No party has ever won 31 seats by itself. Every government has therefore been a partnership, and when a partner walked away the government was in danger. That is why the province has changed its Chief Minister five times in less than nine years, though only **four persons** have held the post.

Twice the question went to the Supreme Court. In 2081 the Court removed a Chief Minister whose claim of a majority counted the Speaker. In 2083 it was asked whether a Chief Minister must seek a new vote of confidence after a partner leaves him; that case was still open when this chapter was checked.

#### 3 · Key facts

**The Provincial Assembly (प्रदेश सभा)**

- **60 members:** 36 directly elected + 24 by proportional representation. **Majority: 31. Two-thirds: 40.**
- It meets at **Pokhara**.
- **First Assembly** (elected in Mangsir 2074): first meeting on 22 Magh 2074 (5 February 2018) ⚖️; Speaker **Netra Nath Adhikari** (CPN (Maoist Centre)); Deputy Speaker **Srijana Sharma** (CPN (UML)).
- **Second Assembly:** elected on **4 Mangsir 2079 (20 November 2022)**; first meeting on 18 Poush 2079 (2 January 2023); Speaker **Krishna Prasad Dhital** (CPN (Maoist Centre)); Deputy Speaker **Bina Kumari Thapa** (CPN (UML)).
- 🔄 **Seats in Mangsir 2083:** Nepali Congress **27**; CPN (UML) **22**; Nepali Communist Party **7** (the former Maoist Centre members, one of them the Speaker); Rastriya Prajatantra Party **2**; Nepal Samajbadi Party **1**; **vacant 1**. That is 59 members in office.
- The vacant seat is **Manang (B)**. Its member, Rajiv Gurung — known as Deepak Manange — was elected as an independent, unopposed, in 2079; he lost the seat after he was sent to prison in a criminal case.
- The **Rastriya Swatantra Party**, which swept the House election of 2082, has **no member** in this Assembly, because the Assembly was elected in 2079.

**Chief Ministers (मुख्यमन्त्री)** — four persons, six appointments

- **Prithvi Subba Gurung** (CPN (UML)) — the **first**; took office on **4 Falgun 2074 (16 February 2018)**. He resigned on 26 Baisakh 2078 (9 May 2021), was appointed again on 29 Baisakh 2078 (12 May 2021) as leader of the largest party, and lost office in Jestha 2078.
- **Krishna Chandra Nepali Pokharel** (Nepali Congress) — from **29 Jestha 2078 (12 June 2021)** to Poush 2079; the first Chief Minister of the province from the Nepali Congress.
- **Khagaraj Adhikari** (CPN (UML)) — from **25 Poush 2079 (9 January 2023)**, after the election of 2079. The Maoist Centre withdrew its support on 10 Chaitra 2079 (24 March 2023); he failed to win a vote of confidence on 9 Baisakh 2080 (22 April 2023).
- **Surendra Raj Pandey** (Nepali Congress) — appointed on **14 Baisakh 2080 (27 April 2023)** under Article 168 (2); won a vote of confidence on 21 Baisakh 2080 (4 May 2023) by **35 votes to 22**. He lost office on 22 Chaitra 2080 (4 April 2024), after the Maoist Centre changed sides.
- **Khagaraj Adhikari** again — appointed on **25 Chaitra 2080 (7 April 2024)**. On 23 Baisakh 2081 (5 May 2024) the Speaker declared that he had won a vote of confidence with **30** votes, when 57 members were present.
  - The **Supreme Court** held the appointment and that vote **unconstitutional**: a majority of the whole Assembly is 31, and the claim behind the appointment had counted the Speaker. The Court ordered the leader of the largest party to be appointed.
- **Surendra Raj Pandey** again — appointed on **16 Jestha 2081 (29 May 2024)** under **Article 168 (3)**, as leader of the largest party. He won a vote of confidence on **28 Jestha 2081 (10 June 2024)** by **31 votes to 28**.
  - For him: Nepali Congress 27, Rastriya Prajatantra Party 2, one independent, and **Phanindra Devkota**, who had been elected on the Maoist Centre's symbol and voted against that party's order.
  - Against him: CPN (UML) 22 and Maoist Centre 6.
- 🔄 **Surendra Raj Pandey is the Chief Minister on 19 November 2026.**

**From partnership to court, 2081–2083** 🔄

- **17 Bhadra 2081 (2 September 2024):** after the Nepali Congress and the CPN (UML) became partners in the federal government, the UML joined Pandey's government with four ministers.
- **2 Baisakh 2083 (15 April 2026):** all eight ministers were changed — four from each party. The Council of Ministers had nine members.
- **10 Bhadra 2083 (26 August 2026):** the **CPN (UML) left the government**; its four ministers resigned. The Chief Minister took their ministries himself.
- **17 Bhadra 2083 (2 September 2026):** Phanindra Devkota was sworn in as Minister for Energy, Water Resources and Drinking Water; Nanda Prasad Neupane moved to physical infrastructure.
- **The dispute.** Article **188 (2)** says that if a party in the government withdraws its support, the Chief Minister must table a motion of confidence **within thirty days**. The UML says he had to do so by 9 Ashoj 2083 (25 September 2026). The Chief Minister says he won his vote of 2081 **without** the UML, that the members who gave him those votes are still with him, and that he therefore still has a majority — 27 + 2 + 1 = **30 of the 59** members now in office.
- **20 Ashoj 2083 (6 October 2026):** the UML's deputy leader in the Assembly, Lil Bahadur Thapa Magar, filed a writ in the Supreme Court.
- **21 Ashoj 2083 (7 October 2026):** a single bench of the Supreme Court issued an **interim order**: until the case is decided, the Chief Minister is to take **no decision of long-term effect**.
- A hearing was fixed for **2 Mangsir 2083 (18 November 2026)**. **Its result was not found.** A report of 1 Mangsir said the government had worked "in uncertainty" for three months and the Council of Ministers was still incomplete.

**Council of Ministers (मन्त्रिपरिषद्)** 🔄

- **Eight ministries** besides the Office of the Chief Minister and Council of Ministers:
  - Economic Affairs (आर्थिक मामिला)
  - Physical Infrastructure Development and Transport Management (भौतिक पूर्वाधार विकास तथा यातायात व्यवस्था)
  - Energy, Water Resources and Drinking Water (ऊर्जा, जलस्रोत तथा खानेपानी)
  - Agriculture, Land Management and Cooperatives (कृषि, भूमि व्यवस्था तथा सहकारी)
  - Industry and Tourism (उद्योग तथा पर्यटन)
  - Health (स्वास्थ्य)
  - Social Development, Youth and Sports (सामाजिक विकास, युवा तथा खेलकुद)
  - Forest and Environment (वन तथा वातावरण)
- After 17 Bhadra 2083 the Chief Minister himself held agriculture, industry and tourism, and health. For the other ministers see "Who is who" at the head of the chapter.

**Province Chief (प्रदेश प्रमुख)**

- Five persons have held the post: **Baburam Kunwar** (from Magh 2074), **Amik Sherchan** (from Kartik 2076), **Sita Kumari Poudel** (Baisakh–Shrawan 2078), **Prithvi Man Gurung** (from Shrawan 2078) and **Dilli Raj Bhatta**.
- 🔄 **Dilli Raj Bhatta**, of Doti, was appointed by the Council of Ministers of Nepal on 28 Falgun 2080 (11 March 2024) and took charge on 4 Chaitra 2080 (17 March 2024).
- The Province Chief appoints the Chief Minister, swears in ministers and authenticates Bills — for example the language Act on 19 Shrawan 2082 (topic 13.4).

**Courts**

- **Pokhara High Court**, with a **bench at Baglung** (Chapter 3); a district court in each of the eleven districts.

**Province Public Service Commission (प्रदेश लोक सेवा आयोग), Gandaki Province**

- Set up under **Article 244** of the Constitution and the province's **Province Public Service Commission Act, 2076**.
- Its first office-bearers were appointed on 7 Chaitra 2077 (20 March 2021): chairperson **Bishnu Prasad Nepal**, a former Principal Secretary of the province, and member Kalpana Lamsal. Yogendra Gauchan is the other member 🔄.
- The term is **six years**, so the chairperson's term ends in Chaitra 2083 🔄.
- Its office is in **Pokhara**. It selects staff for the provincial service and for the 85 local levels — and it sets the examination for which this book is written.
- It gave its **sixth yearly report**, for 2082/83, to the Province Chief on 25 Bhadra 2083 (10 September 2026).

**Other bodies**

- **Province Policy and Planning Commission** — chaired by the Chief Minister; makes the periodic plans (topic 13.5).
- **Provincial Assembly Secretariat**, Pokhara.
- **Koshi Declaration, 2083:** the Chief Minister joined the six others at Biratnagar on 27–28 Ashoj 2083 (13–14 October 2026) to ask for provincial police and a fair share of revenue (Chapter 4).

**Elections**

- **Assembly, 2074:** CPN (UML) 27; Nepali Congress 15; Maoist Centre 12; Rastriya Janamorcha 3; Naya Shakti 2; independent 1.
- **Assembly, 2079:** Nepali Congress 27; CPN (UML) 22; Maoist Centre 8; Rastriya Prajatantra Party 2; independent 1.
- 🔄 **House of Representatives, 21 Falgun 2082 (5 March 2026):** of the province's **18** seats the **Rastriya Swatantra Party won 15**; the **Nepali Congress 2** (Manang and Mustang); an **independent 1** — **Mahabir Pun**, in Myagdi. The CPN (UML) and the Nepali Communist Party won none.
  - Members elected from Gandaki seats sit in the federal government: Dr Swarnim Wagle (Tanahun 1) — finance; Sudhan Gurung (Gorkha 1) — home affairs; Mahabir Pun — science, technology and innovation (Chapter 9).
  - Bina Gurung (Kaski 3) was the only woman directly elected from the province.

#### 7 · Exam traps

- **Four persons**, not six, have been Chief Minister; Adhikari and Pandey each held the post twice.
- The **first** Chief Minister was **Prithvi Subba Gurung** — not Prithvi **Man** Gurung, who was a **Province Chief**.
- Pandey was appointed under **Article 168 (2)** in 2080 and under **Article 168 (3)** in 2081. Read the year in the question.
- The vote of **31 to 28** was in **2081**; the vote of **35 to 22** was in **2080**.
- The **Supreme Court**, not the Assembly, removed Adhikari in 2081.
- In 2083 the Court gave an **interim order**; it did **not** remove the Chief Minister. The final decision was not known on 19 November 2026.
- **Article 188 (2)** is about a vote of **confidence** asked by the Chief Minister; **Article 188 (4)** is about a motion of **no confidence** moved by members (Chapter 4).
- A majority is **31** when all 60 seats are filled. With 59 members in office it is 30.
- The **Rastriya Swatantra Party** won 15 of the 18 **House** seats but has no seat in the **Assembly**.
- The **Speaker** was elected from the Maoist Centre; the **Deputy Speaker** is from the UML — two different parties, as the Constitution requires.
- The **Chief Minister** heads the government; the **Province Chief** is the President's representative. Do not confuse Pandey with Bhatta.
- **Pokhara High Court** has its bench at **Baglung**, not at Gorkha.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Describe the changes of government in Gandaki Province since 2074 and the constitutional questions they raised.

**Model answer.** **Background:** the Assembly has 60 members and no party has ever held the 31 needed, so every government has been a partnership. **Changes:** (1) Prithvi Subba Gurung of the CPN (UML) became the first Chief Minister on 4 Falgun 2074 and led until 2078; (2) Krishna Chandra Nepali Pokharel of the Nepali Congress followed on 29 Jestha 2078; (3) after the election of 2079 Khagaraj Adhikari (UML) took office on 25 Poush 2079 but lost a vote of confidence when the Maoist Centre left him; (4) Surendra Raj Pandey (Nepali Congress) was appointed on 14 Baisakh 2080 under Article 168 (2); (5) Adhikari returned on 25 Chaitra 2080; (6) Pandey returned on 16 Jestha 2081 under Article 168 (3) and won a vote of confidence by 31 to 28. **First question — how is a majority counted?** In 2081 the Supreme Court annulled Adhikari's appointment and his vote of confidence: a majority means 31 of all 60 members, and the Speaker cannot be counted to reach it. **Second question — when must a Chief Minister seek confidence again?** The UML, which had joined Pandey in 2081, left on 10 Bhadra 2083. It relies on Article 188 (2), which requires a vote within thirty days when a party in the government withdraws support; the Chief Minister answers that his majority never depended on the UML. On 21 Ashoj 2083 the Supreme Court ordered him, meanwhile, to take no decision of long-term effect. **Lesson:** frequent changes slow the budget and the plan; clear rules and their honest use are needed for stable provincial government.

## 13.7 The province in numbers and superlatives; fact sheet (तथ्याङ्क, विशेषता र तथ्य-पत्र)

#### 1 · Why it matters

This topic is the page to learn by heart. It collects the figures of topics 13.1 to 13.6, shows where Gandaki is first among the provinces and where it is not, gives one line for each district and lists the events of 2082 and 2083. Candidates of the other six provinces need only this topic.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Remember Gandaki by four ideas.

- **Lake and mountain:** Pokhara and Phewa below, Annapurna, Dhaulagiri and Manaslu above, the dry valleys of Manang and Mustang behind.
- **Few people, good living:** the sixth province in population, but second in income and literacy and first in freedom from poverty.
- **The home of Gorkha:** the kingdom that became Nepal, and the Gurkha soldier.
- **A government in court:** four Chief Ministers, six appointments, two cases in the Supreme Court.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Where Gandaki is first among the seven provinces**

- **Lowest poverty:** 11.88 percent.
- **Highest peak wholly inside Nepal:** Dhaulagiri I, 8,167 m.
- **First 8,000-metre peak ever climbed:** Annapurna I, 1950.
- **Largest protected area:** Annapurna Conservation Area, 7,629 sq km — also the one with the most foreign visitors (299,831 in 2025 🔄).
- **Only hunting reserve:** Dhorpatan (shared with Lumbini).
- **Largest metropolitan city by area:** Pokhara.
- **Wettest and driest places:** Lumle (Kaski) and Mustang.
- **District with the fewest people in Nepal:** Manang, 5,658. The next is Mustang, 14,452.
- **Smallest budget:** Rs 33.00 billion for 2083/84 🔄.

**Where it is second**

- **Literacy** (81.7 percent), **growth** (5.01 percent 🔄) and **income per person** (USD 1,651 🔄) — each time after Bagmati.
- **Second to be named** (22 Ashad 2075), after Karnali.
- **Second to make its own languages official** (2082), after Bagmati 🔄.
- **Second fewest** people (2,466,427), local levels (85) and persons per sq km (115) — each time after Karnali.

**Where it is in the middle**

- **Area:** fourth (21,504 sq km). **Districts:** eleven — fewer than Koshi, Bagmati and Lumbini, more than Karnali, Sudurpashchim and Madhesh.
- **Share of GDP:** fifth (9.0 percent 🔄).
- **Assembly seats:** 60 — more than Karnali (40) and Sudurpashchim (53) only.

**One line for each district**

- **Gorkha:** largest district; Gorkha Durbar; Manakamana; Manaslu; Barpak; touches China.
- **Lamjung:** Besisahar; old Shah kingdom; Middle Marsyangdi; Ghale Gaun; birthplace of Madhav Prasad Ghimire.
- **Tanahun:** Damauli; Bandipur; birthplace of Bhanubhakta; Tanahu Hydropower Project; Devghat.
- **Syangja:** Putalibazar; Kali Gandaki A; oranges; Sirubari; five municipalities.
- **Kaski:** Pokhara, the capital; most people; Phewa; Lumle; Machhapuchhre; Annapurna I.
- **Manang:** Chame; fewest people in Nepal; Tilicho; Thorong La; touches China.
- **Mustang:** Jomsom; Lo Manthang; Muktinath; Korala; apples; "desert of Nepal".
- **Myagdi:** Beni; Dhaulagiri I; the Kali Gandaki gorge at Dana; home district of Mahabir Pun.
- **Parbat:** Kusma; smallest district; long suspension bridges.
- **Baglung:** Baglung; Dhorpatan; Kalika temple; bench of the High Court; "district of lokta".
- **Nawalpur:** Kawasoti; the only plain district; the only border with India; Triveni.

**Events of 2082 and 2083 to remember** 🔄

- **24 Ashad 2082 (8 July 2025):** the Assembly passed the Bill making Magar and Gurung languages of official business; authenticated on 19 Shrawan 2082 (4 August 2025).
- **Year 2025:** a record 299,831 foreign visitors in the Annapurna Conservation Area.
- **21 Falgun 2082 (5 March 2026):** House election; the Rastriya Swatantra Party won 15 of the province's 18 seats.
- **2 Baisakh 2083 (15 April 2026):** all eight ministers of the province were changed.
- **1 Ashad 2083 (15 June 2026):** budget of Rs 33.00 billion.
- **10 Bhadra 2083 (26 August 2026):** the CPN (UML) left the government.
- **23 Bhadra 2083 (8 September 2026):** earthquake of magnitude 4.9 near Lo Manthang, Mustang.
- **21 Ashoj 2083 (7 October 2026):** interim order of the Supreme Court on the vote of confidence.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** What features make Gandaki Province different from the other provinces of Nepal?

**Model answer.** **Land:** within one province the land rises from about 100 metres to 8,167 metres; it has three peaks above 8,000 metres, the highest peak wholly inside Nepal (Dhaulagiri I), the deep gorge of the Kali Gandaki, the wettest place (Lumle) and the driest district (Mustang) of the country. **Nature:** the Annapurna Conservation Area is the largest protected area and the most visited; Dhorpatan is the only hunting reserve; the nine lakes of Pokhara are a Ramsar site. **People:** it is the home of the Gurung and Magar peoples and of the Gurkha tradition; with 2,466,427 people it is sixth in population, and Manang is the least populated district of Nepal. **Living standard:** poverty is the lowest of the seven provinces (11.88 percent); literacy, growth and income are second only to Bagmati's. **History:** Gorkha, the cradle of modern Nepal, lies here. **Town:** Pokhara is the largest metropolitan city by area and the tourism capital of Nepal. **Government:** it has the smallest budget (Rs 33.00 billion) and an Assembly of 60; questions about its Chief Minister have twice gone to the Supreme Court. **Conclusion:** Gandaki is small in people and money but rich in nature, history and human development.

### D. Five short-answer questions

**1. Describe how Province No. 4 became Gandaki Province, and explain why Pokhara was chosen as its capital.**
*Points for the answer:* the rule — Articles 288 (2) and 295 (2): capital and name by two-thirds of all members of the Provincial Assembly, 40 of 60 · the start — formed 3 Ashoj 2072 as Province No. 4; Pokhara named temporary seat on 3 Magh 2074 · the committee — an Assembly committee listed Gandaki, Tamuwan–Magarat, Gorkha and Dhaulagiri as names; report tabled 10 Ashad 2075 · the capital — 18 Ashad 2075, all 52 present for Pokhara · the name — 22 Ashad 2075, 54 for and 2 against; an amendment for "Gandaki–Tamuwan" rejected · why "Gandaki" — the river system drains every district; name of the old zone; a name that belongs to no single people · why Pokhara — largest city and old centre of the Western Development Region; where the Prithvi and Siddhartha highways meet; airport, offices, colleges and hospitals already there; no rival town · result — the second province to be named; 22 Magh kept as Establishment Day.

**2. "Tourism is the backbone of the economy of Gandaki Province." Explain, and state the main problems of the sector.**
*Points for the answer:* base — services give about 56 percent of output, and tourism leads them · attractions — Pokhara and its lakes; three peaks above 8,000 metres; Annapurna Circuit, Annapurna Base Camp, Manaslu and Upper Mustang treks; Muktinath and Manakamana; village homestays (Sirubari, Ghandruk, Ghale Gaun); paragliding and rafting · figures — record 299,831 foreign visitors in the Annapurna Conservation Area in 2025, about a quarter of all visitors to Nepal; Pokhara declared tourism capital on 4 Chaitra 2080 · benefits — jobs in hotels, guiding and transport; income for hill and mountain villages; a market for apples, vegetables and crafts; conservation paid for by entry fees · problems — Pokhara International Airport has few international flights and a loan to repay; roads to Manang and Mustang are rough; visitors stay few days and spend little; disasters (flood, avalanche, earthquake); waste and crowding on trails and lakes; shortage of trained workers because the young go abroad · way forward — regular international flights, better roads, more products outside Pokhara, safety and insurance, local ownership.

**3. Give a short account of the religions, peoples, languages, art, literature and music of Gandaki Province.**
*Points for the answer:* peoples — 107 groups; Brahman (Hill) 20.0, Magar 18.8, Chhetri 13.1, Gurung 11.0 percent; Thakali, Loba, Dura, Chhantyal, Kumal, Gandarbha among the small groups · languages — Nepali 73.4, Magar 9.6, Gurung 8.4 percent; Magar and Gurung made official by the Act of 2082 · religions — Hindu 82.2, Buddhist 11.5, Christian 2.4, Bon 2.2 percent; shared shrines such as Muktinath · customs — Rodhi, Arghun, Dhikur, the lahure tradition, mothers' groups · festivals — Tamu Lhosar (15 Poush), Maghe Sankranti, Tiji, Yartung · art — Gorkha Durbar, Lo Manthang, Bandipur; radi-pakhi, lokta paper, pottery · literature — Bhanubhakta (Tanahun), Lekhnath Paudyal (Kaski), Madhav Prasad Ghimire (Lamjung), Dharma Raj Thapa, Bhupi Sherchan · music — Ghatu, Sorathi, Kaura, Dohori, the sarangi of the Gandarbha · conclusion — unity in variety; culture as a base of tourism; need to protect small languages.

**4. Explain the dispute of 2083 over the vote of confidence in Gandaki Province, with the provisions of the Constitution.**
*Points for the answer:* background — Assembly of 60; Surendra Raj Pandey appointed on 16 Jestha 2081 under Article 168 (3); won confidence 31 to 28 on 28 Jestha 2081 without the CPN (UML); the UML joined his government on 17 Bhadra 2081 · event — the UML left on 10 Bhadra 2083 and its four ministers resigned · the rule — Article 188 (2): when a party in the government withdraws support, the Chief Minister must table a motion of confidence within thirty days · the UML's case — thirty days ended on 9 Ashoj 2083; no motion was tabled · the Chief Minister's case — his majority of 2081 did not include the UML; those who voted for him still support him (30 of 59 members in office) · the Court — writ of 20 Ashoj 2083; interim order of 21 Ashoj 2083: no decision of long-term effect until the case ends; hearing fixed for 2 Mangsir 2083 (result not known here) · comparison — in Koshi the Chief Minister took and won the vote on 8 Bhadra 2083; in Madhesh the Chief Minister resigned on 28 Kartik 2083 · effect — an incomplete Council of Ministers and slow work for three months · lesson — the thirty-day rule should be read plainly, and stability needs agreement among parties.

**5. Give a basic account of the periodic plans of Gandaki Province.**
*Points for the answer:* why — Paper II asks for "information on the current periodic plan of Gandaki Province"; a plan links each year's budget to a five-year aim · who — Province Policy and Planning Commission, chaired by the Chief Minister · first plan — 2076/77–2080/81; long-term vision to 2100 BS "समृद्ध प्रदेश, सुखी नागरिक" · its results — growth 4.23 percent against a target of 10.2; income USD 1,596 against 1,956; poverty about 11 percent against 7.3; electricity 99.5 percent; internet 65 percent against 80; industry far below target · reasons — the pandemic, weak spending (on average 61 percent of the budget), changes of government · second plan — 2081/82–2085/86, the current plan; vision "आत्मनिर्भर र समुन्नत प्रदेश: सुखी प्रदेशवासी"; goal — self-reliance, social justice and advancement; three objectives; eight strategies; fourteen fields; in line with the Sixteenth Plan · link with the budget — Rs 33.00 billion for 2083/84, 60.68 percent of it for capital spending · weakness — targets set too high; dependence on federal grants; the wording of the vision itself changed with the government · conclusion — realistic targets and steady government are needed.

### E. Common mistakes

1. Writing that Pokhara is in "Pokhara district". It is in **Kaski**.
2. Saying the capital and the name were decided on one day. The capital was fixed on **18 Ashad 2075**, the name on **22 Ashad 2075**.
3. Saying the name was passed unanimously. It passed by **54 to 2**; only the **capital** had no vote against.
4. Calling Gandaki **Province No. 3** or **No. 5**. It was **Province No. 4**.
5. Writing **79** or **88** local levels. Gandaki has **85 = 1 + 0 + 26 + 58**.
6. Calling Gandaki the province with the **fewest people**. That is **Karnali**; Gandaki is sixth.
7. Putting **Palpa**, **Gulmi** or **Chitwan** in Gandaki. The first two are in Lumbini, the third in Bagmati.
8. Giving **Annapurna I** as the highest peak of the province. It is **Dhaulagiri I** (8,167 m).
9. Placing **Manaslu** in Manang or **Tilicho** in Mustang. Manaslu is in **Gorkha**; Tilicho is in **Manang**.
10. Calling the **Annapurna Conservation Area** a national park, or **Dhorpatan** a national park.
11. Saying **Prithvi Narayan Shah** founded the kingdom of Gorkha. **Drabya Shah** founded it in 1559.
12. Confusing the two **Nuwakots**: the Chaubise state is in **Syangja**; the fort won in 1744 is in **Bagmati**.
13. Giving **Magar** or **Gurung** as the largest group. **Brahman (Hill)** is the largest, at 20.0 percent.
14. Adding **Bhojpuri** to the official languages. The Act names only **Magar and Gurung**.
15. Saying Gandaki has the **smallest economy**. It has the **smallest budget**; the smallest economy is Karnali's.
16. Counting **six** Chief Ministers, or naming **Prithvi Man Gurung** as the first. **Four persons** have held the post; the first was **Prithvi Subba Gurung**.
