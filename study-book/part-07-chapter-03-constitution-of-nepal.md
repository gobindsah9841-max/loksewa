## 3.1 Constitutional history: the seven constitutions (संवैधानिक इतिहास: सात संविधान)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Examiners ask which constitution came when, who gave it, and what was new in it. "How many Parts, Articles and Schedules?" is a favourite one-line question. This topic joins Chapter 2 (History) to the rest of this chapter.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

A **constitution** (संविधान) is the highest law of a country. It says who holds power, how the government is formed, and what rights the people have. Nepal has had **seven constitutions** between 2004 BS and 2072 BS. The first six were given from above — by a Rana prime minister, by kings, or by a parliament that had not been elected to write a constitution. The seventh, the **Constitution of Nepal**, was written and passed by a **Constituent Assembly** elected by the people. Each constitution followed a political change: the last years of Rana rule, the revolution of 2007, the royal takeover of 2017, the People's Movements of 2046 and 2062/63, and the peace process.

#### 3 · Key facts

**1. Government of Nepal Act, 2004 (नेपाल सरकार वैधानिक कानून, २००४)**

- Announced on **13 Magh 2004 (26 January 1948)** by Prime Minister **Padma Shamsher**.
- It was to start on 1 Baisakh 2005 (13 April 1948). It was **never put into practice**.
- The **first written constitution** of Nepal. **6 Parts, 68 Articles, 1 Schedule.**
- Indian legal experts — Sri Prakash Gupta, Raghunath Singh and Ram Ugra Singh — helped to prepare it.

**2. Interim Government of Nepal Act, 2007 (नेपाल अन्तरिम शासन विधान, २००७)**

- Given by **King Tribhuvan** in **Chaitra 2007 (1951)**, after the fall of Rana rule. ⚖️ Most guidebooks give 29 Chaitra 2007 (11 April 1951); some give 17 Chaitra 2007 (30 March 1951).
- The **first constitution to be used in practice**. **7 Parts, 73 Articles, 3 Schedules.**
- In its time the **Pradhan Nyayalaya** (प्रधान न्यायालय, the highest court) was separated from the executive by an Act of 2008 BS, and the **Public Service Commission** was set up on 1 Ashad 2008 (15 June 1951).

**3. Constitution of the Kingdom of Nepal, 2015 (नेपाल अधिराज्यको संविधान, २०१५)**

- Given by **King Mahendra** on **1 Falgun 2015 (12 February 1959)**. **10 Parts, 77 Articles, 3 Schedules.**
- A parliamentary system with two Houses. The **first general election** (2015 BS) produced a parliament under it.
- Drafted by a commission chaired by **Bhagawati Prasad Singh**, with the British expert **Sir Ivor Jennings** as adviser.
- The King used its **Article 55** for the takeover of 1 Poush 2017.

**4. Constitution of Nepal, 2019 (नेपालको संविधान, २०१९)**

- Given by **King Mahendra** on **1 Poush 2019 (16 December 1962)**. **20 Parts, 97 Articles, 6 Schedules.**
- The party-less **Panchayat** system. Sovereignty lay in the King. One House: the Rastriya Panchayat.
- The first constitution to call Nepal a **Hindu** state.
- Drafted by a committee chaired by **Rishikesh Shah**. **Amended three times:** 2023, 2032 and 2037 BS.

**5. Constitution of the Kingdom of Nepal, 2047 (नेपाल अधिराज्यको संविधान, २०४७)**

- Proclaimed by **King Birendra** on **23 Kartik 2047 (9 November 1990)**, after the People's Movement of 2046. **23 Parts, 133 Articles, 3 Schedules.**
- **Sovereignty was placed in the people for the first time.** Constitutional monarchy and a multi-party parliamentary system.
- Two Houses: House of Representatives (205 members) and National Assembly (60 members).
- Drafted by the Constitution Recommendation Commission chaired by Justice **Bishwanath Upadhyaya**. It was **never amended**.

**6. Interim Constitution of Nepal, 2063 (नेपालको अन्तरिम संविधान, २०६३)**

- Came into force on **1 Magh 2063 (15 January 2007)**, proclaimed by Parliament, not by the King. **25 Parts, 167 Articles, 4 Schedules.**
- Declared Nepal a **secular** state and provided for the election of a **Constituent Assembly**.
- Drafted by a committee chaired by former Justice **Laxman Prasad Aryal**. **Amended twelve times.**
- Under it the first meeting of the Constituent Assembly put the **republic** into effect on 15 Jestha 2065 (28 May 2008).

**7. Constitution of Nepal (नेपालको संविधान)**

- **Passed** by the second Constituent Assembly on **30 Bhadra 2072 (16 September 2015)**. Of the 532 members who voted, **507** voted for and **25** against.
- **Proclaimed** by President **Dr Ram Baran Yadav** on **3 Ashoj 2072 (20 September 2015)**. It came into force the same day (Article 307).
- **35 Parts, 308 Articles, 9 Schedules.**
- Chairperson of the Constituent Assembly: **Subas Chandra Nembang**. Chairperson of its Constitution Drafting Committee: **Krishna Prasad Sitaula**.
- The first constitution of Nepal made by **elected representatives of the people**. It set up a **federal democratic republic** with three levels of government.
- **Amended twice:** 2072 BS and 2077 BS (topic 3.10).
- Article 308 repealed the Interim Constitution of 2063.

#### 6 · Memory aid

- **Years: "04 – 07 – 15 – 19 – 47 – 63 – 72."**
- **Articles: "68 – 73 – 77 – 97 – 133 – 167 – 308."** Each constitution is longer than the one before.
- **Two "interim" constitutions: 2007 and 2063.** Each came just after a movement.
- **Two with "Kingdom" in the name: 2015 and 2047.** Each set up a multi-party parliament under a king.

#### 7 · Exam traps

- The Act of **2004** was announced but **never put into practice**. The first constitution actually used was that of **2007**.
- **1 Poush** appears twice: the royal takeover was on 1 Poush **2017**; the Panchayat constitution came on 1 Poush **2019**.
- **Sovereignty in the people** came first in **2047**, not in 2063 or 2072.
- **Secular state** came first in the Interim Constitution of **2063**.
- The Constitution of **2047 was never amended**. The Constitution of **2019** was amended three times.
- The present constitution was **passed on 30 Bhadra** and **proclaimed on 3 Ashoj** 2072.
- Its short title is **"Constitution of Nepal"**. The year 2072 is not part of the title (Article 307).
- The Constitution was written by the **second** Constituent Assembly, elected in 2070 BS.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Why is the Constitution of Nepal (2072) called a constitution made by the people? Compare it briefly with the earlier constitutions.

**Model answer.** Nepal had six constitutions before 2072 BS. (1) **How the earlier ones were made.** The Act of 2004 was given by a Rana prime minister. The Act of 2007 and the constitutions of 2015, 2019 and 2047 were given by kings, after drafting by small appointed commissions. The Interim Constitution of 2063 was proclaimed by a parliament that had not been elected for that purpose. (2) **How the present one was made.** The people elected a Constituent Assembly. Its second term, elected in 2070 BS, debated the draft, collected public opinion and passed the Constitution on 30 Bhadra 2072 by 507 votes to 25. The President proclaimed it on 3 Ashoj 2072. (3) **What this shows in the text.** The Preamble begins "We, the sovereign people of Nepal". Article 2 places sovereignty and state authority in the people. (4) **What is new in content.** It is the first constitution that is federal and republican together, with three levels of government, 31 fundamental rights and rules for proportional inclusion. **Conclusion.** Earlier constitutions were gifts from a ruler; this one is a decision of the people's own representatives.

## 3.2 Salient features, Preamble, Parts and Schedules (विशेषता, प्रस्तावना, भाग र अनुसूची)

#### 1 · Why it matters

"Which Part deals with …?", "Which Schedule lists …?" and "Which Article says …?" are quick marks. The words of Article 4 and the national symbols in Articles 8 and 9 are asked again and again.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

The Constitution has four building blocks. The **Preamble** (प्रस्तावना) gives the aims. The **Parts** (भाग) group the rules by subject. The **Articles** (धारा) are the rules themselves, numbered 1 to 308. The **Schedules** (अनुसूची) at the end hold designs and lists: the flag, the anthem, the coat of arms, the provinces with their districts, and five lists of powers. The main ideas are easy to state: the people are sovereign; Nepal is a **federal democratic republic**; power is shared among three levels; the government is parliamentary; the courts are independent; and the State must include every group fairly.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Preamble**

- It opens with the words **"We, the sovereign people of Nepal"** (हामी सार्वभौमसत्तासम्पन्न नेपाली जनता).
- It recalls the people's movements, the armed conflict and the sacrifice of martyrs.
- It promises to end discrimination and oppression created by the **feudal, autocratic, centralised and unitary** system of the past.
- It accepts the country's **multi-ethnic, multi-lingual, multi-religious and multi-cultural** character.
- It aims at an equal society built on the **proportional, inclusive and participatory** principle.
- It commits the country to **socialism based on democratic norms and values**: a competitive multi-party system, civil liberties, fundamental rights, human rights, adult franchise, periodic elections, full freedom of the press, an independent judiciary and the rule of law.
- Its goal is **lasting peace, good governance, development and prosperity** through a federal democratic republican system.
- It says the Constitution is passed **through the Constituent Assembly**.

**Part 1 — Preliminary (Articles 1 to 9)**

| Article | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | The Constitution is the **fundamental law** (मूल कानून). A law that conflicts with it is void to the extent of the conflict. Every person must observe it. |
| 2 | **Sovereignty and state authority** lie in the Nepali people. |
| 3 | Defines the **nation** (राष्ट्र): all Nepali people together, with their diversity and common aims. |
| 4 | Nepal is an **independent, indivisible, sovereign, secular, inclusive, democratic, socialism-oriented, federal democratic republican state**. |
| 5 | **National interest**: freedom, sovereignty, territorial integrity, nationality, independence, dignity, the rights of Nepalis, border security, economic well-being and prosperity. |
| 6 | **All mother tongues** spoken in Nepal are **languages of the nation** (राष्ट्रभाषा). |
| 7 | **Nepali in the Devanagari script** is the **official language** (सरकारी कामकाजको भाषा). A province may add one or more languages spoken by a majority of its people. |
| 8 | The **national flag**: two triangles, a crimson base and a deep-blue border; a white crescent moon above and a white sun with **twelve rays** below. |
| 9 | The **anthem** is in Schedule 2 and the **coat of arms** in Schedule 3. National flower: rhododendron (लालीगुराँस). National colour: crimson (सिम्रिक). National animal: cow. National bird: Danphe (डाँफे). |

- In Article 4, **"secular"** (धर्मनिरपेक्ष) means religious and cultural freedom, including protection of the religion and culture handed down from ancient times.

**Salient features**

1. A **written** constitution made by a **Constituent Assembly**.
2. **Sovereignty in the people** (Article 2); the Constitution is the fundamental law (Article 1).
3. A **federal democratic republic** with **three levels**: federation (संघ), province (प्रदेश) and local level (स्थानीय तह) (Article 56).
4. A **secular** and **inclusive** state, **socialism-oriented** (Article 4).
5. A **parliamentary** form of government; the President is head of state, the Prime Minister is head of government (Articles 61, 74, 76).
6. A **two-House** Federal Parliament; a **one-House** Provincial Assembly.
7. A **mixed electoral system**: first-past-the-post and proportional representation.
8. **Thirty-one fundamental rights** and four duties of citizens (Part 3).
9. Directive principles, policies and obligations of the State (Part 4).
10. An **independent judiciary** in three tiers, with a Constitutional Bench in the Supreme Court.
11. **Thirteen constitutional bodies**, including seven commissions for inclusion and rights (Parts 21 to 27).
12. **Proportional inclusion**: at least one-third women in the Federal Parliament from each party; one woman between Speaker and Deputy Speaker; President and Vice-President from different sex or community.
13. **Lists of powers** for each level in Schedules 5 to 9; **residual power** with the federation (Article 58).
14. Amendment by a **two-thirds** majority; sovereignty and territorial integrity cannot be amended away (Article 274).

#### 7 · Exam traps

- Article 4 says **"socialism-oriented"** (समाजवाद उन्मुख), not "socialist".
- **Languages of the nation** (Article 6) are all mother tongues spoken in Nepal. The **official language** (Article 7) is Nepali in the Devanagari script. They are two different ideas.
- The **anthem** is in **Schedule 2**; the **coat of arms** is in **Schedule 3**. The second amendment changed Schedule 3.
- **Schedule 4** lists provinces and districts. It does not list powers.
- Local-level powers are in **Schedule 8**, not Schedule 6.
- **Residual power** belongs to the **federation** (Article 58).
- The capital is fixed by **Article 288**, not by Part 1.
- In the English translation "State" often means **province**. Read the question with care.

## 3.3 Citizenship (नागरिकता)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Citizenship is a short Part with clear rules, so it gives direct questions: the kinds of citizenship, the rights of a non-resident Nepali citizen, and the offices that need citizenship by descent. The citizenship law has also been in the news since 2080 BS.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

**Citizenship** (नागरिकता) is the legal tie between a person and the State. A citizen can vote, hold public office and claim every right the Constitution gives to citizens. **Part 2 (Articles 10 to 15)** sets the main rules. The details — forms, offices, procedure — are in the **Nepal Citizenship Act, 2063**. Nepal has **one citizenship for the whole country**; a province does not give a separate citizenship.

#### 3 · Key facts

**The rules in Part 2**

- **Article 10:** no citizen may be deprived of the right to obtain citizenship. Nepal has **single federal citizenship with provincial identity** (प्रादेशिक पहिचान सहितको एकल संघीय नागरिकता).
- **Article 11** says who is a citizen.
  - **By descent** (वंशजको आधारमा): a person whose **father or mother** was a citizen of Nepal at the time of the person's birth.
  - A child found in Nepal whose parents are unknown is a citizen by descent until a parent is traced.
  - A person born in Nepal to a **Nepali mother**, living in Nepal, whose father is not traced gets citizenship **by descent**. If the father proves to be a foreigner, it is changed to naturalised citizenship.
  - A **foreign woman married to a Nepali citizen** may take **naturalised citizenship** (अंगीकृत नागरिकता) as federal law provides.
  - A person born to a **Nepali mother and a foreign father** may take **naturalised** citizenship if the person has lived permanently in Nepal and has no foreign citizenship.
  - The Government of Nepal may grant **naturalised** and **honorary** (सम्मानार्थ) citizenship under federal law.
- **Article 12:** a person who gets citizenship by descent may have a certificate with gender identity in the name of the **mother or the father**.
- **Article 13:** getting, getting back and ending citizenship are governed by federal law.
- **Article 14:** **non-resident Nepali citizenship** (गैरआवासीय नेपाली नागरिकता) may be given to a person who (a) has taken foreign citizenship, (b) lives **outside the SAARC countries**, and (c) was once a Nepali citizen, or whose father, mother, grandfather or grandmother was. It carries **economic, social and cultural rights** only.
- **Article 15:** records of citizens are kept under federal law.

**Citizenship and public office**

- **Article 289:** only a **citizen by descent** can hold these offices — President, Vice-President, Prime Minister, Chief Justice, Speaker of the House of Representatives, Chairperson of the National Assembly, Province Chief, Chief Minister, Speaker of a Provincial Assembly, and chief of a security body. The Nepali text lists these ten offices; one English translation leaves out the Chairperson of the National Assembly.
- For other constitutional posts, a naturalised citizen must have lived in Nepal for **ten years**, and a citizen by birth for **five years**.
- **Article 291:** a Nepali citizen who holds a **foreign permanent residence permit** cannot be elected, nominated or appointed to an office under the Constitution. The bar ends three months after the permit is given up.

**The law**

- Citizenship laws: Nepal Citizenship Act **2009**, then **2020**, then the present Act of **2063**.
- A citizenship certificate is issued through the **District Administration Office**. Under the Act a person may apply after reaching the age of **16**.
- Nepal does not allow a person to hold Nepali citizenship and a foreign citizenship together.
- 🔄 The **Nepal Citizenship (First Amendment) Act** was authenticated by President Ram Chandra Paudel on **17 Jestha 2080 (31 May 2023)**. It lets the children of citizens by birth take citizenship by descent and opens the way for non-resident Nepali citizenship.
- 🔄 A **second amendment** was authenticated in **September 2025 (Ashoj 2082)**. It eases citizenship in the mother's name where the father is not identified.

## 3.4 Fundamental rights and duties (मौलिक हक र कर्तव्य)

#### 1 · Why it matters

This is the most asked Part of the Constitution. Typical questions: "How many fundamental rights?", "Which Article gives the right to …?", "Which rights cannot be suspended?" and "What are the duties of a citizen?". The five writs join this topic to the judiciary.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

**Fundamental rights** (मौलिक हक) are rights written into the Constitution itself. The State must respect them, and a person whose right is broken can go to court. **Part 3 (Articles 16 to 48)** holds them. **Articles 16 to 46** give **31 rights**. Article 47 tells the State to make the laws needed to carry them out. **Article 48** lists four **duties** (कर्तव्य) of every citizen. Some rights belong to "every person", including a foreigner. Others belong to "every citizen" only.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Count and growth**

- The Interim Constitution of 2063 had **21** fundamental rights. The present Constitution has **31**.
- Rights that appear as separate Articles for the first time include the rights of **victims of crime** (21), the right to **housing** (37), the rights of **Dalit** (40), of **senior citizens** (41) and of **consumers** (44).
- **Article 47:** the State had to make the laws needed to carry out the rights within **three years** of the Constitution's start. The deadline was 2 Ashoj 2075 (18 September 2018). Parliament passed a set of laws — usually counted as sixteen Acts — just before that date.

**Points inside the rights**

- **Article 16:** every person has the right to live with dignity. **No law may provide for the death penalty.**
- **Article 17:** no one may lose personal liberty except by law. Every citizen has **six freedoms**: (a) opinion and expression; (b) peaceful assembly without arms; (c) forming political parties; (d) forming unions and associations; (e) moving and living in any part of Nepal; (f) any profession, occupation, industry, trade or business.
- **Article 18:** all citizens are equal before the law. No discrimination on grounds such as origin, religion, race, caste, tribe, sex, economic condition, language, region or ideology. **Special provisions** may be made for disadvantaged groups. **Equal pay** for the same work. All children have an **equal right to ancestral property**.
- **Article 19:** no censorship of news and other material.
- **Article 20:** a person who is arrested must be told the reason, may consult a lawyer, and must be brought before a judicial authority within **24 hours**, not counting travel time. No punishment under a law made after the act. A person is presumed innocent until proved guilty. No one is tried twice for the same offence. No one is forced to be a witness against oneself.
- **Article 22:** no torture; the victim has a right to compensation.
- **Article 24:** no untouchability or discrimination in any private or public place.
- **Article 26:** freedom of religion. Converting another person from one religion to another is not allowed.
- **Article 27:** every citizen may demand and receive information on matters of personal or public interest.
- **Article 29:** no trafficking, slavery, servitude or forced labour. The State may still require compulsory service for public purposes.
- **Article 31:** basic education is **compulsory and free**; education up to the **secondary level is free**. Citizens with disabilities and poor citizens have a right to free higher education as law provides.
- **Article 33:** every citizen has the right to employment and to choose employment.
- **Article 35:** **free basic health services**; no one may be denied emergency care.
- **Article 38:** women have equal lineage rights, safe motherhood and reproductive health, and a right to take part in all bodies of the State on the principle of **proportional inclusion**. Spouses have equal rights in property and family affairs.
- **Article 39:** rights of the child, including name and birth registration. No child labour in factories, mines or other dangerous work. No child marriage.
- **Article 40:** rights of Dalit, including free education with scholarship from primary to higher level.
- **Article 42:** right to social justice — the right of disadvantaged groups to take part in State bodies on the principle of **proportional inclusion** (worded so by the first amendment).
- **Article 45:** no citizen may be exiled.

**Remedy**

- **Article 46** gives the right to **constitutional remedy** (संवैधानिक उपचारको हक). A person goes to the **Supreme Court under Article 133** or to a **High Court under Article 144**.
- These courts may issue orders and **five writs**: habeas corpus (बन्दीप्रत्यक्षीकरण), mandamus (परमादेश), certiorari (उत्प्रेषण), prohibition (प्रतिषेध) and quo warranto (अधिकारपृच्छा). Their meanings are in topic 3.8.

**In an emergency**

- During a state of emergency, rights in Part 3 may be suspended (Article 273).
- These **cannot** be suspended: Article 16; the freedoms to form parties and to form unions; Article 18; Articles 20, 21, 22 and 24; Articles 29, 30, 31, 32 and 35; Articles 38 and 39; Articles 41, 42, 43 and 45; parts of Articles 19, 26, 36 and 40; the remedy under Article 46 for these rights; and the writ of **habeas corpus**.

**Duties of citizens (Article 48)**

1. To protect the nationality, sovereignty and integrity of Nepal, being loyal to the nation.
2. To obey the Constitution and the law.
3. To give compulsory service when the State requires it.
4. To protect and preserve public property.

The constitutions of 2047 and 2063 listed no duties.

(3) **Wider power.** The Supreme Court may also declare a law void if it conflicts with the Constitution. (4) **In an emergency.** Even then, the writ of habeas corpus cannot be suspended. **Conclusion.** Article 46 is the guard of all the other rights in Part 3.

## 3.5 Directive principles, policies and obligations of the State (राज्यका निर्देशक सिद्धान्त, नीति तथा दायित्व)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Questions are few but regular: the Part number, the meaning of Article 55, and the difference from fundamental rights. This Part also supplies ready points for answers on economy, foreign policy and good governance in later papers.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Fundamental rights are promises that a court will enforce. **Directive principles** (निर्देशक सिद्धान्त) are different: they are **goals** that guide the State when it makes laws, plans and budgets. **Part 4 (Articles 49 to 55)** sets out the principles, a long list of **policies** (नीति) and the **obligations** (दायित्व) of the State. A citizen **cannot go to court** to demand that they be carried out. Instead, the Government reports each year and a committee of Parliament watches the progress.

#### 3 · Key facts

- **Article 49:** the principles, policies and obligations in Part 4 are the guiding principles for governing the State. The State must find the means to carry them out.
- **Article 50 — directive principles.** Four objectives:
  - **Political:** a **public-welfare** system of governance; rule of law; a strong federal democratic republic; relations among the three levels based on **cooperative federalism**; local autonomy and decentralisation.
  - **Social and cultural:** a civilised and **equal** society, free of discrimination, exploitation and injustice.
  - **Economic:** a **socialism-oriented, independent and prosperous economy**, built through the **public, private and cooperative** sectors, with fair sharing of the gains.
  - **International relations:** based on **sovereign equality**, protecting the country's freedom, sovereignty, territorial integrity, independence and national interest.
- **Article 51 — policies of the State.** They are grouped under **13 heads**, (a) to (m), listed in the table below.
  - Under head (b): public administration is to be fair, competent, impartial, transparent, **free from corruption**, accountable and participatory.
  - Under head (m): an **independent foreign policy** based on the **Charter of the United Nations, non-alignment, the principles of Panchsheel, international law and the norms of world peace**; and review of past treaties on the basis of equality and mutual interest.
- **Article 52 — obligation of the State:** to make Nepal prosperous by protecting fundamental rights and human rights, following the directive principles and carrying out the policies step by step.
- **Article 53:** the Government of Nepal submits an **annual report** on the work done under Part 4 to the **President**. The President has it placed before the Federal Parliament through the Prime Minister.
- **Article 54:** a **committee of the Federal Parliament** monitors and evaluates the progress.
- **Article 55:** **no question may be raised in any court** on whether a matter in Part 4 has been carried out.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Distinguish between fundamental rights and the directive principles of the State.

**Model answer.** Both are found near the start of the Constitution, but they differ in five ways. (1) **Place.** Fundamental rights are in Part 3 (Articles 16 to 48); directive principles, policies and obligations are in Part 4 (Articles 49 to 55). (2) **Nature.** Rights belong to persons and citizens; directive principles are goals set for the State. (3) **Enforcement.** A right can be enforced in the Supreme Court or a High Court under Articles 46, 133 and 144. Under Article 55 no court may be asked whether Part 4 has been carried out. (4) **Supervision.** Rights are guarded by the courts. Part 4 is watched through an annual report to the President (Article 53) and a committee of Parliament (Article 54). (5) **Timing.** Rights apply at once; directive principles are achieved step by step as resources allow. **Link between the two.** They work together: Article 52 makes it an obligation of the State to protect fundamental rights while following the directive principles. Many rights, such as education, health and housing, need the policies of Part 4 to become real.

## 3.6 Executive: President, Vice-President and Council of Ministers (कार्यपालिका: राष्ट्रपति, उपराष्ट्रपति र मन्त्रिपरिषद्)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Ages, terms, "who resigns to whom", "who takes the oath before whom" and the clauses of Article 76 are standard questions. The names of the present office-holders are current-affairs questions and are marked 🔄.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Nepal has a **parliamentary** system. The **President** (राष्ट्रपति) is the **head of state**: a symbol of unity who acts on the advice of the government. The **Prime Minister** (प्रधानमन्त्री) is the **head of government** and leads the **Council of Ministers** (मन्त्रिपरिषद्), which holds the real executive power. The Prime Minister must keep the confidence of the House of Representatives. The **Vice-President** (उपराष्ट्रपति) acts when the President is absent. Parts 6 and 7 of the Constitution (Articles 61 to 82) contain these rules.

#### 3 · Key facts

**President (Articles 61 to 66)**

- The President is the **head of state** and promotes national unity. The main duty is to **obey and protect the Constitution**.
- **Election:** by an **electoral college** of the members of the **Federal Parliament** and of the **Provincial Assemblies**. Their votes carry different weights fixed by federal law.
- **Winning:** a majority of the total votes of the electoral college. If no one gets it, the top two candidates go to a second vote.
- **Term: five years.** A person may be elected President **at most twice**.
- **Qualifications:** qualified to be a member of the Federal Parliament; at least **45 years** old; not disqualified by law.
- **Resignation:** in writing **to the Vice-President**. **Removal:** by impeachment under Article 101.
- **Oath:** before the **Chief Justice**.
- **Working rule (Article 66):** the President acts **on the recommendation and with the consent of the Council of Ministers**, given through the Prime Minister, except where the Constitution names another body.
- Other powers named in the Constitution: summons and prorogues Parliament; authenticates Bills; issues Ordinances; appoints the Prime Minister, judges of the Supreme Court, heads of constitutional bodies, Province Chiefs and ambassadors; is **supreme commander-in-chief of the Nepal Army** (Article 267); declares an emergency (Article 273); grants **pardons** (Article 276); confers **titles, honours and decorations** (Article 277).

**Vice-President (Articles 67 to 70)**

- Performs the President's functions when the President is **absent**.
- Qualifications, election and term are the **same as for the President**.
- **Resigns to the President.** Takes the **oath before the President**.
- **Article 70:** the President and the Vice-President must represent **different sexes or communities**.
- **Article 280:** if both offices fall vacant, the **Speaker of the House of Representatives** performs the President's functions.

**Council of Ministers (Articles 74 to 82)**

- **Article 74:** the form of government is multi-party, competitive, federal, democratic, republican and **parliamentary**, based on pluralism.
- **Article 75:** the **executive power of Nepal lies in the Council of Ministers**. Federal executive work is done in the name of the **Government of Nepal**.
- **Article 76** gives four ways of appointing a Prime Minister — see the table.
- A Prime Minister appointed under clause (2) or (3) must win a **vote of confidence within 30 days**.
- If no Prime Minister can be found, the President, on the Prime Minister's recommendation, **dissolves the House** and fixes an election within **six months** (clause 7).
- **Size:** at most **25 ministers, including the Prime Minister**, chosen on the inclusive principle from the members of the Federal Parliament. "Minister" covers Deputy Prime Minister, Minister, Minister of State and Assistant Minister.
- **Responsibility:** the Prime Minister and ministers are **collectively responsible to the Federal Parliament**. Each minister is also individually responsible to the Prime Minister and to Parliament.
- **Article 77:** the Prime Minister **resigns to the President**. The office also falls vacant if a confidence vote is lost, a no-confidence motion passes, or the Prime Minister stops being a member of the House of Representatives. If the Prime Minister dies, the **senior-most minister** acts.
- **Article 78:** a person who is **not a member** of Parliament may be made a minister but must become a member within **six months**. A person **defeated** in the current House of Representatives election cannot be made a minister during that House's term.
- **Article 80:** the Prime Minister, Deputy Prime Minister and Ministers take the oath before the **President**; Ministers of State and Assistant Ministers before the **Prime Minister**.
- **Article 100:** rules on confidence and no-confidence — see topic 3.7.

**National Security Council (Article 266)**

- Seven members: the **Prime Minister** (chairperson); the Ministers for Defence, Home Affairs, Foreign Affairs and Finance; the Chief Secretary; and the Chief of the Army Staff. The Defence Secretary is member-secretary.
- The President declares the mobilisation of the Army on the recommendation of this Council and the decision of the Council of Ministers.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** How is the Prime Minister of Nepal appointed under Article 76 of the Constitution?

**Model answer.** Nepal follows the parliamentary system, so the Prime Minister must have the support of the House of Representatives. Article 76 gives the steps in order. (2) **Coalition — clause (2).** If no party has a majority, the President appoints a member who can command a majority with the support of two or more parties. (3) **Largest party — clause (3).** If that is not possible within thirty days of the final election result, or the person fails to win confidence, the leader of the largest party is appointed. (4) **Vote of confidence — clause (4).** A Prime Minister appointed under clause (2) or (3) must win a vote of confidence within thirty days. (5) **Any member — clause (5).** If the clause (3) Prime Minister fails, a member who shows a ground for winning confidence is appointed. (6) **Dissolution — clause (7).** If this also fails, the President dissolves the House on the Prime Minister's recommendation and an election is held within six months. **Size and duty.** The Prime Minister then forms a Council of not more than 25 ministers, collectively responsible to the Federal Parliament. **Example.** On 13 Chaitra 2082 Balendra Shah was appointed under clause (1), as leader of a party with a majority of its own.

## 3.7 Federal Parliament and law-making (संघीय संसद र कानून निर्माण)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Numbers rule this topic: 275, 165, 110, 59, 56, 3; ages 25 and 35; terms of five and six years; time limits of 15 days, two months and 60 days. Questions on the Speaker and on the path of a Bill also come from here.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

The law-making body of the federation is the **Federal Parliament** (संघीय संसद). It has two Houses. The **House of Representatives** (प्रतिनिधि सभा) is elected by the people every five years; the government is formed from it and answers to it. The **National Assembly** (राष्ट्रिय सभा) is a permanent House that speaks for the provinces and for experience; it is never dissolved. A proposal for a law is a **Bill** (विधेयक). When both Houses pass it and the President authenticates it, it becomes an **Act** (ऐन). Parts 8, 9 and 10 (Articles 83 to 125) contain these rules.

#### 3 · Key facts

**The two Houses**

- **Article 83:** the Federal Parliament consists of the House of Representatives and the National Assembly.
- **House of Representatives (Article 84): 275 members.**
  - **165** elected by the **first-past-the-post** system, one from each of 165 constituencies.
  - **110** elected by the **proportional** system, with the whole country as one constituency and voters voting for parties.
  - **Term: five years**, unless dissolved earlier. During an emergency a federal Act may extend it by up to one year.
- **National Assembly (Article 86): 59 members. A permanent House.**
  - **56 elected — eight from each province.** Each eight must include at least three women, one Dalit and one person with a disability or from a minority.
  - The voters are the members of the Provincial Assembly, the chairpersons and vice-chairpersons of rural municipalities, and the mayors and deputy mayors of municipalities.
  - **3 nominated by the President** on the recommendation of the Government of Nepal, at least one of them a woman.
  - **Term of a member: six years.** One-third of the members retire **every two years**.
- **Qualifications (Article 87):** a citizen of Nepal; aged **25** for the House of Representatives and **35** for the National Assembly; not convicted of a criminal offence involving moral turpitude; not disqualified by federal law; not holding an office of profit. No one may sit in both Houses.
- **Voting age: 18** (Article 84).
- **Women:** at least **one-third** of the members elected from each party to the Federal Parliament must be women.
- **Losing the seat (Article 89):** resignation; loss of qualification; end of term; **absence from ten meetings in a row** without notice; a notice from the member's party that the member has left it; death.
- A seat that falls vacant in the House is filled again, by the same electoral system, if more than **six months** of the term remain.

**Presiding officers**

- The House of Representatives elects a **Speaker** (सभामुख) and a **Deputy Speaker** (उपसभामुख) within **15 days** of its first meeting. **One of the two must be a woman**, and they must come from **different parties** (Article 91).
- The National Assembly elects a **Chairperson** (अध्यक्ष) and a **Vice-Chairperson** within 15 days of its first meeting. One of the two must be a woman (Article 92).
- The Speaker can be removed by a resolution passed by **two-thirds** of all members of the House.

**Sessions and voting**

- The President calls a session within **30 days** of the final result of a House election. The gap between two sessions may not be more than **six months** (Article 93).
- **Quorum** (गणपूरक संख्या): **one-fourth** of all members of the House concerned (Article 94).
- Decisions are taken by a majority of the members **present and voting**. The person in the chair votes only to break a tie (Article 99).
- The President addresses the first session after an election and a joint sitting at the start of each year's first session (Article 95).

**Control over the government**

- **Vote of confidence** (विश्वासको मत) — Article 100. The Prime Minister may ask for it at any time, and **must** ask within **30 days** if his or her party splits or a coalition partner withdraws. It needs a majority of **all** members of the House of Representatives.
- **Motion of no-confidence** (अविश्वासको प्रस्ताव) — Article 100. It is tabled by **one-fourth** of the members and must **name the next Prime Minister**. It cannot be tabled in the **first two years** after a Prime Minister's appointment, nor within **one year** of a failed motion.
- **Impeachment** (महाभियोग) — Article 101. The motion is moved by **one-fourth** of the members of the House of Representatives.
  - Against the **President or Vice-President:** passed by **two-thirds of both Houses**.
  - Against the **Chief Justice, a judge of the Supreme Court, a member of the Judicial Council, or the head or a member of a constitutional body:** passed by **two-thirds of the House of Representatives**. For these office-holders an **Impeachment Recommendation Committee** of **11** members of the House examines the grounds first.

**How a Bill becomes an Act (Articles 109 to 114)**

1. A Bill may be introduced in **either House**. A **Money Bill** (अर्थ विधेयक) may be introduced **only in the House of Representatives**. Money Bills and Bills on the security bodies are introduced only as **Government Bills**.
2. A Bill passed by one House goes to the other House.
3. **Money Bill:** the National Assembly must send it back within **15 days**, with suggestions if any. The House of Representatives decides.
4. **Other Bills from the House of Representatives:** the National Assembly must return them within **two months**. If it does not, the House may send the Bill to the President by a majority of all its members.
5. The Speaker or the Chairperson certifies the Bill and presents it to the President. A Money Bill is certified by the **Speaker**.
6. The President **authenticates** (प्रमाणीकरण) the Bill within **15 days**. The President may send a Bill back **once**, with a message, within 15 days — but **not a Money Bill**. If both Houses pass it again, the President must authenticate it within 15 days.
7. On authentication the Bill becomes an **Act**.

**Ordinance and budget**

- **Ordinance** (अध्यादेश) — Article 114. When **both Houses are not in session** and action is needed at once, the President may issue an Ordinance on the recommendation of the Council of Ministers. It has the force of an Act. It must be placed before both Houses, and it **lapses 60 days** after both Houses meet unless they pass it.
- **Budget** — Article 119. The Finance Minister presents the yearly estimate of revenue and expenditure to a joint sitting of both Houses on **Jestha 15**.
- **Article 115:** no tax may be raised except by law. **Article 116:** government revenue goes into the **Federal Consolidated Fund** (संघीय सञ्चित कोष).

**Model answer.** Under Article 83 the Federal Parliament has two Houses. ## 3.8 Judiciary (न्यायपालिका)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Expect questions on the number of judges, retirement ages, who appoints whom, the members of the Judicial Council, and the meaning of the writs. The name of the Chief Justice is a current-affairs question 🔄.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Courts settle disputes and protect rights. The Constitution keeps one **unified court system** for the whole country, even though the State is federal. There are **three tiers**: the **Supreme Court** (सर्वोच्च अदालत) at the top, a **High Court** (उच्च अदालत) in each province, and a **District Court** (जिल्ला अदालत) in each district. Judges are chosen through independent bodies, hold office until a fixed age, and can be removed only by a set procedure. That is what makes the judiciary **independent**. Part 11 (Articles 126 to 156) contains these rules; Part 12 deals with the Attorney General.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Supreme Court (Articles 128 to 138)**

- A **court of record** (अभिलेख अदालत). It has the **final authority to interpret the Constitution and the law**. All other courts are under it, and everyone must obey its interpretation.
- **Judges:** the **Chief Justice** (प्रधान न्यायाधीश) and **at most 20** other judges.
- **Appointment:** the **President** appoints the Chief Justice on the recommendation of the **Constitutional Council**, and the other judges on the recommendation of the **Judicial Council**.
- **Chief Justice:** must have served at least **three years** as a judge of the Supreme Court. **Term: six years.**
- **Retirement age: 65.** Removal is by **impeachment** (Article 101).
- If the office of Chief Justice is vacant, the **senior-most judge** acts as Chief Justice.
- A former Chief Justice or judge of the Supreme Court may not practise law, and may not be appointed to a government office except as the Constitution allows (Articles 132 and 135).
- **Powers (Article 133):** to declare a law **void** if it conflicts with the Constitution; to enforce fundamental rights; to settle questions of public interest; to issue the **five writs**; to hear appeals; and to review its own judgments.
- **Constitutional Bench** (संवैधानिक इजलास) — Article 137: the **Chief Justice and four other judges** named by the Chief Justice on the recommendation of the Judicial Council. It hears disputes about powers among the federation, provinces and local levels, election disputes of members of the Federal Parliament and Provincial Assemblies, and questions of a member's disqualification.

**High Courts (Articles 139 to 147)**

- **One High Court in each province** — seven in all. They replaced the sixteen Appellate Courts in 2073 BS.
- The **Chief Judge** (मुख्य न्यायाधीश) and judges are appointed by the **Chief Justice** on the recommendation of the **Judicial Council**.
- **Retirement age: 63.**
- A High Court can enforce fundamental rights and issue writs (Article 144), and it hears appeals from District Courts.

**District Courts (Articles 148 to 151)**

- **One in each district** — 77 in all. Local judicial bodies are under the District Court.
- Judges are appointed by the **Chief Justice** on the recommendation of the **Judicial Council**. **Retirement age: 63.**
- Vacant posts of District Judge are filled **20 per cent** by seniority-based evaluation and **40 per cent** by competition, both among officers of the Judicial Service, and **40 per cent** by open competition among lawyers and others with eight years' experience. The Judicial Service Commission holds the examinations.

**Other courts and bodies**

- **Specialised courts** (Article 152) may be set up by federal law for particular kinds of cases — but **not for one single case**. Examples in use: the Special Court, the Labour Court, the Administrative Court and the Revenue Tribunal.
- At the local level a **Judicial Committee** (न्यायिक समिति) of three members, led by the vice-chairperson or deputy mayor, settles small disputes (Article 217).
- **Judicial Council** (न्याय परिषद्) — Article 153. It recommends the appointment, transfer, discipline and dismissal of judges. **Five members:** the Chief Justice (chairperson); the Federal Minister for Law and Justice; the senior-most judge of the Supreme Court; a jurist named by the President on the Prime Minister's recommendation; and a senior advocate or advocate of at least twenty years' standing, appointed on the recommendation of the Nepal Bar Association. The last two serve **four years**.
- **Judicial Service Commission** (न्याय सेवा आयोग) — Article 154. It advises on the appointment, transfer and promotion of officers of the Federal Judicial Service. **Five members:** the Chief Justice (chairperson); the Federal Minister for Law and Justice; the senior-most judge of the Supreme Court; the Chairperson of the Public Service Commission; and the Attorney General.

**Attorney General (Articles 157 to 161)**

- The **Attorney General** (महान्यायाधिवक्ता) is the **chief legal adviser to the Government of Nepal**.
- Appointed by the **President on the recommendation of the Prime Minister**, and holds office **as long as the Prime Minister wishes**.
- Must be qualified to be a judge of the Supreme Court. Decides finally whether the Government will file a case.
- Each province has a **Chief Attorney** (मुख्य न्यायाधिवक्ता), appointed by the Province Chief on the Chief Minister's recommendation.

**History and present** 🔄

- Before the Supreme Court, the highest court was the **Pradhan Nyayalaya** (प्रधान न्यायालय). The Pradhan Nyayalaya Act, 2008 separated it from the executive. The Act came into force on 26 Baisakh 2009, so **Law Day** (कानून दिवस) is kept on **Baisakh 26**.
- The **Supreme Court** replaced the Pradhan Nyayalaya on **8 Jestha 2013 (21 May 1956)**.
- First Chief Justice: **Hari Prasad Pradhan**. First woman Chief Justice: **Sushila Karki**, from 27 Ashad 2073 (11 July 2016).
- 🔄 **Chief Justice: Dr Manoj Kumar Sharma**, appointed and sworn in on **5 Jestha 2083 (19 May 2026)**. The Constitutional Council recommended him on 24 Baisakh 2083 (7 May 2026) and the Parliamentary Hearing Committee approved the name. He was fourth in seniority among the judges. Most reports count him as the 33rd Chief Justice ⚖️.
- The previous Chief Justice, **Prakash Man Singh Raut**, retired on 17 Chaitra 2082 (31 March 2026). The senior-most judge, **Sapana Pradhan Malla**, acted as Chief Justice in between.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Describe the structure of the courts of Nepal under the Constitution.

**Model answer.** Article 127 provides three tiers of courts in a single, unified system. (1) **Supreme Court.** It is the highest court and a court of record, with final authority to interpret the Constitution and the law. It has the Chief Justice and at most twenty judges. The President appoints the Chief Justice on the recommendation of the Constitutional Council and the other judges on that of the Judicial Council. Judges retire at 65. It can declare laws void, enforce fundamental rights through writs, and hear appeals. A Constitutional Bench of five judges hears disputes among the levels of government and election disputes. (2) **High Courts.** There is one in each province. The Chief Justice appoints the Chief Judge and judges on the recommendation of the Judicial Council. They retire at 63. High Courts hear appeals and can issue writs. (3) **District Courts.** There is one in each district. They are the courts of first hearing for most cases, and local judicial bodies are under them. (4) **Other bodies.** Specialised courts may be set up by federal law; a three-member Judicial Committee works at each local level. (5) **Supporting bodies.** The Judicial Council deals with the appointment and discipline of judges, and the Judicial Service Commission with officers of the Judicial Service. **Conclusion.** Appointment through councils, fixed retirement ages and removal only by set procedure keep the courts independent.

## 3.9 Constitutional bodies (संवैधानिक निकाय)

#### 1 · Why it matters

"How many constitutional bodies?", "Which Part?", "Who recommends the appointments?" and "What is the term?" are standard. As a candidate you will also meet one of these bodies in person — a Public Service Commission.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Some jobs must be done **free from government pressure**: fighting corruption, checking the accounts, choosing civil servants, running elections, protecting human rights. The Constitution itself creates bodies for these jobs, so that an ordinary law cannot abolish or weaken them. These are **constitutional bodies** (संवैधानिक निकाय). **Article 306** names **thirteen** of them, set up in Parts 21 to 27. Their heads and members are appointed in the same way, for a fixed term, and can be removed only by impeachment.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Common rules**

- **Appointment:** by the **President**, on the recommendation of the **Constitutional Council** (संवैधानिक परिषद्), after a **parliamentary hearing** (संसदीय सुनुवाइ).
- **Term: six years** from appointment.
- **Age limit: 65.** The Article on the National Human Rights Commission does not state an age limit.
- **No reappointment.** A member may be made head; the two periods are then counted as one term.
- **Minimum age: 45.** The person must not be a member of a political party at the time of appointment.
- **Removal:** by **impeachment** — a motion by one-fourth and a vote of two-thirds of the House of Representatives (Article 101).
- **Report:** each body sends an **annual report to the President**, who has it placed before the Federal Parliament through the Prime Minister (Article 294).
- Appointments must follow the **inclusive principle** (Article 283).
- For the last four commissions (Articles 261 to 264) qualifications and other details are left to federal law.

**Constitutional Council (Article 284)**

- **Six members:** the **Prime Minister** (chairperson); the Chief Justice; the Speaker of the House of Representatives; the Chairperson of the National Assembly; the Leader of the Opposition in the House of Representatives; and the Deputy Speaker.
- It recommends the **Chief Justice** and the **heads and members of constitutional bodies**.
- When it recommends a Chief Justice, the **Minister for Law and Justice** sits as a member.
- **Parliamentary hearing (Article 292):** a joint committee of **15 members** of both Houses hears the persons proposed for Chief Justice, judge of the Supreme Court, member of the Judicial Council, head or member of a constitutional body, and ambassador.

**The bodies one by one**

1. **Commission for the Investigation of Abuse of Authority** (अख्तियार दुरुपयोग अनुसन्धान आयोग) — Part 21. Chief Commissioner and four Commissioners. It investigates **abuse of authority through corruption** by persons holding public office and files cases.
2. **Auditor General** (महालेखा परीक्षक) — Part 22. A single office-holder. Audits the accounts of all federal and provincial offices, local levels, courts, constitutional bodies and security forces, looking at **regularity, economy, efficiency, effectiveness and propriety**.
3. **Public Service Commission** (लोक सेवा आयोग) — Part 23. Chairperson and four members. It conducts examinations to select candidates for the **civil service**, and written examinations for the Nepal Army, Nepal Police, Armed Police Force and public corporations. At least half its members must have twenty years of government service; a **master's degree** is required. **Article 244** gives each province its own Public Service Commission.
4. **Election Commission** (निर्वाचन आयोग) — Part 24. Chief Election Commissioner and four Commissioners. It **conducts, supervises, directs and controls** the elections of the President, Vice-President, Federal Parliament, Provincial Assemblies and local levels; prepares the voters' list; holds referendums; and registers political parties.

(5) **Accountability.** Each body reports every year to the President, and the report goes to the Federal Parliament. **Conclusion.** These bodies check the misuse of power and protect merit, fair elections, sound accounts and the rights of all groups.

## 3.10 Emergency powers, amendment procedure and amendments so far (संकटकालीन अधिकार, संविधान संशोधन र हालसम्मका संशोधन)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Two Articles — 273 and 274 — give many one-line questions: the grounds of an emergency, "one month", "three months", "two-thirds", "thirty days". The two amendments already made are standard questions. The debate on a new amendment is live current affairs and is marked 🔄.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

A constitution must be firm, but not frozen. Two tools deal with unusual times. First, in a grave crisis the President can declare a **state of emergency** (संकटकाल). The government then gets extra powers and some rights can be suspended — but only for a short time, and only if Parliament agrees by a two-thirds vote. Second, the Constitution can be changed by **amendment** (संशोधन). This also needs two-thirds of both Houses, and a few basic things can never be amended away. So far the Constitution has been amended **twice**.

#### 3 · Key facts

**State of emergency — Article 273 (Part 30)**

- **Who declares:** the **President**.
- **Grounds — six:** **war, external aggression, armed rebellion, extreme economic disarray, natural calamity or epidemic** that causes a grave crisis for the sovereignty or territorial integrity of Nepal or the security of any part of it.
- **Area:** the whole of Nepal or any named part.
- A **province** struck by a natural calamity or an epidemic may **ask the Government of Nepal** to have an emergency declared for the province or part of it.
- **Approval:** the declaration is placed before **both Houses within one month**. If **two-thirds of both Houses** approve, it stays in force for **three months from the date it was issued**. If not approved, it lapses.
- **Extension:** once, for not more than **three months**, again by two-thirds of both Houses.
- If the House of Representatives stands dissolved, the **National Assembly** uses these powers.
- **Effects:** the President may issue the orders needed, and they work as law while the emergency lasts. Fundamental rights may be suspended, **except** the protected list (topic 3.4) and the writ of **habeas corpus**. A federal Act may extend the term of the House of Representatives by up to one year (Article 85).
- A person harmed by an official acting in bad faith during the emergency may claim compensation within **three months** after it ends.
- The President may **withdraw** the declaration at any time.

**Emergencies in earlier periods**

| Date | Event | Constitution then in force |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Poush 2017 (15 December 1960) | King Mahendra used emergency powers, dissolved the elected government and parliament | Constitution of 2015, Article 55 |
| 11 Mangsir 2058 (26 November 2001) | Emergency declared during the armed conflict | Constitution of 2047 |
| 19 Magh 2061 (1 February 2005) | King Gyanendra took power and declared an emergency | Constitution of 2047 |

**Amendment of the Constitution — Article 274 (Part 31)**

1. **Limit.** No amendment may go against the **sovereignty, territorial integrity and independence of Nepal, or the sovereignty vested in the people**. This clause itself **cannot be amended**.
2. **Introduction.** A Bill to amend or repeal any Article may be introduced in **either House** of the Federal Parliament.
3. **Publication.** The Bill is published for the public within **30 days** of its introduction.
4. **Provinces.** If the Bill changes the **borders of a province** or a matter in **Schedule 6** (provincial powers), the Speaker or Chairperson sends it to the Provincial Assemblies for consent within **30 days**.
5. **Consent.** A Provincial Assembly accepts or rejects it by a **majority of all its members** within **three months**. If it sends no answer in time, Parliament may go ahead. If a **majority of the Provincial Assemblies** reject the Bill, it lapses.
6. **Passage.** The Bill must be passed by **at least two-thirds of all members of both Houses**.
7. **Authentication.** The **President** authenticates it within **15 days**. The Constitution stands amended from that date.

**The two amendments made so far**

- **First amendment (2072 BS).**
  - Passed by the Legislature-Parliament on **9 Magh 2072 (23 January 2016)** by **461 votes to 7**. Authenticated on 16 Falgun 2072 (28 February 2016).
  - It changed **Articles 42, 84 and 286**.
  - Article 42: the right of disadvantaged groups to take part in State bodies on the principle of **proportional** inclusion.
  - Articles 84 and 286: the 165 constituencies are to be drawn mainly on the basis of **population**, then geography, with **at least one constituency in every district**.
  - Background: the protests in the Terai-Madhesh after the Constitution was proclaimed.
- **Second amendment (2077 BS).**
  - Passed by the House of Representatives on **31 Jestha 2077 (13 June 2020)** — **258 votes for, none against** — and by the National Assembly on **4 Ashad 2077 (18 June 2020)** — **57 for, none against**. President **Bidya Devi Bhandari** authenticated it the same day.
  - It changed **Schedule 3**: the map inside the **coat of arms** now shows **Limpiyadhura, Lipulekh and Kalapani**, following the new map of Nepal published on 7 Jestha 2077 (20 May 2020).

**Referendum — Article 275**

- If **two-thirds of the members of the Federal Parliament** decide that a matter of national importance should be put to the people, a **referendum** (जनमत संग्रह) is held. The Election Commission conducts it.

**The amendment debate of 2082–2083** 🔄

- On 13 Chaitra 2082 the new government adopted a 100-point reform agenda. One point promised a **discussion paper on amending the Constitution**.
- A task force was formed on **16 Chaitra 2082 (30 March 2026)**, led by **Asim Shah**, political adviser to the Prime Minister.
- It handed its report to the Office of the Prime Minister on **32 Ashad 2083 (16 July 2026)**. News reports say the suggestions touch 245 of the 308 Articles. Ideas under discussion include a directly elected executive, a fully proportional parliament and non-party local elections.
- Several opposition parties left the task force before the report was finished.
- The report had not been made public by September 2026, and a member of the governing party told the House that the paper was meant for public debate, not for an immediate amendment.
- **None of the sources read for this chapter, dated up to September 2026, mentions an amendment Bill being registered or passed. The Constitution is therefore treated here as amended twice.** Check again before your examination.
- The arithmetic: the governing party has **182** seats in the House of Representatives; two-thirds is **184**. In the National Assembly two-thirds is **40** of 59.

#### 7 · Exam traps

- The **President** declares an emergency; a **province** can only **request** it.
- An approved emergency lasts **three months**, counted from the date of **issue**, not from the date of approval.
- Approval needs **two-thirds**, not a simple majority.
- The Constitution of **2072** has been amended **twice**. Do not count the twelve amendments of the Interim Constitution.
- The **second** amendment changed a **Schedule** (3), not an Article.
- The first amendment was passed in **Magh 2072**, only four months after the Constitution.
- Provincial consent is needed **only** for Bills on provincial borders or Schedule 6 — not for every amendment.
- **Clause (1) of Article 274** cannot be amended.
- A **referendum** needs a two-thirds decision of the Federal Parliament; the Government alone cannot call one.
- A task-force report is **not** an amendment. An amendment exists only after two-thirds of both Houses pass a Bill and the President authenticates it.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Explain the procedure for amending the Constitution of Nepal.

**Emergency and amendment — Parts 30 and 31**
- Article 273: six grounds; before Parliament in 1 month; two-thirds; 3 months; one extension.
- Article 274: two-thirds of both Houses; President authenticates in 15 days; sovereignty and territorial integrity cannot be amended away.
- First amendment: 9 Magh 2072 — Articles 42, 84, 286. Second amendment: 4 Ashad 2077 — Schedule 3.

### B. Top 20 facts

1. The Constitution of Nepal has **35 Parts, 308 Articles and 9 Schedules**.
2. It was **passed on 30 Bhadra 2072** and **proclaimed on 3 Ashoj 2072 (20 September 2015)**.
3. It is the **seventh** constitution and the first made by a **Constituent Assembly**.
4. **Article 2** places sovereignty and state authority in the **Nepali people**.
5. **Article 4** calls Nepal a **secular, inclusive, socialism-oriented, federal democratic republican** state.
6. **Nepali in the Devanagari script** is the official language (Article 7); all mother tongues are languages of the nation (Article 6).
7. **Part 3 (Articles 16–48)** gives **31 fundamental rights** and **4 duties**.
8. **Article 16** bars the death penalty; **Article 46** gives the right to constitutional remedy.
9. **Part 4 (Articles 49–55)** holds the directive principles; **Article 55** keeps them out of court.
10. The **President** is elected for **five years**, at most **twice**, and must be at least **45**.
11. **Executive power** lies in the **Council of Ministers**, which may have at most **25** members.
12. **Article 76** gives the ways of appointing a Prime Minister; clause (1) is for a party with its own majority.
13. The **House of Representatives** has **275** members (165 + 110); the **National Assembly** has **59** (56 + 3).
14. A **Money Bill** starts only in the House of Representatives; the President authenticates a Bill within **15 days**.
15. An **Ordinance** lapses **60 days** after both Houses meet, unless they pass it.
16. The **Supreme Court** has the Chief Justice and at most **20** judges; they retire at **65**.
17. Judges of **High Courts and District Courts** are appointed by the **Chief Justice** on the recommendation of the **Judicial Council** and retire at **63**.
18. There are **13 constitutional bodies**; their members serve **six years** and are recommended by the **Constitutional Council**, chaired by the Prime Minister.
19. A state of **emergency** (Article 273) needs approval by **two-thirds of both Houses** and then lasts **three months**.
20. The Constitution has been **amended twice**: in **2072** (Articles 42, 84, 286) and in **2077** (Schedule 3).

### D. Five short-answer questions

**1. Describe the fundamental rights given by the Constitution of Nepal.**
*Points for the answer:* Part 3, Articles 16–46 — 31 rights · civil and political: dignity, freedom, equality, communication, justice, against torture and preventive detention, religion, information, privacy · social and economic: education, health, food, housing, employment, labour, social security, clean environment · group rights: women, children, Dalit, senior citizens, social justice, consumers · remedy: Article 46 with Articles 133 and 144, five writs · Article 47 implementing laws; Article 48 duties · limits: reasonable restrictions by law; suspension in an emergency except the protected list.

**2. Write about the functions and powers of the President of Nepal.**
*Points for the answer:* head of state; protects the Constitution; promotes unity (Article 61) · elected by an electoral college; five-year term; at most two terms · acts on the recommendation of the Council of Ministers (Article 66) · executive: appoints the Prime Minister, ambassadors, Province Chiefs · legislative: summons and prorogues Parliament, addresses it, authenticates Bills, issues Ordinances · judicial: appoints the Chief Justice and judges; grants pardons · military and emergency: supreme commander-in-chief; declares emergency · honours and titles · ceremonial nature of the office in a parliamentary system.

**3. How does a Bill become an Act in the Federal Parliament?**
*Points for the answer:* kinds of Bill — Government and private member's; Money Bill only in the House of Representatives · introduction, general discussion, committee stage, passage · sent to the other House · National Assembly: 15 days for a Money Bill, two months for other Bills · joint sitting for disagreements · certification by the Speaker or Chairperson · authentication by the President within 15 days; one return with a message, except for Money Bills · publication · Ordinance as the exception when Parliament is not sitting.

**4. Explain the provisions on a state of emergency in the Constitution.**
*Points for the answer:* Article 273 · six grounds · declared by the President for the whole or part of the country; a province may request · before both Houses within one month; two-thirds; three months; one extension · National Assembly acts if the House is dissolved · effects: orders with the force of law; suspension of rights except the protected list and habeas corpus; extension of the House's term · safeguards: compensation for bad-faith acts; withdrawal at any time · past experience of 2017, 2058 and 2061 BS.

**5. Describe the inclusive features of the Constitution of Nepal.**
*Points for the answer:* Preamble — proportional, inclusive and participatory principle · Article 4 "inclusive" state · Articles 18, 38, 40 and 42 — special provisions and proportional inclusion · proportional electoral system with a closed list for women, Dalit, indigenous nationalities, Khas Arya, Madhesi, Tharu, Muslim and backward regions · one-third women in Parliament; one woman between Speaker and Deputy Speaker; President and Vice-President from different sex or community · reserved places in the National Assembly and in local executives · seven commissions of Part 27 · Article 283 inclusive appointments; Article 285 inclusive civil service · first amendment added "proportional".

### E. Common mistakes

1. Writing **"33 fundamental rights"**. Part 3 has 33 Articles, but only **31** are rights.
2. Placing the **duties** of citizens in Part 4. They are in **Article 48**, Part 3.
3. Calling Nepal a "socialist" state. Article 4 says **socialism-oriented**.
4. Mixing **languages of the nation** (Article 6) with the **official language** (Article 7).
5. Giving the **National Assembly 60** members. It has **59**.
6. Saying the **President** holds executive power. It lies in the **Council of Ministers**.
7. Sending the President's resignation to the Chief Justice. It goes to the **Vice-President**; the **oath** is before the Chief Justice.
8. Counting the Prime Minister **outside** the limit of 25 ministers.
9. Giving **65** as the retirement age of High Court judges. It is **63**.
10. Making the President appoint High Court and District Court judges. The **Chief Justice** appoints them.
11. Putting the **Attorney General** on the Judicial Council. The Attorney General sits on the **Judicial Service Commission**.
12. Listing the **Language Commission** or the **Attorney General** among the thirteen constitutional bodies.
13. Giving the constitutional bodies a **five-year** term. It is **six** years.
14. Saying an emergency lasts **six months**. It lasts **three months**, with one extension of up to three months.
15. Saying the second amendment changed an Article. It changed **Schedule 3**.
16. Treating a task-force report or a proposal as an **amendment**.
