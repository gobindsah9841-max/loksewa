## 9.1 National politics and governance (राष्ट्रिय राजनीति र शासन)

#### 1 · Why it matters

The change of government in 2082 and the work of the new government in 2083 are the largest events of the period. Questions ask for dates, numbers of seats, names of office-holders and "firsts". Every province can ask them.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

The story has five steps.

1. **Protest.** In Bhadra 2082 the government blocked social-media platforms. Young people, already angry about corruption, came into the streets. This is the **Gen Z movement** (जेनजी आन्दोलन).
2. **Fall.** Police fired on the crowd. The Prime Minister resigned the next day, and many public buildings were burnt.
3. **Interim government.** A former Chief Justice, **Sushila Karki**, became Prime Minister. The House of Representatives was dissolved and an election was called.
4. **Election.** On 21 Falgun 2082 a party only a few years old, the **Rastriya Swatantra Party** (राष्ट्रिय स्वतन्त्र पार्टी), won a majority by itself.
5. **New government.** **Balendra Shah** became Prime Minister. His government cut the number of ministries, made two weekly holidays, issued ordinances and faced the Bhote Koshi flood.

#### 3 · Key facts

**From the movement to the election** 🔄

- **19 Bhadra 2082 (4 September 2025):** the government ordered social-media platforms that had not registered in Nepal to be blocked. ⚖️ Most reports count **26 platforms**; one list counts 24.
- **23 Bhadra 2082 (8 September 2025):** protest in Kathmandu and other cities; police fired; 19 people were reported killed that day. Home Minister Ramesh Lekhak resigned and the block was lifted.
- **24 Bhadra 2082 (9 September 2025):** Prime Minister **K.P. Sharma Oli resigned**. The parliament building, Singha Durbar and other public buildings were set on fire. More than **13,500 prisoners** escaped from jails across the country.
- ⚖️ **Dead:** 72 in the Home Ministry's count of that month; 76 in later counts (Chapter 2). **Injured:** 2,660, by the government's figure of 1 May 2026.
- **27 Bhadra 2082 (12 September 2025):** **Sushila Karki** became interim Prime Minister — the **first woman Prime Minister of Nepal**. The House was dissolved, and the election was fixed for 21 Falgun 2082.
- **5 Ashoj 2082 (21 September 2025):** a three-member **commission of inquiry** was formed. Chair: **Gauri Bahadur Karki**, a former judge of the Special Court. It gave its report on 24 Falgun 2082 (8 March 2026).
- **18 Kartik 2082 (4 November 2025):** 45 of the dead were declared **martyrs**.
- **24 Mangsir 2082 (10 December 2025):** the interim government and representatives of the movement signed a **ten-point agreement**.
- **13 Poush 2082 (28 December 2025):** Balendra Shah, then Mayor of Kathmandu, joined the Rastriya Swatantra Party. On 4 Magh 2082 (18 January 2026) the party put him forward as its candidate for Prime Minister.

**The election of 21 Falgun 2082 (5 March 2026)** 🔄

- 275 seats: 165 by first-past-the-post and 110 by proportional representation.
- More than **915,000 new voters** were registered for it, most of them young.
- **Rastriya Swatantra Party: 182 seats** (125 direct + 57 proportional). It is the first single-party majority since 2056 BS.
- **182 is two short of two-thirds.** Two-thirds of 275 is 184. The party cannot amend the Constitution alone.
- Nepali Congress 38; CPN (UML) 25; Nepali Communist Party 17; Shram Sanskriti Party 7; Rastriya Prajatantra Party 5; others 1. **Six** parties are national parties (Chapter 4).
- In **Jhapa–5** Balendra Shah defeated K.P. Sharma Oli by about 50,000 votes.
- The Election Commission was led by **acting** Chief Election Commissioner Ram Prasad Bhandari.

**The new government** 🔄

- **12 Chaitra 2082 (26 March 2026):** the parliamentary party of the Rastriya Swatantra Party chose Balendra Shah as its leader.
- **13 Chaitra 2082 (27 March 2026):** he was appointed and sworn in under **Article 76 (1)**. News reports called him the youngest elected Prime Minister and the first from the Madhesh. The same day the Council of Ministers approved a **100-point agenda of governance reform** and decided to carry out the report of the Karki commission.
- **14 Chaitra 2082 (28 March 2026):** former Prime Minister **K.P. Sharma Oli** and former Home Minister **Ramesh Lekhak** were arrested. The commission had recommended charges of criminal negligence under sections 181 and 182 of the National Penal Code. Both were **released on 26 Chaitra 2082 (9 April 2026)**, after the Supreme Court set a time limit for the investigation. The later course of the case was not followed for this chapter.
- **16 Chaitra 2082 (30 March 2026):** a **task force on amendment of the Constitution** was formed under **Asim Shah**, political adviser to the Prime Minister. It handed in a discussion paper on **32 Ashad 2083 (16 July 2026)**, built on 44,613 suggestions. No amendment Bill was found up to 11 November 2026.
- **19 Chaitra 2082 (2 April 2026):** the new House met. Speaker **Dol Prasad Aryal** was elected on 22 Chaitra and Deputy Speaker **Ruby Kumari Thakur** on 27 Chaitra.
- **22 Chaitra 2082 (5 April 2026):**
  - **two weekly public holidays**, Saturday and Sunday, were announced, to save fuel during the war in the Gulf;
  - **Dr Narayan Datta Kandel** was appointed Attorney General under Article 157 (2).

**Decisions of 2083** 🔄

- **19 Baisakh 2083 (2 May 2026):** an **ordinance** ended, at one stroke, the appointments made before 26 March 2026 in public bodies — more than 1,200 office-holders in about 110 bodies, as reported.
- **23 Baisakh 2083 (6 May 2026):** an ordinance changed the working of the **Constitutional Council** (संवैधानिक परिषद्): as reported, it can meet with four members and decide with three.
- **Baisakh 2083:** the federal ministries were regrouped — **18 in place of 22** (Chapters 4 and 7).
- **5 Jestha 2083 (19 May 2026):** **Dr Manoj Kumar Sharma** became Chief Justice.
- **15 Jestha 2083 (29 May 2026):** Finance Minister **Dr Swarnim Wagle** presented the budget for 2083/84 (topic 9.2).
- **26 Jestha 2083 (9 June 2026):** the Council of Ministers was reshuffled under Article 76 (9). **Sudhan Gurung**, who had resigned as Home Minister on 9 Baisakh 2083 (22 April 2026), returned to the post. **Mahabir Pun** became Minister for Science, Technology and Innovation.
- **7–12 Ashad 2083 (21–26 June 2026):** the Rastriya Swatantra Party held its **first general convention** in **Chitwan**. Its president is **Rabi Lamichhane**.
- **26 Ashad 2083 (10 July 2026):** **Govinda Bahadur Karki** took office as the **30th Chief Secretary**.
- **14 Shrawan 2083 (30 July 2026):** **Man Bahadur Karki** was appointed Chief Election Commissioner.
- **10 Bhadra 2083 (26 August 2026):** the **Bhote Koshi flood** (topic 9.4).
- **25 Bhadra 2083 (10 September 2026):** Industry Minister Gauri Kumari Yadav resigned; **Dipak Kumar Sah** was sworn in the same day.
- **8 Ashoj 2083 (24 September 2026):** the Prime Minister spoke at the General Assembly of the United Nations (Chapter 8).
- **18 Kartik 2083 (4 November 2026):** the Council of Ministers named **Manoj KC** the **34th Inspector General of Police**. Dan Bahadur Karki, the 33rd, was made a security adviser to the Prime Minister.

**Elections to come** 🔄

- The term of the **local levels** ends on **30 Baisakh 2084 (13 May 2027)**.
- The term of the **Provincial Assemblies** ends on **4 Mangsir 2084 (19 November 2027)**.
- In May 2026 the Election Commission proposed holding both elections on one day. No date was found.

#### 6 · Memory aid

- **"19 – 23 – 24 – 27 Bhadra":** block, firing, resignation, new Prime Minister.
- **"21 Falgun votes, 13 Chaitra oath":** twenty-two days from the election to the new government.
- **"182 + 2 = 184":** the ruling party is two seats short of two-thirds.
- **Three Karkis, three jobs:** Sushila Karki — interim Prime Minister; Gauri Bahadur Karki — inquiry commission; Man Bahadur Karki — Chief Election Commissioner. A fourth, Govinda Bahadur Karki, is Chief Secretary.
- **Two Shahs:** Balendra Shah — Prime Minister; Asim Shah — amendment task force.

#### 7 · Exam traps

- Sushila Karki was an **interim** Prime Minister from outside parliament. Balendra Shah is the Prime Minister **elected** through the House.
- The election was for the **House of Representatives only**. The Provincial Assemblies and local levels were not dissolved; their elections fall in 2084.
- **182 is a majority, not two-thirds.**
- The party president is **Rabi Lamichhane**; the Prime Minister is **Balendra Shah**. Do not mix them.
- **Gen Z Martyrs' Day is 23 Bhadra**, the day of the firing — not 27 Bhadra, the day of the new government.
- The **Chief Election Commissioner** during the election was an **acting** one; Man Bahadur Karki came afterwards.
- An **ordinance** (अध्यादेश) is issued by the President on the recommendation of the Council of Ministers when both Houses are not in session (Article 114). It is not an Act passed by Parliament.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Describe in brief the main political events in Nepal from Bhadra 2082 to Chaitra 2082.

**Model answer.** (1) **Cause:** on 19 Bhadra 2082 the government ordered unregistered social-media platforms to be blocked; anger about corruption was already strong among the young. (2) **Movement:** on 23 Bhadra police fired on protesters in Kathmandu and 19 people were reported killed that day; on 24 Bhadra Prime Minister K.P. Sharma Oli resigned, public buildings were burnt and more than 13,500 prisoners escaped. The dead are counted as 72 or 76. (3) **Interim government:** on 27 Bhadra former Chief Justice Sushila Karki became the first woman Prime Minister; the House was dissolved; a commission under Gauri Bahadur Karki inquired into the events. (5) **New government:** on 13 Chaitra 2082 Balendra Shah became Prime Minister under Article 76 (1), with a 100-point reform agenda. **Result:** a change of government through an election within six months, and a new generation in power.

## 9.2 Economy and development (अर्थतन्त्र र विकास)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Budget size, growth rate, the newest policy and the newest large project are asked as current affairs in every province. Chapter 5 gives the full tables. This topic gives the short list a candidate should carry in the head, and adds the economic news of 2082 and 2083.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Four things shaped the economy in this period.

- **Unrest and a new government.** The events of Bhadra 2082 damaged public buildings and business, and growth stayed below 4 percent.
- **War in the Gulf.** From Falgun 2082 the war on Iran raised oil prices, cut fuel supply and put Nepali workers in the Gulf at risk. The government answered with two weekly holidays.
- **A flood.** The Bhote Koshi flood of Bhadra 2083 destroyed hydropower stations, a customs point and a highway.
- **More time.** The United Nations gave Nepal three more years before it leaves the list of least developed countries.

#### 3 · Key facts

**The economy in figures** 🔄 (detail in Chapter 5)

- **Gross domestic product, 2082/83:** about Rs 6,609 billion. **Growth:** 3.85 percent.
- **Budget for 2083/84:** **Rs 2,124.34 billion**, presented on **15 Jestha 2083 (29 May 2026)** by **Dr Swarnim Wagle**. Current 59.8 percent, capital 20.3 percent, financing 19.9 percent. Growth target **7 percent**.
- **Monetary policy for 2083/84:** announced on 23 Ashad 2083 (7 July 2026); the **25th**; inflation to be kept within **5.5 percent**.
- **Remittance, 2082/83:** **Rs 2,363.13 billion** — more than the federal budget.
- **Trade, 2082/83:** exports Rs 315.29 billion; imports Rs 2,096.38 billion.
- **Tourists, 2025:** **1,158,459**; India sent the most.
- **Public debt:** about Rs 3,009 billion in mid-October 2026.

**Graduation delayed** 🔄

- Nepal was to leave the list of **least developed countries** (अतिकम विकसित मुलुक) on 24 November 2026.
- On **12 Kartik 2083 (29 October 2026)** the General Assembly of the United Nations moved the date to **24 November 2029**.
- Nepal had asked for three more years on 30 Baisakh 2083 (13 May 2026). It named slow growth, risks to remittance and tourism, climate shocks and the loss of trade preferences. The same resolution gives Bangladesh the same new date.

**The "grey list"** 🔄

- The **Financial Action Task Force** (FATF) is the world body that sets standards against money laundering (सम्पत्ति शुद्धीकरण) and the financing of terrorism. It was set up in 1989; its office is in Paris.
- In **February 2025** it put Nepal on its **grey list** — the list of countries "under increased monitoring" — for the **second time**. Nepal had been on the list from 2008 to 2014.
- Nepal was given about two years to correct the weak points: action against hundi, and control of risky cooperatives, casinos and dealers in land and gold.
- Nepal **stayed on the list** after the reviews of **June 2026** and **October 2026**. After October 2026 the list had 18 countries.
- On 27 Jestha 2082 (10 June 2025) the **European Union** also placed Nepal on its own list of high-risk countries.

**Effects of the war in the Gulf** 🔄

- The war began on 16 Falgun 2082 (28 February 2026). The Strait of Hormuz, through which much of the world's oil passes, was closed.
- On 17 Falgun 2082 the government stopped labour approvals for the Gulf; they were reopened in part after about 17 days (Chapter 8).
- On **22 Chaitra 2082 (5 April 2026)** it announced **two weekly holidays** to save fuel.
- Prices rose: inflation, 2.13 percent on average in the first eight months of 2082/83, stood at 5.08 percent in mid-July 2026.
- Remittance did not fall in 2082/83; it rose by 37.1 percent.

**Cost of the Bhote Koshi flood** 🔄 (detail in Chapter 7)

- Loss: about **Rs 408 billion**. Need for rebuilding: about **Rs 723 billion**.
- Damaged or destroyed: 13 hydropower projects of 759 megawatts, the Rasuwagadhi customs point, the road from Betrawati to the border, and about 7,570 houses.
- The Rasuwagadhi route is one of the two main trade routes with China.

**Projects and cases in the news** 🔄

- **Nagdhunga–Sisnekhola tunnel:** opened on **11 Shrawan 2083 (27 July 2026)**; 2.688 km; built with a loan from Japan; a toll is charged since Kartik 2083.
- **Millennium Challenge Corporation compact:** work on the transmission line began with a ground-breaking in June 2026 (Chapter 8).
- **Pokhara International Airport:** the Commission for the Investigation of Abuse of Authority has filed **three corruption cases** in the Special Court about its construction.
  - The third, on 8 Chaitra 2082 (22 March 2026), names 21 persons and a loss of about Rs 461.6 million.
  - A case of 24 Baisakh 2083 (7 May 2026) on tax exemptions claims a loss of **Rs 3.62 billion** and names a former Finance Minister.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** What is the "grey list" of the Financial Action Task Force? Why does it matter for Nepal?

**Model answer.** The **Financial Action Task Force**, set up in 1989 with its office in Paris, sets world standards against money laundering and the financing of terrorism. Its **grey list** names countries whose systems have serious weak points and which are placed "under increased monitoring" while they carry out an agreed action plan. **Nepal** was on the list from 2008 to 2014 and was put on it again in **February 2025**; it stayed there after the reviews of June and October 2026. **Why it matters:** (1) foreign banks check payments to and from Nepal more strictly, so trade and remittance become slower and dearer; (2) foreign investment and loans may fall or cost more; (3) the country's image suffers; (4) failure to improve can lead to the black list. **What Nepal must do:** act against hundi and illegal transfers, supervise risky cooperatives, casinos and dealers in land and gold, investigate and prosecute money laundering, and confiscate the proceeds of crime.

## 9.7 Province-wise events (प्रदेशगत घटना)

#### 1 · Why it matters

A provincial commission likes to ask about its own province: the Chief Minister, the budget, a disaster, a new law, a sports event. A candidate should know the news of the home province well and one or two facts about each of the other six. The province chapters (Chapters 10–16) give the full picture; this topic gives the news.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Three things happened in the provinces in this period.

- **Provincial governments did not change with the federal one.** The Provincial Assemblies were elected in 2079, so no Chief Minister comes from the party that now rules at the centre. Three provinces still changed their Chief Minister in Shrawan–Bhadra 2083.
- **Budgets.** All seven presented budgets on 1 Ashad 2083.
- **Shocks.** The Bhote Koshi flood struck Bagmati; communal violence began in Koshi and spread into Madhesh; an earthquake damaged Upper Mustang in Gandaki.

#### 3 · Key facts

**All seven provinces at once** 🔄

- **Chief Ministers by party** (Chapter 4): CPN (UML) — Koshi, Bagmati, Lumbini; Nepali Congress — Madhesh, Gandaki; Nepali Communist Party — Karnali, Sudurpashchim.

**Koshi** 🔄

### D. Five short-answer questions

**1. What were the causes and the results of the Gen Z movement of 2082?**
*Points for the answer:* causes — the order of 19 Bhadra 2082 to block social-media platforms; anger about corruption, already strong among the young · course — protest and police firing on 23 Bhadra; resignation of the Prime Minister on 24 Bhadra; burning of the parliament building, Singha Durbar and other public buildings; escape of more than 13,500 prisoners · human cost — 72 or 76 dead ⚖️; 2,660 injured; 45 declared martyrs · political results — interim government under Sushila Karki; House dissolved; election of 21 Falgun 2082; majority for the Rastriya Swatantra Party; Balendra Shah Prime Minister · other results — the Karki commission and action on its report; the ten-point agreement; Gen Z Martyrs' Day on 23 Bhadra; a task force on amending the Constitution · assessment — a change of government through an election within six months, at a heavy cost in lives and public property.

**2. Nepal's graduation from the list of least developed countries was moved from 2026 to 2029. Why? What should Nepal do in the three years?**
*Points for the answer:* the fact — decision of the General Assembly on 12 Kartik 2083 (29 October 2026); new date 24 November 2029; the same date for Bangladesh · why — Nepal's request of 30 Baisakh 2083 named slow growth, risks to remittance and tourism, climate shocks and the loss of trade preferences; the unrest of 2082, the war in the Gulf and the flood of 2083 made the case stronger · what graduation changes — special treatment in trade and aid ends after a time · what to do — raise exports and their quality; seek new trade arrangements with the main markets; bring in investment; build energy and transport; leave the grey list; raise revenue; prepare a transition plan with the development partners · conclusion — the delay gives time, not a solution.

**3. Describe the Bhote Koshi flood of 2083 and the lessons it gives for disaster management.**
*Points for the answer:* the event — 10 Bhadra 2083 (26 August 2026); ice and rock fell above the Lhende valley near Rasuwagadhi; the flood ran down the Bhote Koshi and the Trishuli through Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading · loss — more than 1,450 dead and about 5,285 missing; about 7,570 houses; 13 hydropower projects of 759 megawatts; the customs point and the road to the border; about Rs 408 billion; about Rs 723 billion needed to rebuild · place among disasters — the worst since the earthquake of 2072; the second flood at Rasuwagadhi in two years · lessons — watch glaciers, lakes and slopes on both sides of the border and share data with China; early-warning systems along the rivers; safe siting of settlements, roads and power stations; a ready fund and insurance; trained local levels; rebuilding to a safer standard · conclusion — mountain hazards grow with a warming climate; being ready costs less than relief.

### E. Common mistakes

1. Calling **Sushila Karki** an elected Prime Minister, or **Balendra Shah** an interim one. She led the **interim** government; he was appointed through the **House** under Article 76 (1).
2. Saying the Rastriya Swatantra Party has **two-thirds**. It has **182**; two-thirds is **184**.
3. Naming **Rabi Lamichhane** as Prime Minister. He is the **party president**.
4. Giving **27 Bhadra** as Gen Z Martyrs' Day. It is **23 Bhadra**, the day of the firing.
5. Thinking the **Provincial Assemblies** were elected in 2082. They were elected in **2079**, and **no Chief Minister** is from the Rastriya Swatantra Party.
6. Mixing the two budgets: **Rs 2,124.34 billion** is for **2083/84**; Rs 1,964.11 billion was for 2082/83.
7. Saying Nepal has **left the grey list**, or calling it the **black list**.
8. Saying Nepal **graduated in 2026**, or that graduation was cancelled. It was **moved to 2029**.
9. Exchanging **COP30 (Belém, 2025)** and **COP31 (Antalya, 2026)**, or writing that the G20 summit of 2026 has already met.
10. Saying **Artemis II landed** on the Moon. It flew **round** it.
11. Giving the tiger number as **355**. That is the count of 2022; the count of 2026 is **429**.
12. Placing the **Bhote Koshi flood of 2083** in Sindhupalchok, or saying the **Mustang earthquake** killed many people. The flood was in **Rasuwa**; the earthquake killed **no one**.
13. Naming **Argentina** as winner of the football World Cup of 2026, or mixing the **Golden Boot** (most goals) with the **Golden Ball** (best player).
14. Mixing the **Booker Prize** with the **International Booker Prize**, or placing the Nobel **Peace** Prize in Stockholm. It is given in **Oslo**.
15. Giving the hosts of **2025** for **2026**: World Environment Day — the Republic of Korea (2025), **Azerbaijan** (2026); World Tourism Day — Malaysia (2025), **El Salvador** (2026). The International Year of **Cooperatives** was 2025.
16. Quoting an office-holder, a number or a "latest" event **without its date**. In current affairs the date is half the answer.
