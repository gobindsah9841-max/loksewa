## 1. Start here

1. Read **Part 2** and find your province. The seven provinces do **not** share one pattern.
2. Read **Part 3** for the marks arithmetic, the study plan and the examination-hall method.
3. Study the chapters in the order of your province's priorities (Part 2, section 2.11).
4. Download the syllabus from your own commission's website and compare it with Part 2. This book is a study aid; the commission's document is the authority.

**Three facts to know before anything else**

- Every province: 50 questions × 2 marks, 45 minutes, **0.4 mark deducted for each wrong answer**.
- General Knowledge : Mental Ability is **30 : 20** in Madhesh, Gandaki, Lumbini, Karnali and Sudurpashchim, **35 : 15** in Koshi and **40 : 10** in Bagmati.
- In **Lumbini** and **Karnali** the objective test is only a screening test. Its marks are not added to the final result.

**No verified past questions.** No official question paper or answer key of any provincial commission could be opened during this research. This book therefore contains **no** question presented as a past question. Websites and applications that publish "previous year questions" without the paper, set and year were not used.

**Two things in this book are the author's reconstruction, not your original wording.** Tell me if they differ from what you asked for, and they will be changed everywhere:

1. the **ten-part layout** of each topic (section 4);
2. the name of the third label, **`[Expected – Practice]`**.

**How facts were checked.** Each chapter ends with a table that separates (a) facts checked against a source during the research, (b) standard reference facts that were cross-checked by arithmetic but not re-opened on an official site, and (c) disputed facts. Government websites could be read only partly, so several current figures rest on press reports that quote the responsible office. These are marked 🔄.

**Dates.** Bikram Sambat and Common Era date pairs after 1975 BS were computed with a calendar-conversion library, not from memory. The library does not cover earlier years, so dates before 1975 BS (mostly in Chapter 2) are standard textbook dates.

**The Constitution.** Chapter 3 was checked Article by Article against public copies of the text — an English translation and the Nepali text. The Nepal Law Commission's own website could not be opened, so the copies are not the official file. Where the two copies differ, the Nepali text is followed and the difference is stated in the chapter.

**Acts and office-holders.** Chapter 4 names many Acts — on local government, fiscal arrangement, good governance, the right to information and the civil service. These Acts were **not** read in full. Their provisions were taken from extracts of the Nepal Law Commission's pages shown in search results and from secondary articles, and a few are standard reference facts that were not re-opened; the table at the end of the chapter says which is which. Names of Chief Ministers, Province Chiefs and heads of commissions come from news reports and encyclopaedia pages, not from the offices' own websites, and they change often.

**Economic figures.** Chapter 5 is full of yearly figures: GDP, growth, trade, remittance, the budget, revenue, debt and interest rates. They were read in news reports and study websites that quote the Economic Survey 2082/83, Nepal Rastra Bank, the Department of Customs and other offices. The official documents were **not** opened. A few reports disagree with one another; the table at the end of the chapter names each case and says which figure was used. Every such figure will be out of date within a year, so it carries the 🔄 mark and its fiscal year.

**Society and culture.** Chapter 6 rests on two kinds of fact. Census figures (population, castes, languages, religions, literacy) are the final results of the Census of 2078, but they were read in news reports and encyclopaedia pages, not in the National Statistics Office's own tables; the province-wise shares in particular are rounded and second-hand. Festivals, dances, instruments, writers, temples and social laws are standard reference facts that were written from general knowledge and not re-opened in an official source this time. The table at the end of the chapter says which is which. One topic, 6.10 Education, was added to the original plan.

**Science, technology and environment.** Chapter 7 mixes two kinds of fact. School science — units, inventors, vitamins, germs, the cell, the six everyday materials, the years of world treaties — is standard reference matter that was written from general knowledge and not re-opened fact by fact. Recent facts — health indicators, cybercrime figures, the tiger count of July 2026, the climate conferences and Nepal's pledges, the Carbon Trade Regulations of 2082, the new names of the ministries — were read in news reports and are marked 🔄. The figures of the **Bhote Koshi flood of 10 Bhadra 2083** are provisional: the count of dead and missing was still changing, and the cause was still being studied, when the chapter was written. The table at the end of the chapter also lists a group of Nepal facts that were written from memory of standard references and should be checked before anyone relies on a single one of them.

**International relations.** Chapter 8 also mixes two kinds of fact. Fixed facts — the founding dates and seats of world bodies, the Charter, the lists of Secretaries-General and summits, treaty dates and signers, the years in which Nepal joined each body — are standard reference matter that was written from general knowledge and not re-opened one by one. Moving facts — office-holders, the number of countries with diplomatic relations (188), Nepal's seats in United Nations bodies, the latest summits, the visits and notes of 2026 — were read in press reports, United Nations news and pages of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, and are marked 🔄. **Six matters were still open on 11 November 2026** and are listed at the head of the chapter: the next Secretary-General of the United Nations had not been chosen; the Prime Minister's visit to India (planned for 22–25 November) had not taken place; the 16th Secretary-General of SAARC had been appointed but not reported in office; the 49th ASEAN Summit and COP31 were not over; and the war in the Gulf was not settled. Anything the chapter says about these is a statement of that day only.

**Current affairs.** Chapter 9 is made only of moving facts, from May 2025 to 11 November 2026. Most of them rest on one or two press reports or on an encyclopaedia year-page, not on a gazette or an official notice; where reports disagree the chapter says so. Some matters were still open on the day of writing — the Prime Minister's visit to India, the next Secretary-General of the United Nations, COP31, the G20 summit, the war on Iran — and the chapter lists them at its head. It grows old faster than any other chapter: use **UPDATE**, and read a daily newspaper in the last weeks.

**Province chapters.** Chapter 10 (Koshi) is the first of seven. It keeps every figure that Chapters 1 to 9 already give and adds the facts of the province itself. These new facts come mostly from encyclopaedia pages, the provincial government's portal and press reports. The plan document, the budget speech, the census tables and the provincial gazette were **not** read; the planning commission's page shows the plan only in a viewer. District populations and many smaller facts — home districts of peoples, holy places, products, persons — were written from standard references and not re-opened; section F of the chapter lists them. Three matters were open on the day of writing, and the chapter says so: no new Province Chief had been reported after 24 Kartik 2083; the chair of the Province Public Service Commission was vacant; and the press report read does not say where about 14 percent of the budget of 2083/84 goes. Chapter 11 (Madhesh) follows the same method. Its special limits are these: the unit titles of the Madhesh syllabus could not be read, so the chapter cannot quote the wording it serves; the plan file, the budget speech and the provincial portal could not be opened, so the plan's number targets rest on third-party notes; the chapter was written two days before a vote of confidence whose result it cannot give; and a number of historical and local facts were written from memory or taken from general-knowledge books — section F of the chapter lists both groups.

**Weightage.** "Priority" reflects the official number of questions per unit. It is not a count of past papers, and no cut-off marks are quoted.

## 8. Relation to the application in this repository

The study book is plain Markdown in this folder. **No file of the application (`src/`) was changed.** Two points were noticed in the application's seed data while reading the repository; they are reported here and were left untouched:

- the seeded sample attempts are scored at **1 mark** per correct answer with **0.2** deducted per wrong answer. The 20% ratio agrees with the syllabi, but the official paper gives **2 marks** per question and deducts **0.4**, so the application's scores are on half the official scale;
- some seeded questions are flagged as previous-year questions and name an examination and a year, but no source is given against which this can be checked. One of them states the lowest point of Nepal as a settled fact, although sources disagree (Chapter 1, topic 1.7).

The book can be connected to the application later (a reader page, or the MCQs imported as question seeds). This has not been done.

Separately from that application, the book is published in two forms that are made from these Markdown files and change nothing in `src/`: an Android app (folder `android-app`) and a web app (folder `web-app`, built into the folder `docs` for GitHub Pages).
