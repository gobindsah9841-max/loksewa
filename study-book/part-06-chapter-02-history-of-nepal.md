## 2.1 Sources of history and the ancient period (इतिहासका स्रोत र प्राचीन काल)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Questions on the oldest dynasties, the first inscriptions and the life of the Buddha are short and factual. They also link with the culture unit (Lumbini, Janakpur) and the province chapters.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

We know the past from **sources**: things written on stone or metal, coins, old buildings, old books and the reports of foreign visitors. For the earliest period of the Kathmandu Valley there are no dated records. Its story comes from chronicles (वंशावली) written many centuries later, so it is part history and part tradition. Dated history begins with the Lichhavi inscriptions.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Sources**

- **Inscriptions (अभिलेख)** on stone and copper are the most reliable source, because they were made at the time of the event.
- The **oldest inscription found in Nepal** is the **Ashoka pillar at Lumbini (249 BC)**. It is in the **Brahmi** script and the Prakrit language. It says that the Buddha was born there and that the village's tax was reduced.
- The **oldest dated inscription of the Kathmandu Valley** is the pillar of King **Manadeva at Changu Narayan (464 AD)**.
- **Coins:** the **Mananka** of Manadeva is regarded as the first coin of Nepal.
- **Chronicles:** the **Gopal Raj Vamshavali**, written in the time of Jayasthiti Malla, is the oldest surviving chronicle.
- **Foreign accounts:** Kautilya's *Arthashastra* mentions the woollen blankets of Nepal. The Allahabad pillar inscription of Samudragupta names Nepal as a frontier state. The Chinese pilgrims **Faxian** (5th century) and **Xuanzang** (7th century) described Lumbini and Kapilvastu.

**Tradition about the Kathmandu Valley**

- The Valley is said to have been a lake. In Buddhist tradition **Manjushri** cut the hill at **Chobhar** and drained it.
- The name "Nepal" is linked by tradition with a sage called **Ne Muni**.

**The early dynasties of the Valley**

- **Gopal dynasty** — cow-herders; the first rulers in the chronicles; 8 kings; first king **Bhuktaman**.
- **Mahispal dynasty** — buffalo-herders; 3 kings.
- **Kirat dynasty** — first king **Yalambar**, last king **Gasti**. Most chronicles count **29 kings**; the Gopal Raj Vamshavali counts 32 ⚖️.
- Tradition says **Gautam Buddha** visited the Valley in the time of the 7th Kirat king, **Jitedasti**, and Emperor **Ashoka** in the time of the 14th, **Sthunko**. Neither visit is proved by a record.
- **Soma dynasty** — 5 kings, from Nimisha to Bhaskar Varma. The Lichhavis followed.

**Outside the Valley**

- **Videha (Mithila)** — capital **Janakpur**; King Janak; birthplace of Sita.
- **Shakya state of Kapilvastu** — capital at **Tilaurakot**; King Suddhodhana, father of the Buddha.
- **Koliya state** — Devdaha and Ramgram; the family of the Buddha's mother, Mayadevi.

**Gautam Buddha**

- Born at **Lumbini** as Siddhartha Gautam, son of Suddhodhana and Mayadevi.
- ⚖️ **Year of birth:** most textbooks give **563 BC**. The World Heritage listing of Lumbini gives **623 BC**.
- Enlightenment at Bodh Gaya; first sermon at Sarnath; death (Mahaparinirvana) at Kushinagar at the age of 80.
- **Ashoka** visited Lumbini in **249 BC**, the 20th year of his reign, and set up the pillar.
- The Lumbini pillar was rediscovered in **1896** by **Khadga Shamsher** and the German archaeologist **Dr Alois Führer**.
- Lumbini was listed as a **World Heritage Site in 1997**.

#### 7 · Exam traps

- **"Oldest inscription in Nepal"** = Ashoka pillar, Lumbini. **"Oldest dated inscription of the Kathmandu Valley"** = Changu Narayan. Read the wording.
- The visits of the Buddha and Ashoka to the Kathmandu Valley are **tradition**, not recorded history.
- **Lumbini is in Rupandehi**; **Tilaurakot is in Kapilvastu**; **Ramgram is in Nawalparasi West**.
- The number of Kirat kings is given as 29 or 32 ⚖️.
- The Buddha's birth year has two versions ⚖️. If only one of them is among the options, choose it.
- Lumbini joined the World Heritage List in **1997**, not 1979. The year 1979 belongs to the Kathmandu Valley and Sagarmatha National Park.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** What are the main sources of the history of ancient Nepal? Why are inscriptions considered the most reliable?

**Model answer.** The sources are of two kinds. **Archaeological sources** are inscriptions on stone and copper, coins, monuments and the finds of excavations at places such as Lumbini and Tilaurakot. **Literary sources** are chronicles such as the Gopal Raj Vamshavali, religious texts, and the accounts of foreigners such as Faxian, Xuanzang and Kautilya. **Inscriptions are the most reliable** because (1) they were written at the time of the event, not centuries later; (2) they usually carry a date and the name of the ruler; (3) they are fixed on stone or metal and cannot easily be changed; (4) they record real acts such as grants, taxes and victories. Chronicles mix history with legend, so they are used only where inscriptions and coins support them.

## 2.2 Lichhavi period (लिच्छविकाल)

#### 1 · Why it matters

This is the first period with dated records, and it is called the golden age of Nepal's history. Rulers, their palaces, coins and titles are favourite matching questions.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

The **Lichhavis** came to the Kathmandu Valley from **Vaishali**, in what is now Bihar. They ruled from about the 4th century AD to the 9th century. Under them Nepal had an organised government, coins, trade with Tibet and India, fine stone sculpture, and peace among Hindus and Buddhists. Their inscriptions are in the **Sanskrit** language.

#### 3 · Key facts

- **Manadeva** (ruled about 464–505 AD) is the first Lichhavi king proved by an inscription.
  - Inscription: **Changu Narayan pillar, 464 AD**. It praises his victories and his mother, Rajyavati.
  - Coin: **Mananka**. Palace: **Managriha**. Monastery: **Mana Vihar**.
- **Amshuverma** (about 605–621 AD) was not a Lichhavi by family. He rose as a high noble (*Mahasamanta*) under King Shivadeva I and then ruled in his own name.
  - Palace: **Kailashkut Bhawan**.
  - Book: ***Shabda Vidya***, a work on grammar.
  - The Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang praised his learning.
  - **Bhrikuti**, who married the Tibetan king **Srong-tsen Gampo** and helped to spread Buddhism in Tibet, is traditionally described as his daughter ⚖️. Some historians call her the daughter of King Udayadeva. In Tibet she is honoured as the Green Tara.
- After Amshuverma, the **Abhira Guptas** (Jishnugupta, Vishnugupta) held real power while a Lichhavi king kept the throne. This is called **dual rule** (द्वैध शासन).
- **Narendradeva** won back full Lichhavi rule with Tibetan help. Palace: **Bhadradhivas**. In his time the road between Tibet and India through Nepal became busy, and Chinese envoys visited the court.
- **Jayadeva II** set up the Pashupati inscription (733 AD), which lists the Lichhavi kings.
- **Nepal Sambat** began in **879 AD**, in the reign of **Raghavadeva**. Tradition links it with the merchant **Shankhadhar Sakhwa**, who is said to have paid off the debts of the people. This date is used to mark the end of the ancient period.

**Government and society**

- Local councils were called **Panchali**. Groups that looked after temples, water and festivals were called **Gosthi** — the root of today's *guthi*.
- **Three taxes:** *bhaga* (on farm produce), *bhoga* (on animals), *kara* (on trade).
- Unpaid compulsory labour was called *vishti*.
- Shaivism, Vaishnavism and Buddhism lived side by side.
- Nepal traded with Tibet and India in wool, musk, herbs and metal goods.

**Art**

- **Changu Narayan** is regarded as the oldest temple of the Valley.
- Famous stone images: **Vishnu Vikranta** (467 AD), the reclining Vishnu of **Budhanilkantha**, and **Palanchok Bhagawati**.

#### 7 · Exam traps

- **Amshuverma was not a Lichhavi** by birth, although he is the best-known ruler of the period.
- The **Changu Narayan inscription is Manadeva's**, not Amshuverma's.
- **Araniko belongs to the Malla period**, not the Lichhavi period.
- **Bhrikuti's father** is given as Amshuverma in most textbooks and as Udayadeva by some historians ⚖️.
- **Nepal Sambat began under Raghavadeva**; Shankhadhar Sakhwa is the merchant linked with it, not a king.
- "Golden age" (स्वर्णयुग) refers to the **Lichhavi period**. Do not give it to the Malla period, which is known for art and architecture.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Why is the Lichhavi period called the golden age of Nepal's history?

**Model answer.** The Lichhavi period is called the golden age for five reasons. **(1) Government:** there was an organised administration with local councils (Panchali), fixed taxes (bhaga, bhoga, kara) and written royal orders. **(2) Economy:** coins such as the Mananka were issued, and trade with Tibet and India made the country rich. **(3) Art:** stone sculpture reached a very high level, as in the Vishnu Vikranta image and the Budhanilkantha Vishnu; Changu Narayan temple dates from this time. **(4) Learning:** Sanskrit was used for inscriptions, and rulers such as Amshuverma were scholars. **(5) Religious harmony and foreign relations:** Hindus and Buddhists lived in peace, and Nepal built friendly ties with Tibet and China, shown by the marriage of Bhrikuti. Because the country made progress in every field at once, historians call it a golden age.

## 2.3 Medieval period (मध्यकाल)

#### 1 · Why it matters

The Malla kings left the temples, palaces and festivals that are still Nepal's best-known heritage. The Karnali syllabus names the **Khas kingdom** and the **Sinja civilisation**; the Lumbini syllabus names the **Malla period**. The Karnat dynasty of **Simraungadh** matters for Madhesh.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

In the medieval period Nepal was not one country. The **Malla** kings ruled the Kathmandu Valley, which split into three kingdoms after 1482. In the west the **Khas** kingdom of Sinja was large and strong, and later broke into the **Baise** (twenty-two) states. The **Chaubise** (twenty-four) states lay in the Gandaki region. In the southern plain the **Karnat** dynasty ruled from Simraungadh, and later the **Sen** kings held Palpa, Makwanpur and the east. One of the Chaubise states, **Gorkha**, would unite them all.

#### 3 · Key facts

**The Malla kings of the Kathmandu Valley**

- **Ari Malla** (about 1200 AD) was the first Malla king.
- **Araniko** (1245–1306) led a team of Nepali artists to Tibet and then to the court of **Kublai Khan** in China. He built the **White Stupa in Beijing**. He is a national hero (राष्ट्रिय विभूति).
- **Shamsuddin Ilyas**, Sultan of Bengal, raided the Valley in **1349** and damaged Pashupati and Swayambhu.
- **Jayasthiti Malla** (ruled 1382–1395) was the great reformer:
  - divided society into **four classes and 64 castes** by occupation;
  - fixed **weights and measures** (*mana*, *pathi*);
  - graded **land into four classes** (*abal, doyam, sim, chahar*);
  - made written rules on houses, dress and crime.
- **Yaksha Malla** (ruled 1428–1482) stretched the kingdom to its widest limits. After his death in **1482** it broke into **Kantipur** (Kathmandu), **Lalitpur** (Patan) and **Bhaktapur** (Bhadgaon).
- **Kantipur:** Ratna Malla (first king of separate Kantipur) · **Mahendra Malla** (Taleju temple, 1564; the silver coin *Mahendramalli*) · **Pratap Malla** (ruled 1641–1674; poet with the title *Kavindra*; **Rani Pokhari**, 1670; the Hanuman image at the palace gate, which gave the name Hanuman Dhoka) · **Jaya Prakash Malla** (last king; the Kumari House, 1757).
- **Lalitpur:** **Siddhi Narsingh Malla** (the stone **Krishna Mandir**, 1637) · Yog Narendra Malla · **Tej Narsingh Malla** (last king).
- **Bhaktapur:** **Bhupatindra Malla** (the five-storey **Nyatapola** temple, 1702; the Palace of 55 Windows) · **Ranajit Malla** (last king; the Golden Gate).
- The Malla period is known for the pagoda style, wood-carving, metalwork, festivals (*jatra*) and literature in Newar (Nepal Bhasa) and Maithili.

**The Khas kingdom and the Sinja civilisation**

- Founded by **Nagaraj** in about the 12th century. Capital: **Sinja**, in today's **Jumla** district. Winter capital: **Dullu**, in **Dailekh**.
- At its height it stretched over western Nepal, Kumaon and Garhwal, and parts of western Tibet.
- **Krachalla** conquered Kumaon (inscription of 1223 AD).
- **Jitari Malla** attacked the Kathmandu Valley in the 1280s.
- **Ripu Malla** visited Lumbini in **1312**; his name is cut on the Ashoka pillar.
- **Prithvi Malla** set up the **Dullu pillar inscription (1357 AD)**, the main record of the dynasty.
- The **Sinja valley is regarded as the birthplace of the Nepali (Khas) language**. The oldest inscriptions in early Nepali come from the Dullu and Sinja area.
- After the late 14th century the kingdom broke into the **Baise states**. **Jumla** was the strongest.
- Sinja is on Nepal's tentative list for World Heritage.

**The Karnat dynasty of Simraungadh**

- Founded by **Nanyadeva** in **1097 AD**. Capital: **Simraungadh**, in today's **Bara** district. The kingdom is also called Tirhut or Mithila.
- The last king, **Harisimhadeva**, fled to the hills in **1324** when **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq** of Delhi attacked. Tradition says his family carried the goddess **Taleju Bhawani** to Bhaktapur.
- The dynasty is remembered for Maithili learning, music and law.

**Sen, Baise, Chaubise and Gorkha**

- **Mukunda Sen** (16th century) made **Palpa** a large kingdom. It later divided; Sen kings ruled **Makwanpur**, **Chaudandi** and **Bijayapur** in the east.
- **Baise states** — about 22 states in the Karnali region. **Doti** was a separate kingdom in the far west.
- **Chaubise states** — about 24 states in the Gandaki region, among them Palpa, Tanahun, Lamjung, Kaski, Parbat and Gorkha.
- **Gorkha:** **Drabya Shah**, a prince of Lamjung, founded the Shah kingdom of Gorkha in **1559 AD (1616 BS)**.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Describe the reforms of Jayasthiti Malla.

**Model answer.** Jayasthiti Malla (ruled 1382–1395) brought order to a society disturbed by raids and weak rule. **Social reform:** he arranged society into four classes and 64 castes according to occupation, and fixed rules of dress, housing and conduct for each. **Economic reform:** he standardised weights and measures such as the *mana* and *pathi*, and graded land into four classes — *abal*, *doyam*, *sim* and *chahar* — so that land could be measured, sold and taxed fairly. **Legal reform:** he had written rules prepared with the help of learned Brahmins and fixed punishments for crimes. **Cultural work:** he encouraged literature and drama, and the Gopal Raj Vamshavali was written in his time. **Assessment:** his rules gave stability and helped trade and crafts, but they also fixed the caste system firmly, and its harmful effects lasted for centuries.

## 2.4 Unification to the Sugauli Treaty (एकीकरणदेखि सुगौली सन्धिसम्म)

#### 1 · Why it matters

The making of modern Nepal is the most examined part of history: the steps of Prithvi Narayan Shah's campaign, the heroes of the war with the British, and the Sugauli Treaty that fixed today's east and west boundaries. The Karnali syllabus names the Shah dynasty.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

In the 18th century the land was divided into more than fifty small states. **Prithvi Narayan Shah**, king of the small hill state of Gorkha, began to join them into one country. He won the Kathmandu Valley in 1768–1769 and moved his capital there. His successors carried the border east to the **Tista** river and west to the **Sutlej**. This growth brought war with the British East India Company in 1814–1816. Nepal lost, and the **Sugauli Treaty** cut the country back to the land between the **Mechi** and the **Mahakali**.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Prithvi Narayan Shah**

- Born on **27 Poush 1779 BS (11 January 1723)** in Gorkha; son of King Nara Bhupal Shah. Poush 27 is kept as **Prithvi Jayanti and National Unity Day**.
- Became king of Gorkha in **1799 BS (1743)**.
- **Nuwakot** — first attack failed in 1743; **won in 1744 (1801 BS)**. It opened the trade road to Tibet and the way to the Valley. **Kalu Pande** was made *Kaji* (chief minister).
- **Kirtipur** — first attack **1757**: defeat, and **Kalu Pande was killed**. Second attack 1764: defeat. Third attack: Kirtipur fell in **1766**.
- **Makwanpur** won in **1762**. In 1763 the Gorkhalis beat the army of Mir Qasim, Nawab of Bengal.
- **Sindhuligadhi, 1767** — the Gorkhalis defeated a British force under **Captain Kinloch**, sent to help the Malla king of Kantipur.
- **Kantipur** won on **13 Ashoj 1825 BS (25 September 1768)**, the day of **Indra Jatra**. **Lalitpur** followed in October 1768 and **Bhaktapur** in **November 1769**.
- The capital moved from Gorkha to **Kathmandu**.
- In 1773–1774 the eastern Sen kingdoms of **Chaudandi** and **Bijayapur** were won.
- Died on **1 Magh 1831 BS (11 January 1775)** at **Devighat, Nuwakot**.
- **Dibya Upadesh** (दिव्य उपदेश, "divine counsel") — his advice to the nation: Nepal is **"a yam between two boulders"** (दुई ढुङ्गाको तरुल); the country is a **garden of four classes and thirty-six castes**; keep friendship with both the northern and the southern neighbour; use home-made goods; punish both the giver and the taker of bribes.

**After Prithvi Narayan Shah**

- Kings: Pratap Singh Shah → Rana Bahadur Shah → Girvan Yuddha Bikram Shah → Rajendra Bikram Shah.
- Regents **Rajendra Laxmi** and **Bahadur Shah** carried on the campaign. The Chaubise and Baise states, including Jumla, were won, and **Kumaon** was taken in 1790.
- **Wars with Tibet and China, 1788–1792** — ended by the **Treaty of Kerung (1789)** with Tibet and the **Treaty of Betrawati (1792)** after a Chinese army entered Nepal.
- **Bhimsen Thapa** was *Mukhtiyar* (chief minister) from **1806 to 1837**. He is counted as the **first Prime Minister of Nepal**. He built the **Dharahara** tower. **Mathbar Singh Thapa** was later the first to use the title "Prime Minister".
- **Amar Singh Thapa** (the elder, *Bada Kaji*) took Garhwal in 1804 and reached **Kangra** and the **Sutlej**.
- **Greatest extent:** from the **Tista** in the east to the **Sutlej** in the west.

**Anglo-Nepal War, 1814–1816**

- **Causes:** dispute over the border lands of **Butwal and Syuraj**; British wish for trade routes to Tibet and for the hill country; fear of Nepal's growth.
- War was declared by Governor-General **Lord Hastings** on **1 November 1814**.
- **Sugauli Treaty:** signed **2 December 1815**; ratified **4 March 1816 (1872 BS)**.
  - Nepal gave up the land **east of the Mechi** and **west of the Mahakali**, and most of the Terai — about **one third** of its territory.
  - A **British Resident** was placed in Kathmandu.
  - The treaty opened the way for the recruitment of Gorkha soldiers into British service.
- A large part of the Terai came back in December 1816. The **Naya Muluk** — Banke, Bardiya, Kailali and Kanchanpur — came back in **1860**.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** What were the causes and results of the Anglo-Nepal War?

**Model answer.** **Causes:** (1) The border between Nepal and the East India Company's lands was unclear, and both claimed Butwal and Syuraj; this was the immediate cause. (2) Nepal lost about one third of its land; the Mechi and the Mahakali became the boundaries. (3) A British Resident came to Kathmandu. (4) Gorkha soldiers began to serve in the British army. (5) The age of expansion ended, and court politics turned inward, which led in time to the rise of the Ranas.

## 2.5 Rana period (राणाकाल), 1846–1951

#### 1 · Why it matters

For 104 years the Rana prime ministers held all power and the kings had none. Questions ask who did what — which prime minister founded the first newspaper, college or bank — and about the movements and martyrs that ended the regime.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

After the death of Bhimsen Thapa the court was full of plots. On one night in 1846 **Jung Bahadur Kunwar** killed his rivals in the **Kot Massacre** and became Prime Minister. He made the post **hereditary in his own family**, passing from brother to brother. The Rana family ruled until 1951. They kept Nepal independent and made some reforms, but they kept the people without rights or schooling, and the country closed to the world.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Rise of Jung Bahadur**

- **Kot Massacre (कोतपर्व): 14 September 1846 (2 Ashoj 1903 BS)**, at the Kot (armoury court) near Hanuman Dhoka. It followed the murder of the queen's favourite, Gagan Singh. Jung Bahadur became Prime Minister and Commander-in-Chief.
- **Bhandarkhal Parva (1846):** a plot against Jung Bahadur failed. **Alau Parva (1847):** King Rajendra's attempt to return failed at Alau in Parsa; **Surendra** became king.
- **Visit to Britain and France, 1850–1851** — the first by a ruler of Nepal.
- **Muluki Ain, 1910 BS (1854)** — the first written national legal code.
- **1856:** a royal order gave Jung Bahadur the title **Maharaja of Kaski and Lamjung** and made the prime ministership hereditary in his family.
- **War with Tibet, 1855–1856** — ended by the **Treaty of Thapathali (1856)**.
- **1857:** Jung Bahadur helped the British to put down the uprising in India. In return the **Naya Muluk** came back in **1860**.
- Jung Bahadur died in 1877.

**Later Rana prime ministers**

- **Ranodip Singh** — murdered in **1885 (1942 BS)** by his nephews, the Shamsher brothers.
- **Bir Shamsher** — **Bir Hospital** (1947 BS), piped drinking water, the clock tower (Ghantaghar).
- **Dev Shamsher** — ruled only **114 days** in 1901. Started the **Gorkhapatra** (24 Baisakh 1958 BS; 6 May 1901) and opened schools for the public. Removed by his brother Chandra.
- **Chandra Shamsher** (1901–1929):
  - **Pharping hydropower** (1911; 1968 BS);
  - **Tri-Chandra College** (1975 BS; 1918), the first college;
  - **abolition of Sati** on 25 Ashad 1977 BS (8 July 1920);
  - **abolition of slavery**, announced on 14 Mangsir 1981 BS (28 November 1924), in force from 1 Baisakh 1982 BS (13 April 1925);
  - **treaty with Britain, 21 December 1923 (6 Poush 1980 BS)**, in which Britain recognised Nepal's independence;
  - **Singha Durbar**; the first railway (Raxaul–Amlekhgunj) and ropeway;
  - division of the Rana family into A, B and C classes.
- **Bhim Shamsher** (1929–1932).
- **Juddha Shamsher** (1932–1945): relief and rebuilding after the **earthquake of 2 Magh 1990 BS (15 January 1934)**; **Biratnagar Jute Mill** (1936), the first modern factory; **Nepal Bank Limited** (30 Kartik 1994 BS; 15 November 1937), the first bank; execution of the **four martyrs** (Magh 1997 BS).
- **Padma Shamsher** (1945–1948): announced the **Government of Nepal Act, 2004** on **13 Magh 2004 BS (26 January 1948)** — Nepal's **first constitution**. It was never properly put into force. He resigned in 1948.
- **Mohan Shamsher** (1948–1951): the last Rana Prime Minister. He signed the **Treaty of Peace and Friendship with India on 31 July 1950 (16 Shrawan 2007 BS)**.

**Movements against the Ranas**

- **Lakhan Thapa Magar** of Gorkha rose against Jung Bahadur and was hanged in **1877 (1933 BS)**. He is honoured as the **first martyr** of Nepal.
- **Makai Parva (1977 BS):** Krishna Lal Adhikari was jailed for his book *Makaiko Kheti* ("The Cultivation of Maize") and died in prison.
- **Library Parva (1987 BS):** young men were punished for planning a public library.
- **Prachanda Gorkha (1988 BS):** a secret group that planned to overthrow the Ranas.
- **Nepal Praja Parishad** — founded on **20 Jestha 1993 BS (2 June 1936)**; the **first political party**; president **Tanka Prasad Acharya**.
- **The four martyrs of 1997 BS (January 1941):**
  - **Shukraraj Shastri** — hanged, 10 Magh;
  - **Dharma Bhakta Mathema** — hanged, 12 Magh;
  - **Dashrath Chand** and **Ganga Lal Shrestha** — shot, 15 Magh.
  - **Martyrs' Day** is kept on **Magh 16**.
- **Biratnagar Jute Mill strike:** 21 Falgun 2003 BS (4 March 1947) — the first large labour movement.
- **Jayatu Sanskritam (2004 BS):** the first student movement.
- **Parties:** Nepali National Congress (12 Magh 2003 BS; 25 January 1947). **Nepali Congress** was formed on **27 Chaitra 2006 BS (9 April 1950)** by its union with the Nepal Democratic Congress. The **Communist Party of Nepal** was founded on **10 Baisakh 2006 BS (22 April 1949)** by **Pushpa Lal Shrestha**.

## 2.6 The revolution of 2007 BS and the decade to 2017 BS (२००७ सालको क्रान्ति र त्यसपछिको दशक)

#### 1 · Why it matters

The Karnali syllabus names "events since 2007 BS". Democracy Day, the first election, the first elected Prime Minister and the royal takeover of 2017 are tested every way: dates, order and matching.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

By 1950 three forces stood against the Ranas: the **King**, who wanted his powers back; the **political parties**, who wanted democracy; and newly independent **India**, which supported change. King Tribhuvan left his palace, the Nepali Congress took up arms, and in a few months the Ranas gave way. Democracy was announced on **7 Falgun 2007**. The next ten years saw many short governments, the first general election in 2015 BS, an elected government under B.P. Koirala — and then its dismissal by King Mahendra on **1 Poush 2017**.

#### 3 · Key facts

**The revolution**

- **21 Kartik 2007 (6 November 1950):** King **Tribhuvan** and his family took refuge in the **Indian Embassy** in Kathmandu. A few days later they flew to Delhi.
- **22 Kartik 2007 (7 November 1950):** the Ranas placed his young grandson **Gyanendra** on the throne. No foreign country recognised him.
- The Nepali Congress **Mukti Sena** (Liberation Army) attacked from the border. **Birgunj** was among the first towns taken.
- **Delhi Agreement** (early 1951) — a three-sided settlement among the King, the Ranas and the Nepali Congress, reached with India's help.
- **4 Falgun 2007 (15 February 1951):** King Tribhuvan returned to Kathmandu.
- **7 Falgun 2007 (18 February 1951):** the King proclaimed the end of Rana rule and the start of democracy. This is **Democracy Day** (प्रजातन्त्र दिवस).
- A joint cabinet was formed: **Mohan Shamsher** as Prime Minister, with **five Rana and five Nepali Congress ministers**. **B.P. Koirala** was Home Minister.
- The **Interim Government of Nepal Act, 2007** — the second constitution — was issued in Chaitra 2007 (1951).

**The decade of trial, 2008–2015 BS**

- **1 Mangsir 2008 (16 November 1951):** **Matrika Prasad Koirala** became the **first Prime Minister from among the people** (not a Rana).
- **21 Magh 2008 (4 February 1952):** the **first budget** of Nepal was presented.
- **30 Falgun 2011 (13 March 1955):** King Tribhuvan died in Zurich. **King Mahendra** followed him.
- **17 Shrawan 2012 (1 August 1955):** diplomatic relations with **China**.
- **29 Mangsir 2012 (14 December 1955):** Nepal became a member of the **United Nations**.
- **2013 BS (1956):** the **First Five-Year Plan** began; **Nepal Rastra Bank** was set up on 14 Baisakh 2013 (26 April 1956); the **Supreme Court** was set up.
- Prime ministers of these years included **Tanka Prasad Acharya** (2012–2014 BS) and **Dr K.I. Singh** (2014 BS). **Subarna Shamsher** led the government that held the election.
- Institutions of the decade: **Radio Nepal** (20 Chaitra 2007; 2 April 1951), **Public Service Commission** (1 Ashad 2008; 15 June 1951), **Tribhuvan University** (2016 BS).

**The first election and the first elected government**

- **1 Falgun 2015 (12 February 1959):** King Mahendra gave the **Constitution of the Kingdom of Nepal, 2015** — the third constitution. It set up a parliament of two houses.
- **First general election:** voting began on **7 Falgun 2015 (18 February 1959)** and went on in stages for about six weeks.
  - **109 seats** in the House of Representatives.
  - **Nepali Congress 74**, Gorkha Parishad 19, Samyukta Prajatantra Party 5, Communist Party of Nepal 4, the two Praja Parishad groups 3, independents 4.
- **13 Jestha 2016 (27 May 1959):** **Bishweshwar Prasad (B.P.) Koirala** became the **first elected Prime Minister**.
- His government abolished **birta** (tax-free land grants), began to end the small vassal principalities (रजौटा), and widened foreign relations.
- **Dwarika Devi Thakurani**, the only woman elected in 2015 BS, became the first woman minister.

**The royal takeover**

- **1 Poush 2017 (15 December 1960):** King Mahendra used **Article 55** (emergency powers) of the 2015 Constitution. He dismissed the government, dissolved parliament and jailed B.P. Koirala and other leaders.
- **22 Poush 2017 (5 January 1961):** political parties were banned.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** What were the causes of the revolution of 2007 BS?

**Model answer.** **(1) Autocratic family rule:** for 104 years the Ranas kept all power, wealth and high posts in one family and gave the people no rights. **(2) Powerless monarchy:** the kings were kept like prisoners in the palace; King Tribhuvan quietly supported the movement. **(3) Political awakening:** the Nepal Praja Parishad, the sacrifice of the four martyrs in 1997 BS, and later the Nepali Congress and the Communist Party organised the people. **(4) Education and outside contact:** Nepalis who studied or worked in India, and soldiers who returned from two world wars, brought new ideas. **(5) Change in India:** India became independent in 1947; the Ranas lost their British protector, and the new Indian government favoured reform. **(6) Division among the Ranas:** the A, B and C classes left many Ranas discontented, and some of them financed the movement. Together these forces made the King, the parties and India act at the same time in 2007 BS, and the regime fell within about three months.

## 2.7 Panchayat period (पञ्चायतकाल), 2017–2046 BS

#### 1 · Why it matters

Thirty years of party-less rule shaped today's administration: the zones and districts, the legal code, land reform and the highways all date from this time. The referendum of 2037 BS and the Zone of Peace proposal are frequent questions.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

After the takeover of 2017 BS, King Mahendra said that the parliamentary system did not suit Nepal. He introduced the **Panchayat system**: councils (*panchayat*) at village, district and national levels, with **no political parties** and with real power in the hands of the King. King Birendra continued it after 2028 BS. A students' movement forced a **referendum** in 2037 BS, which the Panchayat side narrowly won. Ten years later the **People's Movement of 2046** ended the system.

#### 3 · Key facts

**The system**

- **Constitution of Nepal, 2019** — given by King Mahendra on **1 Poush 2019 (16 December 1962)**; the fourth constitution.
- Sovereignty lay with the **King**. Parties were banned. The national legislature was the **Rastriya Panchayat**.
- "Class organisations" of peasants, workers, youth, women and ex-soldiers replaced parties.
- The country was divided into **14 zones and 75 districts** on **1 Baisakh 2018 (13 April 1961)**.
- **Development regions:** four in 2029 BS; the fifth (Far-Western) was added in 2037 BS.
- The 2019 Constitution was amended **three times: 2023, 2032 and 2037 BS**.

**Main works and events**

- **Muluki Ain, 2020** — a new legal code, in force from **1 Bhadra 2020 (17 August 1963)**. It ended legal discrimination by caste.
- **Land Reform Act, 2021** — fixed a ceiling on land-holding and gave rights to tenants.
- **Back-to-the-Village National Campaign** (गाउँ फर्क राष्ट्रिय अभियान), from 2024 BS.
- **New Education System Plan, 2028.**
- **Roads:** the East–West (Mahendra) Highway was begun; the Kodari (Araniko) Highway to the Chinese border was built.
- **Foreign policy:** founding member of the **Non-Aligned Movement (1961)**; **boundary treaty with China, 5 October 1961**; non-permanent member of the United Nations Security Council in **1969–1970** and **1988–1989**.
- **17 Magh 2028 (31 January 1972):** King Mahendra died at Bharatpur. **King Birendra** followed; he was crowned on **12 Falgun 2031 (24 February 1975)**.
- **Zone of Peace** (शान्ति क्षेत्र): King Birendra proposed, at the time of his coronation in 2031 BS, that Nepal be declared a Zone of Peace. **116 countries** supported it.
- **SAARC** was founded on 8 December 1985; its **secretariat** was set up in **Kathmandu** in 1987.

**The referendum**

- A students' movement began in Chaitra 2035 – Baisakh 2036 (1979).
- **10 Jestha 2036 (24 May 1979):** King Birendra announced a **national referendum** (जनमत संग्रह).
- **20 Baisakh 2037 (2 May 1980):** voters chose between a **reformed Panchayat system** and a **multi-party system**.
- **Result:** reformed Panchayat about **54.8%**, multi-party about **45.2%**.
- The **third amendment (2037 BS)** then brought direct election of the Rastriya Panchayat by adult franchise, and a Prime Minister chosen by that house.

**The end of the system**

- **16 Poush 2033 (30 December 1976):** B.P. Koirala returned from exile in India with a policy of **national reconciliation** (राष्ट्रिय मेलमिलाप). He died on 6 Shrawan 2039 (21 July 1982).
- **2042 BS:** the Nepali Congress launched a civil disobedience campaign (*satyagraha*).
- **2045–2046 BS (1989):** trade and transit treaties with India lapsed, and most border points were closed for over a year. Shortages added to public anger.
- **7 Falgun 2046 (18 February 1990):** the People's Movement began (topic 2.8).

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** State the main features of the Panchayat system and the reasons for its fall.

**Model answer.** **Features:** (1) sovereignty and final power lay with the King; (2) political parties were banned and "class organisations" took their place; (3) councils called panchayats worked at village, district and national levels, with the Rastriya Panchayat as the legislature; (4) the system stressed nationalism, one language and one dress, and planned development through zones, districts and development regions; (5) fundamental rights were limited and the press was controlled. **Reasons for its fall:** (1) the people had no real share in power, and the banned parties kept working underground; (2) corruption and the privileges of a small circle caused anger; (3) the referendum of 2037 BS showed that nearly half the voters wanted a multi-party system; (4) educated youth and a growing middle class demanded rights; (5) the closing of border trade points in 2045–2046 BS caused shortages; (6) democratic changes abroad encouraged the movement. The Nepali Congress and the United Left Front joined hands, and the People's Movement of 2046 ended the system.

## 2.8 From the People's Movement of 2046 to the peace of 2063 (२०४६ देखि २०६३ सम्म)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Two people's movements, a ten-year armed conflict, a palace massacre and a peace accord fall in these seventeen years. The dates are asked directly, and the order of events is a standard Level II question.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

The **People's Movement of 2046** (जनआन्दोलन) brought back political parties and made the King a constitutional monarch under the Constitution of 2047. Elected governments followed, but they were short-lived. In 2052 BS the Maoists began an armed revolt. In 2058 BS King Birendra and his family were killed in the palace, and King Gyanendra came to the throne. He took power into his own hands in 2059 and 2061 BS. The parliamentary parties and the Maoists then joined against him. The **second People's Movement, in 2062/63**, forced him to give up power, and the **Comprehensive Peace Accord** ended the war.

#### 3 · Key facts

**The People's Movement of 2046**

- Began on **7 Falgun 2046 (18 February 1990)**, called jointly by the **Nepali Congress** and the **United Left Front**.
- **Ganesh Man Singh** was its supreme leader (सर्वोच्च कमाण्डर). **Sahana Pradhan** chaired the United Left Front.
- Late on **26 Chaitra 2046 (8 April 1990)** King Birendra lifted the ban on parties.
- **6 Baisakh 2047 (19 April 1990):** an interim government was formed under **Krishna Prasad Bhattarai**.
- **23 Kartik 2047 (9 November 1990):** the **Constitution of the Kingdom of Nepal, 2047** — the fifth constitution. It was drafted by a commission chaired by Justice **Bishwanath Upadhyaya**. Features: sovereignty in the people, constitutional monarchy, multi-party parliamentary system, fundamental rights.

**Elected governments**

- **Election of 2048** (29 Baisakh 2048; 12 May 1991): 205 seats; Nepali Congress **110**, CPN (UML) **69**. **Girija Prasad Koirala** became Prime Minister.
- **Mid-term election of 2051** (29 Kartik 2051; 15 November 1994): CPN (UML) **88**, Nepali Congress **83**. **Man Mohan Adhikari** led a minority government — the **first communist Prime Minister**.
- **Election of 2056** (May 1999): Nepali Congress **111**, CPN (UML) **71**.
- From 2051 to 2059 BS governments changed almost every year.

**The armed conflict**

- The Communist Party of Nepal (Maoist) sent the government a list of **40 demands** in Magh 2052.
- **1 Falgun 2052 (13 February 1996):** the Maoists began their "People's War" with attacks in **Rolpa, Rukum, Sindhuli and Gorkha**.
- The conflict lasted **ten years**. About **17,000** people were killed.

**The palace massacre and the royal takeover**

- **19 Jestha 2058 (1 June 2001):** King **Birendra**, Queen Aishwarya and other members of the royal family were killed at **Narayanhiti Palace**.
- Crown Prince **Dipendra**, lying unconscious, was king for three days. **Gyanendra** became king on **22 Jestha 2058 (4 June 2001)**.
- **11 Mangsir 2058 (26 November 2001):** a state of emergency was declared and the army was sent against the Maoists.
- **8 Jestha 2059 (22 May 2002):** the House of Representatives was dissolved.
- **18 Ashoj 2059 (4 October 2002):** King Gyanendra dismissed Prime Minister **Sher Bahadur Deuba**.
- **19 Magh 2061 (1 February 2005):** the King took full power himself.

**The second People's Movement and peace**

- **7 Mangsir 2062 (22 November 2005):** the **12-point understanding** between the **Seven-Party Alliance** and the Maoists, reached in New Delhi.
- **People's Movement II: 24 Chaitra 2062 – 11 Baisakh 2063 (6–24 April 2006)** — 19 days.
- **11 Baisakh 2063 (24 April 2006):** the King restored the dissolved House of Representatives. This is **Loktantra Day** (लोकतन्त्र दिवस). Girija Prasad Koirala became Prime Minister.
- **4 Jestha 2063 (18 May 2006):** the House declared itself supreme, cut the King's powers, put the army under the government and declared Nepal a **secular state**.
- **5 Mangsir 2063 (21 November 2006):** the **Comprehensive Peace Accord** (विस्तृत शान्ति सम्झौता), signed by Prime Minister **Girija Prasad Koirala** and Maoist chairman **Pushpa Kamal Dahal "Prachanda"**. The war was formally over.
- **1 Magh 2063 (15 January 2007):** the **Interim Constitution of Nepal, 2063** — the sixth constitution. An interim parliament of 330 members, with the Maoists in it, replaced the old House.
- The United Nations Mission in Nepal watched over the arms and armies of both sides from 2007 to 2011.
- **Madhesh movement, Magh 2063 (January–February 2007):** protests in the Terai demanded federalism and fair representation. The Interim Constitution was amended to promise a federal system.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Compare the People's Movements of 2046 and 2062/63.

**Model answer.** **Similarities:** both were peaceful mass movements led by an alliance of parties; both were directed against the active rule of the King; both ended when the King gave way after weeks of protest; both were followed by a new constitution. **Differences:** (1) *Target* — the first opposed the party-less Panchayat system; the second opposed the direct rule of King Gyanendra. (4) *Aim and result* — the first accepted a constitutional monarchy and brought the Constitution of 2047; the second led to the peace accord, the Interim Constitution of 2063, a secular state and finally a republic. (5) *Reach* — the second was wider, with rural people, women, professionals and civil society taking part in great numbers. In short, the first movement limited the monarchy; the second opened the way to end it.

## 2.9 Republic, Constitution and events to the present (गणतन्त्र, संविधान र हालसम्मका घटना)

#### 1 · Why it matters

This is where history meets current affairs. The dates of the republic and the Constitution, the "firsts" among office-holders, and the latest change of government are all likely questions. Facts after 2079 BS are marked 🔄 because they are recent.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

After the peace accord, the people elected a **Constituent Assembly** to write a new constitution. Its first meeting ended the monarchy and made Nepal a **republic**. The first Assembly could not finish its work; a second one was elected and delivered the **Constitution of Nepal** in 2072 BS. The Constitution made Nepal a **federal democratic republic** with seven provinces. Since then there have been three general elections, many changes of government, a youth-led protest movement in 2082 BS, and an early election in which a new party won a majority.

#### 3 · Key facts

**The republic**

- **First Constituent Assembly election: 28 Chaitra 2064 (10 April 2008).** 601 members. The Communist Party of Nepal (Maoist) was the largest party.
- **15 Jestha 2065 (28 May 2008):** the first meeting of the Assembly declared Nepal a **Federal Democratic Republic**. The vote was **560 to 4**. The monarchy of about 240 years ended. **Republic Day** is Jestha 15.
- **First President: Dr Ram Baran Yadav**, elected on 6 Shrawan 2065 (21 July 2008). **First Vice-President: Parmananda Jha.**
- **First Prime Minister of the republic: Pushpa Kamal Dahal "Prachanda"**, elected on 31 Shrawan 2065 (15 August 2008).
- The first Assembly's term was extended several times. It ended on **14 Jestha 2069 (27 May 2012)** without a constitution.
- **1 Chaitra 2069 (14 March 2013):** Chief Justice **Khil Raj Regmi** became chairman of an interim council of ministers to hold a new election.

**The Constitution**

- **Second Constituent Assembly election: 4 Mangsir 2070 (19 November 2013).** Nepali Congress **196**, CPN (UML) **175**, Unified CPN (Maoist) **80**.
- The earthquake of 12 Baisakh 2072 pushed the parties to agree. The **16-point agreement** of 25 Jestha 2072 (8 June 2015) settled the main disputes.
- **30 Bhadra 2072 (16 September 2015):** the Assembly passed the Constitution — **507 votes for, 25 against**.
- **3 Ashoj 2072 (20 September 2015):** President **Ram Baran Yadav** proclaimed the **Constitution of Nepal**, the seventh constitution. It has **35 Parts, 308 Articles and 9 Schedules**. **Constitution Day** (National Day) is Ashoj 3.
- Protests in the Terai and a blockade of border points followed in late 2072.
- **First amendment:** authenticated on 16 Falgun 2072 (28 February 2016). **Second amendment:** 4 Ashad 2077 (18 June 2020), for the new map in the coat of arms.

**Putting federalism to work**

- **Presidents:** Ram Baran Yadav (2065) → **Bidya Devi Bhandari**, elected 11 Kartik 2072 (28 October 2015), the **first woman President** → **Ram Chandra Paudel**, elected 25 Falgun 2079 (9 March 2023).
- **Onsari Gharti Magar** became the first woman Speaker in 2072. **Sushila Karki** became the first woman Chief Justice on 27 Ashad 2073 (11 July 2016).
- **Local elections of 2074** — the first in twenty years — were held in three phases: 31 Baisakh, 14 Ashad and 2 Ashoj 2074.
- **First federal and provincial elections under the Constitution:** 10 and 21 Mangsir 2074 (26 November and 7 December 2017). **K.P. Sharma Oli** became Prime Minister on 3 Falgun 2074.
- The House of Representatives was dissolved on **5 Poush 2077** and again on **8 Jestha 2078**. The **Supreme Court restored it both times** (11 Falgun 2077; 28 Ashad 2078). **Sher Bahadur Deuba** became Prime Minister on 29 Ashad 2078.
- **Elections of 2079:** local on 30 Baisakh 2079 (13 May 2022); federal and provincial on **4 Mangsir 2079 (20 November 2022)**. Nepali Congress 89, CPN (UML) 78, CPN (Maoist Centre) 32, Rastriya Swatantra Party 20. **Pushpa Kamal Dahal** became Prime Minister in Poush 2079.
- **31 Ashad 2081 (15 July 2024):** K.P. Sharma Oli became Prime Minister, leading a coalition of the CPN (UML) and the Nepali Congress.

**The movement of 2082 BS and the early election** 🔄

- In Bhadra 2082 the government ordered many social-media platforms to be blocked. Young people, already angry about corruption, called protests. The movement is known as the **Gen Z movement** (जेनजी आन्दोलन).
- **23 Bhadra 2082 (8 September 2025):** police fired on protesters in Kathmandu; 19 people were reported killed that day. **24 Bhadra (9 September):** Prime Minister **K.P. Sharma Oli resigned**. The parliament building, Singha Durbar and other public buildings were set on fire.
- ⚖️ The number of dead in the movement is given as **72** in the Home Ministry's count of that month and as **76** in later counts.
- **27 Bhadra 2082 (12 September 2025):** former Chief Justice **Sushila Karki** became interim Prime Minister — the **first woman Prime Minister of Nepal**. The House of Representatives was dissolved, and an election was called.
- **21 Falgun 2082 (5 March 2026):** election to the House of Representatives. The **Rastriya Swatantra Party won 182 of the 275 seats** — the first single-party majority since 2056 BS.
- **13 Chaitra 2082 (27 March 2026):** **Balendra Shah**, former Mayor of Kathmandu, became Prime Minister.

**Model answer.** (1) **Comprehensive Peace Accord, 5 Mangsir 2063** — the government and the Maoists ended the war and agreed to elect a Constituent Assembly. (2) **Interim Constitution, 1 Magh 2063** — it brought the Maoists into parliament and government; after the Madhesh movement it promised federalism. (3) **First Constituent Assembly election, 28 Chaitra 2064.** (4) **Republic, 15 Jestha 2065** — the Assembly's first meeting ended the monarchy; a President and a new government were elected. (5) **Failure of the first Assembly, 14 Jestha 2069** — the parties could not agree on the number and names of provinces and the form of government. (6) **Interim election government** under Chief Justice Khil Raj Regmi, Chaitra 2069. (7) **Second Constituent Assembly election, 4 Mangsir 2070.** (8) **16-point agreement, 25 Jestha 2072**, after the earthquake. (9) **Adoption on 30 Bhadra 2072** by 507 votes to 25, and **proclamation on 3 Ashoj 2072** by President Ram Baran Yadav. The process took nearly nine years, but it turned a country at war into a federal democratic republic by peaceful means.

## 2.10 Timeline: treaties, wars, constitutions and firsts (सन्धि, युद्ध, संविधान र पहिला घटना)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Level II questions join facts from different periods: "arrange in order", "match the treaty with the year", "which one does not belong". This topic gathers those facts into lists so that you can answer such questions quickly.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

History questions repeat four kinds of list: **treaties** (with whom, when, what result), **wars**, **constitutions**, and **"firsts"** (the first person or institution of a kind). Learn each list in time order. Then a question that mixes periods becomes a matter of placing each item on one line.

#### 3 · Key facts

- Nepal has fought **four wars with outside powers** since unification: with Tibet (1788–1789), with Tibet and China (1791–1792), with the British East India Company (1814–1816) and with Tibet again (1855–1856).
- Nepal has had **seven constitutions**: 2004, 2007, 2015, 2019, 2047, 2063 and 2072 BS.
- The first country with which Nepal set up diplomatic relations was **Britain (1816)**. The second was the **United States (25 April 1947)**, then **India (13 June 1947)**, **France (1949)**, **China (1 August 1955)** and **Japan (1956)**.
- Nepal joined the **United Nations on 14 December 1955**, was a **founding member of the Non-Aligned Movement (1961)** and of **SAARC (1985)**, and joined the **World Trade Organization on 23 April 2004**.
- **National days that come from history:** Poush 27 (Prithvi Jayanti, National Unity Day) · Magh 16 (Martyrs' Day) · Falgun 7 (Democracy Day) · Baisakh 11 (Loktantra Day) · Jestha 15 (Republic Day) · Ashoj 3 (Constitution Day).
- **Kings of the Shah dynasty after unification:** Prithvi Narayan → Pratap Singh → Rana Bahadur → Girvan Yuddha → Rajendra → Surendra → Prithvi Bir Bikram → Tribhuvan → Mahendra → Birendra → Dipendra → Gyanendra.

#### 7 · Exam traps

- **Kerung, Betrawati and Thapathali** are treaties with Tibet or China. **Sugauli** is with the British.
- The **Treaty of 1950** is with **India**; the **Treaty of 1923** is with **Britain**; the **Treaty of 1960** is with **China**.
- **1947 has two meanings:** 1947 **AD** (relations with the United States and India) and 1947 **BS** (Bir Hospital). Check the era.
- "First Prime Minister" has several answers: Bhimsen Thapa (first of all), Jung Bahadur (first Rana), M.P. Koirala (first from the people), B.P. Koirala (first elected), Prachanda (first of the republic). Read the qualifier.
- The **interim** documents of 2007 and 2063 are counted among the seven constitutions.
- **Gyanendra** appears twice in the list of kings.
- The **first census** (1968 BS) and the **first general election** (2015 BS) are often confused in "first" lists.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** List the seven constitutions of Nepal and state one main feature of each.

**Model answer.** (1) **Government of Nepal Act, 2004** — announced by Padma Shamsher; the first written constitution, with a limited legislature under Rana control; it never came into real force. (2) **Interim Government of Nepal Act, 2007** — issued after the revolution; it gave executive power to the King and his council of ministers until an elected body could make a constitution. (3) **Constitution of the Kingdom of Nepal, 2015** — given by King Mahendra; a parliamentary system with two houses, under which the first general election was held. (4) **Constitution of Nepal, 2019** — the party-less Panchayat system, with sovereignty in the King. (5) **Constitution of the Kingdom of Nepal, 2047** — after the People's Movement; sovereignty in the people, constitutional monarchy and multi-party democracy. (6) **Interim Constitution of Nepal, 2063** — after the second People's Movement; it managed the peace process and provided for a Constituent Assembly, a secular state and, by amendment, a federal republic. (7) **Constitution of Nepal (2072)** — written by the elected Constituent Assembly; a federal democratic republic with three levels of government and seven provinces.

### D. Five short-answer questions

**1. Describe the role of Prithvi Narayan Shah in the unification of Nepal.**
*Points for the answer:* condition of the country — more than fifty small states · preparation: weapons, army, alliances, blockade of the Valley · steps: Nuwakot 1744, Makwanpur 1762, Kirtipur 1766, Kantipur and Lalitpur 1768, Bhaktapur 1769, eastern kingdoms 1773–1774 · defeat of Mir Qasim's and Kinloch's forces · capital moved to Kathmandu · Dibya Upadesh: foreign policy, economy, justice · assessment: founder of modern Nepal.

**2. What were the main terms and effects of the Sugauli Treaty?**
*Points for the answer:* signed 2 December 1815, ratified 4 March 1816 · loss of land east of the Mechi, west of the Mahakali and in the Terai — about one third of the territory · British Resident in Kathmandu · limits on Nepal's dealings with other powers · Gorkha recruitment · later return of part of the Terai (1816) and the Naya Muluk (1860) · effects: end of expansion, fixed boundaries, inward-looking politics, boundary questions that continue today.

**3. Give an account of the anti-Rana movements up to 2007 BS.**
*Points for the answer:* Lakhan Thapa (1933 BS) · Makai Parva (1977), Library Parva (1987), Prachanda Gorkha (1988) · Nepal Praja Parishad (1993) and the four martyrs (1997) · Biratnagar Jute Mill strike (2003) and Jayatu Sanskritam (2004) · Nepali National Congress (2003), Communist Party (2006), Nepali Congress (2006) · King Tribhuvan's move to the Indian Embassy; armed struggle; Delhi Agreement; 7 Falgun 2007.

**4. Why did King Mahendra's takeover of 2017 BS take place, and what followed?**
*Points for the answer:* the King's wish for an active role · quarrels among and inside parties · opposition of landowners and former rulers to the reforms of the elected government · use of Article 55 on 1 Poush 2017 · arrest of leaders, ban on parties · Constitution of 2019 and the Panchayat system · reforms (Muluki Ain, land reform, zones and districts) alongside the loss of political freedom.

**5. Explain the importance of the Comprehensive Peace Accord of 2063 BS.**
*Points for the answer:* signed 5 Mangsir 2063 by Girija Prasad Koirala and Prachanda · formal end of a ten-year war with about 17,000 dead · arms and armies placed under United Nations watch · Maoists brought into open politics · commitment to a Constituent Assembly election, human rights, and truth and reconciliation · base for the Interim Constitution, the republic and the Constitution of 2072 · unfinished work: justice for victims of the conflict.

### E. Common mistakes

1. Giving the **Changu Narayan inscription** to Amshuverma instead of Manadeva.
2. Calling **Amshuverma** a Lichhavi by birth, or placing **Araniko** in the Lichhavi period.
3. Mixing the **Malla kings of the Valley** with the **Khas "Malla" kings of Sinja**.
4. Placing **Simraungadh** in Dhanusha instead of Bara.
5. Naming **Kirtipur or Kathmandu** as the first victory of Prithvi Narayan Shah; it was Nuwakot.
6. Writing that the **Sugauli Treaty** returned the Naya Muluk; that happened in 1860.
7. Counting **Tanka Prasad Acharya** among the four martyrs.
8. Giving **Gorkhapatra** to Chandra Shamsher instead of Dev Shamsher.
9. Confusing **M.P. Koirala** (first Prime Minister from the people) with **B.P. Koirala** (first elected Prime Minister).
10. Mixing **Falgun 7** (Democracy Day), **Baisakh 11** (Loktantra Day) and **Jestha 15** (Republic Day).
11. Saying the **multi-party side** won the referendum of 2037 BS.
12. Mixing the **12-point understanding** (2062) with the **Comprehensive Peace Accord** (2063).
13. Saying **Bidya Devi Bhandari** proclaimed the Constitution; it was Ram Baran Yadav.
14. Mixing **BS and AD**: "1947", "1950" and "2007" mean different things in each era.
