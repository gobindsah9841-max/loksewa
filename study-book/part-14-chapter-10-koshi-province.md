## 10.1 Profile of the province (प्रदेशको परिचय)

#### 1 · Why it matters

The profile is the safest source of a question: the name and the day it was given, the capital, the area, the number of districts and local levels, the population, the neighbours. The official syllabus itself prints a model question on the number of local levels. Learn these figures exactly.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Koshi is the **easternmost** of Nepal's seven provinces. It runs from the hot plain on the Indian border to the top of Sagarmatha on the Chinese border. It has **14 districts**, more than any other province, and **137 local levels**, also more than any other province.

The Constitution made the province on 3 Ashoj 2072, but gave it only a number: Province No. 1. The Constitution left two decisions to each Provincial Assembly: the **capital** (Article 288) and the **name** (Article 295). Each needs **two-thirds** of all the members. The Assembly chose **Biratnagar** as capital in 2076 BS. It took until 2079 BS to agree on the name **Koshi** — the last of the seven provinces to be named. The name comes from the Koshi river, the largest river of Nepal.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Name, capital, founding**

- **Formed:** 3 Ashoj 2072 (20 September 2015), the day the Constitution came into force. Schedule 4 of the Constitution lists its 14 districts.
- **Temporary seat:** the Government of Nepal named **Biratnagar** on 3 Magh 2074 (17 January 2018).
- **Capital:** **Biratnagar** (विराटनगर), Morang. The Provincial Assembly decided it by a two-thirds majority on **23 Baisakh 2076 (6 May 2019)** — Article 288 (2).
- **Name:** **Koshi Province** (कोशी प्रदेश). The Provincial Assembly passed it on **17 Falgun 2079 (1 March 2023)** — Article 295 (2). Of 86 members who voted, **82 voted for** and **4 against**. Two-thirds of the 93 members is 62.
- It was the **last** of the seven provinces to be named. The first Assembly (2074–2079) chose the capital but could not agree on a name; the second Assembly, which first met on 17 Poush 2079 (1 January 2023), named the province two months later.
- ⚖️ **The name is still debated.** Groups that ask for a name based on identity — Kirat, Limbuwan, Kirat–Limbuwan or Sagarmatha — have protested since 2079 BS. An amendment asking for such a name was rejected on the day of the vote.
- 🔄 In **October 2026** the Assembly's rules of procedure, as reported, gained a rule (rule 212) by which **one-third** of the members may register a proposal to change the name. To pass it would still need **two-thirds**.

**Position and boundaries**

- **East:** India — the state of **West Bengal**, and **Sikkim** in the north-east. The **Mechi** river is the boundary in the south-east.
- **South:** India — the state of **Bihar**.
- **North:** China — the **Tibet** Autonomous Region.
- **West:** **Bagmati Province** and **Madhesh Province**.
- **Six districts touch India:** Taplejung, Panchthar, Ilam, Jhapa, Morang, Sunsari. **Three touch China:** Taplejung, Sankhuwasabha, Solukhumbu. **Taplejung touches both.**
- Districts on the western edge: Solukhumbu, Okhaldhunga and Udayapur touch Bagmati; Udayapur and Sunsari touch Madhesh.
- The provincial portal gives the position as **26°22′ to 28°06′ north** and **86°09′ to 88°12′ east**.
- Koshi is one of **four** provinces that touch **both** India and China. The others are Bagmati, Gandaki and Sudurpashchim.

**Area and divisions**

- **Area:** **25,905 sq km** — about **17.6 percent** of Nepal. It is the **second-largest** province, after Karnali (27,984 sq km).
- **Districts:** **14** — the most of any province.
  - **Mountain (3):** Taplejung, Sankhuwasabha, Solukhumbu.
  - **Hill (8):** Panchthar, Ilam, Terhathum, Dhankuta, Bhojpur, Khotang, Okhaldhunga, Udayapur.
  - **Terai (3):** Jhapa, Morang, Sunsari.
- **Local levels:** **137** — the most of any province.
  - **1 metropolitan city** (महानगरपालिका): Biratnagar.
  - **2 sub-metropolitan cities** (उपमहानगरपालिका): **Itahari** and **Dharan**, both in Sunsari.
  - **46 municipalities** (नगरपालिका).
  - **88 rural municipalities** (गाउँपालिका).
  - **1,157 wards.**
- **Morang** has the most local levels (17). **Terhathum** has the fewest (6).
- **Largest district:** Taplejung (3,646 sq km). **Smallest:** Terhathum (679 sq km).

**People** (Census of 2078; census day 9 Mangsir 2078, 25 November 2021)

- **Population:** **4,961,412** — **17.01 percent** of Nepal; the **fourth** of the seven provinces.
- **Women:** 2,544,084 (51.3 percent). **Men:** 2,417,328. There are **95 men for every 100 women**.
- **Households:** 1,191,556.
- **Density:** **192** persons per sq km (Nepal: 198).
- **Literacy:** **79.7 percent** (men 86.1; women 73.6).
- The population was 4,534,943 in the Census of 2068. It grew by about 426,000 in ten years.
- **Most populous district:** Morang (about 1.15 million). **Least populous:** Terhathum (about 89,000).
- **The three Terai districts hold about 62 percent of the people on about 18 percent of the land.** The three mountain districts hold about 8 percent of the people on about 40 percent of the land.
- **Largest cities:** Biratnagar (about 245,000), Itahari (about 198,000), Dharan (about 173,000).

**Seats**

- **House of Representatives:** **28** first-past-the-post constituencies — Morang 6, Jhapa 5, Sunsari 4, Ilam 2, Udayapur 2, and one each in the other nine districts.
- **Provincial Assembly:** **93** members — 56 direct (two for each House constituency) and 37 proportional.
- **National Assembly:** **8** members, like every province.

- **"14 – 137 – 93 – 28":** districts, local levels, Assembly seats, House constituencies.
- **"1 + 2 + 46 + 88 = 137":** one metropolitan city, two sub-metropolitan cities, 46 municipalities, 88 rural municipalities.
- **"3 – 8 – 3":** mountain, hill and Terai districts. The outer numbers are the same.
- **Mountain districts, east to west: "Ta – Sa – So"** — Taplejung, Sankhuwasabha, Solukhumbu. The same three touch China.
- **Terai districts, east to west: "Jha – Mo – Su"** — Jhapa, Morang, Sunsari.
- **"Capital in 76, name in 79":** 23 Baisakh 2076 and 17 Falgun 2079.
- **"82 to 4":** the naming vote.

#### 7 · Exam traps

- **Koshi is not the largest province.** Karnali is. Koshi is second in area and fourth in population.
- **Koshi has the most districts (14) and the most local levels (137)** — but not the most people or the most Assembly seats.
- **137, not 136.** Madhesh has 136 local levels.
- The **capital** was decided in **2076**; the **name** in **2079**. Do not join the two dates.
- **Itahari and Dharan** are sub-metropolitan cities. **Biratnagar** is the only metropolitan city. All three are in Morang or Sunsari; Jhapa has none.
- The headquarters of **Sunsari** is **Inaruwa**, not Itahari or Dharan. The headquarters of **Jhapa** is **Bhadrapur**, not Birtamod or Damak. The headquarters of **Udayapur** is **Gaighat**.
- **Udayapur** is counted as a hill district, though much of it is an inner-Terai valley.
- **Siraha and Saptari** were in the old Eastern Development Region, but they are in **Madhesh Province**.
- The province touches **three** Indian states. The provincial portal names only West Bengal and Bihar; **Sikkim** lies beyond Taplejung and Panchthar.
- A province is named by **two-thirds of all members** of its Assembly, not by a majority of those present, and not by the Federal Parliament.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Give a short introduction to Koshi Province.

**Model answer.** **Position:** Koshi is the easternmost province of Nepal. India lies to its east (West Bengal and Sikkim) and south (Bihar), China to its north, and Bagmati and Madhesh Provinces to its west. **Founding:** it was formed on 3 Ashoj 2072 as Province No. 1. ## 10.2 Physical geography (भौतिक भूगोल)

#### 1 · Why it matters

The syllabus asks for the "geographical condition" of the province first of all. Koshi holds many of Nepal's best-known natural features: the highest peak, the largest river, the first Ramsar site, the highest national park. A question on any of them can be asked as a Koshi question.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Think of the province as a staircase that climbs from south to north.

1. **The plain (तराई)** — Jhapa, Morang and Sunsari. Flat, hot and fertile. Height: about 60 to 300 metres.
2. **The Chure and the inner valley** — low hills behind the plain; the valley of the Triyuga river in Udayapur.
3. **The Mahabharat range and the middle hills** — Ilam, Panchthar, Terhathum, Dhankuta, Bhojpur, Khotang, Okhaldhunga. Tea, cardamom and terraced fields.
4. **The Himalaya** — Taplejung, Sankhuwasabha and Solukhumbu. Snow, glaciers and five peaks higher than 8,000 metres.

Large rivers — the **Tamor**, the **Arun**, the **Dudhkoshi** and the **Sunkoshi** — cut through this staircase and join to make the **Saptakoshi**. The summer rain comes from the Bay of Bengal and reaches the east first, so Koshi is among the wettest parts of Nepal.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Height**

- **Highest point:** **Sagarmatha** (सगरमाथा), **8,848.86 m**, in Solukhumbu — the highest point on earth. The height was announced by Nepal and China on 23 Mangsir 2077 (8 December 2020).
- ⚖️ **Lowest point:** textbooks name **Kechana Kawal** in **Jhapa**, at about 58 to 70 m, as the lowest point of Nepal. Recent reports name Mukhiyapatti Musharniya in Dhanusha (Chapter 1). Learn both.
- So the land rises almost 8,800 metres in less than 200 km. No other province has such a range of height.

**Peaks**

- **Five of Nepal's eight peaks above 8,000 m** stand in Koshi or on its border: **Sagarmatha, Kanchenjunga, Lhotse, Makalu, Cho Oyu**.
- Their world ranks are **1, 3, 4, 5 and 6**. Only K2, the second, is missing from the first six.
- **Kanchenjunga** (कञ्चनजङ्घा), 8,586 m, in **Taplejung**, on the border with Sikkim: third in the world, second in Nepal.
- **Makalu**, in **Sankhuwasabha**: ⚖️ 8,463 m in most Nepali lists; 8,485 m in others.
- **Cho Oyu**, in Solukhumbu: ⚖️ 8,201 m or 8,188 m.
- **Ranges** (हिमशृङ्खला) named in the province: **Mahalangur** (Sagarmatha, Lhotse, Cho Oyu, Makalu), **Kumbhakarna** (Kanchenjunga, Jannu), Umbhek, Lumbasumba and Janak.
- **Passes** to Tibet: **Tiptala** (Olangchung Gola, Taplejung), **Kimathanka** or Popti (Sankhuwasabha), **Nangpa La** (Solukhumbu).
- The eastern meeting point of Nepal, India and China is placed by textbooks at **Jhinsang Chuli** in Taplejung.

**Glaciers and glacial lakes**

- ⚖️ **Khumbu** and **Ngozumpa** glaciers, both in Solukhumbu, are each called Nepal's largest (Chapter 1).
- **Imja** lake (Solukhumbu) is a well-known glacial lake.
- **Glacial lake floods:** **Dig Tsho**, 20 Shrawan 2042 (4 August 1985); **Thame**, 32 Shrawan 2081 (16 August 2024). Both are in Solukhumbu.

**Rivers**

- The **Koshi** is the **largest river of Nepal**. It is called **Saptakoshi** (सप्तकोशी) because it has **seven branches**: Indrawati, Sunkoshi, Tamakoshi, Likhu, Dudhkoshi, Arun and Tamor.
- **Inside Koshi Province:** the **Tamor** (from the Kanchenjunga side), the **Arun** (which enters from Tibet near Kimathanka and is the largest branch) and the **Dudhkoshi** (from the Sagarmatha side). The **Likhu** and the **Sunkoshi** run along the western edge. The Tamakoshi and the Indrawati are in Bagmati Province.
- The **Sunkoshi, the Arun and the Tamor meet at Tribeni**, above **Chatara**. From there the river is the Saptakoshi. It passes **Barahakshetra**, enters the plain at Chatara and crosses into India at the **Koshi Barrage**.
- **Other rivers:** the **Mechi** (the eastern boundary of Nepal), the **Kankai** (called the Mai in Ilam), the Ratuwa and the Bakraha in Jhapa and Morang; the **Triyuga** in Udayapur.
- The Koshi basin has a hydropower potential of **22,350 megawatts** in the usual estimate (Chapter 1).

**Lakes, ponds and waterfalls**

- **Gokyo lakes** (Solukhumbu) — high lakes near Cho Oyu; a Ramsar site.
- **Mai Pokhari** (Ilam) — a hill pond and a Ramsar site.
- **Salpa Pokhari** (Bhojpur), **Sabha Pokhari** (Sankhuwasabha), **Dudhkunda** (Solukhumbu) and **Timbung Pokhari** (Taplejung–Panchthar) are holy mountain ponds.
- **Betana** wetland (Morang) and **Barju** lake (Sunsari) lie in the plain.
- ⚖️ **Hyatrung waterfall** (ह्यात्रुङ झरना), **Terhathum**, is given as about **365 m** and called the highest in Nepal by the provincial portal. Pachal waterfall in Kalikot is also so called (Chapter 1).
- **Todke** waterfall (Ilam), **Pokali** waterfall (Okhaldhunga) and **Namaste** waterfall (Bhedetar, Dhankuta) are other well-known falls.

**Climate**

- The monsoon enters Nepal from the east. Rain is heavy in Jhapa and Ilam and less in the high valleys behind the Himalaya.
- Yearly rain, as given by a reference page: **Damak** about 2,618 mm; **Ilam** about 2,552 mm; **Bhadrapur** about 2,352 mm; **Biratnagar** about 1,550 mm.
- Climate changes with height: hot and wet in the plain, mild in the hills, cold in the mountains, and polar on the high peaks.

**Forest and wildlife**

- **Forest covers about 43 percent** of the province, by the provincial portal.
- The **Tinjure–Milke–Jaljale** ridge (तीनजुरे–मिल्के–जलजले), on the borders of **Terhathum, Sankhuwasabha and Taplejung**, is called the "capital of rhododendron". ⚖️ Counts of rhododendron species there vary: 26 in one study, 28 on other pages.
- The only wild herd of **wild water buffalo** (अर्ना) in Nepal lives at **Koshi Tappu**. Counts: **63** in 1976; 433 in 2016; 441 in 2018; **498** in 2021. Fifteen animals were moved to Chitwan in 2017.
- The **red panda** (हाब्रे) lives in the forests of Ilam, Panchthar and Taplejung. The **snow leopard** lives in the Kanchenjunga and Sagarmatha areas.
- Koshi Tappu has more than **500 kinds of birds**.

**Protected areas**

- **Sagarmatha National Park** (1976; 1,148 sq km; Solukhumbu) — the highest national park of Nepal; a **World Heritage Site** (natural) since **1979**.
- **Makalu Barun National Park** (1992; 1,500 sq km; Sankhuwasabha and Solukhumbu).
- **Kanchenjunga Conservation Area** (1997; 2,035 sq km; Taplejung) — managed by the local community.
- **Koshi Tappu Wildlife Reserve** (1976; 175 sq km; Sunsari and Udayapur, with Saptari in Madhesh) — Nepal's **first Ramsar site**, listed in **1987**.
- **Three of Nepal's ten Ramsar sites** are in Koshi: Koshi Tappu (1987), Gokyo (2007) and Mai Pokhari (2008).

**Disasters**

- **Udayapur earthquake:** 5 Bhadra 2045 (21 August 1988). **721** people died in Nepal. ⚖️ Its size is given as 6.9 or 6.5. Dharan was badly damaged.
- **Koshi flood:** on 2 Bhadra 2065 (18 August 2008) the eastern embankment broke at **Kusaha, Sunsari**. The river left its course and flooded Sunsari and a large part of Bihar.
- 🔄 **Boat accident:** on 8 Kartik 2083 (25 October 2026) a passenger boat sank in the Saptakoshi at Tribeni; three people died and three were missing. The provincial government formed an inquiry committee on 10 Kartik 2083 (27 October 2026).

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** Describe the main physical features of Koshi Province.

**Model answer.** **Landform:** the province rises from about 60 metres in Jhapa to 8,848.86 metres at Sagarmatha. It has three belts: the Terai (Jhapa, Morang, Sunsari), the hills with the Chure, the Mahabharat range and the Triyuga valley (eight districts), and the Himalaya (Taplejung, Sankhuwasabha, Solukhumbu). **Peaks:** five of Nepal's eight peaks above 8,000 metres are here — Sagarmatha, Kanchenjunga, Lhotse, Makalu and Cho Oyu — with the Khumbu and Ngozumpa glaciers and lakes such as Imja and Gokyo. **Rivers:** the Tamor, the Arun and the Dudhkoshi drain the mountains; the Sunkoshi, the Arun and the Tamor meet at Tribeni to form the Saptakoshi, the largest river of Nepal; the Mechi, the Kankai and the Triyuga are smaller rivers. **Climate:** the monsoon arrives from the east, so rain is heavy in Jhapa and Ilam; climate changes with height from hot to polar. **Forest and wildlife:** forest covers about 43 percent of the land; the arna of Koshi Tappu, the red panda of the eastern hills, the snow leopard and the rhododendron of Tinjure–Milke–Jaljale are well known. **Protected areas:** Sagarmatha National Park (a World Heritage Site), Makalu Barun National Park, Kanchenjunga Conservation Area and Koshi Tappu Wildlife Reserve (the first Ramsar site). **Hazards:** floods of the Koshi, glacial lake floods, landslides and earthquakes.

## 10.3 History and archaeology (इतिहास र पुरातत्त्व)

#### 1 · Why it matters

History gives the "firsts" of the province: the first modern factory of Nepal, the first Chief Minister, the day of naming. It also explains the present: why the name was disputed, why Dhankuta has a High Court bench, why Siraha and Saptari are not in Koshi. Unit 1.2 (history) and unit 1.5 (Koshi Province) can both ask these points.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

The story has six steps.

1. **Kirat country.** The eastern hills were the home of the Kirat peoples — the Rai and the Limbu — under their own chiefs.
2. **Sen kings.** In the centuries before unification, Sen kings ruled the eastern plain and the lower hills from **Chaudandi** and **Bijayapur**.
3. **Unification.** Gorkha won the two kingdoms in 1773–1774 and made peace with the Limbu chiefs by an oath on salt and water.
4. **A fixed border.** The Treaty of Sugauli (1816) made the **Mechi** river the eastern boundary of Nepal.
5. **Factory town and movements.** In the Rana years Biratnagar became the first factory town. The labour movement and the democratic movement met there. Later came the Jhapa movement and the Chhintang incident.
6. **From region to province.** Under the Panchayat the area was the Eastern Development Region, with its centre at Dhankuta. In 2072 BS it became Province No. 1, and in 2079 BS Koshi Province.

#### 3 · Key facts

**The old Kirat country**

- The eastern hills are the old **Kirat** country (किरात प्रदेश). Tradition divides it into three parts:
  - **Wallo Kirat** (वल्लो किरात, "near Kirat") — west of the Dudhkoshi;
  - **Majh Kirat** (माझ किरात, "middle Kirat") — between the Dudhkoshi and the Arun; the homeland of the **Rai**, also called Khambuwan;
  - **Pallo Kirat** (पल्लो किरात, "far Kirat") — east of the Arun; the homeland of the **Limbu**, called **Limbuwan** (लिम्बुवान).
- The sacred story of the Kirat, the **Mundhum**, names places of the province such as **Tuwachung** (Khotang) and **Khuwalung**, a rock in the Saptakoshi.
- The plain was the old country of **Morang**. Tradition links it with King **Virat** of the Mahabharata; the name Biratnagar recalls him.

**Sen kingdoms and unification**

- The **Sen** kings of the east were a branch of the Sen house of **Palpa** and **Makwanpur** (Chapter 2). They ruled from two seats: **Chaudandi** (चौदण्डी), in today's **Udayapur**, and **Bijayapur** (विजयपुर), on a hill beside today's **Dharan**.
- The Kirat chiefs of the hills kept their own rule under them.
- **Gorkha won Chaudandi and Bijayapur in 1773–1774**, in the last years of Prithvi Narayan Shah. **Abhiman Singh Basnyat** was a commander of the eastern campaign.
- The fighting with the Limbu chiefs (1771–1774) ended in **1774** with an oath taken on salt and water at Bijayapur. It is remembered as the **salt-water treaty** (नुन-पानी सन्धि).
  - The Limbu chiefs accepted the king of Gorkha.
  - They kept their **kipat** (किपट) land and their own headmen, the **subba**.
- **Kipat** was land held in common by a clan; it could not pass to outsiders. It lasted in Limbuwan until the land reforms of the 1960s.

**The border**

- After the east was won, the kingdom reached the **Tista** river.
- By the **Treaty of Sugauli** (in force from 4 March 1816) Nepal gave up the land between the Mechi and the Tista. **The Mechi became the eastern boundary** (Chapter 2).

**Factory town and movements**

- **Biratnagar Jute Mill** (विराटनगर जुट मिल), **1993 BS (1936)** — the **first modern factory** of Nepal, in the time of Juddha Shamsher.
- **Strike at the mill:** 21 Falgun 2003 (4 March 1947) — the first large labour movement. Men who later led the country's politics, among them **Girija Prasad Koirala** and **Man Mohan Adhikari**, took part.
- **Revolution of 2007 BS:** the Liberation Army (मुक्ति सेना) of the Nepali Congress fought across the east; Bhojpur and Biratnagar were among the places it took.
- **Koshi Agreement:** 25 April 1954, with India — the Koshi Barrage and embankments; revised in 1966 (Chapter 8).
- **Jhapa movement:** 2028 BS — an early communist uprising (Chapter 2).
- **Chhintang incident:** 2036 BS, in **Dhankuta** (Chapter 2).
- **Refugees from Bhutan:** from the early 1990s they lived in camps in **Jhapa and Morang**. Most were later resettled in other countries.

**Zones, region, province**

- On 1 Baisakh 2018 (13 April 1961) Nepal was divided into 14 zones and 75 districts. The area of today's province lay in three zones:
  - **Mechi** — Taplejung, Panchthar, Ilam, Jhapa;
  - **Koshi** — Sankhuwasabha, Terhathum, Dhankuta, Bhojpur, Morang, Sunsari;
  - **Sagarmatha** — Solukhumbu, Okhaldhunga, Khotang, Udayapur, with Siraha and Saptari.
- In **2029 BS** these three zones became the **Eastern Development Region** (पूर्वाञ्चल विकास क्षेत्र): **16 districts**; regional centre **Dhankuta**.
- **Today's province = the Eastern Development Region minus Siraha and Saptari.** Those two went to Madhesh Province.
- So "Koshi" was once the name of a **zone of six districts**. The province of 14 districts is much larger.

**The province since 2072 BS** 🔄

- **3 Ashoj 2072 (20 September 2015):** Province No. 1 formed.
- **10 and 21 Mangsir 2074 (26 November and 7 December 2017):** first election. The CPN (UML) won **51 of 93** seats, a majority by itself.
- **22 Magh 2074 (5 February 2018):** first meeting of the Provincial Assembly.
- **28 Magh 2074 (11 February 2018):** **Pradeep Kumar Bhandari** elected the first Speaker.
- **2 Falgun 2074 (14 February 2018):** **Sher Dhan Rai** (CPN-UML) became the **first Chief Minister**. The **first Province Chief** was **Govinda Subba**, appointed in Magh 2074.
- **23 Baisakh 2076 (6 May 2019):** Biratnagar made capital.
- **4 Mangsir 2079 (20 November 2022):** second election. No party won a majority.
- **17 Falgun 2079 (1 March 2023):** named Koshi.
- **2080 BS:** the Chief Minister changed three times between Ashad and Ashoj. The Supreme Court twice set aside the appointment of **Uddhav Thapa** of the Nepali Congress; the dispute was about how his majority of 47 had been counted.
- **27 Baisakh 2081 (9 May 2024):** **Hikmat Kumar Karki** became Chief Minister for the third time.
- **24 Bhadra 2082 (9 September 2025):** public buildings in Biratnagar were burnt during the Gen Z movement (Chapter 9).
- **27–28 Ashoj 2083 (13–14 October 2026):** the seven Chief Ministers met at Biratnagar and issued the **Koshi Declaration, 2083** (Chapter 4).
- **24 Kartik 2083 (10 November 2026):** Parshuram Khapung completed five years as Province Chief — the first Province Chief in Nepal to do so.

**Archaeology and old places**

- **Kichakbadh** (किचकवध), near Bhadrapur in **Jhapa**: excavation found old brick structures, dated to about the second century BC. Tradition says Bhima killed Kichaka here.
- **Bijayapur**, Dharan: the hill seat of the Sen kings; the temples of **Budha Subba** and **Dantakali** stand nearby.
- **Chaudandigadhi**, Udayapur: the fort of the Sen kings. A municipality now carries the name.
- **Bhediyari**, Biratnagar: ruins that tradition links to the palace of King Virat.
- **Barahakshetra**, Sunsari: a pilgrimage place named in old religious texts.
- **Dhankuta** bazaar: the old hill town that became the regional centre.
- **Chainpur**, Sankhuwasabha: an old hill town known for brassware.
- **Halesi** caves, Khotang; **Tengboche** monastery, Solukhumbu.

- The **first modern factory** is the **Biratnagar Jute Mill (1936)**. The **first bank**, Nepal Bank Limited (1937), is in Kathmandu.
- The regional centre of the old Eastern Development Region was **Dhankuta**, not Biratnagar. Biratnagar is the capital of the **province**.
- The old **Koshi Zone** had six districts. **Koshi Province** has fourteen.
- **Chhintang** is in **Dhankuta**, not in Jhapa. The **Jhapa movement** was in 2028 BS; Chhintang in 2036 BS.
- The **first Province Chief** was **Govinda Subba**; the **first Chief Minister** was **Sher Dhan Rai**. Parshuram Khapung was the **third** Province Chief.
- The **Koshi Agreement** (1954) is an agreement with India about the river. The **Koshi Declaration** (2083) is a statement of the seven Chief Ministers.
- The eastern boundary before 1816 was the **Tista**; after it, the **Mechi**.

## 10.4 Society and culture (समाज र संस्कृति)

#### 1 · Why it matters

The syllabus asks for the "social, religious and cultural condition" of the province — three of its five words. Questions ask which people, which language, which festival, which holy place belongs to which district. The language law of 2083 is also fresh current affairs.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Koshi is a province of many peoples, and no one of them is large. The biggest group, the Chhetri, is only about one person in seven.

- In the **hills** live the Kirat peoples — **Rai** west of the Arun and **Limbu** east of it — with Chhetri, Brahman, Tamang, Magar and Newar.
- In the **high valleys** live the **Sherpa**.
- In the **plain** live Tharu, Rajbanshi, Dhimal, Meche, Santhal, Maithili-speaking groups and Muslims, with many families who came down from the hills.

Nepali is the mother tongue of fewer than half the people. The **Kirat religion** has its home here: about one person in six follows it. Because of this variety, the province brought thirteen languages besides Nepali into government use in 2083 BS.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Peoples** (Census of 2078; shares as given by a reference page that quotes the census)

- **Chhetri 15.0 percent; Brahman (Hill) 11.6; Rai 10.2; Limbu 7.8;** Tamang 4.6; Tharu 4.2; Musalman about 4; Magar 4.0; Newar 3.6; Kami (Bishwakarma) 3.4.
- The four largest groups together are fewer than half the people.
- **Rai and Limbu together are about 18 percent.**
- **Limbu** (Yakthung): Taplejung, Panchthar, Ilam, Terhathum, Dhankuta — east of the Arun.
- **Rai** (Khambu): Bhojpur, Khotang, Solukhumbu, Okhaldhunga, Udayapur, Sankhuwasabha.
- **Sherpa:** Solukhumbu and the high valleys of Sankhuwasabha and Taplejung.
- **Yakkha:** Sankhuwasabha and Dhankuta. **Sunuwar:** Okhaldhunga. **Lepcha:** Ilam.
- **Dhimal, Meche, Rajbanshi, Tajpuriya and Santhal (Satar):** Jhapa and Morang.
- **Tharu:** Sunsari, Morang and Udayapur.
- Of the ten indigenous peoples classed as **endangered** (Chapter 6), three live in Koshi: **Lepcha, Meche and Kisan**.

**Languages**

- Mother tongues (Census of 2078): **Nepali 45.3 percent; Maithili 11.7; Limbu 6.7;** Tharu 4.4; Tamang 3.5; Magar 2.9; Bantawa 2.7; Rajbanshi 2.6; Urdu 2.3.
- "Rai" is not one language. **Bantawa, Chamling**, Kulung, Thulung and others are separate languages of the Rai.
- The **Limbu** language is written in the **Sirijanga** script (Chapter 6).
- The **Language Commission** recommended two languages for the province: **Maithili and Limbu**.
- 🔄 **The language law of 2083.** The provincial Act was authenticated on 11 Kartik 2083 (28 October 2026). From **25 Kartik 2083 (11 November 2026)** it brought **13 languages** besides Nepali into government use: **Maithili, Limbu, Tharu, Tamang, Magar, Bantawa, Urdu, Rajbanshi, Rai, Nepal Bhasa, Chamling, Sherpa and Santhali**, as reported.
  - The day chosen was the birth anniversary of **Mahaguru Phalgunanda**.
  - A yearly Province Language Day was also announced.
  - Koshi is the **second** province to use its own languages in government. Bagmati was the first, from 24 Baisakh 2081 (6 May 2024).

**Religion** (Census of 2078, rounded)

- **Hindu about 67 percent; Kirat about 17; Buddhist about 9; Islam about 4; Christian about 2.**
- In the whole country the Kirat religion is 3.17 percent. Koshi is its home.
- The **Kirat religion** honours nature and the ancestors. Its sacred story is the **Mundhum** (मुन्धुम), handed down by word of mouth. The Limbu priest is the **Phedangma**; the Kirat place of worship is the **Manghim** (माङहिम).
- **Mahaguru Phalgunanda Lingden** (महागुरु फाल्गुनन्द):
  - born on **25 Kartik 1942 BS** at Chukchinamba, **Ilam**; died in 2005 BS;
  - reformed Kirat society and religion, and worked for the Limbu language and script;
  - his code of social reform, the *Satya Dharma Muchulka*, is dated 24 Baisakh 1988 BS;
  - declared a **national hero** (राष्ट्रिय विभूति) on 16 Mangsir 2066 (1 December 2009);
  - 25 Kartik 2083 was his 142nd birth anniversary.

**Festivals and dances**

- **Ubhauli** (Baisakh full moon) and **Udhauli** (Mangsir full moon) — the Kirat festivals of going up and coming down; the Rai dance the **Sakela** (साकेला).
- **Chasok Tangnam** (चासोक तङ्नाम) — the harvest festival of the Limbu, at the Mangsir full moon.
- **Dhan Nach** (धान नाच) and the **Chyabrung** (च्याब्रुङ) drum dance — Limbu.
- **Mani Rimdu** at Tengboche, **Dumje** and **Gyalpo Lhosar** — Sherpa.
- **Chhath**, **Jitiya** and **Siruwa** (the new year of the Tharu and Rajbanshi) — the plain.
- **Fairs:** Kartik full moon and Maghe Sankranti at **Barahakshetra**; Bala Chaturdashi, Shivaratri and Ram Navami at **Halesi**; Maghe Sankranti at **Kankai**.

**Holy places**

- **Pathibhara Devi** (पाथीभरा), Taplejung — a hill-top goddess visited from all over Nepal and from India. Kirat groups call the hill **Mukkumlung**.
- **Barahakshetra** (वराहक्षेत्र), Sunsari — where the Koka meets the Koshi; one of the **four dhams** of Nepal, with Pashupat Kshetra, Mukti Kshetra and Ruru Kshetra (Chapter 6).
- **Halesi Mahadev** (हलेसी), Khotang — caves holy to Hindus, Buddhists and the Kirat.
- **Budha Subba**, **Dantakali** and **Pindeshwar** — Dharan, Sunsari.
- **Ramdhuni** — Sunsari. **Chhintang Devi** — Dhankuta.
- **Mai Pokhari** — Ilam. **Larumba**, Ilam — a centre of the Kirat religion.
- **Arjundhara**, **Satasidham** and **Kankai Mai** — Jhapa.
- **Salpa Pokhari** — Bhojpur. **Sabha Pokhari** — Sankhuwasabha. **Dudhkunda** — Solukhumbu.
- **Tengboche Monastery** — Solukhumbu, the best-known monastery of the Sherpa.
- **Sagarmatha National Park** is a World Heritage Site (Chapter 6).

**Crafts and products of culture**

- **Dhaka cloth** of **Terhathum**; the **khukuri** of **Bhojpur** (Chapter 6); brassware of **Chainpur**, Sankhuwasabha.

**Well-known persons**

- **Siddhicharan Shrestha** — the Yuga Kavi (युगकवि); born in **Okhaldhunga**.
- **Pasang Lhamu Sherpa** — of **Solukhumbu**; the first Nepali woman to climb Sagarmatha, on 10 Baisakh 2050 (22 April 1993); a national hero.
- **Girija Prasad Koirala** — of Biratnagar; Prime Minister several times between 2048 and 2065 BS.
- **K.P. Sharma Oli** — born in **Terhathum**; his political home is Jhapa.
- **Bidya Devi Bhandari** — born in **Bhojpur**; the first woman President.
- **Sushila Karki** — of **Biratnagar**; the first woman Chief Justice and, in 2082 BS, the first woman Prime Minister.
- **Dr Sanduk Ruit** — born at Olangchung Gola, **Taplejung**; eye surgeon; Ramon Magsaysay Award, 2006.
- **Kami Rita Sherpa** — of Thame, **Solukhumbu**; 32 ascents of Sagarmatha, the last on 3 Jestha 2083 (17 May 2026) 🔄.
- **Lhakpa Sherpa** (Sankhuwasabha) — the woman with the most ascents of Sagarmatha. **Mira Rai** (Bhojpur) — trail runner.

**Education and health**

- **Literacy:** 79.7 percent — third among the provinces, after Bagmati (82.1) and Gandaki (81.7). Koshi has been declared a literate province (Chapter 6).
- **Universities:** **Purbanchal University** (2050 BS; Gothgaun, Morang); **B.P. Koirala Institute of Health Sciences**, Dharan; **Manmohan Technical University**, Morang, set up by the province.
- **Public health institutions**, as given by a reference page: 18 hospitals, 41 primary health centres and 648 health posts.

**Recent social events** 🔄

- **Pathibhara cable car:** identity groups opposed a cable car to the Pathibhara (Mukkumlung) hill. There was a clash on 12 Magh 2081 (25 January 2025). Soon after, a six-point agreement with the government paused the work. The later course was not followed.
- **Communal violence:** on 10 Shrawan 2083 (26 July 2026) a clash broke out at Dewanganj, **Sunsari**, during a religious procession; three people died in all (Chapter 9).

## 10.5 Economy (अर्थतन्त्र)

#### 1 · Why it matters

"Economic condition" is one of the five words of unit 1.5. Unit 1.4 (economy and development infrastructure) and unit 1.10 can also turn to the province. Tea, cardamom, the jute mill, Arun III and the border points at Biratnagar and Kakarbhitta are the points most easily asked.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Koshi is the **second-largest provincial economy** of Nepal. It gives about one-sixth of the country's output. It stands on four legs.

1. **Farming.** Paddy, maize and jute in the plain; tea, cardamom, ginger and potato in the hills. Almost all of Nepal's tea and most of its cardamom grow here.
2. **Industry.** The oldest industrial belt of Nepal runs from Biratnagar through Duhabi and Itahari to Dharan.
3. **Services.** Trade with India passes through Biratnagar and Kakarbhitta. Visitors come to Sagarmatha, Ilam and the holy places.
4. **Water power.** The Arun, the Tamor and the Dudhkoshi give the province more hydropower every year.

Services are already the largest part — about half. Farming is about one-third.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Size and structure** 🔄 (2082/83, estimates as reported)

- **Share of Nepal's GDP: 15.8 percent** — second, after Bagmati (36.7 percent).
- **Growth: 3.13 percent** (2.72 percent the year before).
- **GDP per person: USD 1,410** — third among the provinces, and below the national figure of USD 1,513.
- **Structure of the provincial GDP:** agriculture **32.5 percent**; industry **17.7 percent**; services **49.8 percent**.
- **Poverty: 17.19 percent** (survey of 2079/80) — below the national rate of 20.27 percent.
- In 2081/82 the provincial government received Rs 33.66 billion and spent Rs 28.18 billion, by the provincial economic survey as reported.

**Farming**

- **Tea** (चिया): about **99 percent** of Nepal's tea is grown in Koshi (99.2 percent as reported).
  - Tea was first planted at **Ilam** in 1920 BS (1863) (Chapter 5).
  - The hills — Ilam, Panchthar, Dhankuta, Terhathum — make leaf ("orthodox") tea. The plain of **Jhapa** makes the stronger CTC tea.
  - **Jhapa** grew about three-quarters of the country's tea in 2081/82: 19.6 of 26.1 million kg.
- **Large cardamom** (अलैँची): ⚖️ one report gives Koshi **94.2 percent** of Nepal's crop; others give 80 to 90 percent.
  - Main districts: **Taplejung**, Panchthar, Ilam, Sankhuwasabha.
  - About 99 percent of the exports go to India.
  - Cardamom was Nepal's second-largest export in 2082/83 (Chapter 5).
- **Shares of the national crop**, as reported: ginger 34.8 percent; potato 33.1; maize 28.3; paddy 21.5.
- **Jhapa** was the first district of Nepal in paddy, maize, potato and tea in 2081/82.
- **Jute** (जुट): Morang, Sunsari and Jhapa.
- **Other products:** broom grass (अम्रिसो), round chilli (अकबरे खुर्सानी), milk and cheese in Ilam; rudraksha in Bhojpur and Sankhuwasabha.
- **Irrigation:** the Sunsari–Morang canal takes water from the Koshi at Chatara; the Kankai canal waters Jhapa.

**Industry and investment**

- The **Sunsari–Morang industrial corridor** — Biratnagar, Duhabi, Itahari, Dharan — is the oldest industrial belt of Nepal (Chapter 5).
- **Biratnagar Jute Mill**, 1993 BS (1936): the first modern factory (topic 10.3).
- **Dharan Industrial Estate** (Chapter 5); **Udayapur Cement**, a public company that uses the limestone of Udayapur; tea factories in Ilam and Jhapa.
- 🔄 **First Koshi Province Investment Summit:** Biratnagar, **18–19 Baisakh 2082 (1–2 May 2025)**. It showed 71 projects worth Rs 173.49 billion; memoranda were signed for 46 projects worth Rs 152.16 billion.

**Trade and border points**

- **Biratnagar** (Jogbani on the Indian side): the **Integrated Check Post** was opened on **7 Magh 2076 (21 January 2020)** by the Prime Ministers of Nepal and India by video. It is the **second** such post on the Nepal–India border; the first is at Birgunj (2018).
- **Kakarbhitta** (Jhapa): the eastern gate of Nepal, and the road towards Bangladesh, which lies beyond a strip of India about 27 km wide (Chapter 8).
- Smaller customs points: Bhadrapur (Jhapa), Pashupatinagar (Ilam) and Bhantabari (Sunsari).
- **Towards China:** Kimathanka (Sankhuwasabha) and Olangchung Gola (Taplejung) (Chapter 8).
- **Railway:** a line from Bathnaha, near Jogbani, to Katahari, near Biratnagar, has been built in stages with Indian help.

**Energy** 🔄 (end of Ashad 2083, from a report of Nepal Rastra Bank's Biratnagar office, as reported in the press)

- **Installed capacity: 994.0 megawatts** — **64 hydropower plants** with 977.2 megawatts, and solar plants with 16.8 megawatts (Morang 6.8; Jhapa 10).
- A year earlier it was 786.7 megawatts. **Seven new hydropower plants** added 206.2 megawatts in 2082/83, as reported.
- **By district:** **Solukhumbu 349.8 megawatts** (10 plants) — the most; **Ilam 200.8** (18 plants — the most plants); Sankhuwasabha 136.7; Taplejung 98.5; Panchthar 71.9; Okhaldhunga 65.1; Bhojpur 31.9; Terhathum 12.3; Khotang 10.2.
- **Arun III** (अरुण तेस्रो): **900 megawatts**; **Sankhuwasabha**; built by an Indian public company (Chapter 8).
  - Work began in Baisakh 2075.
  - Cost: about Rs 188 billion.
  - **75.81 percent** built at the end of Ashad 2083.
  - Target for completion: Poush 2085.
- **In preparation:** **Upper Arun** (about 1,060 megawatts) and **Dudhkoshi** storage project (635 megawatts).
- The second periodic plan aims at **2,200 megawatts** by 2085/86 (topic 10.6).
- The Koshi basin can give 22,350 megawatts in the usual estimate (Chapter 1).

**Tourism**

- **Mountains:** the Sagarmatha region — Lukla, Namche, Tengboche, Kalapatthar, the base camp, Gokyo; the Kanchenjunga and Makalu base camps.
- **Hills:** the tea gardens of **Ilam** — Kanyam, Antu Danda (sunrise), Mai Pokhari; **Bhedetar** and Hile in Dhankuta; Tinjure–Milke–Jaljale.
- **Plain:** bird-watching at **Koshi Tappu**; the Koshi Barrage.
- **Pilgrimage:** Pathibhara, Halesi, Barahakshetra (topic 10.4).
- 🔄 The Policy and Programme for 2083/84 announced:
  - a **Koshi Province Visit Year** from Shrawan 2083 to Ashad 2085;
  - an integrated tourism master plan;
  - a request that the province be allowed to give climbing permits for peaks below 6,000 m;
  - the **Mundhum Trail**, a walking route through the Kirat hills;
  - a road "from Kechana Kawal to Sagarmatha", joining the lowest place to the highest.

**Transport**

- **Mahendra (East–West) Highway:** begins at **Kakarbhitta**, Jhapa.
- **Mechi Highway:** Jhapa – Ilam – Phidim – Taplejung.
- **Koshi Highway:** Biratnagar – Itahari – Dharan – Dhankuta – Hile – Khandbari, and on towards **Kimathanka** on the Chinese border; about 320 km by a reference page. It is the north–south corridor of the east.
- **Mid-Hill (Pushpalal) Highway:** begins at **Chiyo Bhanjyang**, Panchthar (Chapter 5).
- **Madan Bhandari Highway:** begins at **Bahundangi**, Jhapa.
- **Postal (Hulaki) Highway:** begins in Jhapa.
- **Airports:** a reference page lists **13**, among them **Biratnagar**, **Bhadrapur** (Chandragadhi), **Tumlingtar**, **Lukla** (Tenzing–Hillary Airport), Phaplu, Syangboche, Taplejung (Suketar), Bhojpur, Lamidanda and Rumjatar.
- 🔄 The province wants Biratnagar airport to become a regional international airport (Policy and Programme for 2083/84).

**Minerals**

- **Limestone:** Udayapur. **Copper:** Wapsa, Solukhumbu (Chapter 1). Gemstones such as tourmaline are found in Taplejung and Sankhuwasabha.

#### 7 · Exam traps

- Koshi is the **second** economy, not the first; and its income per person is **below** the national figure.
- **Services**, not farming, are the largest part of the provincial GDP.
- Tea was **first planted in Ilam**, but **Jhapa** grows the most today.
- **Arun III is in Sankhuwasabha**, it is **900** megawatts, and it is **not yet finished**. Do not add it to the 994 megawatts.
- **Solukhumbu** has the most installed capacity; **Ilam** has the most plants. Morang and Jhapa have solar plants only.
- The Integrated Check Post at **Biratnagar** is the **second** (2020). The first is at **Birgunj** (2018).
- **Kakarbhitta** is in **Jhapa**; **Jogbani** is the Indian town opposite **Biratnagar**.
- The **Koshi Highway** runs north from Biratnagar. The **Mechi Highway** runs north from Jhapa. The **Mahendra Highway** runs west from Kakarbhitta.
- The **Biratnagar Jute Mill** is in **Morang**, not Sunsari.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** What are the main features, the potential and the problems of the economy of Koshi Province?

**Model answer.** **Features:** Koshi is the second-largest provincial economy, with 15.8 percent of Nepal's GDP in 2082/83, growth of 3.13 percent and USD 1,410 per person. Services give 49.8 percent of its output, agriculture 32.5 and industry 17.7. Poverty is 17.19 percent. **Farming:** it grows about 99 percent of Nepal's tea and most of its cardamom, and large shares of ginger, potato, maize and paddy; Jhapa is the leading farm district. **Industry and trade:** the Sunsari–Morang corridor, begun with the Biratnagar Jute Mill of 1993 BS, is Nepal's oldest industrial belt; Biratnagar and Kakarbhitta are the border gates. **Energy:** 994 megawatts were installed by Ashad 2083, and Arun III (900 megawatts) is three-quarters built. **Potential:** hydropower of the Arun, Tamor and Dudhkoshi; tourism from Sagarmatha to Ilam and Koshi Tappu; high-value hill crops; trade with India, Bangladesh and China through its border points. **Problems:** growth is slow; income per person is below the national figure; factories face weak demand, unreliable power and competition from imports; young people leave for work abroad; hill roads are poor; floods, landslides and glacial lakes threaten life and projects; capital spending is low. **Way forward:** finish Arun III and the north–south Koshi Highway, process tea and cardamom at home, carry out the investment promised in 2082, and use the Visit Year to spread tourism beyond Sagarmatha.

## 10.6 Government and institutions (प्रदेश सरकार र संस्था)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Unit 1.10 names the **provincial periodic plan**, and this topic holds it. The names of the Chief Minister, the Speaker and the ministers, the size of the budget and the seats of the Assembly are also current affairs (unit 1.9). A candidate for a provincial job should know the government he or she wishes to serve.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

A province has its own law-making house and its own executive (Chapter 4).

- The **Provincial Assembly** makes the laws and the budget. In Koshi it has **93 members**.
- The **Province Chief** stands for the federal government. The President appoints him or her.
- The **Chief Minister** and the **Council of Ministers** run the government. They need the support of the Assembly.
- The **courts** are not provincial. The High Court at Biratnagar is part of the single court system of Nepal.

No party has had a majority in the Assembly since 2079 BS, so governments are coalitions, and they change when partners change. The province plans its work for five years at a time (the **periodic plan**) and one year at a time (the **budget**, presented on 1 Ashad).

#### 3 · Key facts

**Provincial Assembly** (प्रदेश सभा)

- **93 members:** 56 first-past-the-post and 37 proportional.
- **Majority: 47. Two-thirds: 62.** The Council of Ministers may have at most **18** members (20 percent of the Assembly).
- **Second Assembly:** elected on 4 Mangsir 2079 (20 November 2022); first met on 17 Poush 2079 (1 January 2023); its term ends on **4 Mangsir 2084 (19 November 2027)**.
- **Seats won in 2079:** CPN (UML) 40; Nepali Congress 29; CPN (Maoist Centre) 13; Rastriya Prajatantra Party 6; CPN (Unified Socialist) 4; Janata Samajbadi Party 1.
- 🔄 **Seats in Kartik 2083:** CPN (UML) **40**; Nepali Congress **29**; Nepali Communist Party **17**; Rastriya Prajatantra Party **5**; Janata Samajbadi Party **1**; vacant 1.
- 🔄 **Speaker:** **Ambar Bahadur Bista** (Rastriya Prajatantra Party), since 24 Poush 2080 (9 January 2024). **Deputy Speaker:** **Sirjana Danuwar** (CPN-UML).
- The Assembly has **seven** subject committees: Justice, Administration and Legislation; Good Governance and Planning; Finance; Public Accounts; Social Development; Industry, Tourism and Environment; Natural Resources and Infrastructure Development.
- **First Assembly (2074–2079):** CPN (UML) 51; Nepali Congress 21; CPN (Maoist Centre) 15; others 6. Speaker: Pradeep Kumar Bhandari.

**Province Chief** (प्रदेश प्रमुख)

- Appointed by the President for five years (Article 163; Chapter 4).
- **Three so far:**
  - **Govinda Subba** — appointed in Magh 2074; removed in Kartik 2076;
  - **Somnath Adhikari** — appointed on 18 Kartik 2076 (4 November 2019); removed before his term ended;
  - **Parshuram Khapung** — appointed on 23 Kartik 2078 (9 November 2021); term ended on **24 Kartik 2083 (10 November 2026)**.
- Khapung is the **first Province Chief in Nepal to complete five years**. One report counts seven changes of government in his time.
- 🔄 No report of a successor was found on 12 November 2026.

**Chief Minister and Council of Ministers** 🔄

- **Chief Minister:** **Hikmat Kumar Karki** of the CPN (UML), a member from Jhapa.
  - First appointed on 25 Poush 2079 (9 January 2023).
  - Appointed for the third time on **27 Baisakh 2081 (9 May 2024)**, under Article 168 (5), on the signatures of 52 members.
- **Coalition:** the Nepali Congress was in the government until **27 Shrawan 2083 (12 August 2026)**, when its four ministers and one minister of state resigned. On **1 Bhadra 2083 (17 August 2026)** the **Nepali Communist Party** joined.
- **Council of Ministers:** **11** members — the Chief Minister, 8 ministers and 2 ministers of state.
- **Vote of confidence:** when a party leaves the government, the Chief Minister must ask for a vote of confidence within 30 days (Article 188). On **8 Bhadra 2083 (24 August 2026)** the Chief Minister won it by **57 votes to 22**. The UML (40) and the Nepali Communist Party (17) voted for; the Nepali Congress voted against; the Rastriya Prajatantra Party stayed neutral; 83 members were present.
- **Six persons** have been Chief Minister: Sher Dhan Rai, Bhim Acharya, Rajendra Kumar Rai, Hikmat Kumar Karki, Uddhav Thapa and Kedar Karki.

**Ministries** (as on the provincial portal)

- The **Office of the Chief Minister and Council of Ministers**, and **eight** ministries:
  - Economic Affairs and Planning;
  - Internal Affairs and Law;
  - Physical Infrastructure Development;
  - Social Development;
  - Health;
  - Industry, Agriculture and Cooperatives;
  - Tourism, Forests and Environment;
  - Drinking Water, Irrigation and Energy.

**Other bodies**

- **Province Public Service Commission** (प्रदेश लोक सेवा आयोग) — set up under Article 244 and a provincial Act of 2076. It holds the examination for which this book is written.
  - 🔄 Its chairperson, **Mahendra Prasad Guragain**, appointed on 23 Ashad 2081 (7 July 2024) for six years, resigned on 10 Kartik 2083 (27 October 2026) during a dispute about extra allowances, as reported. The resignation took effect on 16 Kartik 2083 (2 November 2026). No successor was found.
- **Province Planning Commission** (प्रदेश योजना आयोग) — it writes the periodic plan. The Chief Minister chairs it; a vice-chairperson leads its daily work.
- **Chief Attorney** (मुख्य न्यायाधिवक्ता) — the legal adviser of the provincial government (Article 160).
- **Province Investment Authority** — it held the investment summit of 2082.
- **Manmohan Technical University** — a university set up by the province.
- **"Hello Chief Minister"** — the government's complaint-hearing service, named on the portal.

**Courts and local levels**

- **High Court: Biratnagar.** Benches: **Dhankuta, Ilam and Okhaldhunga**. District Courts: **14** (Chapter 3).
- **Local governments: 137.** District Coordination Committees: **14**.
- **National Assembly:** the province elects 8 members. The voters are the 93 Assembly members and the 274 heads and deputy heads of the local levels — 367 in all (Chapter 4).

**Periodic plans** (आवधिक योजना)

- **First plan: 2076/77–2080/81.**
  - It set the **long-term vision** of the province: **"स्वच्छ, सुखी र समुन्नत प्रदेश"** — a clean, happy and prosperous province. The three words are its three pillars.
  - Aims: average growth of 9.7 percent a year; income of USD 1,620 per person.
  - A review in 2081 found that most targets were not met. Income per person was USD 1,299 in 2079/80.
- **Second plan: 2081/82–2085/86** — the plan now in force. It runs for the same five years as the national Sixteenth Plan.
  - Released on **31 Shrawan 2081 (15 August 2024)** by the Province Planning Commission.
  - It keeps the long-term vision above. Its own vision, as reported at the release: **"सुशासित, खुसी, आर्थिक रूपले गतिशील र समुन्नत प्रदेश"** — a well-governed, happy, economically dynamic and prosperous province.
  - **Targets for 2085/86:** average growth **7.55 percent**; income **USD 1,645** per person; poverty **12 percent**; unemployment 5.4 percent; literacy 90 percent; life expectancy 72 years; Human Development Index 0.62; installed power **2,200 megawatts**.

**Budget for 2083/84** 🔄

- **Rs 40.45 billion** (Rs 40,449.8 million).
- Presented on **1 Ashad 2083 (15 June 2026)** by the Minister for Economic Affairs and Planning, **Bidur Kumar Lingthep**. Passed on 24 Ashad 2083 (8 July 2026).
- **Recurrent:** Rs 13.98 billion (34.55 percent). **Capital:** Rs 20.76 billion (51.32 percent). **Financing:** Rs 0.15 billion (0.37 percent). The report read does not say where the rest goes.
- It is **12.74 percent** larger than the budget of 2082/83 (Rs 35.88 billion) — the largest rise among the seven provinces (Chapter 9).
- **Sources**, as reported: internal revenue Rs 5.50 billion; revenue sharing Rs 12.87 billion; equalisation grant Rs 9.12 billion; conditional grant Rs 5.95 billion; complementary grant Rs 0.64 billion.
- Federal grants are Rs 16.47 billion in all, about 40.7 percent of the budget (Chapters 4 and 5).
- The budget aims at growth of 5 percent.

**Policy and Programme for 2083/84** 🔄

- Read to the Assembly by Province Chief Parshuram Khapung on **7 Jestha 2083 (21 May 2026)**.
- Main points: the Koshi Province Visit Year; a tourism master plan; Biratnagar airport as a regional international airport; the road from Kechana Kawal to Sagarmatha; the Mundhum Trail; a programme named "Saksham Chhori" (सक्षम छोरी, "capable daughter").

- **Rs 40.45 billion** is the budget of **2083/84**. Rs 35.88 billion was the budget of 2082/83.

- The **Province Public Service Commission** (Article 244) is not the federal **Public Service Commission** (Article 242).

## 10.7 The province in numbers and superlatives; fact sheet (तथ्याङ्क, विशेषता र तथ्य-पत्र)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Two questions in unit 1.5 mean two short facts. This topic puts the facts of the whole chapter on one page, and sorts the "firsts" and "mosts" that examiners like. Candidates of other provinces can read this topic alone: Koshi's superlatives are also national general knowledge.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

A **superlative** is a "most", a "first" or an "only". Koshi has three kinds.

1. **Firsts and mosts of the world and of Nepal** — the highest peak, the largest river, the first Ramsar site, the first modern factory.
2. **Its rank among the seven provinces** — first in districts and local levels; second in area and economy; third or fourth in most other things.
3. **Its share of Nepal** — by almost every measure, Koshi is about **one-sixth** of the country.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Firsts and mosts of the world and of Nepal**

- **Highest point on earth:** Sagarmatha, 8,848.86 m (Solukhumbu).
- **Third-highest peak in the world:** Kanchenjunga, 8,586 m (Taplejung).
- **Five of the world's six highest peaks** touch the province.
- **Largest river of Nepal:** the Koshi.
- **First Ramsar site of Nepal:** Koshi Tappu (1987).
- **First natural World Heritage Site of Nepal:** Sagarmatha National Park (1979).
- **Only natural home of the wild water buffalo (arna) in Nepal:** Koshi Tappu.
- **First modern factory of Nepal:** Biratnagar Jute Mill (1993 BS; 1936).
- **First tea garden of Nepal:** Ilam (1920 BS).
- **Almost all of Nepal's tea** and **most of its cardamom** grow here.
- **Easternmost province**; the Mahendra, Mid-Hill and Madan Bhandari Highways all begin here.
- **First Nepali woman on Sagarmatha:** Pasang Lhamu Sherpa of Solukhumbu (2050 BS).
- **First woman President** (Bidya Devi Bhandari) and **first woman Chief Justice and Prime Minister** (Sushila Karki) come from the province.
- 🔄 **First Province Chief in Nepal to complete five years:** Parshuram Khapung (24 Kartik 2083).
- ⚖️ Two disputed "mosts": the **lowest point** of Nepal (Kechana Kawal, Jhapa) and the **highest waterfall** (Hyatrung, Terhathum).

**Rank among the seven provinces**

- **First:** districts (14); local levels (137); peaks above 8,000 m (5); Ramsar sites (3).
- **Second:** area (after Karnali); share of GDP (after Bagmati).
- **Third:** Assembly seats (93); House constituencies (28); literacy (79.7 percent); GDP per person; budget for 2083/84.
- **Third lowest:** poverty (17.19 percent).
- **Fourth:** population; density; growth in 2082/83.
- **Last:** to be named (2079 BS).

**One-sixth of Nepal**

- One-sixth is 16.7 percent. Koshi has 17.6 percent of the area, 17.01 percent of the people, 18.2 percent of the districts and local levels, 17.0 percent of the House constituencies and 15.8 percent of the GDP.

4. Placing **Siraha and Saptari** in Koshi. They were in the Eastern Development Region but are in **Madhesh Province**.
5. Naming Itahari, Dharan, Birtamod or Damak as a district headquarters. **Sunsari–Inaruwa; Jhapa–Bhadrapur.**
6. Placing **Kanchenjunga** in Solukhumbu or **Makalu** in Taplejung. Kanchenjunga is in **Taplejung**; Makalu in **Sankhuwasabha**.
7. Exchanging the kinds of protected area: Makalu Barun is a **national park**, Kanchenjunga a **conservation area**, Koshi Tappu a **wildlife reserve**.
8. Saying the Saptakoshi is formed at Chatara or at the barrage. The three rivers meet at **Tribeni**.
9. Exchanging the Sen seats: **Chaudandi** is in **Udayapur**; **Bijayapur** is near **Dharan**.
10. Giving Biratnagar as the centre of the old Eastern Development Region. It was **Dhankuta**.
11. Exchanging the dances: **Dhan Nach** is **Limbu**; **Sakela** is **Rai**.
12. Misplacing the holy places: **Pathibhara–Taplejung; Halesi–Khotang; Barahakshetra–Sunsari; Mai Pokhari–Ilam**.
13. Saying the province adopted **2** languages, or that the Language Commission recommended **13**. The Commission recommended **2**; the law adopted **13**.
14. Adding **Arun III** to the installed power, or placing it in Solukhumbu. It is **900 megawatts**, in **Sankhuwasabha**, and **not yet finished**.
15. Mixing the two plans. The **first** ran **2076/77–2080/81**; the **second** runs **2081/82–2085/86**. "स्वच्छ, सुखी र समुन्नत प्रदेश" is the **long-term** vision kept by both; the second plan was reported with a longer wording of its own as well.
16. Using last year's facts: Parshuram Khapung as the sitting Province Chief after **24 Kartik 2083**; the Nepali Congress as a ruling party after **Shrawan 2083**; Rs 35.88 billion as the budget of **2083/84**.

**Where sources disagree**

- **Lowest point of Nepal; heights of Makalu and Cho Oyu; highest waterfall; largest glacier:** see Chapter 1. ⚖️
- **Cardamom:** 94.2 percent of Nepal's crop in one report; 80 to 90 percent in others. ⚖️
- **Udayapur earthquake:** magnitude 6.9 or 6.5. ⚖️
- **Rhododendron species at Tinjure–Milke–Jaljale:** 26 in a journal article; 28 on trekking pages. ⚖️
- **Dates of office:** pages can differ by a day or two, because one gives the day of appointment and another the day of the oath. For Parshuram Khapung this book gives the appointment, 23 Kartik 2078, as in Chapter 4. Kedar Karki became Chief Minister on 27 Ashoj 2080 in one press report and on 15 October 2023 (28 Ashoj) on an encyclopaedia page; the chapter gives only the month.
- **Budget:** some reports round it to Rs 40.44 billion. This book writes Rs 40.45 billion, as in Chapter 5.
- **The provincial portal** prints the population of the Census of 2068, leaves Sikkim out of the neighbours and gives an old, undated hydropower figure. The chapter does not follow it on these points.
- **Upper Arun:** about 1,060 megawatts; reports give 1,061 or 1,063.
- **The thirteenth language:** the press list read gives "Rai" beside Bantawa and Chamling. The chapter repeats the list as reported.
- **Where the Assembly meets:** an encyclopaedia page and the portal name different buildings in and near Biratnagar. The chapter names neither.
- **Vision of the plan:** not a disagreement. "स्वच्छ, सुखी र समुन्नत प्रदेश" is the long-term vision; the longer wording was reported as the second plan's own vision. Chapter 5 was corrected to say so.
