## 4.1 Three tiers of government and Schedules 5 to 9 (तीन तहको सरकार र अनुसूची ५ देखि ९)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Bagmati's unit 1.8 names the "three tiers of government". The commonest question asks you to match a Schedule with its list, or a subject with its level. Everything else in this chapter rests on this topic.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

**Federalism** (संघीयता) means that the constitution itself divides the power of the State among levels of government. Each level has its own elected government, its own budget and its own list of work. No level is simply a branch office of another.

Before 2072 BS Nepal was a **unitary** state: one central government held the power and lent some of it to districts, villages and towns. The Constitution of Nepal changed that. **Article 56** says the main structure of the State has **three levels**: the **Federation** (संघ), the **Province** (प्रदेश) and the **Local level** (स्थानीय तह).

The work of each level is written in five lists, **Schedules 5 to 9**. Three lists are **exclusive** (एकल अधिकार) — one level alone. Two are **concurrent** (साझा अधिकार) — shared. Anything not written in any list belongs to the Federation. The three levels are meant to work together, not compete: their relations rest on **cooperation, coexistence and coordination** (सहकारिता, सहअस्तित्व र समन्वय).

#### 3 · Key facts

- **Part 5** of the Constitution (Articles 56 to 60) is "Structure of State and Distribution of State Power".
- **Article 56 (1):** three levels — Federation, Province and Local level.
- **Article 56 (3):** the provinces are made up of the districts listed in **Schedule 4**.
- **Article 56 (4):** the local level consists of **rural municipalities (गाउँपालिका), municipalities (नगरपालिका) and District Assemblies (जिल्ला सभा)**. Federal law fixes the number of wards.
- **Article 56 (5):** federal law may create a **special, protected or autonomous region** (विशेष, संरक्षित वा स्वायत्त क्षेत्र) for social and cultural protection or economic development.
- **Article 56 (6):** all three levels must protect Nepal's freedom, sovereignty, territorial integrity, independence and national interest, and the democratic, republican and federal system.
- **Number of governments:** 1 federal + 7 provincial + 753 local = **761**.
- **Article 57** divides State power through the five Schedules (table below). The lists hold **35, 21, 25, 22 and 15** items — **118** in all.
- **Article 57 (6):** a provincial or local law on a shared subject must not conflict with federal law. **Article 57 (7):** a local law must not conflict with provincial law. A conflicting law is void to the extent of the conflict.
- **Article 58 — residual power (अवशिष्ट अधिकार):** any subject not written in any list belongs to the **Federation**. Item 35 of Schedule 5 repeats this.
- **Article 59:** each level makes its own laws, yearly budget, policies and plans on money matters within its list. Only the **Government of Nepal** may take foreign aid and loans (Article 59 (6)).
- **Article 137:** disputes about jurisdiction between the Federation and a province, between provinces, between a province and a local level, and between local levels go to the **Constitutional Bench** of the Supreme Court (the Chief Justice and four other judges).
- **How federalism came:** after the Madhesh movement of 2063 BS the Interim Constitution was amended to promise a federal system (Chapter 2). The Constitution of **3 Ashoj 2072 (20 September 2015)** put it into effect. All three levels were first elected in **2074 BS**.
- **How the local levels were drawn:** a commission under Article 295 (3), led by **Balananda Paudel**, proposed 719 local levels. The Government put **744** into effect on **27 Falgun 2073 (10 March 2017)**, replacing the old Village Development Committees and municipalities. The number later became **753**.

#### 6 · Memory aid

- **"5 Federal – 6 Province – 7 both – 8 Local – 9 all three."** Even numbers 6 and 8 belong to the two lower levels alone; 7 and 9 are the shared lists.
- **Counts: "35 – 21 – 25 – 22 – 15."** The federal list is the longest; the list shared by all three is the shortest.
- **Three "स" of Article 232:** **स**हकारिता, **स**हअस्तित्व, **स**मन्वय.
- **"Left-over goes up":** whatever no list names goes to the Federation (Article 58).

#### 7 · Exam traps

- A **district is not a level of government**. The District Assembly belongs to the local level (Article 56 (4)) and only coordinates.
- **Residual power is with the Federation**, not with the provinces.
- **Schedule 7** is shared by **two** levels; **Schedule 9** by **three**.
- **Basic and secondary education** is a **local** power. Universities are federal or provincial.
- **Citizenship and passports** are federal; so are **customs, excise, value-added tax and income tax**.
- **Disaster management** appears in both Schedule 8 and Schedule 9. **Cooperatives** appear in Schedules 8 and 9 too. If a question says "concurrent power of all three levels", the answer is Schedule 9.
- The English translation says "State" for a province. Do not confuse it with "the State" meaning the country.
- **Management of trusts (गुठी)** is the last item of the **provincial** list.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** What are the three tiers of government in Nepal, and how does the Constitution divide power among them?

**Model answer.** Article 56 of the Constitution makes Nepal a State of three levels: the **Federation**, seven **Provinces** and 753 **Local levels** (rural municipalities and municipalities, with District Assemblies for coordination). Each level has its own executive, its own law-making body and its own budget. **Division of power:** Article 57 uses five lists. **Schedule 5** (35 items) is for the Federation alone — defence, foreign affairs, currency, the main taxes. **Schedule 6** (21 items) is for the Province alone — provincial police, provincial highways, agriculture. **Schedule 8** (22 items) is for the Local level alone — basic and secondary education, basic health, local roads. **Schedule 7** (25 items) is shared by the Federation and the Province, and **Schedule 9** (15 items) by all three. Anything left out belongs to the Federation (Article 58). On shared subjects a lower-level law must not conflict with a higher-level law. Relations among the levels rest on cooperation, coexistence and coordination (Article 232), and disputes about jurisdiction go to the Constitutional Bench of the Supreme Court.

## 4.2 Provincial executive, Provincial Assembly and provincial institutions (प्रदेश कार्यपालिका, प्रदेश सभा र प्रदेशका संस्था)

#### 1 · Why it matters

The provincial system copies the federal one, but the numbers differ: 35 and 25 years, 20 percent, 15, 30 and 35 days. Examiners use those differences. Names of Chief Ministers and Province Chiefs are regular current-affairs questions.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Each province has a small copy of the national system.

- A **Province Chief** (प्रदेश प्रमुख) stands for the Government of Nepal in the province, as the President stands for the nation. The post is mostly ceremonial.
- A **Chief Minister** (मुख्यमन्त्री) and the **Provincial Council of Ministers** (प्रदेश मन्त्रिपरिषद्) hold the real executive power.
- A **Provincial Assembly** (प्रदेश सभा) with **one house** makes provincial laws and passes the provincial budget. The Chief Minister must keep its confidence.
- Each province also has a **High Court**, a **Chief Attorney** and a **Province Public Service Commission**.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Province Chief — Articles 163 to 167**

- The Province Chief is the **representative of the Government of Nepal** in the province.
- **Appointed by the President** — one for each province. In practice the President acts on the recommendation of the federal Council of Ministers.
- **Term: five years**, unless the President removes the holder earlier. A person **cannot be Province Chief twice in the same province**.
- **Qualification:** qualified to be a member of the Federal Parliament, and **35 years** of age.
- Takes the oath before the **President**. If the post falls vacant, the President may ask the Chief of another province to act.
- Acts on the recommendation of the Provincial Council of Ministers, given through the Chief Minister (Article 166).

**Provincial executive — Articles 162, 168 to 174**

- Executive power of the province lies in the **Provincial Council of Ministers**. All work is done in the name of the Provincial Government.
- **Article 168** copies Article 76:
  - (1) the leader of the party with a **majority** in the Assembly becomes Chief Minister;
  - (2) if no party has a majority, a member who can command a majority **with the support of two or more parties**;
  - (3) if that fails, the leader of the **largest party**;
  - (5) then any member who shows a ground for winning confidence;
  - (7) if all fail, the Assembly is dissolved and an election is held **within six months**.
- A Chief Minister appointed under clause (2) or (3) must win a **vote of confidence within 30 days**.
- The Council of Ministers may have **at most 20 percent** of the total members of the Assembly, **including the Chief Minister**.
- A person who is not a member may be made a minister, but must become a member **within six months** (Article 170).
- The Chief Minister and ministers take the oath before the **Province Chief**. The Chief Minister keeps the Province Chief informed (Article 173).

**Provincial Assembly — Articles 175 to 196**

- **One house** (एक सदनात्मक). Term **five years**; during an emergency a provincial Act may extend it by up to one year.
- **Composition (Article 176):** seats filled by first-past-the-post are **twice** the number of House of Representatives constituencies in the province. These are counted as **60 percent**; the other **40 percent** are filled by proportional representation.
- **Member:** citizen, voter of the province, **25 years** of age. At least **one third** of each party's members must be women.
- **Speaker and Deputy Speaker** (प्रदेश सभामुख, उपसभामुख): elected **within 15 days** of the first meeting; one of the two must be a **woman**, and they must be from **different parties**.
- First session called **within 20 days** of the final results. The gap between two sessions may not exceed **six months**.
- **Quorum:** one fourth of the total members. A member who is absent from **ten meetings in a row** without notice loses the seat.
- **No-confidence motion (Article 188):** moved by **one fourth** of the members; it must name the next Chief Minister; it cannot be moved in the **first two years** after appointment, nor within **one year** of a failed motion.

**Law-making and money — Articles 197 to 213**

- The Assembly makes laws on Schedules 6, 7 and 9.
- The Province Chief gives assent to a Bill **within 15 days**, and may send back a Bill (not a Money Bill) once.
- **Ordinance (अध्यादेश):** issued by the Province Chief on the recommendation of the Council of Ministers when the Assembly is not sitting. It lapses **60 days** after the Assembly meets unless passed.
- No tax without law. A province may not raise a loan or give a guarantee except as federal law allows (Article 203).
- **Provincial Consolidated Fund** (प्रदेश सञ्चित कोष): Article 204. The provincial Finance Minister presents the yearly estimate (Article 207); federal law fixes the date as **1 Ashad**.

**Other provincial institutions**

- **Chief Attorney** (मुख्य न्यायाधिवक्ता), Article 160: appointed by the Province Chief on the recommendation of the Chief Minister; works under the Attorney General; chief legal adviser of the Provincial Government.
- **High Court** in every province (Chapter 3). **Province Public Service Commission** (Article 244; topic 4.6).
- The **Auditor General** of Nepal audits provincial and local accounts as well (Article 241).
- A province's **capital** and **name** are decided by a **two-thirds majority** of its Assembly (Articles 288 (2) and 295 (2)).

**The present Assemblies** 🔄

- The Provincial Assemblies were **not** dissolved in 2082 BS. They are the ones elected on **4 Mangsir 2079 (20 November 2022)**, and their term runs to Mangsir 2084.
- **Parshuram Khapung**, Province Chief of Koshi, was appointed on 23 Kartik 2078, and his five-year term was due to end on **24 Kartik 2083 (10 November 2026)**. Reports published in the first days of November 2026 call him the first Province Chief to complete a full term. No report naming a successor was found when this chapter was checked.

- **Ages "35 – 25 – 21":** Province Chief 35, Assembly member 25, local representative 21.
- **Days "15 – 20 – 30 – 35":** Speaker in 15, first session in 20, confidence vote in 30, Chief Minister process in 35.
- **"Twice and sixty":** first-past-the-post seats are **twice** the province's House constituencies, and they are **sixty** percent of the Assembly.
- **"168 is 76 in the province."**

#### 7 · Exam traps

- The Province Chief is **appointed, not elected**, and is **not** the head of the provincial government.
- The **Province Chief** appoints the Chief Minister; the **President** appoints the Province Chief.
- The 20 percent limit **includes** the Chief Minister.
- The **Chief Attorney** (province) is not the **Attorney General** (federation).
- A province fixes its own capital and name by **two thirds of its Assembly**; the Federal Parliament does not vote on it.
- A Provincial Assembly has **no upper house**.
- Ministers of State take the oath before the **Chief Minister**; ministers before the **Province Chief**.
- The news often says "Governor" for the Province Chief. The Constitution's word is प्रदेश प्रमुख.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** How is a provincial government formed under the Constitution? Describe the role of the Province Chief in it.

**Model answer.** Article 168 sets out the steps. (1) The Province Chief appoints as **Chief Minister** the leader of the party that has a majority in the Provincial Assembly. (2) If no party has a majority, the Province Chief appoints a member who can command a majority with the support of two or more parties. (3) If that is not possible within 30 days of the final results, or the person fails the confidence vote, the leader of the largest party is appointed. A Chief Minister appointed under (2) or (3) must win a **vote of confidence within 30 days**. (4) If this also fails, any member who shows a ground for winning confidence may be appointed. If no government can be formed, the Assembly is dissolved and an election is held within six months. The Chief Minister then forms a **Council of Ministers** of at most **20 percent** of the Assembly's members, on inclusive lines. **Role of the Province Chief:** appointed by the President as the representative of the Government of Nepal, the Province Chief appoints the Chief Minister and ministers, gives them the oath, summons the Assembly, assents to Bills and issues ordinances — but acts on the recommendation of the Council of Ministers. The post is ceremonial; the Chief Minister governs.

## 4.3 Local government: structure, powers and the Local Government Operation Act (स्थानीय सरकार: संरचना, अधिकार र स्थानीय सरकार सञ्चालन ऐन, २०७४)

#### 1 · Why it matters

The local level is the government closest to the citizen, and the office where many of you will work. Its numbers — 4 and 2, 5 and 3, 3, 9, 21 — are easy to set as questions. Koshi's official model question on the number of local levels shows the style.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Nepal has **753 local levels**. A local level is either a **rural municipality** (गाउँपालिका) or a **municipality** (नगरपालिका); large municipalities are called sub-metropolitan or metropolitan cities. Each is divided into **wards** (वडा).

A local level is a full government in small form:

- an **executive** — the Village Executive (गाउँ कार्यपालिका) or Municipal Executive (नगर कार्यपालिका);
- a law-making **assembly** — the Village Assembly (गाउँ सभा) or Municipal Assembly (नगर सभा);
- a **Judicial Committee** (न्यायिक समिति) that settles small disputes.

In each district the heads of the local levels meet as a **District Assembly** (जिल्ला सभा), which elects a **District Coordination Committee** (जिल्ला समन्वय समिति). It coordinates; it does not govern.

The **Local Government Operation Act, 2074** (स्थानीय सरकार सञ्चालन ऐन, २०७४) turns the short lists of Schedules 8 and 9 into detailed duties.

#### 3 · Key facts

**In the Constitution**

- **Part 17** (Articles 214–220) Local Executive; **Part 18** (Articles 221–227) Local Legislature; **Part 19** (Articles 228–230) Local Financial Procedures.
- **Numbers:** 753 local levels = 6 metropolitan cities + 11 sub-metropolitan cities + 276 municipalities + 460 rural municipalities; **6,743 wards** (Chapter 1).
- **Head and deputy:** a rural municipality has a **Chairperson** (अध्यक्ष) and **Vice-Chairperson** (उपाध्यक्ष); a municipality has a **Mayor** (प्रमुख) and **Deputy Mayor** (उपप्रमुख). Voters elect them directly by first-past-the-post.
- **Village Executive (Article 215):** Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson, all ward chairpersons, **4 women** members and **2 members from Dalit or minority communities**.
- **Municipal Executive (Article 216):** Mayor, Deputy Mayor, all ward chairpersons, **5 women** members and **3 members from Dalit or minority communities**.
- These extra members are chosen by the Assembly **within 15 days** of the final election results.
- **Ward Committee (Articles 222, 223):** a ward chairperson and **4 members**. Each ward must send at least **2 women** to the Assembly.
- **Assembly:** the head, the deputy, the ward chairpersons, the four members from every ward and the Dalit or minority members of the Executive. The head and deputy of the Executive are also the chair and vice-chair of the Assembly (Article 224) — there is no separate Speaker.
- **Minimum age** for any local post: **21**. **Term: 5 years.** A person elected head for two terms cannot stand again. The next election must be held **within six months** of the end of the term (Article 225).
- **Judicial Committee (Article 217):** **3 members**. The **Vice-Chairperson** or **Deputy Mayor** coordinates it; the Assembly elects the other two from among its members.
- **District Assembly and District Coordination Committee (Article 220):**
  - the District Assembly consists of the Chairpersons and Vice-Chairpersons of the rural municipalities and the Mayors and Deputy Mayors of the municipalities in the district;
  - it elects a District Coordination Committee of **at most 9 members**: a Chief, a Deputy Chief, at least **3 women** and at least **1** Dalit or minority member;
  - term 5 years; a person elected to the Committee gives up the seat in the Village or Municipal Assembly;
  - work: coordinate the local levels; monitor development work for balance; coordinate between federal and provincial offices and the local levels in the district.
- **Law-making:** the Assemblies make laws on Schedules 8 and 9 (Article 226).
- **Money:** no tax except by law (Article 228); every local level has a **Local Consolidated Fund** (स्थानीय सञ्चित कोष, Article 229); the Executive presents the yearly estimate to the Assembly (Article 230). Federal law fixes the date as **10 Ashad**.

**In the Local Government Operation Act, 2074**

- Authenticated on **29 Ashoj 2074 (15 October 2017)**.
- **Wards:** a rural municipality has **5 to 21** wards; a municipality **9 to 35**.
- **Classification of municipalities (section 8):**
  - **sub-metropolitan city** — at least **200,000** permanent residents and an average yearly internal income of at least **Rs 250 million** (25 crore) over the last five years;
  - **metropolitan city** — at least **500,000** permanent residents and at least **Rs 1 billion** (1 arab);
  - both must also meet standards for roads, hospitals, water supply and other city services.
- **Meetings:** the Executive meets at least **once a month**; the Assembly normally meets **twice a year**.
- **Judicial Committee (section 47):** it may **decide 13 kinds** of dispute and may settle **11 more kinds only through mediation** (मेलमिलाप) — 24 in all. It must first try to bring the two sides to agreement, and may set up mediation centres in the wards.
- The **Chief Administrative Officer** (प्रमुख प्रशासकीय अधिकृत) heads the staff of a local level.

**Elections** (see also topic 4.5)

- **2074 BS:** three phases — 31 Baisakh (14 May 2017), 14 Ashad (28 June 2017) and 2 Ashoj (18 September 2017). They were the first local elections in about twenty years.
- **2079 BS:** one phase — **30 Baisakh 2079 (13 May 2022)**.
- **Next** 🔄: the present term ends in Baisakh 2084 (May 2027).
- **Posts filled by direct election:** 753 heads + 753 deputies + 6,743 ward chairpersons + 26,972 ward members = **35,221**.
- 🔄 In September 2026 the Government was reported to be preparing rules for merging local levels and reviewing their classification after the next election.

- **753**, not 744 — 744 was the first number, in Falgun 2073.
- The local Assembly has **no Speaker**; the head of the Executive chairs it.

## 4.4 Inter-governmental relations and fiscal federalism (अन्तर-सरकारी सम्बन्ध र वित्तीय संघीयता)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Karnali's syllabus names the "revenue jurisdiction of the three tiers" inside its economy unit. The sharing ratios 70 : 15 : 15 and 50 : 25 : 25, the four grants and the budget dates make clean one-line questions. Budget figures come up in current affairs every year.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Three levels of government must not pull in three directions. The Constitution gives them rules for working together — **inter-governmental relations** (अन्तर-सरकारी सम्बन्ध) — and rules for dividing money — **fiscal federalism** (वित्तीय संघीयता).

The money problem is simple to state. The big taxes (customs, value-added tax, income tax) belong to the Federation, but much of the work (schools, health posts, local roads) belongs to the provinces and local levels. So money must flow down. It flows in two ways: **revenue sharing** (राजस्व बाँडफाँट), where a fixed share of certain taxes is handed over, and **grants** (अनुदान). An independent body, the **National Natural Resources and Fiscal Commission** (राष्ट्रिय प्राकृतिक स्रोत तथा वित्त आयोग), recommends how much each government should get.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Relations — Part 20, Articles 231 to 237**

- **Article 232 (1):** relations among the three levels rest on **cooperation, coexistence and coordination**.
- **Article 232 (2):** the Government of Nepal may give directions to a Provincial Council of Ministers on matters of national importance and on matters to be coordinated among provinces.
- **Article 232 (3):** if an act that seriously harms Nepal's sovereignty, territorial integrity, nationality or independence takes place in a province, the **President** may warn the Provincial Council of Ministers, or **suspend or dissolve** it and the Assembly **for up to six months**.
- **Article 232 (4):** the Federal Parliament must ratify this **within 35 days**. ⚖️ The Nepali text read for this book requires a **majority** of all existing members; the English translation read says two thirds. Follow the Nepali text.
- **Article 232 (5)–(7):** if a dissolution is ratified, a new Assembly is elected **within six months**; meanwhile there is **federal rule** (संघीय शासन) in the province and the Federal Parliament may make laws on Schedule 6.
- **Article 232 (8):** the Government of Nepal may help and direct a Village or Municipal Executive, itself or through the Provincial Government.
- **Article 231 (3):** if two or more provinces ask, the Federal Parliament may make a law on a Schedule 6 subject for those provinces.
- **Article 233:** provinces help one another and give equal treatment to residents of other provinces.
- **Article 234 — Inter-Province Council (अन्तर प्रदेश परिषद्):** settles **political disputes** between the Federation and a province and between provinces. Members: the **Prime Minister** (chair), the **Home Minister**, the **Finance Minister** and the **Chief Ministers** of the provinces concerned.
- **Article 235:** the Federal Parliament makes a law for coordination; a Provincial Assembly may settle political disputes between the province and its local levels, working with the District Coordination Committee.
- **Article 236:** no province or local level may block, tax or discriminate against goods and services moving to another.
- **The coordination Act:** the **Federation, Province and Local Level (Coordination and Inter-relation) Act, 2077** creates a **National Coordination Council** (राष्ट्रिय समन्वय परिषद्) chaired by the **Prime Minister** and a **Province Coordination Council** (प्रदेश समन्वय परिषद्) chaired by the **Chief Minister** in each province.
- The **Inter-Governmental Fiscal Council** (अन्तर-सरकारी वित्त परिषद्) is chaired by the federal **Finance Minister** and includes the provincial finance ministers and local representatives.
- 🔄 **Koshi Declaration, 2083:** the Chief Ministers met at Biratnagar on 27–28 Ashoj 2083 (13–14 October 2026). They asked for equalisation grants to be paid in full, for the Federal Civil Service Bill to be passed quickly and for police to be handed to the provinces, and set up an Inter-Province Chief Ministers' Partnership Forum.

**Money — Articles 59 and 60, and the Inter-Governmental Fiscal Arrangement Act, 2074** (अन्तर-सरकारी वित्त व्यवस्थापन ऐन, २०७४)

- **Article 60 (1):** each level may levy taxes on subjects in its own list.
- **Article 60 (3):** the amount of fiscal transfer to provinces and local levels is fixed on the **recommendation of the National Natural Resources and Fiscal Commission**.
- **Revenue sharing:**
  - **value-added tax** and **excise on domestic products** go into a Federal Divisible Fund and are shared **70 percent Federation : 15 percent provinces : 15 percent local levels**;
  - **royalty from natural resources** (mountaineering, electricity, forests, mines and minerals, water) is shared **50 percent Federation : 25 percent the province concerned : 25 percent the local level concerned**.
- **Single tax administration** (एकल कर प्रशासन) for taxes that appear in both the provincial and the local list:
  - **vehicle tax** — the province fixes the rate and collects it; shared **60 province : 40 local**;
  - **house and land registration fee** and **entertainment tax** — the province fixes the rate, the local level collects; shared **60 local : 40 province**;
  - **advertisement tax** — the local level fixes the rate and collects it; shared **60 local : 40 province**.
- **Four grants (Article 60 (4)–(6)):**
  - **fiscal equalisation grant** (वित्तीय समानीकरण अनुदान) — based on the need for spending and the capacity to raise revenue; free to use;
  - **conditional grant** (सशर्त अनुदान) — tied to a named programme or standard;
  - **complementary grant** (समपूरक अनुदान) — matching money for infrastructure projects;
  - **special grant** (विशेष अनुदान) — for a specific purpose such as basic services, balanced development or the uplift of disadvantaged groups.
- Each province also gives equalisation grants to its own local levels (Article 60 (5)).
- **Borrowing:** only the Government of Nepal may take **foreign** loans and aid. Provinces and local levels may raise **internal** loans within the limit recommended by the Commission and with the consent of the Government of Nepal.
- **Budget days:** Federation **15 Jestha**; province **1 Ashad**; local level **10 Ashad**.
- **Three funds:** Federal Consolidated Fund (Article 116), Provincial Consolidated Fund (Article 204), Local Consolidated Fund (Article 229).

**National Natural Resources and Fiscal Commission — Articles 250 and 251**

- **At most 5 members**, including the Chairperson; appointed by the President on the recommendation of the Constitutional Council.
- Term **6 years**; leaves at age **65**; minimum age **45**.
- Work: fixes the basis for sharing revenue; recommends equalisation grants; studies the basis for conditional grants; recommends the **ceiling for internal loans** of all three levels; fixes how investment in and returns from natural resources are shared; studies disputes over natural resources.

**Fiscal year 2083/84** 🔄

- Grants to provinces and local levels: about **Rs 424 billion** (table below). Revenue sharing was estimated at a further **Rs 175 billion**.
- Loan ceiling recommended by the Commission for provinces and local levels: up to **12 percent** of their shared revenue plus their own revenue.

- **Foreign loans:** the Federation only. A province needs the "consent of the Centre" even for foreign grants (Schedule 6, item 2).
- The Commission **recommends**; the Government of Nepal **distributes**.
- The Commission has **at most five** members — like most constitutional commissions — but its members need **20 years'** experience in the field.
- **Royalty** is shared with the province and local level **concerned**, not with all.
- The Federal Parliament, not the President alone, has the last word on suspending a provincial government.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** State the revenue jurisdiction of the three tiers of government and explain how revenue is shared among them.

A **local level** levies property tax, house rent tax, business tax and land tax, and shares the four taxes just named (Schedule 8). Taxes common to provinces and local levels are collected by one level and shared 60 : 40. In addition the Federation gives four **grants** — equalisation, conditional, complementary and special. The **National Natural Resources and Fiscal Commission** recommends the shares and the equalisation grants, so that the division is balanced and transparent, as Article 60 requires.

## 4.5 Elections and the political system (निर्वाचन र राजनीतिक प्रणाली)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Bagmati's unit 1.8 names the "political system". Election dates, the two voting systems, the 3 percent threshold and the newest results are standard questions in every province's current-affairs share.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Nepal is a **federal democratic republic** with a **multi-party, competitive** system. The people are sovereign and use that power by voting. Every citizen aged **18** may vote. Governments at all three levels are formed through elections held every five years.

Two voting systems are used. In **first-past-the-post** (पहिलो हुने निर्वाचित हुने) the candidate with the most votes in a constituency wins. In **proportional representation** (समानुपातिक) the voter chooses a party, and parties get seats in proportion to their votes. The House of Representatives and the Provincial Assemblies use **both** (a mixed system). Local elections use only first-past-the-post.

The **Election Commission** (निर्वाचन आयोग), a constitutional body, runs all elections. **Political parties** (राजनीतिक दल) must register with it.

#### 3 · Key facts

**The system**

- Form of government: **parliamentary**, with the Prime Minister answerable to the House of Representatives (Chapter 3).
- Article 56 (6) lists what all levels must protect: a multi-party, competitive, democratic, republican, federal system; human rights; rule of law; separation of powers and checks and balances; inclusive representation.
- **Voting age: 18.** Minimum age of candidates: local level **21**; Provincial Assembly and House of Representatives **25**; National Assembly **35**; President **45**.

**Election Commission — Articles 245 to 247**

- A **Chief Election Commissioner** and **4 Commissioners**; appointed by the President on the recommendation of the Constitutional Council; term **6 years**; leave at age **65**.
- It conducts, supervises, directs and controls the elections of the **President, Vice-President, members of the Federal Parliament, members of the Provincial Assemblies and members of the local levels**, and prepares the voters' roll.
- It holds a **referendum** when the Federal Parliament decides on one by a two-thirds majority (Article 275).
- It registers political parties. All three levels of government must give it staff and help (Article 247).
- 🔄 **Chief Election Commissioner:** **Man Bahadur Karki**, appointed on 14 Shrawan 2083 (30 July 2026). Before him, Ram Prasad Bhandari led the Commission as acting chief during the election of March 2026.

**Who is elected how**

| Body | Seats | System |
|---|---|---|
| House of Representatives | 275 | 165 first-past-the-post + 110 proportional |
| National Assembly | 59 | 56 elected by provincial electoral colleges (8 from each province) + 3 nominated by the President |
| Provincial Assemblies | 550 | 330 first-past-the-post + 220 proportional |
| Local levels | 35,221 posts | First-past-the-post only |
| President and Vice-President | — | Electoral college of members of the Federal Parliament and the Provincial Assemblies |

**Thresholds and party status**

- A party needs at least **3 percent** of the valid proportional votes to get proportional seats in the **House of Representatives**.
- For a **Provincial Assembly** the threshold is **1.5 percent**.
- **National party** (राष्ट्रिय दल): under section 52 of the **Political Parties Act, 2073**, a party needs **3 percent** of the proportional votes **and at least one first-past-the-post seat** in the House of Representatives.
- 🔄 National parties after each election: **5** after 2074, **7** after 2079 and **6** after 2082 — Rastriya Swatantra Party, Nepali Congress, CPN (UML), Nepali Communist Party, Shram Sanskriti Party and Rastriya Prajatantra Party.

**Political parties — Part 29, Articles 269 to 272**

- People who share a political idea may form a party; it must **register with the Election Commission**.
- Its constitution and rules must be **democratic**; its office-bearers at federal and provincial levels must be elected **at least once every five years**; its committees must be inclusive.
- **Article 270:** any law or decision that bans parties, or allows only one party or one ideology, is void.
- Women: at least **one third** of each party's members in the Federal Parliament and in each Provincial Assembly must be women.

The mixed system tries to give both a clear local representative and fair, inclusive representation; at least one third of each party's legislators must be women.

## 4.6 Civil service and the Public Service Commissions (निजामती सेवा र लोक सेवा आयोग)

#### 1 · Why it matters

You are sitting an examination of a Province Public Service Commission to enter a provincial or local service. Questions on the Commission, on Article 244 and on the civil service are natural choices for examiners, and they return in the subjective papers.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

Ministers change; offices must keep working. The **civil service** (निजामती सेवा) is the body of permanent government staff who carry out law and policy. It is often called the "permanent government". Staff are chosen on **merit** through open competition, not by the favour of leaders.

To keep selection clean, the Constitution gives the job to an independent body, the **Public Service Commission** (लोक सेवा आयोग). Under federalism there are now eight such bodies: one federal Commission and a **Province Public Service Commission** (प्रदेश लोक सेवा आयोग) in each of the seven provinces. There are also three kinds of service: federal, provincial and local.

#### 3 · Key facts

**In the Constitution**

- **Article 285:** the Government of Nepal may form the **federal civil service** and other federal services under a **federal Act**. Posts are filled by **competitive examination** on the **open and proportional inclusive** principle. Provinces and local levels may form their own services **by law**.
- **Article 302:** staff serving when the Constitution began could be **adjusted** to the federal, provincial and local levels. This was done under the **Employee Adjustment Act, 2075**.
- **Federal Public Service Commission — Articles 242 and 243:**
  - a **Chairperson and 4 members**, appointed by the President on the recommendation of the Constitutional Council; term **6 years**; leave at age **65**;
  - at least **half** of the members must have worked **20 years or more** in government service;
  - it holds examinations to select candidates for the **civil service**;
  - it holds the **written examinations** for the Nepal Army, Nepal Police, Armed Police Force, other federal services and public corporations in which the Government owns more than half;
  - it must be consulted on the laws on conditions of service and on the principles of appointment, promotion and departmental action.
- **Province Public Service Commission — Article 244:** each province has one. Its work is fixed by **provincial law**; the grounds and standards are fixed by **federal law** — the **Province Public Service Commission (Basis and Standards Determination) Act, 2075**.
- A Province Public Service Commission selects staff for the **provincial civil service** and the **local services**.
- In Gandaki, for example, the provincial Act provides for a Chairperson and members recommended by a committee led by the Chief Minister, appointed by the Province Chief, for a term of six years. Each province has its own Act; read your own province's Act for details.
- The federal Commission was founded on **1 Ashad 2008 (15 June 1951)**.

**The present law**

- The federal civil service still runs under the **Civil Service Act, 2049** and the Civil Service Rules, 2050. 🔄
- **Classes:** gazetted (राजपत्राङ्कित) — special, first, second and third class; and non-gazetted (राजपत्र अनङ्कित) — first to fifth class.
- **Ten services** under the Act: Economic Planning and Statistics; Engineering; Agriculture; Judicial; Foreign; Administration; Audit; Forest; Miscellaneous; Education. The Health Service and the Parliament Service have Acts of their own.
- **Compulsory retirement age: 58** under the present Act.
- **Inclusion:** **45 percent** of posts filled by open competition are set aside. Taking that share as 100: women **33**, Adivasi Janajati **27**, Madhesi **22**, Dalit **9**, persons with disabilities **5**, backward areas **4**.
- **Heads of administration:** the **Chief Secretary** (मुख्य सचिव) for the Federation; a **Principal Secretary** (प्रमुख सचिव) in each province; a **Chief Administrative Officer** (प्रमुख प्रशासकीय अधिकृत) in each local level.
- **Civil Service Day** (निजामती सेवा दिवस) is **22 Bhadra**, recalling the first Civil Service Act of 2013 BS.

**Federal Civil Service Bill** 🔄

- Article 285 calls for a federal Act. A Bill was passed by the House of Representatives on **15 Ashad 2082 (29 June 2025)** and by the National Assembly on **17 Bhadra 2082 (2 September 2025)**, but it had not become law when the House was dissolved on 27 Bhadra 2082.
- A new **Federal Civil Service Bill, 2083** was registered in the House of Representatives on **15 Ashoj 2083 (1 October 2026)**. Reports call this the third time such a Bill has reached Parliament. **It was still a Bill when this chapter was checked.**
- Reported proposals, which may change: a system of **levels** in place of classes; a retirement age of **60** with transitional rules; a **two-year "cooling-off" period** before retired senior officials may take other public appointments.
- Because the Act is missing, provinces and local levels have faced staff shortages. The Chief Ministers' Koshi Declaration of Ashoj 2083 asked for the Bill to be passed without delay.

**The Commission today** 🔄

- Chairperson **Madhav Prasad Regmi** left office on **3 Ashad 2083 (17 June 2026)** on reaching the age limit of 65. Member **Madhav Belbase** became acting Chairperson. A new Chairperson was not confirmed when this chapter was checked.

## 4.7 Good governance, right to information and control of corruption (सुशासन, सूचनाको हक र भ्रष्टाचार नियन्त्रण)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Citizen charter, information officer, 15 days, 35 days, the anti-corruption bodies and the yearly corruption index are short, checkable facts. They also give you ready points for essays in the subjective papers.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

**Good governance** (सुशासन) means governing in a way that is lawful, open, fair, quick and answerable to the people. Its usual signs are: **participation, rule of law, transparency, responsiveness, consensus, equity and inclusion, effectiveness and efficiency, and accountability**.

Three tools support it.

- **Right to information** (सूचनाको हक): citizens may ask public bodies what they are doing.
- **Control of corruption** (भ्रष्टाचार नियन्त्रण): bodies that prevent, investigate and punish the misuse of public office for private gain.
- **Service standards**: a **citizen charter** (नागरिक बडापत्र), public hearings and complaint handling.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Good governance**

- **Article 51 (b)** of the Constitution: public administration is to be fair, competent, impartial, transparent, **free from corruption**, accountable and participatory, so that people get services easily and equally.
- **Good Governance (Management and Operation) Act, 2064** (सुशासन (व्यवस्थापन तथा सञ्चालन) ऐन, २०६४): authenticated on **23 Magh 2064 (6 February 2008)**; its Rules came in 2065. It requires:
  - a **citizen charter** displayed in every office that serves the public;
  - **public hearings** (सार्वजनिक सुनुवाइ) by heads of offices at province, district and local level;
  - a **complaint box**, opened **every three days**, and an officer responsible for complaints (nodal officer);
  - a **spokesperson** (प्रवक्ता) in every ministry, department and office;
  - use of **information technology** in office work.
- A citizen charter tells the service-seeker **which service, what documents, what fee, how long and which officer**.
- Provinces may make their own laws; Karnali, for example, has a Province Good Governance Act of 2076.

**Right to information**

- **Article 27:** every citizen has the right to ask for and receive information on any matter of personal or public concern, except information that the law keeps secret.
- **Right to Information Act, 2064** (सूचनाको हक सम्बन्धी ऐन, २०६४), with Rules of 2065.
- Every public body appoints an **Information Officer** (सूचना अधिकारी).
- Information must be given **at once** if possible, otherwise **within 15 days**.
- If it is refused, the applicant complains to the **head of the office within 7 days**, and may then appeal to the **National Information Commission within 35 days**.
- Public bodies must publish their main information on their own initiative **every three months**.
- **National Information Commission** (राष्ट्रिय सूचना आयोग): a **Chief Information Commissioner and 2 Information Commissioners**; term **5 years**; recommended by a committee chaired by the **Speaker**; formed on **22 Baisakh 2065 (4 May 2008)**. It is created by the Act, not by the Constitution.
- **National Information Day** is **3 Bhadra**.

**Control of corruption**

- **Commission for the Investigation of Abuse of Authority** (अख्तियार दुरुपयोग अनुसन्धान आयोग), Articles 238 and 239: a Chief Commissioner and four Commissioners; it investigates corruption by holders of public office and files cases (Chapter 3).
- **Laws:** Prevention of Corruption Act, 2059; Commission for the Investigation of Abuse of Authority Act, 2048; Special Court Act, 2059; Money Laundering Prevention Act, 2064; Public Procurement Act, 2063.
- **Special Court** (विशेष अदालत): hears corruption cases; appeals go to the Supreme Court.
- **National Vigilance Centre** (राष्ट्रिय सतर्कता केन्द्र): set up on **27 Shrawan 2059 (12 August 2002)** under section 37 of the Prevention of Corruption Act, under the direct supervision of the **Prime Minister**. Its work is **preventive**: awareness, monitoring and watching property statements.
- **Other watchdogs:** the Auditor General; the Public Accounts Committee of the House; the Department of Money Laundering Investigation; the Department of Revenue Investigation; the Public Procurement Monitoring Office.
- **United Nations Convention against Corruption:** adopted on 31 October 2003; Nepal is a party. **International Anti-Corruption Day** is **9 December**.
- 🔄 **Corruption Perceptions Index 2025** (Transparency International, published 10 February 2026): Nepal scored **34 out of 100** and ranked **109th of 182** countries. The score was the same as the year before; the world average was 42.
- 🔄 **Property inquiry:** on **2 Baisakh 2083 (15 April 2026)** the Government formed a commission led by former Supreme Court judge **Rajendra Kumar Bhandari** to collect and examine the property details of those who held high public office from 2062/63 to 2082/83.
- Anger at corruption and poor governance was a main cause of the Gen Z movement of Bhadra 2082 (Chapter 2).

## 4.8 Electronic governance and public service delivery (विद्युतीय सुशासन र सार्वजनिक सेवा प्रवाह)

#### 1 · Why it matters

Karnali's syllabus names electronic service delivery and e-governance. In every province, questions on the Nagarik App, the National Identity Card and "Digital Nepal" appear in the science and current-affairs share. This topic is also what the public will expect of you in office.

#### 2 · Simple explanation

**Public service delivery** (सार्वजनिक सेवा प्रवाह) is the way the State hands its services to people: a citizenship certificate, a birth registration, a health check, a road, an allowance. Good delivery is **easy to reach, quick, fair, low in cost and answerable**.

**Electronic governance** or **e-governance** (विद्युतीय सुशासन) means using information and communication technology — computers, mobile phones and the internet — to give services, share information and run offices. The citizen does not have to stand in a queue or meet a middleman; the record stays in the system; the work can be traced. It is a tool of good governance, not a goal in itself.

#### 3 · Key facts

**Ideas**

- **Four kinds of e-governance link:** Government to Citizen (**G2C**), Government to Business (**G2B**), Government to Government (**G2G**) and Government to Employee (**G2E**).
- **Stages of growth:** giving information online → two-way interaction → full transactions online → joined-up services across offices.
- **Benefits:** faster service; lower cost; service at any hour and from any place; transparency; fewer chances for corruption; better records.
- **Challenges:** the **digital divide** between town and village; weak internet and electricity; low digital literacy; **cyber security** and data protection; systems of different offices that do not talk to each other; staff habits.

**Laws and policies**

- **Electronic Transactions Act, 2063** (विद्युतीय कारोबार ऐन, २०६३): gives legal recognition to **electronic records and digital signatures** and punishes computer crimes.
- **Good Governance (Management and Operation) Act, 2064:** tells offices to use information technology in their work.
- **Information and Communication Technology Policy, 2072.**
- **Digital Nepal Framework, 2076:** approved by the Council of Ministers on **5 Kartik 2076 (22 October 2019)**; **8 sectors** and **80 initiatives**. The sectors are digital foundation, agriculture, health, education, energy, tourism, finance and urban infrastructure.
- **National Cyber Security Policy, 2080.**
- **Institutions:** 🔄 the **Ministry of Information and Communication** (called the Ministry of Communication and Information Technology until the ministries were regrouped in Baisakh 2083, May 2026); the Department of Information Technology, which has been under the **Office of the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers** since that regrouping; the National Information Technology Centre, which runs the Government Integrated Data Centre at Singha Durbar; the Office of the Controller of Certification (digital signatures); the Department of National ID and Civil Registration; the **e-Governance Board** at Singha Durbar.
- 🔄 The e-Governance Board began under an order of 2079 BS as the Electronic Good Governance Commission. An amendment to the order in 2083 adds a government software development and operation centre.

**Services in use**

- **Nagarik App (नागरिक एप):** one mobile application for many government services; launched in Magh 2077 (January 2021).
- **National Identity Card (राष्ट्रिय परिचयपत्र):** a card with a unique number and biometric details. Distribution began on **3 Mangsir 2075 (19 November 2018)** in **Panchthar** district.
- **Hello Sarkar (हेल्लो सरकार):** the complaint service at the Office of the Prime Minister; toll-free number **1111**.
- **Others:** online application to the Public Service Commissions; electronic passport; online driving licence application; online tax filing and Permanent Account Number; the electronic procurement portal for public tenders; online company registration; online registration of personal events; electronic payment of government revenue.
- 🔄 **Policy and programme for 2083/84:** at least **100 government services** through the Nagarik App; a database built on the rule "give your details once, use them for every service"; single service centres in all offices; essential services open seven days a week; the National Identity Card to be linked with banking, health, education and social security.

**Public service delivery**

- **Tools required by law:** citizen charter; help desk; complaint box and nodal officer; public hearing; spokesperson and Information Officer (topic 4.7).
- **Other tools:** token system; mobile service camps; one-door (single window) service; client satisfaction surveys; public and social audits.
- **Ways of delivery:** directly by government offices; through public–private partnership; by contract with private firms; through communities and cooperatives.
- **At the ward office:** recommendations; registration of the five **personal events** — birth, death, marriage, divorce and migration; social security allowances.
- Under federalism most everyday services belong to the **local level** (Schedule 8), which is why the ward office matters most to citizens.

#### 10 · Short-answer question

**Question.** What is e-governance? State its benefits and the main challenges it faces in Nepal.

**Model answer.** **E-governance** is the use of information and communication technology by the government to deliver services, share information, involve citizens and manage its own work. It links government with citizens (G2C), businesses (G2B), other government offices (G2G) and employees (G2E). **Benefits:** (1) services become faster and cheaper; (2) people can use them at any time and from any place; (3) work becomes transparent and traceable, so corruption and middlemen are reduced; (4) records are safe and easy to find; (5) offices can share data and plan better. **Nepal's steps:** the Electronic Transactions Act, 2063; the Digital Nepal Framework, 2076 with 8 sectors and 80 initiatives; the National Identity Card; the Nagarik App; online tax, procurement and Public Service Commission applications; Hello Sarkar. **Challenges:** the digital divide between towns and remote villages; weak internet and electricity; low digital literacy; cyber security and protection of personal data; systems of different offices that are not linked; and resistance to change. **Way forward:** reliable infrastructure, one shared database, strong data-protection law, trained staff and help desks for citizens who cannot use the technology.

### D. Five short-answer questions

**1. Distinguish between exclusive and concurrent powers under the Constitution, with examples.**
*Points for the answer:* Article 57 · exclusive = one level alone: Schedule 5 (federal, 35 — defence, foreign affairs, currency), Schedule 6 (provincial, 21 — provincial police, provincial highways), Schedule 8 (local, 22 — basic and secondary education, local roads) · concurrent = shared: Schedule 7 (Federation and Province, 25 — criminal procedure, casino, tourism), Schedule 9 (all three, 15 — cooperatives, agriculture, disaster management) · lower-level law must not conflict with higher-level law (Article 57 (6)–(7)) · residual power with the Federation (Article 58) · disputes to the Constitutional Bench.

**2. Write about the appointment, term and role of the Province Chief.**
*Points for the answer:* Articles 163–167 · representative of the Government of Nepal · appointed by the President; 35 years; qualified for the Federal Parliament · five years unless removed earlier; not twice in the same province · oath before the President · appoints the Chief Minister and ministers; summons and addresses the Assembly; assents to Bills; issues ordinances · acts on the recommendation of the Provincial Council of Ministers · exercises executive power during federal rule (Article 162 proviso) · ceremonial post.

**3. What is a District Coordination Committee? State its formation and functions.**
*Points for the answer:* Article 220 · District Assembly = heads and deputies of all local levels in the district · it elects the Committee: at most 9 members — Chief, Deputy Chief, at least 3 women, at least 1 Dalit or minority member · term 5 years · functions: coordinate the local levels; monitor development work for balance; coordinate between federal and provincial offices and the local levels; other work under provincial law · helps the Provincial Assembly settle disputes (Article 235) · it coordinates, it does not govern; 77 in all.

**4. Describe the composition and functions of the National Natural Resources and Fiscal Commission.**
*Points for the answer:* Articles 250–251 · at most 5 members including the Chairperson; appointed by the President on the recommendation of the Constitutional Council; 6 years; age limit 65; minimum age 45; 20 years' experience · functions: basis for sharing revenue from the Federal Consolidated Fund; recommends equalisation grants; studies the basis of conditional grants; sharing between province and local levels; ceiling for internal loans; sharing of investment in and returns from natural resources; studies likely disputes · importance: balanced and transparent fiscal federalism (Article 60).

**5. How can a citizen obtain information from a public office? Explain the procedure.**
*Points for the answer:* Article 27; Right to Information Act, 2064 · apply to the Information Officer · information at once or within 15 days · if refused, complain to the head of the office within 7 days · appeal to the National Information Commission within 35 days · the Commission may order the information to be given · some information may lawfully be kept secret (security, investigation, privacy and the like) · offices must publish key information every three months · links with the citizen charter and public hearing.

### E. Common mistakes

1. Calling the **district** a tier of government. The tiers are Federation, Province and Local level.
2. Giving **residual power** to the provinces. It belongs to the Federation.
3. Mixing **Schedule 7** (two levels) with **Schedule 9** (three levels).
4. Writing that the Province Chief is **elected**. The President appoints.
5. Writing **25 ministers** for a province. The limit is 20 percent of the Assembly.
6. Swapping **4 + 2** (village) and **5 + 3** (municipality).
7. Making the **Mayor** the head of the Judicial Committee. It is the deputy.
8. Writing **744** local levels. There are 753.
9. Applying **70 : 15 : 15** to all taxes. It applies to value-added tax and domestic excise only.
10. Saying the Fiscal Commission **distributes** grants. It recommends; the Government distributes.
11. Saying the Provincial Assemblies were re-elected in 2082. Only the House of Representatives was.
12. Using the **3 percent** threshold for Provincial Assemblies. There it is 1.5 percent.
13. Writing that the **Federal Civil Service Act** has been passed, without checking.
14. Counting the **National Information Commission** as a constitutional body.
15. Reading a **higher** corruption-index score as more corruption.
16. Treating a **government website** as the whole of e-governance.
